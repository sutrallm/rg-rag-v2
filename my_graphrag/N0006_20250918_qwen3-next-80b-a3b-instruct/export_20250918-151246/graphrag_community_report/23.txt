**Title: Zhishi, Sanghui, and the Path of the Shengzhe: Liberation Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved through direct, non-judgmental observation of bodily and mental phenomena, which reveals their impermanent and non-self nature. This awareness, cultivated by mindfulness, dismantles clinging and afflictions—root causes of suffering. Those who fully embody this awareness, known as shengzhe, have transcended cyclic existence by integrating moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom. Without this direct perception, attachment persists; with it, letting go becomes inherent, and freedom is realized.

**Zhishi arises from direct observation of impermanence and non-self**  
True wisdom emerges not through intellectual effort but through undistorted attention to the constant flux of thoughts, emotions, and sensations. When seen as transient and unownable—like clouds, waves, or wind—the illusion of a fixed self dissolves. This perception is the foundation for releasing attachment to meditation experiences, powers, outcomes, or any conditioned state.

**Mindfulness is the practice through which wisdom is cultivated**  
By sustaining clear awareness of present-moment phenomena, one recognizes their fleeting and selfless character. This continuous seeing, not conceptual understanding, generates the transformative knowledge that dismantles mistaken views of permanence and identity. Without this observational method, wisdom cannot arise.

**Liberation depends entirely on wisdom, not on concentration alone**  
Stable mental focus provides the clarity needed to observe, but without the resulting understanding of impermanence and non-self, even deep concentration becomes another object of attachment. Wisdom prevents this trap, ensuring that meditative states do not reinforce clinging but lead instead to release.

**The shengzhe are defined by perfected wisdom, not by supernatural attainments**  
What distinguishes the shengzhe is not ritual, power, or asceticism, but their complete realization that all phenomena lack inherent selfhood and permanence. This unshakable seeing erases ignorance and craving, making them free from the forces that bind beings to recurring existence.

**Afflictions sustain cyclic existence; their cessation ends rebirth**  
Suffering and rebirth are fueled by active mental disturbances rooted in the misperception that transient experiences are lasting or belong to a self. When wisdom reveals these as illusory, afflictions lose their power, and the cycle of birth and death unravels.

**Wisdom directly enables letting go**  
Recognition of impermanence and non-self does not require effort to relinquish—attachment naturally dissolves when reality is seen as it is. This is not suppression or denial, but the spontaneous cessation of clinging born of clear perception.

**Transcendence of cyclic existence results from the integration of moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom**  
Freedom is not achieved through any single practice, but through the coordinated unfolding of ethical living, mental stability, and direct seeing. Only when all three are matured does the full abandonment of ignorance and craving occur.

**Wrong perceptions are dissolved through mindful observation**  
The habitual misapprehension of experience as stable, satisfying, or self-owned is undone by sustained attention to its true nature. This process, grounded in mindfulness, breaks the automatic chains that perpetuate suffering and rebirth.
