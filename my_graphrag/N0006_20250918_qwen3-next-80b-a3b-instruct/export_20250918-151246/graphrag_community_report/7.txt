**Title: Chansu and the Path to Unconditioned Peace**

**Summary**  
Chansu, a state of meditative stillness grounded in focused mindfulness, enables mental clarity and emotional ease. It serves as a foundation for wisdom, which in turn dissolves attachment not only to worldly objects but also to spiritual achievements, including the very state of chansu. True peace emerges not through attainment but through non-clinging — letting go of all concepts, even those of progress, enlightenment, or moral superiority. Sustaining chansu requires persistent effort, yet its ultimate purpose is to be released, not preserved.

---

**Mental Calm Enables Wisdom to Arise**  
The stability and focus cultivated through chansu create the inner conditions necessary for wisdom to emerge. Without this foundational stillness, the mind remains turbulent and unable to perceive beyond surface-level patterns. Chansu does not produce wisdom directly, but it removes distractions that obscure deeper understanding.

**Wisdom Dissolves Attachment to Attainment**  
As wisdom arises, it reveals that clinging to any mental state — including the calm of chansu or the label of enlightenment — perpetuates bondage. Recognizing that even spiritual milestones are conceptual constructs allows the mind to release its grip on them, leading to a freedom unbound by achievement or identity.

**Effort Establishes, but Non-Attachment Completes**  
Sustaining chansu demands consistent and disciplined practice. Without persistent effort, the state cannot be maintained. Yet, the final stage of this path is not about deepening or preserving chansu, but allowing it to dissolve naturally when no longer needed — signifying that liberation lies beyond effort, in ease without demand.

**True Peace Lies in Non-Clinging, Not in States**  
The highest form of peace is not found in any attainable condition, whether meditative, moral, or spiritual. Even saintly qualities, when held as goals or markers of superiority, become obstacles. The enduring freedom comes only when all forms of clinging — including to stillness, purity, or progress — are abandoned.

**Freeing Is the Action of Letting Go**  
Freeing is not a passive condition but an active expression of wisdom — the continual choice to release attachment to any experience, even those considered sacred or desirable. This action is not a rejection of practice, but the fulfillment of its intent: to transcend the need for any reference point.
