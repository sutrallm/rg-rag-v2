Title: Internal Observation as the Path to Liberation

Summary:  
The core practice for achieving liberation lies in turning attention inward to observe mental phenomena—such as breath, thoughts, and emotions—as they arise in the present moment. This direct, unmediated awareness, performed without preference or aversion, is not merely a technique but the very substance of the practice. External rituals, places, or teachers are not central; liberation arises from dismantling delusion through consistent internal observation.

Internal Observation Is the Sole Path to Liberation  
Liberation is achieved exclusively through the inward examination of mental processes. External methods such as rituals, pilgrimage, or reliance on teachers are not decisive. The removal of delusion occurs from within, through sustained attention to the mind’s unfolding content, making internal observation the foundational and exclusive route to freedom.

Present-Moment Awareness Is Both Method and Content  
The act of observing breath, thoughts, and emotions as they appear in the now is not separate from the object of observation. The method of paying attention and the content being observed are two descriptions of the same unified practice. There is no distinction between how one observes and what is observed—both are inseparable aspects of the same discipline.
