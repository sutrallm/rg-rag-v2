**Title: Daily Practice as the Path of Chanding and Mental Purification**

**Summary**  
Sustained mindfulness in ordinary activities—such as walking, eating, breathing, and observing thoughts—constitutes the true path of 修 行. This approach, known as daily practice, is not dependent on retreat or ritual but on continuous awareness during routine life. Through this process, chanding matures into stable awareness, mental defilements are gradually cleansed, and the understanding of impermanence and non-self deepens, forming the basis of transcendent wisdom.

**Daily Practice Sustains and Matrices Chanding**  
Chanding develops stability and depth not through secluded meditation alone, but through the consistent application of mindfulness in everyday actions. The act of remaining present during walking, sitting, speaking, or breathing is not merely an accessory to meditation—it is its lived expression. Without this continuous engagement in ordinary moments, chanding remains unstable and ungrounded.

**Mindfulness Reveals the Transient Nature of Thought and Identity**  
By observing the mind continuously in daily life, patterns of thought and the sense of a fixed self are seen as fleeting and conditioned. This repeated recognition dissolves attachment to identity and reinforces the understanding that no permanent, unchanging self exists beneath the flow of mental activity.

**Ordinary Mindfulness Purifies the Mind of Afflictive States**  
The steady practice of awareness in routine activities serves as the primary mechanism for cleansing the mind of habitual afflictions. Letting go of reactions, resisting conformity to ingrained mental habits, and observing without judgment weaken the grip of craving, aversion, and ignorance, leading to progressive mental clarity.

**Daily Mindfulness Cultivates Transcendent Wisdom**  
The foundation of higher wisdom arises not from isolated insight moments but from the persistent observation of impermanence and non-self in the flow of daily experience. This ongoing discipline transforms perception, allowing wisdom to emerge naturally from lived awareness rather than from conceptual understanding.
