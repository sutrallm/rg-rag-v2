**Title: Zaizhi and Wu Zeng Bu Jian: The Path of Non-Attached Awakening**

**Summary**  
The text describes a developmental trajectory in meditative practice centered on zaizhi—transcendent wisdom that sees impermanence, suffering, and non-self—and its culmination in wu zeng bu jian, the unfluctuating enlightened mind. Chanding provides the foundational stability for zaizhi to emerge, yet it is not the final goal. Those who embody wu zeng bu jian no longer cling to meditative states or reject disturbances, having transcended all attachment, including to the practices that once led them there. Saintly beings are defined by this complete liberation from attachment and undistorted perception of reality.

**Chanding Enables Transcendent Wisdom**  
Mental stability cultivated through chanding is essential for zaizhi to arise. Without this steadiness, the recognition of impermanence and non-self cannot fully take root. Chanding serves as the reliable vessel through which deeper understanding manifests, but it does not, in itself, constitute liberation.

**Zaizhi Leads to Liberation from Attachment**  
Zaizhi allows one to relinquish attachment not only to worldly outcomes but also to the very meditative experiences that precede deeper realization. This wisdom dismantles the tendency to seek or hold onto any state, enabling a clear, unfiltered perception of reality as it is.

**Wu Zeng Bu Jian Represents the Mature State of Non-Attachment**  
The enlightened mind characterized by wu zeng bu jian neither amplifies nor diminishes experience. It observes without preference, recognizing that meditative states are transient tools. This state is not an achievement to be maintained but a natural expression of understanding—free from striving, rejection, or identification.

**Saintly Beings Are Defined by Complete Non-Attachment**  
Those termed saintly beings have fully realized zaizhi, and thus no longer bind themselves to any experience—whether pleasurable, painful, or meditative. Their state is marked by unshaken clarity and the absence of clinging, reflecting direct perception of reality in its ever-changing nature.
