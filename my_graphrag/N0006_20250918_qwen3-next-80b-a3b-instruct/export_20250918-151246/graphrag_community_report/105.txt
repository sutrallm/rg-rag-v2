**Title: Nirvana, the Saintly Being, and the Path of Mindful Liberation**

**Summary**  
Liberation is not a distant goal but a present-moment realization arising from the complete dissolution of craving, ignorance, and the illusion of self. It is achieved through unwavering mindfulness, ethical conduct, and meditative stability, which together extinguish mental defilements and reveal the mind’s inherent purity. A saintly being embodies this state—free from emotional reactivity, settled in stillness, and untouched by the cycles of birth and death. Liberation requires persistent dedication; without it, mental stability and wisdom cannot take root. True freedom is found not in attainment, but in the absence of clinging—to self, to practice, or to results. Nirvana is the natural condition of a mind no longer deceived by illusion.

**Ethical Conduct Enables Mental Clarity**  
Clean moral discipline removes harmful mental agitation and karmic obstacles, creating the inner conditions necessary for meditative stability and wisdom to flourish. By purifying action, ethical precepts reduce hindrances that obscure the mind, allowing it to settle into stillness and perceive reality without distortion.

**Meditative Stability is the Foundation of Wisdom**  
Chanding—the sustained, unwavering focus of the mind—provides the clarity and calm required for wisdom to arise. It is not a passive state but an active stabilization of awareness, enabling direct insight into impermanence, non-self, and the absence of inherent suffering. Without this stability, wisdom cannot take hold.

**Mindful Awareness Is the Living Path to Liberation**  
Continuous, non-judgmental attention to the present moment—through breath, feelings, and mental states—is not merely a technique but the very expression of freedom. Each moment of clear awareness dissolves attachment and reveals the nature of reality as it is, without fabrication. This is not preparation for Nirvana; it is Nirvana in action.

**The Illusion of Self Is the Root of Suffering**  
The belief in a fixed, independent “I” or “mine” is the fundamental deception sustaining bondage. Liberation occurs only when this construct is fully seen through and abandoned. When the mind no longer clings to a sense of self, craving, aversion, and ignorance naturally cease, and the cycle of rebirth ends.

**Nirvana Is the Absence of Clinging, Not an Attainment**  
Nirvana is not a state to be gained, a place to reach, or a reward to be earned. It is the cessation of all desire—包括对“修行”本身的执着—when the mind no longer grasps at gain or rejects loss. It is the pure, unchanging condition that remains when all delusions are dissolved, revealing the mind’s original purity.

**Dedication Is Essential to Sustain the Path**  
Persistent, diligent practice without slackness is the indispensable force behind mastery of meditative stability and the realization of wisdom. Without continuous effort, even the clearest understanding remains theoretical. True liberation requires unyielding commitment to the present practice.

**Inner Peace Arises from the Eradication of Defilements**  
Tranquility is not the result of external calm, but the natural outcome of mental defilements—desire, hatred, and delusion—being fully extinguished. This peace is unshakable, unaffected by pleasure or pain, because it is rooted in the direct perception of impermanence and non-self.

**Equal Mind Is the Manifestation of Nirvana**  
A mind that remains non-reactive, non-discriminating, and evenhanded toward all experience is not striving toward peace—it is peace itself. This balanced awareness, undisturbed by worldly conditions, is the immediate expression of Nirvana, not a remote ideal.

**Wisdom and Ethics Are the Twin Pillars of Liberation**  
Ethical conduct purifies external behavior, while wisdom purifies internal understanding. Together, they destroy the root causes of suffering: craving born of ignorance and action fueled by delusion. One cannot stand without the other; both are necessary to fully realize the mind’s true nature.

**The Mind’s True Nature Is Inherently Pure**  
Afflictions do not belong to the mind—they are temporary obscurations, like clouds over the sun. Liberation is not about creating purity, but removing what obscures it. When delusion is dispelled, the mind’s original clarity reveals itself, and with it, the entire realm is seen as pure.
