Title: Ru Shi Guan Zhao and Zi Li in Spiritual Practice

Summary:  
True spiritual practice is grounded in direct, personal observation of the mind and body, which reveals the impermanent and non-self nature of all experience. Liberation is achieved not through external authority or belief systems, but through individual mindfulness and the dissolution of habitual patterns. Mental clarity, cultivated through chanding, is essential to sustain this observation, and the resulting understanding naturally dissolves attachment.

—

Transcending attachment arises from direct observation  
When one observes the mind and body with precision, the illusion of a permanent self and ownership over experiences becomes evident. Feelings and thoughts are seen as transient and conditionally arising, which dissolves clinging to them. This unbinding is not an intellectual conclusion but a direct result of sustained awareness.

Self-reliance is the foundation of authentic practice  
Liberation cannot be delegated to teachers, doctrines, or miraculous interventions. It emerges only when the individual takes full responsibility for examining inner processes, questioning inherited beliefs, and purifying ingrained reactions. External guidance may point the way, but transformation occurs through personal effort.

Mental clarity enables accurate observation  
Chanding, the practice of calming and stabilizing the mind, is not an end in itself but a necessary condition for perceiving reality without distortion. Without this clarity, habitual patterns and emotional reactivity obscure the true nature of experience, rendering observation superficial or biased.

Accurate observation and self-reliance are mutually dependent  
One cannot maintain true observation without committing to self-reliance, and self-reliance without accurate observation becomes mere willfulness. The practice demands both: the discipline to look inward without dependence on external validation, and the precision to see clearly what is actually occurring in the present moment.
