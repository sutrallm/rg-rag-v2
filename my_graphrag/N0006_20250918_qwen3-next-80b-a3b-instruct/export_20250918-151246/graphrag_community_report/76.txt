**Title: Wu Wo Kong Xing and the Nature of Perception in Dependent Origination**

**Summary**  
The realization of non-self and emptiness (Wu Wo Kong Xing) arises from the understanding that all phenomena, including the six sense objects and the five aggregates, lack inherent existence and arise solely through interdependent conditions (Yuan He). This understanding eliminates attachment to “I” and “mine,” resulting in a mind unbound by clinging, expressed naturally as Wu Zhuo. The six sense objects are not inherently real or substantial; their emptiness is recognized upon seeing their conditioned nature.

**Perception of the Six Sense Objects Is Rooted in Conditioned Appearance**  
The six sense objects—sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, and thought—are not intrinsically real but are perceived as empty when the nature of dependent origination is understood. Their appearance does not imply permanence or selfhood; they are transient expressions of interdependent causes, and thus are neither to be grasped nor rejected.

**Non-Self Realization Unfolds Through the Five Aggregates**  
The recognition that the five aggregates (Wu Yun) have no permanent, independent self is central to the realization of non-self (Wu Wo Kong Xing). This understanding dismantles the illusion of a fixed identity, revealing that what is taken as “I” is merely a collection of transient, conditioned elements.

**Emptiness Is Rooted in Dependent Origination**  
The principle of dependent origination (Yuan He) is the foundational framework for realizing emptiness. All phenomena, including the self and sensory objects, arise only through interdependent conditions—no entity exists independently. This truth directly supports the dissolution of attachment and the emergence of unattached awareness.

**Letting Go Arises Naturally from Realization**  
When the nature of non-self and emptiness is fully realized, the expression of release (Wu Zhuo) occurs not as an effort but as a spontaneous consequence. Clinging ceases because the basis for attachment—the belief in inherent existence—is no longer sustained by perception or thought.
