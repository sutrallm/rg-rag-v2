**Title: Present-Moment Awakening and the Three Marks of Existence as the Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
Liberation arises not through future promise or external rituals, but through direct, sustained awareness of the present moment. This awareness is cultivated by observing the mind’s patterns—especially craving and aversion—and recognizing the Three Marks of Existence: impermanence, suffering, and no-self. These marks are not abstract concepts but the living reality of experience, forming the core of authentic teaching. Self-reflection is the practical means to sustain this awareness, allowing continuous correction of mental states and alignment with the Dharma’s essential meaning beyond form.

**Observing the Present Moment Reveals Liberation**  
Liberation is not a distant goal but an immediate realization that occurs when awareness is clear and non-reactive. It unfolds in the here and now, not through belief or future expectations, but through direct perception of mental and sensory phenomena as they arise. This requires sustained focus to see reality without distortion, allowing attachment and aversion to be recognized and released in real time.

**The Three Marks Are the Unchanging Core of Reality**  
Impermanence, suffering, and no-self are not philosophical ideas but fundamental truths evident in all experience. They are the essential meaning of the Dharma, transcending names, rituals, and teachers. Understanding them is not intellectual but experiential—they must be seen directly in current awareness to transform perception. Their presence in every moment is what makes liberation possible.

**Self-Reflection Sustains Awareness and Corrects Mental Deviation**  
Continuous self-observation is the practice that maintains steady attention and prevents mental complacency or discouragement. By watching thoughts and emotional reactions, one identifies craving and aversion as misguided views and returns to balance. This practice is not passive contemplation but active correction—it ensures awareness remains alive and accurate, making present-moment awakening consistent and reliable.

**The Dharma Exists Beyond Form, Only Revealed Through Direct Understanding**  
True teaching is not found in ceremonies, symbols, or authority figures, but in the unwavering law of reality as directly perceived. To follow it requires bypassing superficial interpretations and engaging with its essence—the Three Marks. Without this deeper recognition, even practiced rituals become empty. Authentic understanding emerges only when one sees impermanence, suffering, and no-self unfolding in every moment of awareness.
