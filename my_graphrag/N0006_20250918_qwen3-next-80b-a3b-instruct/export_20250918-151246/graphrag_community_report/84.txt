**Title: The Root Causes of Samsara and the Path of the Enlightened Sage**

**Summary**  
The cycle of rebirth and suffering arises from three pervasive afflictions: delusion, greed, and aversion. Delusion—the fundamental misunderstanding that phenomena are permanent, independent, or self-existent—is the origin of all other afflictions. It fuels the false sense of “I” and “mine,” which in turn gives rise to craving and clinging. Greed manifests as attachment to sensory pleasures, meditative states, and existence itself, perpetuating the cycle of birth and death. Aversion, as a reaction to unpleasant experience, disrupts inner peace and reinforces suffering. Liberation is achieved only through the eradication of these three afflictions by cultivating wisdom that sees reality as it is: impermanent, non-self, and interdependent. The enlightened sage, having uprooted delusion, greed, and aversion through moral discipline and meditative clarity, dwells in unshakable stillness, free from craving and aversion, no longer bound by the illusion of self or ownership.

**Delusion is the foundational error that perpetuates cyclic existence**  
Delusion arises from a failure to recognize reality’s true nature—misperceiving phenomena as permanent, independent, or inherently self-existing. This misconception manifests as clinging to the notions of “I” and “mine,” which obscures wisdom and sustains the cycle of rebirth. It is the root of all other afflictions and is equated with ignorance. Only through direct realization of non-self and impermanence can this distortion be dissolved. Its removal is synonymous with the attainment of liberation and the cessation of suffering.

**Greed binds beings to samsara through craving and attachment**  
Greed drives fixation on sensory pleasures, possessions, meditative attainments, and even existence itself. It originates from delusion and manifests as an insatiable thirst for gratification, reinforcing the illusion of ownership. This clinging sustains the machinery of rebirth by preventing release from attachment to transient conditions. Its presence obstructs meditative stability and inner clarity. The sage, having free from greed, no longer grasps at any condition, internal or external, and thus breaks the chain of craving that fuels cyclical existence.

**Aversion disrupts inner peace and reinforces suffering**  
Aversion emerges as a reaction to unpleasant experience and functions as a source of mental agitation and negative karma. It destabilizes equanimity and interferes with meditative practice. While distinct from greed, it shares its origin in delusion and plays an equal role in binding beings to the cycle of suffering. Its cessation occurs through insight into the nature of phenomena, leading to a mind undisturbed by attraction or repulsion.

**The enlightened sage embodies complete freedom through wisdom and non-attachment**  
The sage has fully realized the non-self nature of the five aggregates and eradicated all three afflictions—delusion, greed, and aversion. This realization extinguishes the basis for ownership (“mine”) and self-centered craving. The sage dwells in unshakable stillness, neither seeking praise nor avoiding blame. Liberation is marked not by external achievements but by the absence of clinging, aversion, and false perception. This state is the culmination of moral discipline and meditative insight, leading to the cessation of rebirth.

**Wisdom alone dismantles the structure of suffering**  
The only means to uproot delusion is through wisdom that directly perceives reality as impermanent, non-self, and empty of inherent existence. This wisdom disassembles the misconception of “I” and “mine,” depriving greed of its foundation. Clarification of the true nature of phenomena is not theoretical but experiential—it terminates erroneous cognition and ends the momentum of rebirth. No other practice, however virtuous, can replace this transformative understanding.

**Meditative equipoise serves as the method to subdue greed and aversion**  
The cultivation of inner stillness functions as a tool to distance the mind from the disturbances of craving and aversion. It is not an end in itself but a means to weaken the grip of these afflictions by creating space for clear perception. In this state, the practitioner observes phenomena without reactivity, allowing the underlying delusion to unravel. The goal is not to suppress desire temporarily but to remove its root through sustained insight into the nature of experience.

**Delusion and greed form the dual engine of samsara**  
These two forces operate in tandem: delusion generates the false sense of self and ownership; greed then propels action to acquire, retain, and reproduce the conditions that reinforce this illusion. Without the misperception of independence and permanence, craving could not arise. Without craving, the cycle of birth and death would lose its momentum. Their interdependence is central to the mechanism of suffering—neither operates in isolation.

**The destruction of self-attachment ends the cycle of grasping**  
The belief in a fixed, sovereign self is the scaffold upon which all clinging is built. When one sees that the aggregates are not “I” nor “mine,” the impulse to possess, avoid, or annihilate dissolves. This insight is the cornerstone of freedom—no longer invested in preserving a fictitious identity, the mind ceases to generate karma through desire. The sage’s liberation is inseparable from this annihilation of self-centered perception.
