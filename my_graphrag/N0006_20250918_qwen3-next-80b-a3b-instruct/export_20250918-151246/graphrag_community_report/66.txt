**Title: Chao Yue Zhi Zhuo and the Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
True liberation arises from the complete dissolution of clinging, achieved not through passive calm but through active realization of impermanence, non-self, and the non-possessible nature of all phenomena. Those who have fully embodied this understanding—known as Sheng Zhe—exhibit a mind unbound by conceptual fixation, likened to empty space. This state is not an endpoint of stillness but the natural outcome of wisdom that sees through the illusion of ownership and identity.

**Mind Like Empty Space**  
The condition of complete non-clinging is consistently described as “mind like empty space,” reflecting a state where conceptual boundaries and attachments have fully dissolved. This imagery is not metaphorical but a direct representation of mental freedom that emerges when one no longer identifies with or attempts to possess any experience, thought, or outcome.

**Non-Self as the Foundation of Release**  
The capacity to transcend attachment is rooted in the realization that there is no permanent, independent self to hold onto things. When the notion of a fixed identity is seen through, the impulse to grasp—whether toward material objects, emotional states, or spiritual achievements—loses its foundation and naturally ceases.

**Impermanence as the Catalyst for Letting Go**  
Letting go does not arise from willpower or discipline alone but from direct perception of all phenomena as transient. Recognizing that nothing endures creates the natural conditions for release, eliminating the illusion that anything can be permanently held or controlled.

**Transcendent Wisdom as the Essential Union with Calm**  
Calmness alone does not lead to liberation. True Chan Ding—the unified state of meditative absorption—is only authentic when inseparably joined with the wisdom that sees through attachment. Without this wisdom, stillness remains superficial; with it, the roots of clinging are uprooted, and freedom becomes tangible.

**Sheng Zhe as Embodiments of Non-Attachment**  
Those who have fully realized the nature of phenomena are distinguished by their complete absence of clinging. They do not merely understand these truths intellectually; their very being reflects the absence of grasping, making them living expressions of the state described as mind like empty space.
