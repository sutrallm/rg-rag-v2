**Title: Zhengnian as the Path to Liberation: The Foundational Role of Mindful Awareness in Daily Life**

**Summary**  
Liberation is realized not through external rituals, symbols, or future states, but solely through continuous, non-reactive awareness of the present moment in ordinary activities. This awareness—referred to as Zhengnian—serves as the sole method for dissolving suffering, cultivating wisdom, and revealing the nature of non-self. It is the indispensable foundation of Chanding practice and transforms every moment of daily life into a place of cultivation. True practice is measured by internal freedom from craving, aversion, and delusion, not by outward forms. The enlightened being embodies this awareness as a natural, unshakable state, where mindfulness is not a technique but the spontaneous expression of freedom.

---

**Mindfulness is the Only Path to Liberation**  
Liberation arises exclusively through sustained present-moment awareness. No external practice—whether rituals, chants, postures, or religious symbols—leads to freedom. The cessation of suffering is directly experienced now, not in some distant future or transcendent realm. This awareness dismantles ignorance at its root, making it the sole vehicle through which one exits the cycle of conditioned existence.

**Every Moment Is a Place of Practice**  
The location of spiritual cultivation is not confined to temples, meditation cushions, or designated times. Walking, eating, speaking, or even sitting in silence—all become opportunities for awakening when approached with undistracted attention. The integrity of practice lies not in where one sits, but in how one is present. This transforms the mundane into the sacred without requiring any change in external conditions.

**True Practice Is Internal and Formless**  
Spiritual progress is not measured by adherence to rituals, clothing, posture, or outward discipline. It is assessed by the absence of greed, hatred, and delusion in the mind, and by the continuous, non-reactive observation of thoughts and feelings. When practice is free from attachment to form, it aligns with the natural state of awareness that reveals the truth of reality without distortion.

**Mindfulness Sustains Concentration and Deepens Insight**  
Without continuous present-moment awareness, focused meditation cannot be stabilized or expanded. Zhengnian provides the grounding through which concentration arises, allowing stillness and undistracted clarity to develop. This awareness is not passive observation but an active, vigilant presence that sustains the depth and continuity of meditative absorption, making it inseparable from the practice itself.

**Awareness Dissolves Suffering Through Non-Attachment**  
By observing thoughts, emotions, and sensations as they arise and pass—without resistance, suppression, or identification—they lose their power to bind. This non-reactive noticing allows attachments to dissolve naturally, revealing their transient and selfless nature. The release of clinging is not achieved through willpower, but through the clarity that arises when one simply sees things as they are.

**Wisdom Emerges Directly from Mindful Observation**  
True understanding does not come from texts, doctrines, or mystical experiences. It arises from directly witnessing the constant flux of bodily sensations, mental states, and phenomena. This clear seeing generates insight into impermanence and non-self, dismantling foundational delusions and replacing them with unshakable clarity.

**The Enlightened Person Embodies Continuous Awareness**  
Liberation is not a static achievement but a lived reality. The saintly person remains anchored in the present, observing mental states without being carried away by them. Their freedom is expressed not through extraordinary feats, but through unwavering non-reactivity and presence in every moment, demonstrating that awakening is not separate from ordinary life.

**Mindfulness Is the Means to Realize Non-Self**  
By attentively observing the arising and dissolution of thoughts, feelings, and perceptions, one sees clearly that none of these phenomena constitute a fixed, enduring self. This direct perception weakens identification with mental content and opens the door to the realization of non-self—not as a concept, but as an experiential truth.

**Freedom Requires Constant Vigilance**  
Mindfulness is not maintained by occasional effort but by relentless, alert attention. Without disciplined vigilance, awareness slips into distraction, re-engaging the patterns of craving and aversion. True practice is sustained through ongoing, unwavering presence—moment after moment, activity after activity.

**Liberation Is Not a Goal, But a Natural Expression**  
Enlightenment is not something to be attained through striving. It is the revelation of what is already present when seeking, controlling, and resisting cease. Zhengnian is not a path to something new; it is the return to the innate clarity of awareness that exists beneath layers of conditioning.

**Daily Life Is the Sole Arena for Awakening**  
Spiritual cultivation occurs nowhere else but in the flow of everyday activities. Whether sitting silently or moving through the world, the practice is identical: to be fully present, watching the mind and body without interference. This integration makes awakening accessible to all, at all times, independent of circumstance or environment.
