**Title: Ethical Conduct, Mindfulness, and the Realization of Noble Ones**

**Summary:**  
The text describes a progressive spiritual path grounded in ethical conduct and sustained mindfulness, leading to the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. Those who attain this state—referred to as Noble Ones or beings of sainthood—are marked by unwavering equanimity, inner purity, and freedom from worldly influences. Their liberation is not abstract but manifest in non-reactivity, non-attachment, and the absence of suffering. Ethical discipline is the essential foundation for meditative stability, which in turn enables wisdom to arise. This wisdom, particularly the direct realization of core truths, is the defining feature of those who have transcended cyclic existence. Persistent effort is required throughout this process, as liberation cannot be achieved without sustained moral and mental discipline.

---

**Ethical Conduct as the Unshakable Basis**  
Moral discipline in action, speech, and thought establishes the mental clarity and stability required for meditative development. It is consistently described as foundational, not optional—without clear ethical conduct, deep meditation cannot arise. The saintly state is marked by perfected ethical behavior, evidenced by the complete absence of harmful actions and the full cultivation of virtue. This conduct is not merely ritualistic but an expression of inner purity, inseparable from the qualities of those who have reached advanced stages.

**Mindfulness Sustains Meditative Stability**  
Right mindfulness—the continuous, non-judgmental awareness of the present moment—is the mechanism through which meditative focus is maintained. It prevents distraction and supports the cultivation of stable mental concentration. The saintly being continuously upholds this awareness, enabling them to remain unperturbed by emotional fluctuations or external conditions. This sustained presence is not passive but an active, disciplined practice required to preserve the gains of ethical discipline.

**Wisdom Emerges from Integrated Practice**  
Wisdom does not arise independently but develops through the prior foundations of ethical conduct and meditation. The direct realization of core truths—particularly the Three Characteristics—is the hallmark of those who have completed the path. This wisdom is not theoretical; it is transformative, resulting in the complete uprooting of greed, hatred, and delusion. Its presence is confirmed by the individual’s freedom from suffering and their capacity to remain unmoved by pleasure, pain, or status.

**Liberation is Embodied in Non-Attachment and Equanimity**  
Those who have fully realized this path—Noble Ones—are defined not by titles or attainments but by their observable qualities: stillness, non-reactivity, and freedom from clinging. They do not grasp even to spiritual achievements. Their liberation is evidenced by an inner condition untouched by worldly or celestial influences. This state is synonymous with the end of cyclic existence and is revered by both human and celestial beings.

**Persistent Discipline is Non-Negotiable**  
Attaining the saintly state demands consistent, effortful practice that actively resists laziness and distraction. It is not a passive state but the result of rigorous moral and mental training. Without this persistent discipline, neither ethical purity nor wisdom can be fully developed. The path demands ongoing, unwavering commitment—it is through this sustained effort that ignorance and craving are ultimately overcome.
