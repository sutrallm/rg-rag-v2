**Title: Saints, Moral Discipline, and the Cessation of Afflictions**

**Summary**  
Saints are fully liberated beings who have eradicated all mental afflictions—greed, hatred, delusion, desire, anger, and ignorance—through the integrated cultivation of pure moral conduct, sustained discipline, unwavering effort, and profound wisdom. Their freedom from suffering arises not from external conditions but from inner transformation: ethical purity enables mental stability, which in turn fosters clarity and insight, culminating in the complete extinction of afflictions and liberation from the cycle of birth and death. Their state is self-sustaining, free from attachment to recognition, and marked by enduring peace and unwavering diligence.

**Pure moral conduct is the foundational requirement for mental stability**  
Ethical behavior that is clean, intentional, and aligned with spiritual principles creates the necessary mental groundwork for deeper states of concentration. Without this ethical clarity, mental disturbances persist, obstructing the development of stability. Thus, the cultivation of pure morality is not optional but essential, serving as the bedrock upon which higher spiritual attainments rest.

**Chanding arises from ethical clarity and leads to the natural subsiding of afflictions**  
When mental stability and clarity emerge from pure moral conduct, afflictions such as greed and distraction lose their grip. This is not achieved through forceful suppression but through the natural quieting of the mind that follows sustained clarity. Chanding, as a state of deep mental composure, is both the product of ethical purity and the mechanism through which afflictions dissolve without resistance.

**Saints have extinguished all afflictions through wisdom and discipline, not through avoidance**  
The liberation of saints is not the absence of experience but the complete transformation of perception. By developing wisdom and discipline, they have uprooted the mental poisons that bind ordinary beings. Their freedom is internal and irreversible: they no longer generate greed, hatred, or delusion because they see all phenomena as impermanent, selfless, and unsatisfactory.

**Liberation is the result of integrated practice, not isolated effort**  
Saints are defined not by a single virtue, but by the seamless harmony of moral discipline, persistent effort, and penetrating wisdom. Each quality supports the others: morality enables stability, stability cultivates insight, and insight fuels continued diligence. Their state is the cumulative outcome of sustained, unified practice—no single element can produce liberation on its own.

**Saints embody unwavering exertion and live free from attachment to praise or fear of death**  
Their path is marked by unceasing, enthusiastic perseverance—never slackening, never complacent. Yet their achievement is not motivated by external reward or fear of consequence. They are untouched by worldly recognition or dread of mortality because their being is grounded in an unshakeable inner truth. Their virtue inspires others not because it is performed for admiration, but because it radiates naturally from a mind entirely free.
