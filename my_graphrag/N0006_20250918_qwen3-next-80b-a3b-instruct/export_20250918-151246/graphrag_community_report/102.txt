Title: Spontaneous Freedom and Inherent Peace in the Unconstructed Mind

Summary:  
True freedom and peace arise not through effort, achievement, or external conditions, but from letting go of competition, fame, attachment to past or future, and all forms of grasping. This state is naturally present, complete in itself, and manifests as lived experience rather than abstract idea.

Spontaneous Freedom is Experienced, Not Conceptualized  
The liberation described is not a theory or goal to be reached, but a direct, lived reality. It emerges when one stops seeking, striving, or clinging, and instead simply exists in the natural state of the mind. This state is not constructed through practice or discipline, but revealed through the absence of internal struggle.

Inherent Peace Requires No External Conditions  
Peace is not dependent on success, recognition, or favorable circumstances. It is found in the abandonment of competition and the pursuit of fame, as well as in releasing fixation on what has been or what might be. This peace is self-sustaining, arising from the mind’s unconstructed nature, not from any change in the external world.

The Unconstructed Mind is Complete and Unshakable  
The fundamental state of mind described is inherently whole — neither gained nor lost, untouched by external influences. It does not need to be fixed, improved, or acquired. Its presence is guaranteed not by action, but by the cessation of grasping and contention, both inward and outward.
