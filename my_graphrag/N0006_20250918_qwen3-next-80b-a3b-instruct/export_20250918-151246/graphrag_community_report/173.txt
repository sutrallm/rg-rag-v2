**Title: The Buddha’s Path to Liberation: Morality, Mindfulness, and Wisdom as the Sole Means to Freedom**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved solely through the integrated practice of ethical conduct, sustained mental focus, and liberating wisdom, as taught by the Buddha. This path is not reliant on rituals or external forces but on immediate, present-moment awareness that reveals the impermanent and selfless nature of all experience. Greed and ignorance fuel continuous suffering, while continuous mindfulness dismantles attachment, allowing wisdom to arise and freedom to be realized here and now. Those who have fully realized this state are no longer bound by affliction or rebirth.

**Continuous Mindfulness Is the Operational Foundation of Meditative Concentration**  
The practice of moment-to-moment awareness of breath, emotions, and thoughts—known as Jien—is how meditative concentration is maintained. It is not a passive state but an active, unbroken observation that prevents afflictions from arising and creates the conditions for wisdom to emerge. Without this steady awareness, concentration cannot be sustained or deepened.

**Transcending Attachment Is the Direct Result of Seeing Impermanence and Non-Self**  
Wisdom that recognizes all phenomena as transient and lacking inherent self—Zhizhi—naturally dissolves clinging. This understanding reveals that nothing can be possessed, leading to non-attachment. When the mind observes experience without grasping, the power of greed and aversion is broken, leaving no foundation for suffering to take root.

**The Threefold Training Is the Only Path to Liberation**  
Ethical conduct, mental focus, and liberating wisdom form a unified, indispensable system. Each component supports the others: morality creates the stability for concentration, concentration deepens through insight, and insight uproots ignorance. This framework, taught by the Buddha, is presented as the sole method through which liberation is attained—no alternative route is acknowledged.

**Liberation Is an Immediate Present-Moment Realization, Not a Future Goal**  
Freedom from suffering and rebirth is not something to be achieved later; it is the clarity of awareness that arises when the mind is free from delusion in the here and now. Each moment of unbroken mindfulness is itself the beginning of liberation. This freedom is not ceremonial or aspirational—it is the direct outcome of dismantling ignorance through inner practice.

**Ignorance and Craving Sustain the Cycle of Birth and Death**  
The endless cycle of suffering is driven by two interlocked forces: ignorance of reality’s true nature and the emotional grip of craving. Ignorance blinds beings to impermanence and non-self, while craving binds them to repeated existence. These are not abstract concepts but active forces perpetuating bondage until their roots are exposed and removed by sustained mindfulness and wisdom.

**Perfect Liberation Is Embodied in Those Who See All Phenomena as Empty**  
Those who have fully realized liberation—the Shengzhe—live without clinging or aversion. They are not defined by achievement or status but by the complete absence of attachment, rooted in the unwavering perception that all things are empty of lasting substance. Their state is the natural expression of perfected wisdom.

**Sustained Effort Is Required for Wisdom to Mature**  
The development of liberating insight does not occur passively. Jingjin—the continuous, determined effort—must be applied to uphold ethical conduct, maintain concentration, and deepen wisdom. Without this persistent application, wisdom cannot grow, and the conditions for freedom remain unmet.
