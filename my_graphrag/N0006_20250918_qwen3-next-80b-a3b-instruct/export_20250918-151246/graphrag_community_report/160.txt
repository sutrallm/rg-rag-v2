**Title: Shenge and the Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary**  
Shenge represents the ideal state of a holy being, defined by the complete elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion. This state is achieved through the cultivation of moral discipline and wisdom, resulting in a mind of serene stillness. The cessation of these three afflictions is not merely a characteristic but the essential condition for liberation from cyclic existence.

**The Holy Being Is Defined by the Absence of the Three Poisons**  
A being considered holy or enlightened is not merely virtuous or wise but is distinguished solely by the total removal of greed, hatred, and delusion. These three forces are identified as the direct causes of bondage to samsara. Their absence is not incidental—it is the foundational condition upon which holiness is built.

**Serene Stillness Arises from Discipline and Wisdom**  
The tranquil, unmoving mind of Shenge is not a passive state but the result of active cultivation. It is sustained by moral discipline and guided by wisdom. These two elements work in tandem to dissolve the root afflictions, making serene stillness a natural consequence rather than an isolated trait.

**Liberation Requires the Complete Eradication of the Three Root Afflictions**  
Freedom from cyclic existence is not achieved through partial effort or temporary suppression. The text explicitly links liberation to the total elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion. Any presence of these forces implies continued entanglement, making their full removal the non-negotiable pathway to true release.
