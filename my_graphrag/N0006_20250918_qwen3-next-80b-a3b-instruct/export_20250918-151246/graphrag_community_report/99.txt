**Title: Letting Go as the Path to Spiritual Freedom**

**Summary**  
True spiritual freedom arises not from pursuing pleasurable states or performing external rituals, but from the internal practice of releasing attachment—to desires, identities, outcomes, and even the joys of meditation. Clinging, even to spiritual experiences, hinders liberation. Recognizing the transient nature of all phenomena naturally weakens attachment, making relinquishment the direct and essential pathway to freedom.

**Recognizing Impermanence Dissolves Attachment**  
When all experiences—pleasure, meditation bliss, and self-identities—are seen as transient, attachment naturally loses its hold. This perception does not require forceful suppression but arises from clear recognition of impermanence, which undermines the foundation of clinging.

**Liberation Comes Through Internal Release, Not External Practice**  
Spiritual freedom is not achieved through rituals or outward performances. It emerges solely from the internal act of letting go—relinquishing attachment to desires, deluded thoughts, and false conceptions of self. The mechanism of liberation lies in this inner release, not in external forms.

**Even Spiritual Joy Must Be Relinquished**  
The bliss experienced in deep meditation, though desirable, is not the ultimate aim. Clinging to such states—even those labeled spiritual—becomes a subtle form of attachment. True freedom requires releasing even these elevated experiences, as they too are impermanent and can obstruct deeper liberation.
