**Title: Wu Zhuo, Tan Yu, and Sheng Si: The Dynamics of Liberation in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved through non-attachment, which dismantles desire rooted in ignorance. Those who embody non-attachment have transcended clinging to experiences and attainments, thereby breaking the karmic chains that perpetuate rebirth. Desire fuels the cycle, while ignorance sustains desire. Active practice is required to overcome both attachment and laziness, leading to freedom from continual rebirth.

---

**Non-attachment is the direct expression of realizing emptiness and non-self**  
When one perceives the illusory and impermanent nature of all phenomena, clinging naturally dissolves. This letting go is not passive resignation but an active state of being that arises from direct realization of non-self and emptiness. The practitioner, called Sheng Zhe, exemplifies this state by residing in freedom from attachment to even spiritual achievements.

**Desire binds beings to the cycle of rebirth**  
Tan Yu, or craving, is not merely a personal whim but a fundamental force that perpetuates samsara. It emerges from a mistaken belief in the permanence and solidity of experiences, causing beings to grasp at transient conditions. This clinging generates karma that necessitates repeated existence in the cycle of birth and death.

**Ignorance is the origin of desire and thus the root of suffering**  
Wu Ming, or delusion, precedes Tan Yu. Without the fundamental misperception that phenomena are real and enduring, desire would not arise. The cycle of rebirth is maintained not by external forces but by this inner misalignment—believing in permanence where there is only impermanence.

**Active practice is necessary to transcend the cycle of birth and death**  
Passive understanding is insufficient. Jing Jin—the disciplined and vigilant engagement with practice—is required to dismantle attachments, including those tied to spiritual progress. Laziness, as a form of clinging to comfort or complacency, must be actively countered to break free from samsara.

**Non-attachment is the decisive factor in liberation**  
The cessation of rebirth is not achieved through ritual or asceticism alone, but through the complete release of attachment. As clinging is the mechanism that binds consciousness to samsara, Wu Zhuo disrupts this mechanism at its source. Without attachment, the conditions for rebirth cease to exist.
