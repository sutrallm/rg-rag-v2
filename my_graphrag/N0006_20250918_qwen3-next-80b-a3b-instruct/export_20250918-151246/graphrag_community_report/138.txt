**Title: The Liberation Path of the Shengzhe: From Mental Afflictions to Buddha-Nature**

**Summary**  
Mental afflictions — greed, hatred, and delusion — arise from ignorance and sustain cyclic existence. They are not conquered through suppression but dissolved through mindful observation, ethical discipline, and wisdom. Daily practice of non-reactive awareness weakens these afflictions, leading to non-attachment, mental stillness, and the revelation of the mind’s innate clarity. The enlightened being, or Shengzhe, has eradicated all afflictions by realizing non-self; this state is synonymous with a purified mind, true tranquility, and Buddha-nature. Liberation is not found externally but through the complete uprooting of delusion from within.

---

**The Three Poisons as the Core of Suffering**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion — collectively known as Tanchengchi — are the specific manifestations of Fannao, the fundamental mental poisons. These arise not from external circumstances but from wuming (ignorance), the misperception of reality. They bind beings to cyclic existence by fueling craving and aversion, and their persistence prevents mental clarity and spiritual advancement. Their cessation is not optional but essential for liberation.

**Non-Attachment as the Expression of Wisdom**  
Chize — the wisdom that sees all phenomena as impermanent and non-substantial — directly uproots afflictions by dissolving clinging to experiences. When one no longer grasps at pleasure or resists pain, afflictions cease to arise. This state of non-attachment is not passive detachment but an active recognition of reality’s true nature, and it defines the Shengzhe, the noble one who has transcended suffering.

**Mind Training as the Practical Path**  
Xiuxin — the daily practice of observing thoughts and emotions without reaction — is the methodology through which afflictions are weakened. This non-reactive awareness cultivates Chanding (meditative focus), which in turn enables the perception of impermanence. Over time, this sustained attention erodes the habit of identification with mental states, allowing the mind to return to its innate purity.

**Stillness as the Natural Result of Purification**  
Jingmi — inner calm and stability — emerges not through forceful quieting but as the natural consequence of affliction removal. When greed, hatred, and delusion no longer agitate the mind, tranquility arises spontaneously. This state is not a temporary meditation outcome but the enduring condition of the Shengzhe, whose mind remains unshaken by outer conditions.

**Non-Self as the Fundamental Key**  
WUWO — the realization of non-self — dismantles the root of all afflictions. The belief in a fixed, independent self is the foundation upon which craving and aversion are built. When this mistaken view is penetrated, the structures of greed, hatred, and delusion collapse naturally, since they have no valid object to cling to. This understanding is not theoretical but experiential, and it is the decisive turning point on the path.

**Diligent Effort as the Sustaining Force**  
Jingjin — continuous, unwavering effort — is indispensable. Without it, afflictions cannot be overcome; delusion and suffering persist. This diligence is not rigid self-discipline but persistent, gentle attention to the mind’s movements, ensuring that mindfulness is not sporadic but habitual. It is the thread that connects practice to realization.

**The Purified Mind as Buddha-Nature**  
Jingxin — the innate clarity of the mind — is not something to be created but revealed. When afflictions are removed through wisdom and practice, this clarity emerges. It is identical to Buddha-nature, meaning that Fo is not an external deity or distant ideal but the realized state of one’s own mind. The Sage embodies this state fully; thus, the Sage is Fo.

**Liberation as the Cessation of Afflictions**  
Niepan is not a transcendent realm but the complete and final elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion. It is the end of the cycle sustained by these poisons, achieved not through escape but through total transformation of perception. The Shengzhe resides in this state — no longer bound, no longer seeking, fully at peace.
