**Title: Stillness and Liberation in the State of the Enlightened Being**

**Summary**  
Inner stillness is the foundational and enduring condition of a liberated being, cultivated through mindfulness, ethical conduct, and the dissolution of greed, hatred, and delusion. This stillness is not passivity but an active, unshaken awareness that arises when mental disturbances cease. An enlightened being embodies this condition fully—free from suffering, unaffected by external circumstances, and characterized by unwavering equanimity, wisdom, and moral purity. Stillness and liberation are inseparable; one is the experiential expression of the other, achieved through the deliberate practice of calmness and non-reactive observation.

**Stillness Arises from the Cessation of Mental Disturbances**  
Mental chatter, craving, aversion, and agitation are systematically quieted through mindfulness and meditation, resulting in a profound inner quietude. This state is not emptiness but a clear, alert presence. The cessation of these disturbances is not incidental but cultivated—each moment of non-reactive awareness contributes to the deepening of stillness until it becomes stable and unshakable.

**Ethical Conduct Enables the Emergence of Inner Calm**  
Pure moral behavior removes the internal turbulence caused by unethical actions. When conduct is aligned with integrity, the mind naturally settles. Disturbances rooted in guilt, fear, or regret diminish, allowing stillness to arise without force. This demonstrates that external alignment supports internal transformation.

**Stillness and Calmness Are Mutually Reinforcing**  
The practice of calming the mind (chanding) directly produces stillness, and stillness, in turn, deepens the capacity for calm. A tranquil mind is required to enter profound meditative states, and those states further stabilize the mind’s quietude. This reciprocal relationship establishes stillness as both destination and vehicle on the path to liberation.

**Liberation Is Embodied in Unshakable Stillness**  
The state of liberation is not abstract—it is the lived experience of a mind that no longer reacts to craving or aversion. An enlightened being abides in this stillness continuously, undisturbed by internal storms or external conditions. This is the defining quality of complete freedom: the mind rests without needing to change or escape anything.

**Wisdom Is the Unshakeable Understanding That Liberates**  
True wisdom, as expressed by the enlightened being, is not intellectual knowledge but a direct, penetrating clarity into the nature of reality. It permits the being to remain undisturbed by worldly conditions because it sees through the roots of suffering—greed, hatred, and delusion—fully dissolved. This wisdom is inseparable from stillness, as it arises only when the mind is free from distortion.

**Enlightenment Is the Full Embodiment of Liberation**  
To be enlightened is not merely to attain a momentary state of peace but to have permanently uprooted all defilements. The enlightened being is liberated not because of special circumstances but because the inner conditions of craving, aversion, and delusion have been eradicated. This eradication results in a natural, radiant state of freedom—unconditional, unwavering, and beyond dependence on external factors.
