**Title: The Shengzhe: Embodiment of Liberation Through the Eradication of Greed, Hatred, and Delusion**

**Summary:**  
The Shengzhe is a fully liberated being who has uprooted greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering—and resides in constant inner stillness, wisdom, and non-attachment. Their existence is marked by unshakable moral purity, undisturbed mindfulness, and self-illumination, free from all dependence on external rituals, achievements, or validations. This state is not attained through supernatural displays or fleeting meditative experiences, but through sustained diligence, inward observation, and transcendent insight into impermanence and non-self. The Shengzhe’s liberation is so complete that even divine beings revere them not for power or form, but for the quiet radiance of their moral and spiritual completeness. Their freedom is lived in every moment—unmoved by pleasure or pain, unburdened by ego, and unattached even to spiritual attainments.

**

**The eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion defines the liberated state.**  
These three afflictions—repeatedly named as tan, chen, chi; san du; tanchendu; saishang—are the foundational obstacles binding beings to samsara. Their complete removal is not optional but essential to becoming a Shengzhe. Every description of enlightenment centers on their utter extinction: desire no longer arises, aversion no longer stirs, and ignorance no longer clouds perception. This is not a temporary suppression but a total dismantling of their roots, resulting in a mind that is unconditionally at peace. The Shengzhe’s freedom is measured solely by the absence of these three poisons; their presence anywhere would indicate the absence of true liberation.

**

**Inner stillness and constant mindfulness are the natural expression of liberation.**  
The Shengzhe does not occasionally enter meditative states but abides permanently in anzhu—unperturbed stillness—and zhengnian—unbroken mindfulness. Neither pleasure nor pain, neither acclaim nor insult, disturbs this state. This is not passive detachment but active, awake awareness, maintained through daily practice as a spontaneous way of being. Inward observation (neiguan) is not a technique they apply, but the lens through which they experience reality at all times. Their mind remains anchored in the present, observing without reaction, thus preventing the re-emergence of afflictions. This continuity transforms mindfulness from a practice into the very fabric of their existence.

**

**Transcendent wisdom reveals impermanence, non-self, and emptiness as the nature of reality.**  
The Shengzhe sees all phenomena—whether ordinary experiences or spiritual attainments—as impermanent, devoid of inherent self, and empty of fixed substance. This is not theoretical understanding but direct, effortless perception (wuzhizhou, zhizhang, zahi, chize, shenghui). This wisdom prevents clinging even to meditation, enlightenment, or moral purity. They do not grasp at states of calm or insight; they do not reject suffering. This non-grasping is not indifference but clarity: everything is seen as it is, without distortion, without need to possess or avoid. This is the source of their complete freedom—liberation arises not from achieving something, but from seeing through illusion.

**

**Diligent, unwavering practice is the path and the manifestation of enlightenment.**  
Jingjin—the relentless, unceasing effort—is not merely a means to reach liberation; it is the character of the liberated. The Shengzhe’s life is not one of idleness after realization but of continuous refinement. Their discipline is unwavering, never relaxed, never abandoned. This is not forced or strained effort, but a natural extension of wisdom and moral purity. It is the daily manifestation of ethical conduct (jie), meditative stability (chanding), and transcendent insight (zhihui). Without such diligence, the defilements would return; their presence confirms that liberation is sustained, not static.

**

**Non-attachment extends even to spiritual achievements and external forms.**  
The Shengzhe does not cling to meditation, enlightenment, or supernatural experiences. They have transcended wai xiang—rituals, miracles, sensory pleasures—and even wuzhu—a state of non-attachment to attainments. Their freedom is so complete that they do not seek to be free, nor do they identify with any spiritual identity. They do not claim to be enlightened; they simply live without clinging. This includes letting go of the notion “I am liberated.” True mastery is seen when even the attainment of liberation is not clung to. Their liberation is uncontrived, reflected in the absence of pride, comparison, or identity around spiritual progress.

**

**Compassion arises naturally from the absence of craving and aversion.**  
Cibei—the boundless, unconditional compassion—is not a deliberate act but the inevitable ripple of a mind free from greed and hatred. When one no longer sees themselves as separate, when the illusion of “I” and “mine” dissolves, the suffering of all beings is known as one’s own. The Shengzhe responds not out of duty, moral obligation, or emotional reaction, but simply because there is no barrier between self and other. This compassion is equal, untainted by preference, and unbound by expectation. It flows because afflictions are gone; it does not arise to fix suffering, but because suffering is clearly seen—and nothing resists it.

**

**Liberation is not a transcendence of worldly life but its complete transformation.**  
The Shengzhe is not removed from the world; they live fully within it—walking, speaking, eating, responding—but are no longer moved by it. Their enlightenment is not confined to meditation halls or mountain retreats. It is expressed in ordinary conduct: eating without craving, speaking without deceit, acting without ego. They do not seek isolation to remain pure; they remain pure whether surrounded by chaos or solitude. This is the living expression of fangxia and jiefu—utter non-attachment realized in daily interaction. Their freedom is not otherworldly; it is precisely what allows them to be fully present in the world without being ensnared by it.

**

**The Shengzhe is revered even by divine beings, not for power, but for moral completeness.**  
Even celestial beings, who dwell in higher realms of existence, recognize and honor the Shengzhe. Their reverence is not for miracles, longevity, or supernatural abilities, but for the unblemished calm, the absence of defilement, and the quiet dignity of one who has gone beyond all conditioning. The Shengzhe does not perform, proclaim, or seek acknowledgment. Their radiance lies in their purity—the elimination of the three poisons—and in this, they become a true refuge. Their authority is not inherited or earned through status, but revealed through the totality of their being: no greed, no hatred, no delusion, no self.

**

**The Shengzhe embodies the integration of discipline, concentration, and wisdom as a single, inseparable reality.**  
Jie (moral conduct), ding (concentration), and hui (wisdom) are not sequential stages but interwoven dimensions of one awakened life. Moral discipline supports mental stillness; stillness deepens insight; insight reinforces ethical action. There is no division between practice and attainment. The Shengzhe does not “have” jie, ding, or hui—they are these qualities in motion. They are jiede, chanding, zhizhi, zhihui, all experienced at once. Liberation is not the result of sequencing these elements, but the natural flowering of their unified presence. Their very existence demonstrates that enlightenment is not achieved by accumulating qualities, but by embodying their seamless unity.
