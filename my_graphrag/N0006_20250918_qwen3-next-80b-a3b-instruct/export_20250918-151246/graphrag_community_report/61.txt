**Title: Wisdom, Attachment, and the Nature of Meditation Practice**

**Summary:**  
True meditative practice is rooted in non-attachment to pleasurable states and spiritual experiences. Such attachment arises from ignorance and manifests as self-identification with progress, which obstructs liberation. Wisdom, as the clear recognition of impermanence and non-self, is the only force capable of dissolving this attachment and returning practice to its essential nature.

**Wisdom dissolves attachment to meditative experiences**  
The clarity that arises from seeing all phenomena as impermanent and without inherent self enables one to let go of attachment to joy and spiritual states experienced in meditation. Without this understanding, even deep meditative states become objects of clinging, preventing true freedom.

**Attachment distorts the essence of meditation**  
Those who genuinely cultivate meditative discipline do not cling to bliss or perceived achievements. To hold onto such experiences is not progress, but a deviation from the core purpose of practice, turning meditation into a pursuit of personal gain.

**Attachment originates in ignorance**  
The root of clinging is not desire alone, but a fundamental misunderstanding of reality. When one fails to perceive the true nature of experience—as transient and not belonging to a fixed self—attachment naturally arises as a mistaken reliance on fleeting conditions.

**Self-conceit manifests as attachment to spiritual progress**  
A form of attachment is the sense of superiority or identity tied to one’s meditative accomplishments. This self-centered view, even when framed as spiritual growth, reinforces the illusion of a permanent self and inhibits deeper release.
