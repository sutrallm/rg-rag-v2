**Title: The Nature of Mind and Liberation in the Doctrine of Non-Self**

**Summary:**  
The mind’s true nature is originally pure, not corrupted but obscured by transient afflictions. Liberation is not achieved by acquiring qualities, powers, or states, but through the dismantling of delusion and attachment—including attachment to the practice itself. Realizing that there is no fixed self to be saved or defiled reveals that freedom is the natural state when craving and clinging cease. Nirvana is not an end to be reached but the full亮相 of what was always present, once obscurations are removed.

**The mind’s purity is intrinsic, not attained**  
The mind is described as inherently pure, with afflictions likened to clouds temporarily blocking sunlight. This purity is not something forged through effort but is the fundamental condition of the mind. Effort, therefore, does not aim to create purity but to reveal it by removing what covers it.

**Liberation is freedom from craving, not achievement of a state**  
True freedom is not found in attaining sainthood, heaven, or supernatural abilities. It emerges solely when all forms of craving—包括对“修行”的执着—are abandoned. This state, known as Nirvana, is characterized by the absence of desire and the absence of any sense of gaining or losing.

**Non-self confirms original purity**  
The understanding that there is no enduring “I” means there is no entity capable of being defiled. This realization of non-self directly upholds the notion of original purity, because without a self to be stained, the mind’s nature remains untouched by afflictions.

**Nirvana is the cessation of all clinging**  
Nirvana is not a destination or reward but the complete ending of attachment. It is the direct manifestation of “no desire, no attainment,” a condition where the mind no longer seeks, grasps, or resists—existing in perfect equilibrium of no gaining, no losing.

**True freedom requires relinquishing attachment to practice**  
Even the pursuit of spiritual practice, if attached to as a means to an end, becomes an obstruction. True freedom arises only when one lets go of all goals—including the goal of liberation—so that the mind rests in its natural state, free from striving.
