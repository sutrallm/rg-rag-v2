**Title: Liberation through Present-Moment Awareness and the Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is the complete cessation of suffering and the cycle of rebirth, achieved solely through inner transformation—rooted in direct, non-reactive awareness of the present moment and the total dissolution of greed, hatred, and delusion. It is not attained through rituals, supernatural experiences, meditative states, or external authorities, but through sustained ethical conduct, mental discipline, and wisdom. The realization of non-self dismantles the illusion of identity, ending clinging and afflictions. Stillness arises naturally when mental agitation ceases, serving as both foundation and expression of liberation. Continuous mindfulness in ordinary activities, not secluded practice, is the true path. Diligent, non-complacent effort is indispensable; without it, ignorance persists. True freedom is the spontaneous state of non-attachment—where nothing is sought, nothing is clung to, and the mind rests in unconditioned peace.

---

**Foundational Understanding Requires Right View**  
The beginning of liberation rests in zhengjian—the clear comprehension of karma, impermanence, and non-self. Without this foundational insight, all other practices remain entangled in samsara. It is not theoretical knowledge but a lived recognition that dismantles false assumptions about permanence and selfhood, generating the dispassion necessary for progress.

**Liberation Emerges Only Through the Cessation of the Three Poisons**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the root obstacles to freedom. Their complete eradication is synonymous with liberation. No meditative state, ritual, or external intervention can replace their removal. The mind cannot be free while any trace of craving, aversion, or ignorance remains active.

**Stillness Is the Natural State of a Freed Mind**  
Stillness is not passive numbness but an active, unshakable calm—a mind no longer stirred by craving, aversion, or conceptual noise. It arises when mental fetters dissolve through awareness and non-attachment. This state is both the result of liberation and its experiential hallmark, evident in the unflinching equanimity of those who have transcended suffering.

**Present-Moment Awareness Is the Sole Path to Realization**  
Liberation is found not in retreats or extraordinary experiences, but in the continuous, non-reactive observation of ordinary activities—walking, eating, breathing, speaking. This awareness reveals the transient nature of all phenomena, dissolving attachment as it arises. It is the direct mechanism through which ignorance is weakened and wisdom emerges.

**Non-Attachment Is the Defining Condition and Expression of Freedom**  
True liberation cannot exist without the complete letting go of clinging—to objects, identities, spiritual achievements, and even meditative bliss. Non-attachment is not suppression but clear seeing: recognizing phenomena as they are, without grasping or resistance. It is both the pathway to liberation and its natural fruit, manifesting as effortless presence and inner stillness.

**Diligent Practice Is the Essential Driver of Progress**  
Jing-jin—persistent, non-lazy effort—is the force that prevents regression into ignorance and complacency. Without sustained exertion in ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom, the mind remains bound. Effort is not about strain, but unwavering consistency in cultivating mindfulness and insight through daily life.

**No-Self Is the Decisive Insight That Unmakes Suffering**  
The realization that there is no permanent, independent “I” dismantles the foundation of egoic clinging. This insight does not come through belief but through direct observation of mental and bodily phenomena as they arise and pass. When the illusion of self collapse, craving and aversion cease—ending the cycle of rebirth.

**Meditative States and Psychic Powers Are Distractions, Not Goals**  
Even the most peaceful or blissful meditative experiences, or extraordinary mental abilities, do not lead to liberation if clung to. Attachment to these states reinforces the illusion of a self that attains or possesses enlightenment. True freedom ignores all such phenomena as transient conditions, not achievements.

**Liberation Is Not Achieved, but Revealed**  
It is not an external goal to be reached, a reward to be earned, or a state to be acquired. Liberation is the natural unfolding of clarity when delusion and attachment are removed. There is nothing to gain—only what was obscured is seen. This is non-attainment: the cessation of striving, the end of seeking.

**Awakening Is Self-Realized, Not Granted**  
No deity, guru, ritual, or external force can bestow freedom. The path is walked alone, through personal observation and inner effort. The Buddha pointed the way, but each being must realize truth for themselves—through direct insight, not faith or dependency.

**The Saint Embodies Liberation in Daily Living**  
One who has attained liberation does not dwell in isolation or perform special acts. Their presence is marked by moral purity, unwavering calm, non-reactivity, and radiant wisdom. They move through the world without clinging, unaffected by gain or loss—living the truth they have realized. Their life is the demonstration of the path itself.

**True Practice Is Non-Practicing**  
When liberation is fully realized, effort dissolves into spontaneity. There is no longer a sense of “I am practicing” or “I must achieve.” The mind rests in natural awareness—neither grasping nor rejecting. This is non-practice: freedom expressed not through action, but through the absence of internal struggle.
