**Title: Chao Yue Zhi Zhuo and the Path to Liberation Through Non-Self and Impermanence**

**Summary**  
True liberation arises from the complete dissolution of attachment, which is made possible only through the direct realization that all phenomena—including the sense of self—are impermanent and devoid of inherent essence. This understanding is cultivated through mindfulness sustained in all activities, supported by meditative stillness and transcendent wisdom. Liberation is not merely a goal but a state of being free even from the notion of attainment, marked by a mind like empty space, unshaken by pleasure, pain, or spiritual achievements. Ethical conduct, concentration, and wisdom must integrate to uproot the illusion of self, ending suffering and the cycle of rebirth.

**Transcending All Attachment is the Essence of Liberation**  
Liberation cannot be achieved without relinquishing all forms of clinging, including attachment to spiritual outcomes such as liberation itself. Those who have fully realized this state do not identify as practitioners or claim attainment; they abide without grasping, even to the idea of having reached freedom. This radical non-attachment is not passive indifference but the natural condition of a mind that sees through all illusions of permanence and ownership.

**The Self is an Illusion Constructed from Transient Aggregates**  
The sense of “I” or “mine” is not a fixed entity but a temporary gathering of five components: form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness. These aggregates are in constant flux, and the belief that any of them constitute a permanent self is the root of suffering. When this illusion is directly observed and understood, the basis for clinging dissolves, and with it, the cycle of bondage.

**Impermanence is the Foundation for Recognizing Non-Self**  
All phenomena—physical, mental, and spiritual—arise and pass due to causes and conditions. This constant change is not abstract but must be seen directly through meditative observation. When one perceives that nothing remains stable, even the notion of a self collapses, since there is no enduring core to hold onto. This realization of impermanence is the very doorway through which non-self is perceived and attachment is released.

**Transcendent Wisdom Arises Only Through Meditative Stillness**  
Mental clarity cultivated in meditation is not an end in itself but the necessary ground for wisdom to emerge. Stillness allows for the clear perception of impermanence and non-self. Without this foundation, even ethical conduct or disciplined practice remains incomplete. True meditative absorption must be unified with this corrective insight—calmness alone cannot uproot attachment or lead to liberation.

**Mindfulness Sustains Liberation in Everyday Life**  
Liberation is not confined to formal meditation but must be lived continuously through unwavering awareness in all actions—walking, standing, sitting, lying down. This daily practice is described as the permanent refuge because it prevents the mind from drifting into fixation on past or future, anchoring presence as the only stable ground. Through mindfulness, wisdom becomes active, and the state of non-attachment is not occasional but constant.

**The Mind Like Empty Space is the Expression of Freedom**  
When clinging and conceptual fixation dissolve, the mind is no longer bound by boundaries, identities, or desires. It becomes vast, open, and unobstructed—comparable to empty space. This state is not a mystical experience but the natural result of recognizing that nothing within experience can be possessed, owned, or held. It is the endpoint of eradicating afflictions and the only condition in which true peace arises.
