**Title: Cultivating Chanding Through Present-Moment Awareness to Counter Wangnian**

**Summary**  
Chanding is established and deepened through consistent awareness of the present moment during all daily activities, not merely during formal meditation. Delusory mental states—wangnian—arise from craving, aversion, or ignorance, diverting attention from the present and fragmenting mental stability. The practice of non-reactive observation directly neutralizes wangnian, restoring clarity and stillness. Without present-moment awareness, chanding cannot be sustained.

**Chanding Requires Continuous Present-Moment Engagement**  
The stabilization of chanding is not confined to meditation sessions. Every routine action—breathing, walking, eating, sitting—becomes a vehicle for cultivating chanding when performed with undistracted, non-reactive attention. This means that the practice is embedded in the fabric of daily life, making awareness the ongoing foundation of mental clarity rather than an isolated exercise.

**Wangnian Arises From Attachment and Disrupts Mental Stability**  
Delusory thoughts, known as wangnian, originate from attachment to self or external phenomena, fueled by craving, aversion, or ignorance. These mental distortions manifest as wandering, discursive thinking that actively pulls awareness away from the present. Their effect is to scatter mental focus and undermine the stillness necessary for chanding to take root.

**Non-Reactive Observation Eliminates Delusory Mental States**  
By observing thoughts and sensations without reaction or identification, the mind ceases to be swept away by wangnian. This method of awareness does not suppress distractions but disarms them by withholding engagement, thereby preventing the cycle of mental dispersion. The cessation of reactivity is the key mechanism through which clarity and stability are restored.
