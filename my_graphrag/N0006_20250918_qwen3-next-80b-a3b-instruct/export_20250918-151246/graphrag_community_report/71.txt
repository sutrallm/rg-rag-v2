**Title: Sheng Hui, Sheng Yue, and the Path of the Sage**

**Summary**  
True freedom is achieved not through clinging to meditative joy or concentration, but through transcendent wisdom, which recognizes all experiences as impermanent and devoid of self. The sage is distinguished by this wisdom and stable concentration, which together prevent attachment to even the most pleasant meditative states.

**Transcendent Wisdom Transcends All Meditative States**  
Transcendent wisdom renders meditative experiences—both joyful and concentrated—no longer objects of attachment. It reveals their impermanent and non-self nature, allowing the practitioner to move beyond them rather than remain fixed in pursuit of pleasant states.

**Meditative Joy Is Not the Final Goal**  
The pleasant state of meditative joy, though valuable, is recognized by the wise as temporary and not an endpoint. Clinging to it impedes progress; its value lies only in how it is seen through the lens of impermanence.

**The Sage Is Defined by Non-Attachment and Stability**  
The sage does not achieve liberation through transient experiences but through the continuous presence of transcendent wisdom and unshakable concentration. These two qualities together ensure that no meditative state, however refined, is mistaken for ultimate freedom.

**Realization of Non-Self Eliminates All Notions of Attainment**  
Understanding that phenomena lack inherent self removes the basis for claiming progress or achievement in practice. This realization is not a milestone to be reached but a continuous perspective that dissolves attachment to any state, including meditative ones.
