**Title: The Holy One: Mastery of the Three Poisons and the Path to Transcendence**

**Summary:**  
The enlightened being, known as Sheng Zhe, has wholly severed greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering and rebirth. This liberation is not achieved through meditative concentration alone, but through the irreversible integration of moral discipline, sustained diligent practice, and transcendent wisdom. Such a being remains unmoved by sensory stimuli, maintains unwavering mindfulness, and embodies pure tranquility. Even celestial beings, though powerful and long-lived, hold the Sheng Zhe in reverence, recognizing their complete transcendence of all worldly and heavenly conditions.

---

**Mastery of the Three Poisons Defines Enlightenment**  
The complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion is the singular criterion of the Sheng Zhe’s awakening. These poisons are the foundational sources of bondage to cyclic existence; their absence distinguishes the enlightened from all other beings, including celestial beings. No degree of power, longevity, or celestial status equates to this liberation.

**Diligent Practice Sustains the Enlightened State**  
The Sheng Zhe is not a passive state but the continuous outcome of Je Jin Xiu—uninterrupted, vigilant effort in daily life. This diligent practice is not a temporary discipline, but a permanent habit of guarding the mind, preventing regression, and deepening stability. It preserves moral conduct and meditative concentration against the pull of distraction and attachment.

**Moral Discipline Anchors Spiritual Realization**  
Pure moral conduct, or Jie Jing, is the essential foundation for inner clarity and stability. It eliminates remorse and mental turbulence, creating the conditions for concentration to arise and endure. Without it, even deep meditative states remain fragile and incomplete.

**Concentration Without Wisdom Is Incomplete**  
Chan Ding, or meditative concentration, is necessary but insufficient on its own. The Sheng Zhe embodies concentration not as an isolated achievement, but as the natural expression of a mind unified by wisdom and ethical purity. True tranquility arises not through force, but through the harmonious unfolding of all three qualities.

**Transcendent Wisdom Encompasses Non-Attachment to Attainment**  
The Sheng Zhe possesses wisdom that sees beyond all phenomena, including the notion of enlightenment itself. Liberation includes freedom from clinging—even to the idea of having transcended. This is the hallmark of transcendental understanding: no attachment remains, not even to spiritual accomplishments.

**Guarding the Six Sense Faculties Maintains Inner Stillness**  
The eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, and mind are the gateways through which distraction enters. The Sheng Zhe continuously restrains these faculties not through suppression, but through unwavering mindfulness. This constant vigilance prevents sensory input from stirring the poisons and preserves the natural peace of an unconditioned mind.

**Even Celestial Beings Revere the Transcendent**  
Though heavenly beings enjoy superior realms of pleasure and power, they remain entangled in samsara. The Sheng Zhe, having severed all ties to birth and death, is revered by them—not as a distant ideal, but as a living embodiment of what is beyond all conditioned existence. Their admiration reflects the undeniable supremacy of complete liberation over all forms of worldly or heavenly attainment.

**Purity and Tranquility Are the Natural Byproducts of Eradication**  
Qing Jing—purity and stillness—is not cultivated as a goal, but emerges spontaneously when greed, hatred, and delusion are fully uprooted. The Sheng Zhe does not strive for peace; they dwell in it, because the causes of disturbance no longer exist. This state is inseparable from the perfection of moral discipline, meditative stability, and transcendent wisdom.
