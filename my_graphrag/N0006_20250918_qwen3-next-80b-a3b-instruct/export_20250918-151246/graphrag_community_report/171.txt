**Title: Jie Ding Hui and the Realization of Liberation in Daily Life**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is not a future attainment but an immediate realization possible in every present moment through the unified cultivation of ethical conduct, meditative stability, and penetrating wisdom. This triad uproots the roots of suffering—greed, hatred, and delusion—by revealing the interdependent, impermanent nature of all phenomena. Awakening arises not through external rituals or special places, but through undisturbed, non-attached awareness in ordinary activities. The self is seen as a construct that dissolves in clear observation, and freedom is synonymous with seeing reality as it is, without grasping or resistance.

---

**The Triad of Ethical Conduct, Meditative Stability, and Wisdom is the Complete Path to Freedom**  
When ethical purity, stable meditative focus, and clear wisdom are fully developed together, they dismantle the threefold source of suffering: greed, hatred, and delusion. This unified practice is not partial or sequential but essential in its wholeness. Only through their joint cultivation can one transcend the cycle of conditioned existence and realize true liberation.

**Liberation Is Found in the Present Moment, Not in Future States or External Places**  
Freedom is not deferred to an afterlife, a distant enlightenment, or a sacred location. Every breath, thought, and action—whether walking, eating, or sitting—is a direct opportunity to awaken. The present moment is not a stepping stone but the very ground of liberation, identical with nirvana when the mind is undisturbed and unattached.

**Mindful Observation Unfolds Inner Clarity Without Effort or Suppression**  
The practice of witnessing thoughts, emotions, and sensations as they arise and pass—without reaction, indulgence, or resistance—is the means by which the mind becomes still and clear. This gentle awareness dissolves identification with mental States and reveals their transient nature, freeing the mind from the compulsion to cling or reject.

**All Phenomena Arise and Cease Due to Conditions, and Seeing This Is Liberation Itself**  
The dynamic, interdependent nature of existence explains why suffering persists: attachment arises from mistaking impermanent conditions for lasting selves or objects. When this principle is directly seen, the mind no longer grasps or resists, and liberation unfolds naturally. This understanding is not intellectual but experiential, embedded in every moment of observation.

**The Idea of “Self” Is the Root of Bondage, and Its Release Is Nirvana**  
The belief in a separate, enduring self fuels the cycle of suffering. True freedom emerges when even the notion of the “practitioner” dissolves. This is not annihilation but the end of identity-based clinging. Nirvana is the state where the mind no longer constructs or defends a self, and thus no longer suffers.

**Every Moment Is the Field of Practice—There Is No Separation Between Life and Awakening**  
There is no distinction between formal meditation and daily life. What is called the meditation field exists wherever mindful awareness is present. Walking, thinking, and eating are not distractions from practice but the very medium of it. Awakening happens not apart from life, but within its most ordinary occurrences.

**Ethical Conduct, Meditative Stability, and Wisdom Define the Awakened Being**  
A person who has fully realized freedom is marked by no external sign but by the inner perfection of moral purity, undisturbed focus, and penetrating understanding. These qualities are not goals to be achieved but natural expressions of a mind unbound by illusion.

**Liberation and Nirvana Are Not Distinct States—They Are the Same Realization**  
The freedom from suffering and the direct perception of reality as it is are not two ends of a spectrum. Both refer to the same unforced, unclinging awareness that sees the arising and ceasing of phenomena without resistance. One is not the cause of the other; they are two names for the same unconditioned state of mind.
