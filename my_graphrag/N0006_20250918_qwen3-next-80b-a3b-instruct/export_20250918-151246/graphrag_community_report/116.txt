**Title: The Eradication of Greed as the Path to Liberation: Teachings on Wuyu, Non-Seeking, and Sacred Qualities**

**Summary:**  
The path to liberation requires the complete uprooting of greed, one of the three root afflictions alongside hatred and delusion. Greed manifests as attachment to the five sensual desires (wuyu)—form, sound, smell, taste, and touch—which are inherently impermanent and bound to suffering. Temporary calm (sedation) and spiritual striving, including the desire for enlightenment, are not liberation but obstacles, as they still stem from craving. True freedom arises through non-seeking, authentic awareness, and the cultivation of sacred qualities such as moral discipline, wisdom, and equanimity. Only those who have dissolved all attachment, including subtle greed for spiritual attainment, attain liberation. The Sage and enlightened beings embody this state, having transcended greed through insight into impermanence and non-self, thereby ending the cycle of rebirth.

**Transient Desires Bind to Suffering**  
The five sensual desires (wuyu) are the primary objects of greedy attachment. Though pleasurable in the moment, they are likened to honey-coated blades—sweet to the taste but harmful in consequence. Pursuit of these experiences fuels clinging, reinforces the illusion of permanence, and anchors the mind in samsara. Liberation requires not moderation but complete abandonment of attachment to these transient stimuli.

**Spiritual Craving Perpetuates Suffering**  
The desire to attain enlightenment, sanctity, or meditative states is still a form of greed. Goal-oriented striving, even for noble ends, creates conceptual grasping and obstructs natural awareness. True liberation arises only when the mind ceases all seeking—when even the pursuit of liberation is relinquished.

**Calmness Without Insight Is Insufficient**  
Sedation—temporary tranquility or mental stillness—may arise during practice but fails to uproot greed. It is a superficial stillness, not the deep clarity of wisdom. Without insight into impermanence and non-self, calmness alone cannot dissolve attachment or free the mind from cycles of craving and rebirth.

**Wisdom and Equanimity Dissolve Attachment**  
Transcendent wisdom reveals the unsatisfactory and unstable nature of all objects of desire, thereby dismantling the foundation of greed. Equanimity emerges as the natural result of this understanding: the mind no longer reacts to pleasure or pain, clinging or rejecting. This balanced observation, free from identification, weakens greed’s hold.

**Sacred Qualities Are the Result, Not the Goal**  
Moral discipline, deep wisdom, and equanimity are not practices to be pursued as achievements, but the natural expressions of a mind free from greed, hatred, and delusion. Those who possess these qualities have already severed attachment to experiences, states, and outcomes. Their presence signals the end of suffering, not a milestone on the path.

**Non-Seeking Is the True Path**  
Liberation is not attained by effort or accumulation but by the cessation of striving. The non-seeking mind rests in authentic awareness, undisturbed by the pull of desire or the fear of loss. It is in this stillness—free from goals, expectations, and conceptual grasping—that freedom is realized.

**Greed and Delusion Mutually Sustain Each Other**  
Delusion—the misperception of permanence, self, and lasting satisfaction—gives rise to greed. Greed, in turn, reinforces delusion by compelling the mind to chase illusory sources of happiness. Together, they distort perception and bind beings to samsara. Only the clear seeing of reality can break this cycle.

**Enlightened Beings Have Fully Abandoned Greed**  
Those who have attained liberation—Sages, Saintly beings, Sanctified practitioners—have completely eradicated greed as a root affliction. They no longer cling to sensory pleasures, meditative attainments, or spiritual outcomes. Their freedom is not theoretical; it is embodied in non-reactivity, moral purity, and unshakable equanimity.

**Regret and Disturbance Stem from Unfulfilled Craving**  
When desires are unmet, the mind turns to regret and remorse, further clouding clarity and undermining meditative stability. This mental turmoil is a direct consequence of greed’s grip, revealing how attachment distorts the present moment through fixation on what has been lost or what was never attained.

**Nirvana Is Freedom from the Five Desires**  
Liberation is not an exotic state achieved through force, but the natural dissolution of craving for the five sensual experiences. When the mind no longer entangles itself with form, sound, smell, taste, or touch, it is no longer bound to samsara. This letting go is the essence of nirvana.
