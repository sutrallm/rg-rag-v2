**Title: Clinging, the Three Poisons, and the Path to Liberation in Buddhist Tradition**

**Summary:**  
Clinging to impermanent phenomena—including the notion of self, possessions, and emotional states—is the core mechanism sustaining suffering and cyclic existence. It arises from delusion, which misperceives transient experiences as lasting and self-owned. This mental grasping manifests as the three poisons: greed, hatred, and delusion, each of which binds the mind to rebirth cycles. True freedom emerges only through their complete eradication, resulting in inner stability, non-reactivity, and the cessation of rebirth. Liberation is inseparable from the end of clinging; it is not an external achievement but the dissolution of internal attachments, cultivated through persistent mindfulness and exertion.

**Clinging Sustains Cyclic Existence**  
The persistent attachment to experiences, identities, possessions, and even spiritual attainments perpetuates the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth. This grasping is not limited to pleasant stimuli but also includes aversion to unpleasant ones, both of which reinforce karmic patterns and obscure the understanding of impermanence and non-self. Clinging is the active force that fuels karma and desire, making it the direct driver of continued existence within samsara.

**The Three Poisons Originate in Delusion**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are not separate entities but interrelated expressions of the same root error: misperceiving reality. Delusion—ignorance of impermanence and the absence of an inherent self—leads to greed (clinging to desire) and hatred (clinging to aversion). These poisons form the foundation of suffering, and their presence ensures the persistence of rebirth. Their eradication is not optional but necessary for liberation, and their complete absence defines the realized state.

**Liberation Is the Cessation of Clinging**  
Freedom is not obtained through external conditions or social recognition but through the total abandonment of attachment. The end of clinging directly results in the end of rebirth; they are mutually exclusive states. A being who has let go of clinging no longer generates the mental conditions for rebirth. This cessation is synonymous with enlightenment, marked by inner purity, stability, and non-reactivity regardless of surrounding circumstances.

**Non-Attachment Is the Counterforce to Clinging**  
Attachment and non-attachment cannot coexist in full realization. Where clinging is present, freedom is blocked; where non-attachment is established, clinging dissolves. This state is not indifference but a profound clarity that no longer identifies with or resists experience. It is cultivated through continuous mindfulness, which allows one to observe phenomena without grasping, thereby dissolving the self-narrative that reinforces the illusion of permanence and ownership.

**The Self-Narrative Reinforces Bondage**  
The internal story of “I am this” or “I have attained” is the vehicle through which clinging operates, embedding the false sense of a separate, enduring self. Even spiritually refined experiences become objects of clinging when framed within this narrative. Liberation requires the cessation of this story, not its refinement, so that identity drops away and awareness remains uncolored by personal acquisition or achievement.

**A Realized Being Is Defined by Inner Purity**  
The measure of spiritual attainment is not status, authority, or visible accomplishment but the complete absence of greed, hatred, and delusion. A Standard Sage is recognized by enduring inner silence, freedom from reactivity, and the quiet awe inspired by unwavering virtue. This state emerges not from avoidance of suffering but from the dissolution of its source: clinging rooted in ignorance.
