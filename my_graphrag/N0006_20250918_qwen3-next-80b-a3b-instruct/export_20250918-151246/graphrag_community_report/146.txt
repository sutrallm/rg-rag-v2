**Title: The Path to Liberation: Moral Conduct, Insight, and the Transcendence of Attachment**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is achieved through the complete dissolution of attachment—not only to material and sensual pleasures, but also to meditative states, spiritual achievements, and the notion of self. This is accomplished through the interdependent cultivation of ethical conduct, mental stabilization, and direct wisdom that perceives all phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and without inherent self. The saintly being, who has eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion, embodies this freedom, living in unshakable stillness beyond pleasure and pain. Nirvana is not a future goal or post-mortem state but the present-moment cessation of clinging, realized when the mind no longer grasps at anything—包括 spiritual attainments. Even the pursuit of enlightenment must be free from attachment to its outcome.

**Moral Conduct as the Foundation for Mental Clarity**  
Ethical behavior forms the necessary ground for deeper meditative absorption and wisdom. Without disciplined conduct, the mind remains agitated and undisciplined, unable to sustain the stability required for insight. This foundation supports the weakening of reactive patterns and creates the inner purity essential for progress on the path.

**Mental Stability Enables Insight, Insight Deepens Stability**  
The practice of mental calming provides the quiet, focused state in which clear perception can arise. Yet this stability alone is insufficient for liberation if it becomes an object of clinging. True progress occurs when insight into impermanence and non-self transforms the mind’s relationship to all experiences—deepening calm by revealing its impermanent basis, thus preventing attachment to tranquil states.

**Attachment is the Root of Suffering and the Cycle of Rebirth**  
Clinging to transient phenomena—whether sensory, emotional, or spiritual—binds the mind to desire and aversion. This dualistic response, rooted in the misperception of permanence and self, fuels the continuous cycle of existence and suffering. Liberation is not possible as long as any form of grasping persists, even the attachment to spiritual progress or meditative bliss.

**Wisdom Reveals the Illusory Nature of Self and Phenomena**  
Through direct perception, one sees that all experiences arise and pass without an enduring core. This realization dismantles the belief in a fixed self, which is the foundation of all clinging. When no “I” is found to possess, protect, or pursue, attachment loses its footing and dissolves naturally.

**Nirvana is the Immediate Cessation of Clinging**  
This state is not a distant reward or afterlife condition but the present-moment freedom from all forms of grasping. It is recognized by the mind’s complete stillness, free from defilements, and characterized by unshakable peace. Its attainment is marked not by supernatural powers or elevated states, but by the total relinquishment of desire and aversion toward any experience.

**The Saint Embodies Complete Transcendence**  
One who has fully cultivated ethical conduct and wisdom no longer responds to pleasure or pain. The three poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—are extinguished. This being lives in constant mindfulness, manifesting purity and humility, and is revered even by celestial beings for having moved entirely beyond worldly conditions.

**Self-Views Sustain Clinging and Obscure Reality**  
Fixed identities and conceptual notions of “I,” “mine,” or “spiritual achiever” create subtle yet powerful attachment. These constructs are maintained by ignorance and fuel ongoing craving. Wisdom dissolves them by revealing that all mental constructs are conditioned processes, not enduring entities to be defended or celebrated.

**The Pursuit of Enlightenment Must Be Without Attachment**  
Striving for awakening becomes a new form of bondage if the goal itself is clung to. True liberation arises only when the practitioner lets go of all outcomes, including the desire to be free. The final step is not reaching, but releasing—letting go of even the aspiration to attain.

**Non-Attachment Arises Naturally from Seeing Reality Clearly**  
When impermanence and non-self are directly perceived, clinging loses its rationale. There is nothing substantial to hold onto. The mind, no longer misled by illusion, rests effortlessly in equanimity—neither grasping at pleasure nor recoiling from pain. This is the essence of freedom.
