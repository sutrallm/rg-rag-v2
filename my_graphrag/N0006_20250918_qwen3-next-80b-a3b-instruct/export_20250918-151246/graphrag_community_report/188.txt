Title: The Path of the True Sage: Jieming, Chanding, Zhishui, and Jiedu

Summary:  
The cultivation of ethical discipline, mental concentration, and deep wisdom forms an integrated path leading to complete liberation. Ethical conduct establishes the foundation for mental stability, which in turn allows for clear observation of reality. This observation reveals impermanence, non-self, and unsatisfactoriness, dissolving attachment and uprooting suffering. A true sage embodies all three qualities fully, lives in unwavering stillness, and manifests liberation as a silent, inspiring presence.

Ethical Discipline as the Foundation  
Moral conduct creates the necessary mental clarity and stability for deeper practice. Without it, unwholesome actions cloud perception and prevent the mind from settling. This discipline is not an end in itself but the essential starting point that allows concentration to develop and wisdom to emerge.

Concentration Enables Clear Observation  
A stabilized mind, free from distraction, permits direct and unfiltered awareness of mental and sensory phenomena. This focused stillness is not passive but a portal through which insight arises—without it, the nature of experience remains unseen.

Wisdom Arises from Direct Observation  
Deep understanding of impermanence, non-self, and unsatisfactoriness emerges only through careful, non-reactive awareness. This wisdom is not conceptual but experiential, born from turning inward and observing the mind’s patterns without interference, leading to the dissolution of ignorance and craving.

Liberation Is the Culmination of Integrated Practice  
Freedom from suffering and cyclic existence is achieved only when ethical conduct, mental concentration, and wisdom are fully developed and harmonized. None alone can bring about this completeness; they must be practiced as one continuous path.

The True Sage Personifies Liberation  
One who has perfected ethical discipline, maintained unwavering mental stillness, and realized ultimate wisdom lives as a living embodiment of freedom. Their existence is marked by silence, purity, and the absence of greed, hatred, and delusion—offering inspiration not through words, but through presence.
