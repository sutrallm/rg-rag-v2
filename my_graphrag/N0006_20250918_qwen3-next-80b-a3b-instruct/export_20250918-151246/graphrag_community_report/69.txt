**Title: Zhi Hui, Fan Nao, and the Path to Liberation Through Chan Ding and Diligent Observation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is achieved only through the complete eradication of mental afflictions, which are not to be suppressed but transformed by wisdom arising from concentrated observation of body, mind, and external phenomena. This wisdom reveals the impermanent, empty, and interdependent nature of all experience, dissolving the illusion of a fixed self. Without persistent effort and sustained attention, this wisdom cannot deepen or endure. Afflictions serve as signals of clinging, and their observation—guided by stillness and diligence—becomes the very path to freedom.

---

**The Role of Chan Ding in Awakening Wisdom**  
A stable and still mind, cultivated through Chan Ding, is the essential foundation for wisdom to arise. Only through concentrated observation can the true nature of phenomena—impermanent, non-self, and interdependently originated—become clearly perceived. Without this mental stillness, the depth of understanding required to transcend attachment cannot be reached.

**Afflictions as Seen through the Lens of Wisdom**  
Mental disturbances such as desire, anger, and ignorance are not external enemies to be eliminated but internal signals of clinging. When observed without suppression or avoidance, their arising and ceasing reveal their empty nature. This observation, guided by wisdom, transforms afflictions from sources of suffering into catalysts for awakening.

**The Illusion of the Self as the Core of Suffering**  
The belief in a permanent, independent self arises from ignorance and fuels all afflictions. Wisdom directly reveals that the self is an illusion constructed from transient aggregates. This realization dissolves the illusion of “I,” removing the root of attachment and the cycle of suffering.

**The Necessity of Relentless Effort in Sustaining Wisdom**  
Wisdom does not emerge or endure through passive insight alone. Relentless effort—through sustained attention and consistent practice—is the fuel that allows wisdom to deepen and stabilize. Without this diligence, even the clearest understanding fades, and afflictions resurface.

**The Three Domains of Experience as the Field for Liberation**  
Body, mind, and external phenomena together form the totality of experience. When observed with clarity, each is seen as empty, impermanent, and interdependent. ThisSeeing reveals the absence of inherent existence in all domains, enabling non-attachment and supporting the collapse of clinging.

**Liberation as the Culmination of Eradication, Not Suppression**  
True freedom is attained only when afflictions are completely uprooted, not merely restrained. This requires the transformative power of wisdom applied to all aspects of experience. Liberation is not a state of tranquil absence but the fullness of clear seeing that renders clinging impossible.
