**Title: The Eradication of Delusion and the Emergence of Saintly Beings in Buddhist Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Delusion—fundamental ignorance of reality’s impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature—is the root cause of craving, aversion, and the endless cycle of rebirth. It fuels attachment and hostility by distorting perception, leading beings to cling to fleeting phenomena as stable and self-owned. Liberation is achieved only when delusion is fully uprooted through wisdom, mindfulness, and disciplined practice. Saintly beings embody this liberation: they have extinguished greed, hatred, and delusion, transcending all craving and aversion through direct insight into non-self and dependent origination. Their moral purity, meditative stability, and unwavering effort sustain freedom from suffering and rebirth.

**Delusion Drives the Cycle of Rebirth**  
Delusion, as ignorance of impermanence, suffering, and non-self, is the foundational cause of samsara. It falsely perceives transient phenomena as permanent, satisfying, and self-owned, generating craving and aversion. These mental afflictions produce karmic momentum that sustains birth, death, and rebirth. Without dispelling this ignorance, liberation is impossible, as all suffering stems from this misapprehension of reality.

**Wisdom Is the Sole Means to Uproot Delusion**  
True liberation arises not from rituals or external forces but from direct insight into reality. Transcendent wisdom reveals the non-self nature of all phenomena, dissolving the illusion of permanence and ownership that fuels attachment. This wisdom, cultivated through mindfulness and meditative stability, is the only force capable of completely eradicating delusion and its衍生 afflictions.

**Saintly Qualities Manifest as Freedom from the Three Poisons**  
Those who have attained saintly qualities have fully eliminated greed, hatred, and delusion. Their moral discipline, consistent mindfulness, and unwavering wisdom lead to inner stillness, non-clinging, and freedom from reactive mental states. They embody equanimity, compassion, and boundless clarity—no longer driven by desire for pleasure or aversion to pain—because they see all experience as it is, without distortion.

**Precise Practice Sustains Liberation**  
Liberation is not passive but depends on continuous, disciplined effort. Precise practice defends against laziness, distraction, and indulgence by cultivating awareness and discernment. It safeguards meditative stability and ensures wisdom remains active, preventing regression into ignorance. Without sustained exertion, delusion reasserts itself, and the cycle of suffering resumes.

**Aversion and Craving Are Dual Expressions of Delusion**  
Both craving and aversion arise from the same root: mistaken belief in a self that seeks pleasure or fears pain. Craving clings to what is perceived as desirable; aversion rejects what is perceived as threatening. Both reinforce each other and are intensified by delusion’s misperception of reality. Only through realizing non-self do these dual responses lose their foundation and dissolve.

**Chanding and Saintly Conduct Are Interdependent Foundations**  
Chanding, the mental stabilization that dispels distractions and妄想, creates the clarity necessary for wisdom to arise. It is not an end but a means—its perfection supports mindfulness and moral purity. Saintly beings consistently cultivate chanding as a core discipline, ensuring their conduct, awareness, and insight remain unified and unshaken.

**Enlightenment Is Defined by the Annihilation of Self-Illusion**  
The realization of non-self directly dismantles delusion at its source. When the belief in a fixed, independent self is seen through, clinging and aversion cease. Enlightened beings have achieved this realization completely—no trace of self-identification remains. Their freedom is not conceptual but experiential, marked by the absence of reaction, desire, or resistance.

**Indulgence and Sloth Reinforce Delusion**  
Laziness, distraction, and sensory gratification are not mere weaknesses but active barriers to awakening. They nourish ignorance by preventing the cultivation of awareness, allowing delusion to dominate perception. True progress requires constant vigilance: effort is the counterforce that prevents the mind from sinking back into obscurity and suffering.

**Liberation Is the Cessation of All Defilements**  
True freedom is not partial but total: it is the complete disappearance of greed, hatred, and delusion. These defilements are the engines of karma and rebirth. Their eradication, brought about by wisdom and disciplined practice, ends the cycle of suffering. Those who attain this state are no longer bound by birth or death—their mind, like space, is unsoiled, unobstructed, and free.
