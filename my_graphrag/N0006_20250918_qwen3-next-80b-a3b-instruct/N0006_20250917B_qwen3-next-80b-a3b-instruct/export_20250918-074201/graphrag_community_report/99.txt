**Title: Chizhu, Wuzi, and Jiefu: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Emptiness**

**Summary:**  
Liberation arises not through effort or external means, but through the natural dissolution of clinging, enabled by direct observation of the mind and body’s impermanent, empty nature. This process is cultivated through continuous mindfulness, guided by wisdom that recognizes the absence of inherent self in all phenomena. Those who fully realize this truth transcend rebirth, sever all afflictions, and embody complete freedom—no longer attached even to the notion of liberation itself.

**Non-Attachment is the Direct Path to Liberation**  
Letting go of attachment—to meditative states, supernatural powers, spiritual achievements, or even the idea of attaining freedom—is not a step toward liberation, but the very means by which liberation manifests. Clinging, in any form, obstructs the natural emergence of peace and clarity. Liberation is not grasped; it is revealed when attachment is released.

**Emptiness Undermines the Basis of Suffering**  
All phenomena lack inherent, independent existence. They are impermanent, interdependent, and constantly changing. Recognizing this truth—emptiness—dissolves the foundation of ignorance that fuels clinging and afflictions. Without grasping at things as solid or permanent, the mind no longer generates suffering.

**Liberation Is the Cessation of Rebirth Through Realization**  
Freedom from the cycle of birth and death is not achieved by fleeing the world or seeking mystical realms, but by seeing the true nature of present-moment experience. When one fully perceives non-self and impermanence, clinging ends, afflictions dissolve, and the cycle of rebirth is broken entirely.

**Mindfulness Is the Continuous Practice That Unveils Truth**  
Constant, undeluded awareness of the mind and body’s nature is the direct path to freedom. This practice does not aim to create a new state, but to reveal what is already present when obscurations are cleared. Through mindful observation, the mind recognizes its own clarity and peace, unobstructed by illusion.

**Afflictions Arise from Ignorance of Emptiness**  
All mental disturbances stem from misunderstanding reality—believing in permanence, selfhood, or independent existence. When emptiness is directly seen, the root of these disturbances is uprooted. Without this understanding, efforts to attain peace remain bound to the very structures that cause suffering.

**The Liberated One Has Transcended All Attachments, Including Liberation Itself**  
One who has fully realized freedom no longer clings to any identity, state, or achievement—not even to the concept of being liberated. This complete non-attachment defines the liberated being: untouched by desire, untouched by the idea of attainment, fully at rest in the flow of phenomena as they are.
