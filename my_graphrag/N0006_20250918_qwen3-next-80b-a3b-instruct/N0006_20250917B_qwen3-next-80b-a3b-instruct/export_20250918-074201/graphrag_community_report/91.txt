**Title: The Three Poisons and the Path to Liberation: Stages of Spiritual Traversing**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death arises only through the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. These three root afflictions sustain ongoing existence by reinforcing false notions of self, attachment to ephemeral experiences, and resistance to reality. Delusion is the foundational error — the mistaken belief in a permanent, independent self — which gives rise to attachment and aversion. Those who have transcended this cycle have fully relinquished all three, resulting in inner stillness, freedom from external conditioning, and unshakable peace. Progress requires vigilant effort, mindfulness, and the cultivation of wisdom that dismantles the illusion of self.

**Delusion is the Root of Self-Attachment and the Cycle of Existence**  
The persistent belief in a fixed, independent “I” constitutes the deepest layer of ignorance. This erroneous perception of self is not merely a thought pattern but the structural basis for all suffering. It leads to the false identification of “mine” — ownership over objects, experiences, and identities — and anchors consciousness within the loop of birth and death. Without dissolving this misapprehension, all other efforts remain superficial.

**Greed Sustains Attachment and Prevents Freedom**  
Craving for sensory pleasures and continued existence binds beings to the cycle. This attachment manifests as an insatiable pursuit of satisfaction through external conditions, never achieving lasting peace. Its source lies in the illusion of self, as one clings to what is deemed “mine.” Complete release is impossible while any form of craving persists, making its abandonment essential for liberation.

**Hatred Arises from the Illusion of Self and Deepens Suffering**  
Anger and aversion emerge whenever the imagined self is perceived as threatened. This reaction is not an independent emotion but a defensive response rooted in the false sense of identity. By resisting reality and clinging to distinction between self and other, hatred reinforces separation and prolongs suffering. Only when the notion of self dissolves does hatred lose its ground.

**Mental Stillness and Vigilance Are the Path Out of the Cycle**  
Tranquility is not passive tranquility but the natural state of one who no longer reacts to external stimuli through craving or aversion. This condition is achieved through disciplined practice — calming agitation, resisting distraction, and staying fully present. Each moment of carelessness renews the cycle; only unwavering awareness breaks its continuity. The cultivated mind becomes unmoved by conditions, not through suppression, but through direct realization of their impermanent and selfless nature.

**Effort and Wisdom Are the Twin Pillars of Release**  
Liberation is not attained by chance but through sustained action. Diligence counters sloth and complacency, preventing the recurrence of ignorance. Yet effort alone is insufficient without the clear vision that sees through the delusion of self. True advance occurs when disciplined practice aligns with penetrating understanding — when one no longer mistake the transient for permanent, the impersonal for personal, and the suffering for solace.
