**Title: The Path to Sainthood Through Moral, Mental, and Wisdom Cultivation**

**Summary**  
Sainthood is achieved only through the complete elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion by means of disciplined ascetic practice focused on moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom. Those who attain this state embody boundless stillness, inner peace, and a direct realization of ultimate truth, having fully uprooted the three root afflictions that obscure spiritual clarity.

---

**Disciplined Practice is the Sole Path to Sainthood**  
The attainment of sainthood is not accidental or passive; it requires sustained, structured effort through ascetic practice. This practice is defined by three interdependent elements—moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom—each necessary and cultivated over time. Without this rigorous process, the required transformation cannot occur.

**Eradication of the Three Root Afflictions Defines Sainthood**  
Saints are distinguished by the total removal of greed, hatred, and delusion. These are not merely subdued or managed but uprooted entirely. Their absence is not incidental but the defining condition of the state, confirming that liberation is measured by their complete elimination.

**Inner Peace and Stillness Emerge Naturally from Liberation**  
The presence of boundless stillness and inner peace in saints is not a goal pursued separately but a direct consequence of having removed all afflictions. These qualities manifest spontaneously once greed, hatred, and delusion are fully eradicated, indicating they are byproducts of inner purity rather than techniques to be forced.

**Ultimate Truth is Realized Only When Delusion Is Uprooted**  
Delusion, described as the fundamental ignorance, is the primary obstacle to seeing ultimate truth. Its removal is not one among many achievements but the critical turning point that enables direct realization. Without this, no other progress leads to the complete awakening described in the state of sainthood.
