**Title: Wu Chang, Zhì Huì, and the Path of Present-Moment Awareness**

**Summary**  
True liberation arises from directly observing the transient nature of all experiences—thoughts, sensations, and phenomena—through continuous mindfulness in daily life. This observation weakens clinging to pleasure and aversion to pain, dissolves the illusion of a fixed self, and cultivates a steady, unshaken awareness. Enlightenment is not distant or exclusive to formal practice; it emerges in ordinary moments when the mind perceives impermanence without attachment.

**Observing Impermanence Undermines Clinging and Aversion**  
The constant flux of all phenomena—whether pleasant or unpleasant—reveals their lack of enduring substance. When one directly perceives that pleasure is fleeting, the impulse to grasp it diminishes. Likewise, recognizing that discomfort is temporary removes the compulsion to resist or fight against it. This dual reduction in attachment and aversion arises not from intellectual understanding, but from firsthand observation in each moment.

**Mindfulness in Daily Activity Is the Source of Clarity**  
Wisdom is not confined to meditation cushions or ritual spaces. Every act—breathing, walking, eating—becomes a gateway to seeing reality as it is. By maintaining continuous awareness during routine tasks, one repeatedly confirms the impermanent, conditioned nature of experience. This sustained attention transforms ordinary life into a continuous practice that deepens understanding without requiring external conditions.

**Realization of Non-Self Emerges Through Moment-to-Moment Observation**  
As attention is held on the shifting stream of sensations and thoughts, no fixed, unchanging core is found. The sense of a permanent “I” dissolves not through doctrine, but through direct perception: each experience arises and passes, unowned and unanchored. This seeing exposes the illusion at the heart of ego-based identity and releases the grip of self-centered attachment.

**Enlightenment Is Rooted in Everyday Perception**  
The state of being unshaken by change—as exemplified by Sheng Zhe and Wu Chang—is not attained through extraordinary means, but through the disciplined, unbroken observation of impermanence in the flow of ordinary life. Liberation is accessible in the next breath, the next step, the next thought. The totality of existence becomes the field where clarity unfolds, requiring no separation between sacred and mundane.

**Deep Stillness Enables Recognition of Reality**  
A mind undistracted by turbulence, cultivated through sustained concentration, becomes a clear mirror. In this stillness, the fragile, dependent nature of all things becomes visible without distortion. Without this calm, insight remains superficial; with it, the truth of impermanence penetrates deeply, transforming perception and freeing the mind from illusion.
