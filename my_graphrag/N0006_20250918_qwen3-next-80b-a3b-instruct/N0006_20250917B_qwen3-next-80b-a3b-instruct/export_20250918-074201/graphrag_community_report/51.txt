**Title: Sheng Zhe: The Embodiment of Liberation Through Moral Purity, Meditative Stability, and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
Sheng Zhe represents the fully realized being who has vanquished the three root afflictions—greed, hatred, and delusion—thereby attaining complete liberation from suffering and the cycle of rebirth. This state is anchored in unshakable moral discipline, deep meditative stability, and transcendent wisdom that sees reality without attachment. The sage’s inner stillness, ethical purity, and equal-mindedness arise not from external achievement but from the permanent cessation of mental defilements, rendering them untouched by change, unaffected by circumstance, and free from egoic clinging. Liberation is not an abstract goal but an embodied condition, sustained through continuous mindful practice and moral integrity.

**Sheng Zhe is Defined by the Complete Eradication of the Three Root Afflictions**  
The being known as Sheng Zhe is distinguished by the total uprooting of greed, hatred, and delusion—these are not merely subdued but entirely extinguished. This elimination is not symbolic but foundational: without these three poisons, the conditions that bind one to suffering and rebirth vanish. The sage’s freedom is not partial or conditional; it is absolute and irreversible, arising directly from the annihilation of the mental sources of cyclic existence.

**Moral Discipline Forms the Indispensable Foundation of Enlightenment**  
Pure ethical conduct—Qing Jing Jie Xing—is not an optional practice but the essential bedrock upon which all spiritual progress rests. The sage’s moral purity is not merely external adherence to rules but an internal state of harmony and integrity that supports the development of concentration and wisdom. This conduct is continuous, sustained through Jing Jin, and inseparable from the sage’s identity; without it, the stability required for liberation cannot be established.

**Mediative Stability is the Unmoving Core of the Enlightened State**  
Chan Ding is not a temporary trance or isolated experience but the permanent condition of the sage’s mind. It is characterized by total non-disturbance, free from agitation, distraction, and reactivity. This inner equanimity is not passive but the result of disciplined mental training, manifesting as unshakable stillness and pure awareness. The stability of Chan Ding enables the sage to dwell in tranquility regardless of external conditions, making it both the means and the expression of liberation.

**Wisdom Beyond Attachment Defines True Liberation**  
The sage’s wisdom—Zhi Hui—is not intellectual knowledge but the direct realization of reality as it is: impermanent, without self, and devoid of inherent existence. This wisdom prevents clinging to any phenomenon, including meditative states or supernatural abilities. The sage does not seek to preserve or enhance experience; they see all things with clarity and let them go. This non-attachment is what enables transcendence of the cycle of birth and death and is the hallmark of the highest realization.

**Equal-Mindedness and Timeless Emancipation Are the Natural Expressions of Freedom**  
The sage remains undisturbed by gain or loss, praise or blame, because their inner condition is no longer shaped by external events. This equal-mindedness—the result of having severed egoic clinging and eradicated ignorance—is not detachment born of indifference but of profound understanding. True emancipation is not a future destination but a present, self-sustaining state, untouched by time, change, or circumstance, and thus described as timeless.
