**Title: CHAOYUE ZHUZHE, JIE TU, AND JUEXING: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Direct Awareness**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved solely through continuous, mindful observation of body-mind phenomena, leading to the direct realization that all experiences—whether meditative bliss or afflictions—are impermanent and lacking a fixed self. This understanding eliminates clinging, even to spiritual methods and enlightenment itself. True freedom is not found in external conditions, sacred spaces, or divine assistance, but in the unwavering inner stability cultivated by moment-to-moment awareness in all activities. The path is practical, universal, and accessible to all—defined not by mystical experiences, but by the consistent letting go of identification with any transient condition.

---

**Impermanence Is the Foundation for Dissolving the Illusion of Self**  
All physical and mental phenomena—including feelings, thoughts, and perceptions—are observed to arise and pass away. This constant flux reveals that nothing can be a stable, independent “self.” Recognizing impermanence is not abstract philosophy; it is the direct, experiential key that dismantles the sense of “I” and “mine,” rendering attachment unsustainable.

**Non-Self Realization Naturally Ends Clinging**  
When phenomena are seen as dependently originated and devoid of an enduring core, the impulse to grasp or reject them dissolves. This includes letting go of attachment to meditative states, spiritual achievements, and even the teachings themselves. The absence of a fixed self means there is nothing to defend, possess, or cling to—freedom emerges not through effort to attain, but through seeing through illusion.

**True Liberation Requires Inner Stability, Not External Conditions**  
Sacred environments, divine aid, or favorable circumstances offer no lasting release. Liberation arises only through internal transformation—consistent awareness and mental clarity in all activities, regardless of setting or experience. Inner peace is not something achieved by altering one’s surroundings, but by cultivating unshakable presence amid pleasure, pain, or indifference.

**Awakening Is the Practical Realization of Reality, Not a Mystical State**  
Enlightenment is not an otherworldly reward or exotic experience. It is the clear recognition that all conditioned things are temporary, all feelings are in flux, and the sense of “I” is a construct. This awareness manifests as equanimity—remaining undisturbed by bliss or suffering, and detached even from the idea of being “awakened.”

**Daily Life Is the Arena for Practice, Not a Distraction From It**  
Mindfulness is not confined to meditation cushions. True concentration extends to walking, standing, sitting, lying down, and engaging with both favorable and adverse situations. Liberation is forged in the ordinary moments of life, where the insight of impermanence and non-self is continually tested and deepened.

**All Teachings Are Means to an End—Not Ends Themselves**  
The Buddha’s methods, doctrines, and practices are likened to rafts or medicine: useful for crossing over, but to be released once their purpose is served. Clinging to any teaching—even the most revered—creates a new form of bondage. Authentic practice requires flexibility and the courage to let go, even of the path itself.

**Continuous Diligence Is the Only Path Through Samsara**  
No shortcuts, revelations, or external interventions can substitute for sustained, moment-to-moment awareness. Without persistent effort, the mind remains conditioned by habitual clinging. Liberation is not granted—it is earned through unceasing observation, leading to the natural unraveling of ego-driven identification.

**The Enlightened Being Embodies Transcendent Wisdom in Action**  
The one who has realized liberation does not dwell in seclusion or ecstasy. They move through the world with quiet freedom—neither grasping at meditative highs nor avoiding discomfort. Their presence demonstrates that true wisdom is lived, not proclaimed; it is seen in how one responds to all conditions without reactivity or attachment.
