**Title: The Sense of Self and the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
The illusion of a permanent, separate self drives all worldly pursuits—seeking comfort, pleasure, status, or security—and sustains suffering. Liberation arises through the realization of no-self, which dismantles this illusion and enables non-attachment. The transworldly path, grounded in this realization, contrasts entirely with worldly activities, which are inherently bound to self-centered clinging.

**Worldly Pursuits Stem from the Illusion of a Separate Self**  
All desires for comfort, pleasure, status, or security originate from the belief in a fixed, independent identity. These pursuits do not merely reflect attachment—they actively reinforce the illusion, creating a cycle in which the sense of self is continually strengthened through external gains and avoidance of loss.

**Transworldly Practice Emerges from the Realization of No-Self**  
Freedom from suffering is achieved not through refinement of the self, but through its negation. The transworldly path arises when one sees all phenomena as dependently arisen, dissolving the ground for clinging or aversion. This orientation is defined by the absence of self-centered striving.

**Worldly and Transworldly Dharmas Are Fundamentally Opposed**  
One cannot exist without the other being negated. Worldly dharma is anchored in grasping and the endurance of separation; transworldly dharma is sustained by release from both. The distinction is not one of degree, but of kind: the first perpetuates bondage, the second unbinds.
