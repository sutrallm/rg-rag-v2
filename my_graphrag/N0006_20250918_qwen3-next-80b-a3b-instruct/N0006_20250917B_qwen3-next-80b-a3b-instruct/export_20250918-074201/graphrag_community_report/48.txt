**Title: The Path of Practice and the Cultivation of True Chan Ding**

**Summary:**  
The spiritual path is a structured progression through ethical discipline, meditative concentration, and liberating wisdom, each stage dependent on the others. True meditative stability cannot be achieved without persistent effort and the release of attachment to meditative experiences. This freedom emerges from the direct realization that all phenomena lack inherent self or owner. The perfected embodiment of this path is a Saintly Being, whose completeness arises only when all stages—ethics, concentration, and wisdom—are fully integrated and sustained by unwavering diligence.

**Zealous Practice Sustains Every Stage of the Path**  
Diligent, persistent effort is essential not only to initiate but to maintain progress through ethical discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Without this continuous drive, mental restlessness, laziness, and habitual patterns reemerge, eroding discipline and mindfulness. It is the foundational energy that keeps the entire structure of practice moving forward.

**True Meditative Concentration Requires Freedom from Attachment**  
Meditative stability cannot be considered complete if one clings to its outcomes—whether powers, states of mind, or experiences. The presence of attachment undermines genuine freedom. Only when the practitioner recognizes the impermanent, selfless, and illusory nature of all phenomena can meditative focus become unshakable and authentic.

**Wisdom Emerges from Realizing Non-Self**  
Liberating understanding arises not through intellectual study but through direct perception that no phenomenon possesses an owner, controller, or inherent identity. This realization dismantles the root of clinging and enables the transition from conditioned states to true liberation, marking the culmination of the path.

**The Path Culminates in a Fully Integrated Saintly Being**  
Perfection of the path is not selective—it requires the seamless completion of ethics, meditative concentration, and wisdom. One who embodies this completion is recognized as a Saintly Being, where each stage has been fully realized and sustained, leaving no residue of ignorance or attachment.
