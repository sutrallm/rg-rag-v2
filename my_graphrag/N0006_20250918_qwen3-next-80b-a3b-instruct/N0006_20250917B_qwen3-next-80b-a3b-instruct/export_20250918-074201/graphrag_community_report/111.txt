**Title: Shengbze and the Path of Continuous Practice to Liberation**

**Summary**  
Individuals who embody Shengbze achieve complete freedom from suffering and rebirth through unwavering daily mindfulness and meditation. Their state of being is marked by total non-attachment—neither clinging to calm nor fearing its absence—and is free from mental obstructions, aversion, and habitual patterns. This liberated condition is not reached abruptly but through persistent, diligent practice that dismantles ignorance and leads to ultimate stability.

**Continuous Practice Governs Liberation**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is contingent upon unbroken daily mindfulness and meditation. This practice is not optional or intermittent; it is a necessary, steady effort that gradually erodes deep-rooted ignorance and subtle mental habits. Without this relentless diligence, progress toward freedom cannot occur.

**The State of Liberation Is Defined by Non-Attachment**  
Those who have transcended suffering do not grasp at states of peace nor resist their dissolution. Their mental equilibrium is unconditional—unaffected by the presence or absence of calm. This perfect non-attachment reflects a mind no longer bound by desire or fear.

**Liberated Beings Possess an Unobstructed Mind**  
The final condition of the enlightened is one where the mind is entirely free from clinging, aversion, and mental fabrications. There is no internal resistance or external distortion; perception remains clear and unfiltered, untouched by the conditioning that binds ordinary experience.

**Shengbze Are Recognized by Mastery of Mindfulness in Daily Life**  
The qualities of the enlightened are not abstract or occasional but are expressed in routine actions. Their daily practice of mindfulness, particularly through chanding, is the active foundation of their liberation. This habitual practice distinguishes them from those who merely contemplate or occasionally meditate.
