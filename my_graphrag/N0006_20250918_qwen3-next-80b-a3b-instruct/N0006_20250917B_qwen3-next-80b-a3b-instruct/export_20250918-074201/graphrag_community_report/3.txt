**Title: Shengzhe: The Embodied State of Liberation Through Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary:**  
The Shengzhe is a fully liberated being who has completely extinguished greed, hatred, and delusion—the three root afflictions binding sentient beings to suffering and cyclic existence. This state is not attained through external achievements, supernatural powers, or spiritual rewards, but through relentless, lifelong discipline, unwavering mindfulness, and direct realization of non-self and impermanence. The Shengzhe dwells in perpetual stillness, free from attachment to all phenomena—including meditative experiences, praise, or identity—and radiates inner purity that commands reverence even from celestial beings. Liberation is defined not by attainment, but by the absence of defilements and the collapse of the illusion of self.

---

**Perfected Stillness as the Mark of Enlightenment**  
The Shengzhe abides constantly in inner stillness—free from agitation, craving, or aversion—regardless of external conditions. This state, known as Jingjian, is not a temporary meditative trance but an unshakable, continuous condition of the mind. It arises only after the complete uprooting of greed, hatred, and delusion, and is sustained by unwavering mindfulness. Unlike ordinary calm, this stillness is not passive; it is the active expression of a mind no longer entangled in conceptualization, reaction, or identification. It is the natural outcome of having seen through the illusory nature of all experiences.

**Mindfulness as the Unbroken Foundation**  
The Shengzhe maintains nian—continuous, non-judgmental awareness—without interruption, day or night. This is not intermittent practice but the very fabric of their existence. Every thought, sensation, and perception is observed without clinging or rejection. This constant mindfulness is not a technique but a signature of the purified mind, enabling one to recognize the arising and passing of afflictions before they take root. It is inseparable from moral discipline and is the unwavering lens through which reality is perceived, ensuring no regression into ignorance or attachment.

**Non-Attachment Beyond Spiritual Achievements**  
True freedom is demonstrated by the absence of clinging—not only to worldly pleasures and recognition, but also to spiritual attainments, meditative bliss, or even the experience of emptiness. The Shengzhe does not seek or preserve any state, including enlightenment, as an object of identity. This transcendence of all attachments, termed Wuzhizhidi, distinguishes them from those who mistake transient experiences for final liberation. Their wisdom sees even the most refined spiritual states as impermanent and thus not to be held. Relinquishing all notions of “I am liberated” completes the dissolution of ego.

**Eradication of the Three Poisons as the Sole Criterion**  
The defining feature of the Shengzhe is the total and irreversible extinction of greed, hatred, and delusion—the triad known as Tan Chen Chi, Si Du, or San Du. These are not merely suppressed but uprooted at their source: ignorance (wuming) of reality’s true nature. Without these afflictions, there is no fuel for samsara, no basis for suffering, and no grounds for self-reactivity. All other qualities—moral purity, wisdom, compassion, tranquility—are natural consequences of this eradication. No being is recognized as liberated unless these three are entirely absent.

**Liberation from Samsara Through Realization of Non-Self**  
The Shengzhe has fully realized wuwo—the absence of a permanent, independent self. This is not an intellectual understanding but a direct, experiential dissolution of the center of “I” and “mine.” With the collapse of ego-clinging, the mechanisms of craving, aversion, and delusion lose their foundation. As a result, the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth—the very definition of samsara—is broken. Liberation (jietuo, jiefu) is not a destination but the natural state of a mind no longer bound by illusion. This realization is the core of zhinen and shengli zhihui—the wisdom that penetrates the emptiness of the five aggregates.

**Relentless Effort as the Only Path**  
The attainment of the Shengzhe state is exclusively the result of jingjin—the continuous, unyielding, and uncompromising cultivation of discipline and mindfulness. No sporadic practice, ritual, prayer, or divine intervention can lead to this state. It is the cumulative effect of lifelong, unwavering effort that purifies the mind, prevents regression, and ensures the permanent extinction of defilements. This is not a matter of inspiration or occasional devotion, but a profound and unbroken commitment to ethical conduct, meditative steadiness, and penetrating insight. Without this relentless practice, liberation is unattainable.

**Reverence from Beyond Human Realms**  
The Shengzhe, having transcended all craving and identity, radiates a purity that naturally inspires reverence—from humans and celestial beings alike. Even divine entities, though powerful and long-lived, remain within samsara and are thus bound by their own afflictions. The Shengzhe, free from all such burdens, embodies a state so profound and unconditioned that it is recognized as transcendent, even by those who dwell in higher planes of existence. Their presence is not sought for blessings, power, or favor, but as a living testament to the possibility of complete release.
