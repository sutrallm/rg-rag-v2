**Title: Delusion, Greed, and Hatred as Root Causes of Samsara — The Path of the Saint and Sage**

**Summary:**  
The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth is sustained by three unified mental afflictions: delusion, greed, and hatred. Delusion — the fundamental misunderstanding of impermanence, non-self, and suffering — is the root from which greed and hatred arise. Greed manifests as attachment to pleasurable experiences and outcomes, while hatred arises as aversion toward unpleasant conditions, both fueled by the illusion of a permanent self. Liberation is achieved only through the complete eradication of these three, accomplished by wisdom, mindfulness, and meditative practice. Those who have uprooted them — Saints and Sages — dwell in unwavering stillness, free from craving, aversion, and ignorance, having transcended samsara and realized Nirvana.

---

**Delusion is the Origin of All Afflictions**  
Delusion, understood as ignorance of reality’s true nature — specifically the mistaken belief in a permanent self, inherent existence, and lasting satisfaction — underlies all suffering and binds beings to cyclic existence. It obscures the mind’s clarity and prevents direct perception of impermanence and non-self. This fundamental misperception gives rise to greed by creating the illusion that external objects or states can fulfill lasting desire, and fuels hatred by reinforcing false divisions between self and other, leading to resistance and aggression. Without the eradication of delusion, no other defilement can be fully uprooted.

**Greed Sustains Attachment and Rebirth**  
Greed, characterized by craving and clinging to sensory pleasures, material possessions, spiritual attainments, and even meditative states, is a direct consequence of delusion. It perpetuates samsara by generating karma through attachment, binding the individual to repeated cycles of birth and death. Greed obstructs meditative concentration, distorts spiritual practice into spiritual materialism, and prevents the emergence of true wisdom. Its cessation requires disciplined mindfulness and the dissolution of the illusion that anything conditioned can provide enduring happiness.

**Hatred Arises from Misperceived Threat and Separation**  
Hatred, or aversion, emerges as a reaction of repulsion toward unpleasant experiences, rooted in the same delusion of a fixed, separate self. When reality is misperceived as threatening or undesirable, the mind generates anger, hostility, or resistance — all forms of hatred. This mental state creates suffering, generates negative karma, and reinforces the cycle of rebirth. Hatred is not an independent force but a derivative of delusion; it cannot be overcome without seeing through the illusion of inherent existence and self-other duality.

**Liberation Is Defined by the Eradication of the Three**  
True freedom from suffering and the cycle of rebirth is attainable only when delusion, greed, and hatred are entirely extinguished. Those who have achieved this state — Saints, Sages, and Noble Ones — are marked by complete inner stillness, moral purity, and unwavering wisdom. They no longer react to pleasant or unpleasant conditions, having transcended craving and aversion. Their minds are free from inner disturbances, and their presence reflects the spontaneous clarity that arises when ignorance is fully dispelled.

**Wisdom and Meditative Practice Are the Means to Freedom**  
Wisdom — cultivated through mindfulness, meditation, and direct insight into impermanence and non-self — is the sole antidote to the three root afflictions. It dismantles delusion by revealing the transient and selfless nature of all phenomena. Meditative steadiness (Chan Ding) supports this process by calming the mind, reducing attachment, and stabilizing awareness. Sustained effort is required to maintain this practice, as mental sloth and habitual patterns continually threaten progress. Only through persistent cultivation does clarity emerge, leading to the complete liberation embodied by the Saint and the Sage.
