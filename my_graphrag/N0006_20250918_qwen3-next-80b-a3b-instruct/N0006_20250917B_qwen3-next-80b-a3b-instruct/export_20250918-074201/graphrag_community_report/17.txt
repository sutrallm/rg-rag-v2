**Title: Saintly Qualities and the Path to Liberation in Advanced Practitioners**

**Summary:**  
Advanced practitioners who achieve liberation have completely eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion—the three root defilements—through sustained mental discipline, ethical conduct, and transcendent wisdom. Their state is marked by inner stillness, freedom from attachment, equanimity, and direct realization of non-self and emptiness. These qualities are not abstract ideals but observable outcomes of rigorous, lifelong cultivation, resulting in transcendence of birth, aging, sickness, and death. Liberation is self-realized, untouched by external validation, and revered even by celestial beings.

**Cultivation of Saintly Qualities Requires Relentless and Sustained Practice**  
These qualities do not arise spontaneously or through fleeting effort. Only through consistent, long-term mental discipline—especially the practice of chanding—do individuals develop moral purity, wisdom, and mental stillness. The transformation is gradual, demanding unwavering commitment to ethical living and meditative stability over time.

**Eradication of the Three Root Defilements Defines the Saintly State**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are systematically uprooted in those who attain liberation. The complete absence of greed eliminates desire and attachment; the eradication of hatred removes reactive anger and replaces it with equanimity; and the dissolution of delusion—rooted in ignorance of non-self—brings about direct insight into true reality. These are not partial reductions but total eliminations, forming the foundation of their purified inner condition.

**Liberation Is Manifested Through the Realization of Non-Self and Emptiness**  
The five components of human existence—form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are seen as empty of inherent self. This direct perception dismantles the illusion of a fixed identity, which is the cornerstone of transcendent wisdom. It is through this realization that suffering ceases, and freedom from cyclic existence becomes irreversible.

**Saintly Qualities Are the Natural Expression of Transcendence, Not External Performance**  
These qualities—moral discipline, mental stillness, wisdom, non-attachment, and equanimity—are not adopted behaviors but spontaneous expressions of inner transformation. They emerge without effort once afflictions are extinguished. Their presence is not dependent on recognition or external reward; they are grounded in an unshakable inner peace that exists beyond the fluctuations of worldly experience.

**Liberation Means Transcendence of the Four Universal Sufferings**  
Those who attain this state are no longer bound by the suffering inherent in birth, aging, sickness, and death. Their freedom is not escape from physical processes but liberation from the mental clinging that turns these natural phenomena into sources of distress. This transcendence is the direct result of aligned ethical conduct, wisdom, and mental cultivation.
