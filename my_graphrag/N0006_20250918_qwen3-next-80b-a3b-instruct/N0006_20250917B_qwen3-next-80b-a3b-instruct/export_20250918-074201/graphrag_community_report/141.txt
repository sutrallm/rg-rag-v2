**Title: Chaowu Zhuo, Wuwo Guan, and the Path of Shengbze to Sikong**

**Summary:**  
The path to ultimate liberation entails the progressive dissolution of self-identification through continuous mindfulness and meditative insight. Central to this process is the practice of non-self observation, which unravels the illusion of permanence and ownership over experience. Those who embody moral discipline and meditative stability—referred to as Shengbze—attain unshakable equanimity, free from attachment to even spiritual attainments. Their unwavering practice leads to the state of Juewuze, where all phenomena are seen as illusory and responded to without clinging or aversion. This culminates in Sikong—the complete release from any notion of self or possession—transcending the cycle of birth and death. Supernatural powers, calm states, and meditative bliss are recognized as transient and must not be grasped. Only sustained daily effort dismantles subtle habitual patterns, leading to a mind that is unstained, unobstructed, and utterly free.

**Chaning as the Foundation for Non-Attachment**  
Stable meditation (Chanding) provides the clarity necessary to perceive impermanence and non-self in all experiences. When combined with the wisdom of transcending attachment (Chaowu zhuo), it becomes a means to authentic realization—not a state to be clung to. Those who have perfected Chanding, such as Juewuze and Shengbze, maintain mental stability regardless of external conditions or internal fluctuations, demonstrating that meditative stillness is not an end but a vehicle.

**Non-Self Observation as the Dismantling of Suffering**  
The practice of Wuwo guan targets the root of all affliction: the belief in an inherent self. By observing the body and mind as impermanent, interdependent, and lacking fixed identity, this practice dissolves attachment, fear, and suffering. It directly enables Chaowu zhuo, as the realization that “I” and “mine” are illusions removes the basis for clinging—even to meditative states or spiritual powers.

**Transcending Attachment as the Defining Wisdom**  
Chaowu zhuo is the awakened perspective that all phenomena—including pleasure, pain, gains, losses, and supernatural abilities—are impermanent, non-self, and empty. This wisdom prevents spiritual materialism by teaching that even meditative bliss and supernatural powers (Guang) must be released. Those who embody this wisdom, such as Shengbze and Juewuze, do not grasp at calm nor fear its loss, remaining perfectly equanimous regardless of experience.

**The Enlightened State as Moral and Meditative Perfection**  
Shengbze are characterized by pure moral discipline, unwavering mindfulness, and freedom from defilements. Their daily practice of continuous effort (Chixu xiulian) prevents complacency and dismantles subtle habitual patterns. This sustained diligence leads to the state of Wuzhan wuai—a mind utterly free from clinging, aversion, and conceptual elaboration—aligning them with the qualities of Juewuze.

**Ultimate Liberation as the Realization of Non-Self**  
Sikong is not an attainment but the complete cessation of identification with self and possessions. It is achieved only through sustained effort to release all notions of “I” and “mine,” made possible by the deep insight of Wuwo guan and the expansive wisdom of Chaowu zhuo. Juewuze, having fully realized Sikong, experience unconditioned freedom—they are no longer bound by the ocean of birth and death (Shengsi kuhai), having seen all phenomena (Wanfa) as transient and selfless.

**Continuous Practice as the Path Across the Cycle of Suffering**  
Escaping the endless cycle of birth and death requires daily, unceasing mindfulness and meditation. There is no momentary breakthrough sufficient for liberation; only the persistent, undisturbed practice of observing non-self and cultivating non-attachment dismantles ignorance and leads to ultimate stability.
