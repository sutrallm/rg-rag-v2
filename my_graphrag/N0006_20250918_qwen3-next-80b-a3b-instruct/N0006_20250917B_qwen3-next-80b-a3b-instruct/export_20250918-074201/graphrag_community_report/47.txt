**Title: Chan Ding as the Path to Liberation Through Mindfulness and Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
Chan Ding is a meditative practice rooted in sustained mindfulness and diligent effort, designed to cultivate mental stability, clarity, and freedom from attachment, desire, and mental distractions. It is not a passive state but an active discipline requiring continuous zeal and ethical grounding. True Chan Ding transcends attachment to pleasant meditative experiences, relying instead on wisdom to perceive the impermanent and illusory nature of all phenomena. When integrated into daily life—through walking, eating, breathing—it transforms ordinary actions into vehicles of liberation. The state embodied by Sheng Zhe reflects its highest achievement: a mind permanently free from agitation, stabilized through non-attachment and disciplined practice. Chan Ding, paired with ethical conduct and wisdom, forms the central artery of the path to liberation, dissolving greed, anger, delusion, and drowsiness by revealing their transient nature.

---

**Mental Stability Arises from Sustained Mindfulness and Diligent Effort**  
Chan Ding is not achieved through occasional practice but through unbroken mindfulness anchored in the present moment. This stability is cultivated by diligent effort—Zealous Practice and Jing Jin—which actively suppress wandering thoughts, drowsiness, and mental restlessness. Without this consistent energy, meditation degenerates into distraction. The mind becomes calm not by force but by the steady, non-judgmental observation of experience, allowing it to settle naturally.

**Liberation Requires Transcending Attachment to Meditative States**  
Pleasant experiences during meditation—such as joy, light, or tranquility—are not ends in themselves. True practice involves recognizing these states as impermanent and illusory, and letting go of clinging to them. Attachment to such experiences obstructs deeper freedom. Authentic Chan Ding is marked by non-attachment (Fei Zhu), ensuring the practice remains a means to liberation rather than another form of entanglement.

**Everyday Activities Are the Ground of Meditative Practice**  
Chan Ding is not confined to seated meditation or secluded retreats. It is realized in the midst of daily life—while eating, walking, standing, or lying down—by maintaining continuous awareness in each moment. This transforms routine actions into expressions of meditative concentration, proving that liberation is found not in escaping the world, but in perceiving it clearly without attachment.

**Deluded Thoughts and Afflictions Dissolve Through Clarity, Not Suppression**  
Wandering thoughts, delusion, desire, anger, and drowsiness are not eliminated by force but by the radiant clarity that arises from deep concentration. Chan Ding stabilizes the mind enough to observe these afflictions as transient, reducing their power. Greed fades as attachment to sensory pleasures weakens; anger dissolves without fuel; drowsiness is countered by alertness. Wisdom emerges from this clarity, revealing their root in impermanence and non-self.

**Chan Ding is the Foundation for Wisdom and Ethical Practice**  
Chan Ding serves as the necessary stabilizing force that allows wisdom (Zhineneng and Zhi Hui) to arise. It prepares the mind to perceive reality without distortion, enabling the insight that all phenomena are non-self and impermanent. This wisdom, in turn, deepens the practice by dissolving attachment. Ethics provide the essential clarity and discipline required for Chan Ding to take root, making the three elements—ethics, concentration, and wisdom—interdependent and inseparable.

**Sheng Zhe Embodies the Fully Realized State of Chan Ding**  
Sheng Zhe represents the culmination of this practice: a mind completely stable, entirely free from disturbance, and unmoved by external conditions. This state is not mystical but the direct result of prolonged, disciplined meditation and unwavering non-attachment. The sage’s purity and calm are not innate; they are the outcome of sustained mindfulness, effort, and the transcendence of all clinging, including to meditative achievements.
