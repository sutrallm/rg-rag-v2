**Title: The Eradication of Anger and the Nature of the Liberated Saint**

**Summary:**  
Anger, rooted in aversion and resistance to unpleasant experiences, is a primary obstacle to inner peace and liberation. It is sustained by delusion, which reinforces false separations between self and other, and is closely linked with greed as one of the three root afflictions. Moral discipline provides the necessary foundation for calming the mind, enabling the stabilization of attention that allows anger to be observed and dissipated without reaction. A truly liberated being has completely uprooted anger, greed, and delusion, embodying perfect moral discipline, unshakable equanimity, and freedom from the cycle of birth and death.

**Anger arises from resistance to reality and perpetuates suffering**  
Anger emerges when the mind resists or rejects unpleasant experiences, generating agitation, hostility, and inner turmoil. This resistance is not mere preference but a deep-seated refusal to accept reality as it is, which binds the individual to cycles of distress and obstructs peace.

**Delusion sustains anger through false distinctions**  
The mind’s mistaken belief in fixed, separate identities—self and other—fuels aversion by creating illusory boundaries. This deluded perception reinforces resentment and blocks the clarity needed to see the impermanent and interconnected nature of experience, thereby extending the grip of anger.

**Moral discipline enables mental clarity and stability**  
Ethical conduct forms the essential ground upon which mental stabilization and wisdom can grow. Without the purity of behavior, the mind remains too agitated or distracted to engage in the deep observation required to dissolve emotional reactivity.

**Calm attention dissolves anger through non-reactive observation**  
When the mind is stabilized through mindful calm, anger no longer controls reaction. Instead, it is seen clearly as transient, lacking inherent substance, and fades without being fed. This stability prevents the mind from being swept into hostility, allowing aversion to dissolve naturally.

**A liberated being has eradicated all three root afflictions**  
The perfected individual has completely uprooted greed, hatred, and delusion. This state is not partial restraint but total freedom—marked by permanent peace, absence of reactive emotions, and transcendence of worldly and divine attachments.

**True liberation results from non-attachment cultivated through wisdom**  
Freedom from suffering is not achieved through suppression or avoidance but through the deep realization that clinging and aversion are illusions. Wisdom reveals the nature of experience as impermanent and selfless, dissolving the conditions that give rise to anger.

**The saintly nature includes perfected moral discipline**  
The liberated person does not merely follow moral rules; their actions naturally reflect purity because greed and hatred are no longer present. Moral conduct is not an external practice for them but an intrinsic expression of their unconditioned state.

**Liberation ends the cycle of birth and death**  
The complete dissolution of anger, greed, and delusion leads to the cessation of the conditions that perpetuate rebirth. This transcendence is the ultimate outcome of a mind no longer driven by aversion, attachment, or ignorance.
