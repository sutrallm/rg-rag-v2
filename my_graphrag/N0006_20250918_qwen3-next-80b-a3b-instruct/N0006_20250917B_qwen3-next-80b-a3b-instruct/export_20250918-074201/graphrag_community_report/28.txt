**Title: Delusion, Greed, and the Saintly Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Delusion—the mistaken belief in a permanent self and the enduring nature of phenomena—is the foundational cause of suffering and the cycle of rebirth. It gives rise to greed, hatred, and aversion, which bind beings to continuous existence. Liberation is achieved only through the complete eradication of these three disturbances, accomplished by wisdom cultivated via mindfulness, meditation, and direct realization of impermanence and non-self. Those who have fully overcome them—Saints—dwell in unwavering stillness, free from craving, aversion, and ignorance, embodying pure awareness and transcendental peace. Sustained effort is essential to maintain progress and prevent regression into delusion and attachment.

**Delusion as the Root Cause of Suffering**  
Delusion, defined as ignorance of reality, is the fundamental affliction that obscures the true nature of existence: impermanence, non-self, and unsatisfactoriness. It manifests as the mistaken belief in a fixed, enduring self and the permanence of external phenomena. This misperception is not merely conceptual but actively sustains the cycle of rebirth by fueling attachment and aversion. Without confronting and dissolving this core illusion, no liberation is possible. It is the source from which all other disturbances arise, and only clear insight into reality uproots it.

**Greed as a Manifestation of Delusion**  
Greed emerges directly from delusion, as the false belief in lasting satisfaction through external objects, experiences, or states generates clinging and craving. It attaches to sensory pleasures, material possessions, spiritual attainments, and even meditative states, distorting practice into spiritual materialism when unbalanced by wisdom. This attachment perpetuates karma and rebirth, binding the individual to samsara. Its cessation requires dismantling the underlying delusion that anything transient can provide enduring fulfillment, and only through stabilized awareness can craving be calmed and transcended.

**Saints as Embodiments of Freedom**  
Saints are defined by the total extinction of delusion, greed, and hatred. They have realized non-self and impermanence through unwavering wisdom, eradicating all root defilements and transcending desire, aversion, and ignorance. Their minds are characterized by stable stillness, unmoved by pleasure or pain, and their presence reflects pure clarity unobscured by self-referential thought. They dwell in liberation—not as a mystical state, but as the natural result of perfected discipline and continuous awareness, free from the mechanisms that drive rebirth.

**Chan Ding as the Practice of Liberation**  
Chan Ding is the stabilizing practice that directly counters greed and delusion by calming the mind and reducing attachment to sensory and mental phenomena. It enables clear observation of the mind’s patterns, revealing the transient and selfless nature of experience. When properly guided by wisdom, it dissolves attachment and prevents spiritual degradation. Saints possess perfected Chan Ding—not as a technique, but as a continuous state of presence where afflictions no longer arise, and clarity remains unshaken.

**Effort as the Sustaining Force**  
Persistent, disciplined effort is indispensable to avoid regression into ignorance and attachment. Without consistent mindfulness and vigilance, the mind defaults to delusion, reinforcing cravings and aversions that perpetuate suffering. The path demands active cultivation—not passive reception. Diligence upholds the continuity of insight, prevents complacency in practice, and ensures that the arising of wisdom is not undermined by mental sloth or distraction. Liberation is not achieved by accident, but through sustained, unwavering commitment.
