**Title: Xinjieputo and the Path Beyond Lunhui: Cultivating Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Diligence**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of rebirth, known as Lunhui, is achieved through the complete cessation of clinging, resulting in Xinjieputo—ultimate mental liberation. This state is realized by eliminating ignorance and desire, primarily through sustained meditation (Chanding) and unwavering diligence (Jingjin). Those who have transcended Lunhui, such as Shengzhe, embody this freedom by no longer being bound by craving or attachment. Liberation is neither external nor forced but arises from inner transformation rooted in non-attachment and mindful awareness.

**Cultivation of Mental Liberation Requires Sustained Meditation**  
Xinjieputo is not attained through passive observation but through the disciplined practice of Chanding, which retrains the mind to disengage from automatic responses and rest in authentic awareness. This continuous training dismantles the habitual patterns that bind awareness to transient thoughts and conditions, enabling a direct experience of present-moment ease.

**Diligence Prevents Repetition of the Cycle of Rebirth**  
Laziness permits ignorance and desire to grow unchecked, reinforcing the conditions that sustain Lunhui. Jingjin—the persistent, active effort to remain awake and aligned with truth—is the essential counterforce. Without it, the momentum of craving continues, trapping individuals in endless cycles of suffering.

**Liberation Emerges from the Complete Abandonment of Attachment**  
Xinjieputo is not partial freedom but the total ending of clinging to any state, outcome, or identity. It arises when Wuzhui (non-attachment) is fully realized, rendering the fuel of rebirth—desire and ignorance—ineffective. This is not an intellectual understanding but a lived cessation of attachment that transforms the very nature of experience.

**Transcendence of Rebirth Is Demonstrated by the Absence of Craving and Ignorance**  
Shengzhe represents the embodiment of liberation, having dismantled the root causes of Lunhui. Their state is not defined by ritual or doctrine but by the absence of the inner forces—ignorance and attachment—that perpetuate suffering and rebirth. Their existence confirms that liberation is a realizable condition, not a distant ideal.
