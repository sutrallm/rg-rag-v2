**Title: The Enlightened Being: Ethical Conduct, Meditative Stability, and Non-Clinging Wisdom**

**Summary**  
The enlightened being emerges as a unified expression of perfected moral discipline, unwavering meditative stability, and direct, non-attached wisdom. These elements are mutually reinforcing: ethical purity establishes the conditions for meditative calm, which in turn enables clear perception of reality, and that perception dissolves all attachment—including to spiritual states. The three root obstacles of greed, hatred, and delusion are entirely absent, not suppressed but uprooted. This state is not transient attainment but a stable, natural way of being, sustained by the absence of clinging to any experience, even meditative ones.

**Ethical Conduct as Foundational Stability**  
Pure moral discipline is not merely behavioral adherence but an integral part of the perfected nature. It creates the necessary external and internal conditions for meditative stability to arise and endure. Without this foundation, mental clarity cannot take root, and deeper understanding remains inaccessible. The saintly one embodies this conduct not as effort, but as inherent expression.

**Meditative Stability as Natural Abiding**  
Chanding is not a temporary achievement but a sustained, effortless state resulting from perfected mindfulness and mental purity. It is not pursued as an end but arises as a consequence of ethical integrity and the absence of inner conflict. This stability is not passive; it provides the clear mental field required for true wisdom to emerge.

**Non-Clinging Wisdom as the Core of Realization**  
True wisdom is defined by direct, non-conceptual perception of impermanence, non-self, and emptiness. This understanding removes all forms of attachment—even to meditative states or spiritual accomplishments. Wisdom does not exist in isolation; it deepens chanding by dissolving attachment, and chanding refines wisdom by sustaining mental clarity. The absence of greed, hatred, and delusion confirms this wisdom as complete and unshakable.

**Interdependence of Ethical Practice, Calm, and Wisdom**  
These three components do not operate sequentially but recursively reinforce one another. Ethical conduct supports meditative clarity, clarity enables non-attached understanding, and understanding purifies conduct further. The perfected being reflects this cycle as a single, harmonious whole—where moral action, mental stillness, and penetrating insight are inseparable expressions of the same liberated condition.
