**The Threefold Training and the Path to Liberation: Moral Discipline, Concentration, and Wisdom**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achievable only through the integrated practice of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Correct understanding — particularly of impermanence, non-self, and the nature of reality — is central to wisdom and is a defining trait of awakened beings. Persistent effort is required to apply all three components consistently. Meditative absorption is the specific form of concentration in this framework, and wisdom is explicitly rooted in correct understanding.

**Moral Discipline, Concentration, and Wisdom Form the Sole Path to Liberation**  
The combination of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom is presented as the only true route to breaking free from the cycle of birth and death. No other approach is acknowledged; these three elements are inseparable in their role as the foundational structure of spiritual emancipation.

**Correct Understanding Is the Essence of Wisdom and the Marker of Awakened Beings**  
Wisdom is not abstract knowledge but is defined by correct understanding — specifically, the clear perception of impermanence, non-self, and the true nature of reality. This understanding is not merely a part of the training; it is the very core of wisdom. Furthermore, those who have attained awakening are distinguished by the presence of this correct understanding as an intrinsic quality.

**Meditative Absorption Is the Specific Form of Concentration in the Threefold Training**  
The term “concentration” within the threefold training is not generic. It precisely denotes meditative absorption (CHANDING), indicating that the mental focus required is of a deep, disciplined, and stabilized nature, distinct from ordinary attention or calm.

**Persistent Effort Is Necessary to Sustain Practice Across All Three Components**  
The effective application of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom demands continuous diligence. Without persistent effort, none of the components can be fully realized or maintained, emphasizing that spiritual progress is an active, sustained undertaking — not a passive or occasional practice.
