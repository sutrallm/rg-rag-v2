**Title: The Role of Meditation and Effort in the Threefold Practice of Mind Training**

**Summary:**  
Meditation serves as the second stage in the sequence of mind training, consolidating inner strength to enable the emergence of wisdom. Continuous effort sustains the practice of discipline, meditation, and wisdom throughout the entire process. The theoretical structure of mind training is realized in daily life through the practice direction of Buddhist teachings, which emphasizes awareness and letting go to unify discipline, meditation, and wisdom.

**Meditation Consolidates Inner Strength for Wisdom**  
Meditation is positioned as the second stage in the sequence of mind training, functioning not as an end in itself but as a necessary step to gather and focus mental energy. This consolidation creates the stability required for wisdom to arise, indicating that wisdom does not emerge spontaneously but depends on the prior development of mental cohesion.

**Continuous Effort Sustains the Threefold Practice**  
Effort is not confined to a particular stage but operates throughout the entire process of mind training. It provides the driving force that maintains discipline, supports meditation, and enables the cultivation of wisdom. Without sustained effort, the progression from one stage to the next cannot be maintained, revealing effort as the underlying constant supporting all phases.

**Daily Practice Integrates Discipline, Meditation, and Wisdom**  
The theoretical model of mind training becomes active and meaningful through the practice direction of Buddhist teachings, which translates abstract principles into moment-to-moment living. This is achieved by cultivating awareness and releasing attachment, ensuring that discipline, meditation, and wisdom are not practiced in isolation but are lived as an integrated whole in everyday experience.
