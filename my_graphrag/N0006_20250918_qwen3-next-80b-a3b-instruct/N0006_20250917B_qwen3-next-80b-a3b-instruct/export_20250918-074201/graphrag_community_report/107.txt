**Title: Sheli, Shengyi, and Xiuxing: The Path of Renunciation and Transcendent Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
True freedom arises through the uninterrupted practice of mindful presence in daily life, which cultivates a stable mental foundation. From this, a clear seeing of all phenomena as impermanent and without self naturally leads to the effortless release of all attachments—including those to spiritual experiences. This renunciation weakens craving and removes the basis for suffering, on which the attainment of Nirvana depends. The process is not forced but flows from direct understanding: when there is no sense of self to cling to, letting go becomes natural.

**Sheli Arises from the Realization of Wuwo**  
When one directly perceives that all phenomena—including thoughts, emotions, and spiritual attainments—lack inherent selfhood, the need to possess, protect, or identify with them dissolves. Renunciation is not an act of will or denial, but the spontaneous outcome of this seeing. Attachment cannot persist where no “I” exists to hold on.

**Shengyi Reveals Emptiness and Non-Self**  
Transcendent wisdom functions as the clear, unobstructed perception of reality’s true nature: all things are empty of fixed identity and constantly changing. This awareness does not emerge from intellectual understanding but from sustained mental clarity, allowing one to see beyond appearances and dissolve the illusion of a separate, enduring self.

**Xiuxing Sustains Chandigh in Daily Life**  
Meditative stability is not achieved only in isolation but through the continuous application of moment-to-moment awareness in ordinary actions—eating, walking, breathing. This daily practice is the living ground where mental steadiness is built, enabling the arising of profound vision without retreat from the world.

**Sheli Weakens Tanai by Removing Its Fuel**  
Craving and attachment thrive on the belief that sensory experiences—form, sound, smell, taste, touch—can bring lasting satisfaction. By seeing these as transient and unsatisfying, one ceases to pursue or resist them. This cessation starves the mental habits that bind, gradually untangling the root of suffering.

**Sheli Is the Necessary Condition for Nirvana**  
No state, however refined—whether bliss, psychic power, or insight into liberation—can be clung to without perpetuating bondage. True freedom demands the complete absence of grasping, even toward spiritual milestones. Only when all forms of attachment are released, including those disguised as attainment, does the mind become fully unshakable.

**Shengyi Leads to Sheli, Not the Reverse**  
It is not renunciation that creates wisdom, but wisdom that makes renunciation natural. The deeper one sees the selfless, empty nature of all experience, the less need there is to hold onto anything—including the idea of being free. Thus, the path culminates not in achieving a goal, but in ceasing to grasp at any destination.
