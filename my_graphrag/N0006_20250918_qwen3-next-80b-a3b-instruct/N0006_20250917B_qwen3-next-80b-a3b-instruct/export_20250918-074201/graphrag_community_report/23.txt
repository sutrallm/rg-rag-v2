**Title: Liberation Through the Uprooting of Mental Afflictions**

**Summary:**  
Mental afflictions such as greed, laziness, attachment, craving, anger, and delusion are the fundamental causes of suffering and the continuous cycle of birth and death. Their presence binds beings to rebirth, while their complete eradication defines the state of saintly beings who have attained liberation. Freedom from suffering is achieved not through passive observation alone, but through disciplined, effortful practice that cultivates non-attachment and creates mental space where these afflictions cannot manifest.

**Afflictions Sustain the Cycle of Rebirth**  
The recurring pattern of birth, death, and rebirth is not accidental but directly driven by mental defilements. These afflictions act as the root fuel that perpetuates existence within saṃsāra, making their removal essential to breaking the cycle. Without addressing them, liberation remains unattainable.

**Non-Attachment Is the Antidote to Core Defilements**  
The twin forces of grasping and aversion are identified as the primary sources of mental suffering. Non-attachment dismantles these forces at their core, reducing the mind’s vulnerability to afflictions and enabling a stable state free from reactive disturbance.

**Mental Clarity Weakens Afflictions**  
The practice of Chanding establishes a mental environment in which afflictions are unable to take root or gain strength. This conditioned space does not suppress them forcefully but renders them ineffective by depriving them of the conditions they need to flourish.

**Saintly Status Is Defined by Total Eradication**  
Those who have achieved transcendental peace are not merely calm or composed—they have severed all afflictions entirely. This complete uprooting is not a partial achievement but the defining characteristic of their awakened state, distinguishing them from those still entangled in suffering and rebirth.

**Effortful Practice Is Necessary for Transformation**  
Liberation does not arise spontaneously. It requires sustained, intentional effort to resist the arising of afflictions before they dominate the mind. Passive awareness is insufficient; active discipline is the means by which progress is secured and maintained.
