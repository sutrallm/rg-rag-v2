**Title: Shengli Zhihui, Wuwo Zhihui, and Jiexi: The Path to Liberation in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**  
Liberation from suffering is achieved through the cultivation of transcendent wisdom and the dissolution of the illusion of self, supported by mindful awareness and persistent effort. These inner practices directly eliminate mental afflictions such as craving and aversion, leading to a state of unattached clarity. Liberation is not an external destination but a continuous, moment-to-moment realization grounded in the direct observation of impermanence and non-self.

---

**Transcendent Wisdom as the Core Path to Freedom**  
Shengli zhihui—the clear perception of impermanence, non-self, and the ungraspable nature of all phenomena—is the direct means to liberation. It dismantles the roots of suffering by removing the basis for attachment and aversion. Without this wisdom, liberation cannot occur, as it is the only force capable of uprooting the deep-seated misunderstandings that sustain suffering.

**Wisdom of Non-Self as the Practical Expression of Liberation**  
Wuwo zhihui is the lived application of transcendent wisdom, specifically centered on releasing the illusion of "self" and "belonging." This dissolution of self-referential thinking is not abstract but experiential; it is the very act through which clinging and suffering cease. It is inseparable from liberation itself, as the eradication of "I" and "mine" removes the core mechanism of affliction.

**Mental Stillness and Mindful Awareness as Essential Conditions**  
Chanding creates the inner stillness necessary for transcendent wisdom to emerge, enabling direct observation of reality without distortion. Zhengnian, or mindful awareness, is the daily embodiment of liberation, transforming freedom from a distant ideal into a present-moment practice. Together, they provide the stable ground upon which wisdom takes root and flourishes.

**Persistent Effort as the Driver of Transformation**  
Shengli zhihui cannot arise without jingjin—sustained, unwavering effort. Insight demands consistent, non-judgmental observation and the refusal to tolerate delusion. Passive awareness is insufficient; only through disciplined, continuous practice does the mind gradually release its grip on illusion.

**Afflictions as the Target of Liberation**  
Fannuo—craving, aversion, and pride—are the mental disturbances born of ignorance. They are not mere emotions but mechanisms of bondage. True liberation is measured not by external conditions but by the complete absence of these inner defilements. Their eradication is the defining sign of freedom.

**Liberation as the Culmination of Integrated Practice**  
Jiexi is the ultimate outcome where transcendent wisdom, non-self realization, mindful awareness, and persistent effort converge. It is not a passive state but an active transformation—achieved only when the mind no longer clings, reacts, or identifies. Liberation is present whenever the conditions of wisdom and awareness are fully maintained in each moment.
