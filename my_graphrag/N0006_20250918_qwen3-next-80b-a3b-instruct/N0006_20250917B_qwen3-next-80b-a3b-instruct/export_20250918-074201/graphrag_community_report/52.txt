**Title: Chao Yue Zhu Zhai and the Path of Continuous Practice to Liberation**

**Summary**  
True freedom is achieved not through rituals or external forces, but through persistent mindful observation that dissolves attachment to all experiences—whether pleasurable, meditative, or spiritual. This non-attachment arises from directly perceiving the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all phenomena. Those who embody this understanding, known as Sheng Zhe, are the culmination of daily, diligent practice. Without this wisdom, even spiritual attainments become chains; with it, liberation is realized.

**Transcending Attachment Defines Liberation**  
Liberation is inseparable from the release of clinging—not only to worldly desires but to meditative states, supernatural powers, and even the notion of liberation itself. To remain attached to any experience, no matter how elevated, prevents true freedom. Only when the mind no longer identifies with or grasps at phenomena—whether blissful or transcendent—can final release occur.

**Impermanence, Unsatisfactoriness, and Non-Self Are the Foundations**  
The direct perception that all phenomena are transient, inherently incapable of providing lasting satisfaction, and lacking an independent self dismantles the root of attachment. This understanding allows the practitioner to observe all experiences without investment, turning even the most desirable spiritual states into objects of discernment rather than objects of craving.

**Continuous Practice Is the Daily Expression of Diligence**  
Freedom is not attained in a single moment but through the steady, unrelenting practice of mindful awareness. This daily discipline, born of persistent effort, gradually reshapes perception and habit, allowing insight into emptiness to deepen over time. It is not passive observation but active, consistent vigilance over the mind’s tendencies.

**The Saintly One Embodies This Wisdom Naturally**  
Sheng Zhe, who is Chao Yue Zhu Zhai in full realization, no longer clings to any experience or goal. The absence of attachment is not a technique but the natural condition of one who sees all things exactly as they are—without distortion, desire, or identification. This state is thefruit of lifelong cultivation, not sudden enlightenment.

**Wisdom Beyond Attachment Is the Core of True Chan Ding**  
Meditative depth is not measured by intensity of experience but by the absence of clinging to those experiences. Authentic Chan Ding arises only when the practitioner observes all mental states—peace, power, stillness—with equanimity. Without this non-attachment, meditation remains bound to ego and desire, however refined.
