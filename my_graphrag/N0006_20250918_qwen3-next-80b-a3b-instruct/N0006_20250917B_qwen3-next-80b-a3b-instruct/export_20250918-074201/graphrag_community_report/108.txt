**Title: Transcending Attachment and the Realization of Niepan through Non-Self Observation**

**Summary**  
The path to Niepan — the complete cessation of suffering — is achieved not through external pursuit but through direct, moment-to-moment awareness of the impermanent and selfless nature of all experience. This is realized by observing the five aggregates as transient and unowned, dissolving the illusion of a fixed self. Through mindful awareness in daily life, attachment and aversion are relinquished, greed, hatred, and delusion are extinguished, and freedom arises. Practice is not confined to meditation but encompasses every action, with the mind itself as the sole ground of awakening.

**Non-Self Observation Dissolves the Illusion of a Permanent Self**  
By continuously recognizing that physical and mental phenomena — including form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness — arise from interdependent causes and are neither owned nor controlled, the illusion of a fixed self unravels. This observation directly targets the five aggregates as the foundation of the mistaken belief in “I” and “mine,” leading to the release of clinging and the cessation of conflict.

**Transcending Attachment Is the Path to Niepan**  
Niepan is not a distant state but the immediate freedom that emerges when attachment to pleasure and aversion to pain are abandoned. This is accomplished through the deep understanding that all phenomena are impermanent and non-self. The relinquishment of grasping and resistance — whether to existence or non-existence — allows reality to be experienced as it is, resulting in a mind untroubled and unconditioned.

**Mindful Awareness in Daily Life Sustains Liberation**  
Awakening occurs not only in seclusion but through continuous mindfulness during walking, standing, sitting, and lying down. Every thought, sensation, and action becomes an occasion for noticing emotional reactions without engagement or judgment. This steady presence, known as mindful awareness, prevents the reactivation of attachment and aversion, turning ordinary activities into acts of spiritual cultivation.

**The Mind Itself Is the Ground of Enlightenment**  
True freedom is found within the present-moment awareness of one’s own breath and mental states. There is no external destination or post-death realm to attain. Enlightenment arises when the practitioner recognizes that all conditions — including the five aggregates — are transient, and the mind’s natural state is already free when not clouded by craving, aversion, or delusion.

**The Extinguishing of Greed, Hatred, and Delusion Defines Niepan**  
Niepan is characterized by the total cessation of these three afflictions, which are the roots of suffering. Non-self observation directly undermines them by revealing that the self is not a solid entity but a fleeting aggregation. When this illusion is seen through, greed loses its object, hatred loses its target, and delusion dissolves — leaving only unshakable peace.

**Non-Self Observation Is the Practical Means to Realize Niepan**  
This practice is not theoretical but experiential. By observing the five aggregates without identification or attachment, the mechanisms of clinging are dismantled. Each moment of clear observation weakens the hold of the egoic structure, making liberation not an ideal but a tangible outcome of sustained awareness.
