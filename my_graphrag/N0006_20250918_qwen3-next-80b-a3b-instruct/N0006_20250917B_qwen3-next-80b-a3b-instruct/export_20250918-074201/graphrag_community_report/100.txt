**Title: Wuming, Fannao, and Jingjin: The Path to Jietuo**

**Summary**  
Liberation (Jietuo) arises only through the cessation of ignorance (Wuming), the root of all mental afflictions (Fannao). These afflictions—greed, anger, delusion, and clinging—originate from the mistaken belief in permanence, satisfaction, and a permanent self. Sustained, vigilant effort (Jingjin), meditative concentration (Chanding), and mindfulness (Zhengnian) are indispensable for uprooting ignorance. Wisdom (Zhihui), cultivated through direct observation of dependent origination (Yuanhe), non-self (Wuwo), and impermanence (Wuchang), dissolves craving (Yuwan) and terminates the cycle of birth and death (Sihai). Those who have achieved liberation (Shengzhe) have eradicated all defilements and no longer generate suffering; they exemplify the truth that awakening is inward, not granted by external forces.

---

**Ignorance is the Root of All Suffering**  
Wuming—the failure to perceive reality as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of inherent self—is the foundational cause of all distress. This misperception gives rise to craving, aversion, and the illusion of “I” and “mine,” which in turn fuel the cycle of rebirth (Sihai) and the suffering inherent in birth and death (Shengsi ku). Without Wuming, the mental afflictions (Fannao) and karmic chains that bind beings to cyclic existence would not arise.

**Mental Afflictions Manifest from Ignorance**  
Fannao—including greed, anger, jealousy, anxiety, and delusion—are not independent entities but expressions of Wuming. They emerge from clinging to the false notions of permanence and selfhood, and from the insistence that phenomena “should” or “should not” be as they are. These defilements sustain suffering by reinforcing attachment and aversion, and their presence ensures continued entanglement in Sihai. Their cessation is not optional but necessary for freedom.

**Vigilant Effort Sustains Liberation**  
Jingjin—the unwavering, non-lazy cultivation of mindfulness and ethical conduct—is the essential engine of transformation. It prevents the mind from lapsing back into delusion, sustains meditative concentration (Chanding), and directly combats the inertia of laziness and craving. Without this continuous, disciplined effort, wisdom cannot take root, and the cycle of rebirth persists. Jingjin is not merely support but the active force that dismantles ignorance.

**Wisdom Dissolves Illusion Through Direct Realization**  
Zhihui—wisdom grounded in direct observation—is the sole antidote to Wuming. It arises not from belief or ritual, but from seeing the truth of dependent origination (Yuanhe), non-self (Wuwo), and impermanence (Wuchang). This insight unravels the fabricated narratives of “I” and “mine,” dissolving clinging and its resultant suffering. True liberation (Jietuo) is possible only when wisdom reveals the illusory nature of all phenomena, including spiritual goals and meditative states.

**Liberation is the Cease of Clinging, Not External Gain**  
Jietuo is not conferred by deities, rituals, or supernatural powers. It is the complete extinction of the causes of rebirth: ignorance and clinging. Those who have attained liberation (Shengzhe) are defined by the total absence of Fannao and the cessation of shengsi ku. They embody a state beyond birth and death (Xiuwu guishu), where dependent origination is fully understood and no entity remains to be reborn. The Buddha serves only as a guide—the awakening must come from within.

**Mindfulness and Concentration Establish the Conditions for Awakening**  
Zhengnian (mindfulness) anchors awareness in the present, preventing the mind from constructing deluded narratives of past and future. This continuous presence illuminates ignorance and allows afflictions to be seen as they arise—naturally dissolving before they take root. Chanding (concentration) complements this by calming mental turbulence, reducing reactivity, and creating the stability required for wisdom to penetrate deeply. Together, they form the practical framework for uprooting Wuming.
