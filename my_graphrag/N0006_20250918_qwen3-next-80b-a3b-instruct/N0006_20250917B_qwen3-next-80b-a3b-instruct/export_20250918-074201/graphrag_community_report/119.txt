**Title: Buddhist Liberation and the Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved solely through the complete elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion. These three factors are identified as the root causes of suffering and the sole drivers of rebirth. Those who attain liberation—referred to as Noble Ones or Saints—embody this eradication, dwelling in unshakable peace and non-attachment. Their state is synonymous with nirvana, which is the absolute cessation of reincarnation. Effort in spiritual practice is directed exclusively toward removing these poisons, as their absence defines sanctity and awakening.

**The Three Poisons Sustain the Cycle of Rebirth**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are repeatedly identified as the fundamental causes that perpetuate rebirth. They are described as the driving forces behind karma, suffering, and continued existence in the cycle of samsara. Their presence ensures the continuation of karmic bonds, while their absence terminates it. No other causes are mentioned as primary; these three are consistently named as the source and mechanism of entrapment.

**Liberation Is Defined by the Complete Removal of the Three Poisons**  
Freedom from rebirth is not achieved through ritual, study, or external conditions, but exclusively through the total eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. This removal is the single criterion for describing an awakened being, a Saint, or a Noble One. Their state is not characterized by supernatural abilities or mystical experiences, but by the absence of these three defilements, making their liberation concrete and measurable.

**The State of Nirvana Is the Direct Opposite of Rebirth**  
Nirvana and the cycle of reincarnation are presented as mutually exclusive conditions—where one exists, the other cannot. Liberation is not a transformation of the cycle but its complete termination. This dichotomy underscores that spiritual progress is not about improving one’s position within samsara, but about ending it entirely. The state of nirvana is described as the cessation of rebirth, not an alternative realm or higher plane.

**Noble Ones Dwell in Effortless Peace Through Non-Attachment**  
Those who have attained liberation are not only free from the three poisons but also from clinging—even to meditative bliss or spiritual achievements. Their peace is unshakable and不受外界影响, stemming solely from inner eradication rather than external circumstances. This trait distinguishes them from those who may experience temporary calm but remain bound by attachment or the desire for outcomes.

**Effort in Practice Is Targeted Exclusively at Eliminating the Three Poisons**  
Spiritual discipline, mindfulness, and ethical conduct are not pursued generically, but specifically to destroy greed, hatred, and delusion. The text explicitly links sustained effort to breaking the karmic chains that bind beings to rebirth. This effort counters sloth and ignorance—both of which are themselves expressions of the three poisons—confirming that all practice is focused on their removal as the only path forward.
