Title: The Nature of Emptiness and the Dissolution of Self in Buddhist Philosophy

Summary:  
The core teaching centers on the understanding that all phenomena lack independent existence, leading to the relinquishment of attachment to self and possessions. This realization serves as the foundational basis for liberation, directly undermining the illusion of a fixed, autonomous self. The concepts of dependent origination and emptiness are presented as synonymous expressions of the same fundamental truth within the Buddhist framework.

---

All phenomena are devoid of intrinsic nature  
Every phenomenon arises dependently and possesses no permanent, independent essence. This absence of inherent existence is not a metaphysical speculation but the defining characteristic of reality, directly countering the assumption of fixed, self-sustaining entities.

The illusion of self is dismantled through this understanding  
The conviction that “I” and “what belongs to me” are real and enduring is shown to be a distortion. Recognizing the emptiness of all things renders these notions unsustainable, thereby eliminating the root cause of clinging and suffering.

Dependent origination and emptiness are equivalent expressions  
These two terms are not distinct doctrines but alternate formulations of the same principle. The assertion that all things arise conditionally and thus cannot possess self-nature is the unifying core of the teaching, forming the indispensable basis for transcendence.
