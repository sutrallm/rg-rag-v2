**The Path to Liberation: Realizing Innate Buddha Nature Through Right View, Mindfulness, and Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
True liberation is achieved through an internal, self-directed process that rejects external rituals and supernatural dependencies. It requires the eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering—by cultivating right view and steady mindfulness. These practices, sustained through daily effort, enable the uncovering of one’s innate, unconditioned awareness. The culmination of this process is the state of the Sage, marked by complete freedom from craving and delusion, and the realization of a state beyond birth, death, and conceptual fixation.

**Right View as the Guiding Compass**  
Accurate perception of reality—impermanence, suffering, and non-self—serves as the foundational orientation for the entire journey. This understanding acts as the means to navigate beyond ignorance, forming the basis for all progress. Without this clarity, actions and intentions remain misaligned with the nature of existence, perpetuating bondage.

**Right Mindfulness as the Active Tool**  
Moment-by-moment awareness is the practical mechanism that maintains forward movement along the path. It prevents regression into delusion by anchoring experience in direct observation rather than conceptual elaboration. This continuous presence is not passive but actively sustains momentum toward awakening.

**Non-Attachment as the Core Wisdom**  
Letting go of clinging—not just to objects, but to outcomes, states, and even spiritual achievements—is the essential practice that unlocks freedom. It is through this relinquishment that the mind ceases to be bound by the five aggregates and the six sense objects, allowing the true nature to emerge.

**Effortful Practice as the Driving Force**  
Progress along the path is inseparable from sustained, disciplined effort. It is not a passive revelation but an active unfolding fueled by consistent, intentional action. Without this persistent engagement, the dismantling of deluded patterns cannot occur.

**Daily Practice as the Living Expression**  
The path is not confined to formal meditation or special occasions. It is enacted in every ordinary moment of life, transforming routine experience into the ground for awakening. This integration ensures that awareness and non-attachment become habitual, not temporary states.

**The Three Poisons as Obstructions Rooted in Ignorance**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion arise from a failure to recognize impermanence and non-self. Their persistence depends entirely on this foundational ignorance; when impermanence is clearly seen, the fuel for craving and aversion diminishes, leading to their natural dissolution.

**Innate Buddha Nature as the Hidden Reality**  
All beings possess an unconditioned awareness, but it remains concealed by deluded thoughts. Realization does not involve acquiring something new, but recognizing what has always been present—by removing the veils of attachment and conceptual fixation.

**The Sage as the Embodiment of Liberation**  
The fully realized person is defined by the total absence of greed, hatred, and delusion. This state reflects the unobstructed manifestation of innate awareness—pure, unconditioned, and free. The Sage is not defined by title or status, but by the completeness of their inner transformation.

**The Unborn, Undying, and Unabiding State as the Final Destination**  
The ultimate aim is not a mystical realm or transcendent experience, but a condition beyond all forms of arising and ceasing. It is freedom from conceptual grasping, where even the notion of self or attainment no longer applies—only unconditioned peace remains.
