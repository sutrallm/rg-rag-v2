**Title: The Cultivation of Non-Dual Awareness Through Daily Practice in 修行**

**Summary:**  
True liberation arises not through isolated spiritual events or ritualistic acts, but through the steady, unbroken practice of present-moment awareness embedded in every ordinary activity. This continuous mindfulness dissolves conceptual boundaries—such as self and other, sacred and profane—and fosters a state where observation occurs without attachment, leading to the natural fading of delusion and the unfolding of wisdom. Those who embody this state remain steadily present, free from the pull of dualistic thought, regardless of circumstance.

**Daily Practice is the Sole Path to Sustained Awareness**  
The uninterrupted observation of bodily and mental phenomena during routine activities—walking, standing, sitting, lying—is not merely a technique but the essential context for cultivation. It is through this consistent, unassuming presence in each breath, thought, and emotion that awareness becomes stable and enduring. No special environment or temporary experience substitutes for this daily, lifewide engagement.

**Non-Duality Emerges from Present-Moment Observation Without Separation**  
Liberation is not attained by withdrawing from the world, but by dissolving the mental constructs that divide experience into opposites. When awareness is fully present—without resistance or pursuit—the distinction between observer and observed ceases. This unified mode of perception reveals reality as it is, beyond conceptual labeling.

**Chanding is Made Real Through Continuous Awareness**  
The depth of meditative concentration known as chanding does not arise in isolation or through forced stillness. It is actively sustained by the persistent, non-attached observation of each moment. Awareness is not a passive state but the living force that both supports and expresses chanding, making it inseparable from the rhythm of daily life.

**Liberation is Gradual and Rooted in Consistency, Not Epiphany**  
Freedom from delusion does not come from sudden revelations or occasional spiritual practices. It unfolds slowly, through the unwavering repetition of mindful attention in the most ordinary moments. Only through this steady, unglamorous discipline does the mind naturally stabilize beyond attachment and conceptual clinging.

**Saints Are Defined by Uninterrupted Present-Moment Awareness**  
Those who have matured in this path remain continuously luminous in awareness, untouched by the habitual patterns of conceptual thinking. Their state is not extraordinary in form—it manifests in ordinary acts—but is distinguished by the absence of separation between awareness and experience.
