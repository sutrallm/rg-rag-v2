**Title: KU, Zhi Hui, and Qing Xing: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**  
Suffering arises from clinging to a false sense of self and the illusion of permanence. Liberation is achieved through present-moment awareness, which cultivates wisdom that sees all phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and without inherent self. This wisdom, grounded in mindfulness, dismantles attachment naturally, leading to the cessation of suffering without force or resistance.

---

**Suffering is Inherent in Conditioned Existence**  
All conditioned experiences are marked by dissatisfaction, not as a temporary state but as an intrinsic quality. This unsatisfactoriness stems from attachment and ignorance, particularly the mistaken belief in permanence and self. Observing this condition is not for despair but to motivate release, as recognizing it is the first step toward freedom.

**Wisdom Arises from Seeing Impermanence, Suffering, and Non-Self**  
True understanding comes not from intellectual agreement but from direct observation of the three fundamental qualities of existence: impermanence, suffering, and non-self. These are not abstract concepts but observable realities. Recognizing them dismantles the structure of clinging and allows liberation to occur spontaneously.

**Present-Moment Awareness is the Foundation of Meditative Stability**  
Mindfulness of breath, sensations, and thoughts in the here and now is not merely a technique but the very essence of concentrated mind. This non-judgmental observation creates the mental clarity needed for wisdom to emerge, making it the practical core of all authentic practice.

**Clinging to Self is the Root of Suffering**  
The belief in a fixed, independent identity—ignoring the truth of non-self—directly fuels dissatisfaction. When this illusion is seen clearly, suffering loses its power. The dissolution of ego-clinging is not an achievement but the natural result of seeing reality as it is.

**Concentration Supports Wisdom, But Mindfulness is Its True Form**  
A stable mental state is necessary for clear perception, yet true concentration is not forced stillness. It is found in the continuous, open awareness of the present moment. When mindfulness is steady, insight arises without effort, revealing the nature of experience as it unfolds.

**Liberation is the Cessation of Clinging, Not the Attainment of a State**  
Freedom is not a destination reached through effort or supernatural power, but the complete letting go of attachment to all experiences—including meditative ones. This is accomplished by seeing all phenomena as inherently unsatisfactory and selfless, ending the pursuit of temporary pleasure.

**Mindfulness Enables Non-Reaction, Allowing Suffering to Dissolve**  
Witnessing the arising of dissatisfaction without reacting or identifying with it permits its natural unraveling. This is not suppression or avoidance, but a clear seeing that removes fuel from the fire of suffering.

**Wisdom Transcends All Experiences, Including Spiritual Achievements**  
True understanding does not favor pleasant states over unpleasant ones. It applies equally to meditative calm, sensory pleasure, or supernatural phenomena. Only when even these are seen as conditioned and impermanent can attachment be fully released.

**Recognizing Unsatisfactoriness Leads to Release from the Pursuit of Pleasure**  
When all experiences are seen as inherently flawed, the hunger for temporary satisfaction fades. This shift does not require denial of pleasure but realization that no conditioned experience can provide lasting fulfillment.

**Non-Self is the Key to Dissolving Egoic Structures**  
The insight that there is no fixed, independent self undermines the basis of all clinging. This is not a philosophical dismissal of identity but a direct perception that erodes the foundation of suffering at its source.
