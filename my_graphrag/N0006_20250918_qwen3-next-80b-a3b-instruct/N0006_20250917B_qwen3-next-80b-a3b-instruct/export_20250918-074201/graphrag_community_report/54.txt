**Title: Transcending Attachment in Chan Ding and Dependent Origination**

**Summary**  
The path to transcending attachment is cultivated through direct observation of impermanence, non-self, and interdependence. Chan Ding serves as the method to quiet the mind and perceive reality without distortion. True liberation emerges not from clinging to meditative states or supernatural abilities, but from the sustained realization that all phenomena arise conditionally and lack inherent existence. The sage exemplifies this by remaining unattached even to profound experiences.

**Observing Impermanence Undermines Clinging**  
Direct awareness of the transient nature of all experiences—whether ordinary sensations or elevated meditative states—erodes the foundation for attachment. When phenomena are seen as fleeting, the impulse to hold onto them, including blissful or powerful states, naturally diminishes.

**Realizing Non-Self Reduces Attachment**  
The illusion of a permanent, unchanging self is the root of clinging. When this illusion is dissolved through observation, the sense of ownership or identity tied to experiences dissolves as well, making attachment unsustainable.

**Dependent Origination Reveals Non-Inherent Existence**  
All phenomena are understood as arising from a network of conditions, not as independent or self-originated entities. Recognizing this conditional nature removes the perception of solidity or permanence, which in turn eliminates the basis for grasping or aversion.

**Chan Ding Enables Clear Perception of Reality**  
By calming mental activity, Chan Ding creates the conditions for undistorted observation. It is not an end in itself but the practical approach through which impermanence, non-self, and interdependence are directly seen.

**The Sage Does Not Cling to Experiences or Powers**  
Mastery is demonstrated not by the presence of profound meditative states or supernatural abilities, but by the absence of clinging to them. The sage remains equanimous, recognizing even these states as impermanent and conditioned.
