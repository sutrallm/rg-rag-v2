**Title: Sheng Zhe and the Path of Transcendent Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Sheng Zhe, the fully enlightened being, manifests profound inner freedom through the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. This state is sustained by unwavering moral discipline, deep meditative stability, and a wisdom that sees all phenomena—including spiritual attainments—as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of self. Liberation is not achieved through ritual or divine grace, but through continuous, mindful practice that transforms habitual patterns of clinging. The sage embodies tranquility, non-reactivity, and equal-mindedness, unaffected by external conditions or internal states, and stands as the living expression of transcendence beyond birth and death.

---

**Foundation of Ethical Purity**  
Pure moral discipline is the essential groundwork for all spiritual advancement. It ensures ethical conduct that clears the mind of corruption, enabling the stability and clarity required for deep meditation and transcendent insight. Without this foundation, progress toward liberation is unsustainable. The sage fully upholds this discipline not as external rule-following, but as an intrinsic expression of an unclouded heart.

**Meditative Stability as Intrinsic State**  
The mind of the sage is permanently stabilized, free from agitation, distraction, and disturbance. This state, known as Chan Ding, is not a temporary absorption but an enduring condition born of sustained mental training. It is characterized by unshakable calm, pure awareness, and the absence of inner turmoil—resulting directly from the eradication of defilements and the cultivation of discipline.

**Wisdom Beyond Attachment**  
True awakening arises from seeing all experiences—including bliss, supernatural powers, and the notion of liberation—as transient and without inherent substance. This understanding, known as Chao Yue Zhu Zhai, prevents clinging even to the most refined spiritual achievements. It is this non-attachment to outcomes, states, or goals that distinguishes genuine freedom from mere spiritual accumulation.

**Eradication of the Three Root Poisons**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the fundamental sources of suffering and the cycle of rebirth. The sage has utterly severed these root afflictions, removing the causes that bind consciousness to cyclic existence. Their elimination is not partial or conditional—it is complete, irreversible, and marks the definitive threshold of enlightenment.

**Continuous Practice as the Mechanism of Transformation**  
Liberation is not sudden or accidental. It emerges through persistent, daily mindfulness and vigilant attention to the mind’s habits. This ongoing effort, manifesting as Jing Jin, gradually dismantles egoic patterns, deepens insight into impermanence and non-self, and consolidates moral and meditative achievements into an unwavering identity.

**Transcendence of Self and Impermanence**  
The sage has fully realized the absence of a fixed self and the inherent instability of all phenomena. This realization dissolves identification with thoughts, emotions, and experiences. As a result, the sage remains untouched by gain or loss, praise or blame, and is fully at ease in a world of constant change.

**Unblemished Wisdom as the Hallmark of Enlightenment**  
The enlightened state is defined by wisdom that is free from distortion, contamination, or clinging. This wisdom sees reality as it is—not filtered through desire, fear, or conceptual fixation. It is the natural function of a mind cleansed of defilements and is inseparable from the sage’s presence, conduct, and perception.

**Tranquility as the Expression of Freedom**  
Inner stillness is not an end in itself, but the inseparable quality of a mind no longer tormented by craving or aversion. The sage abides in this peace not by escaping the world, but by seeing through the illusions that create disturbance. This tranquility is the natural outcome of moral purity, meditative depth, and transcendent understanding.

**Liberation as Inner Realization**  
Freedom is not an external condition, celestial reward, or mystical event. It is an internal transformation—a complete uprooting of ignorance and clinging—as embodied by the sage. No ritual, prayer, or external authority brings about this emancipation; it arises solely through autonomous, disciplined awakening.
