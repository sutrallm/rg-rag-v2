**Title: Non-Attachment, Compassion, and Wisdom in Saintly Beings**

**Summary**  
Saintly beings demonstrate complete freedom from clinging and aversion through the harmonious union of compassion and wisdom. This balance is cultivated through Chanding, which quiets the mind and disengages identification with temporary experiences. Neither compassion nor wisdom alone suffices—each must be tempered by the other to avoid attachment or coldness, resulting in lasting inner stability and spiritual fulfillment.

**Cultivation Through Mental Quietude**  
Chanding facilitates the release of identification with transient experiences by stilling the mind. This practice is essential for developing non-attachment, as it interrupts the habitual patterns of grasping and rejecting, allowing the practitioner to remain undisturbed by external conditions.

**Interdependence of Compassion and Wisdom**  
Compassion without wisdom results in attachment, while wisdom devoid of compassion leads to emotional detachment or coldness. Only when these two qualities operate together do they prevent both clinging and aversion, forming a complete and balanced approach to ethical action and inner peace.

**Exemplification by Saintly Beings**  
Saintly beings serve as the ultimate model of this balance: they have fully eradicated clinging and aversion through deep realization and embody the perfect integration of compassion and wisdom. Their state represents the highest expression of non-attachment and spiritual fulfillment.
