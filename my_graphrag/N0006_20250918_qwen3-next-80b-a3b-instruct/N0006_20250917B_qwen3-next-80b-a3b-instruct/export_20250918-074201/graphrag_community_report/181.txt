Title: Feifa and Kongtan in Buddhist Practice

Summary:  
True Buddhist practice is defined by continuous, mindful engagement with daily life, grounded in direct awareness rather than ritual or intellectual exposition. Practices lacking authentic presence—termed Kongtan—are explicitly dismissed as unaligned with the Dharma. Feifa embodies the lived expression of meditation, extending it beyond seated practice into the nuances of breath, thought, and action.

Feifa is the embodiment of meditative awareness in daily living  
It transforms Chanding from a formal exercise into a seamless, ongoing orientation toward reality. This practice demands attention to the present moment through breath, thoughts, and actions, making spiritual discipline inseparable from ordinary experience. The emphasis is not on external forms but on internal clarity and responsiveness.

Kongtan is identified as counterfeit practice  
Any religious activity performed without genuine awareness—whether ritualistic or discursive—is labeled Kongtan. It is characterized by performance over presence, form over substance, and is consistently rejected by the Buddha’s teachings as ineffective for true transformation. The distinction is not about the action itself, but the absence of conscious engagement within it.
