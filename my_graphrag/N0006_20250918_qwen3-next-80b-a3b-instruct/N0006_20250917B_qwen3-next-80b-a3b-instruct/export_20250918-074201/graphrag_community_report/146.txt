**Title: Juezhizhi Guichu and the Path of the Sage: Transcending Samsara Through Non-Self and Continuous Awareness**

**Summary:**  
The path to liberation is defined by the cultivation of continuous, non-judgmental awareness—Juezhizhi Guichu—which dissolves the illusion of self and ends the cycle of suffering. This practice, rooted in ethical purity, wisdom, and relentless effort, transforms mental conduct to achieve unwavering stillness and freedom from craving and ignorance. The sage, having eradicated delusion, no longer identifies with “I” or “mine,” and remains untouched by external conditions, embodying liberation through direct, moment-to-moment observation of body and mind as transient flows.

**Manifestation of Liberation Through Continuous Awareness**  
Juezhizhi Guichu—the practice of maintaining non-reactive, present-moment awareness—is not a means to an end but the very expression of liberation. It unifies all spiritual methods by rendering the observer and the observed as inseparable, transient phenomena. This awareness, when sustained effortlessly, dissolves attachment and aversion, directly aligning with the sage’s state of inner freedom and non-reactivity. It is the culmination of Chanding, where mindfulness becomes natural, unforced, and all-encompassing.

**Eradication of Delusion as the Foundation of Freedom**  
Wujing, the root of all suffering, is the failure to perceive reality without distortion. Its removal is not theoretical but requires sustained practice: observing thoughts, sensations, and emotions as they arise and pass without attributing them to an enduring self. The sage’s mind is free from afflictions not because of external conditions, but because ignorance has been completely uprooted. This absence of delusion is the sole reason for the absence of craving, which in turn ends the cycle of birth and death.

**Transcendence of Cyclic Existence Through Non-Self in Daily Life**  
Shengbu—the relentless cycle of rebirth—is fueled entirely by craving and ignorance. It is broken not by ritual or belief but by Wuwo Zhengjian Shilian: the lived realization that no “I” owns or controls experience. This is practiced through Jingjin, the persistent, moment-by-moment release of the notions of “I” and “mine.” When every action, sensation, and thought is seen as a passing flow without an owner, the mechanisms that bind beings to samsara cease to operate.

**The Sage’s Conduct as an Embodiment of Direct Observation**  
The qualities attributed to the sage—ethical purity, deep wisdom, stillness, and unconditional freedom—are not cultivated in isolation but emerge as natural consequences of Chaoguan Zhizhu: observing all mental and physical states without clinging or pushing away. This steady, non-attached observation renders external circumstances powerless to disturb inner equilibrium. The sage needs no validation because the illusion of a separate self that seeks approval has dissolved entirely. Their conduct is not exemplary—it is unavoidable, the spontaneous expression of a mind no longer under the sway of delusion.
