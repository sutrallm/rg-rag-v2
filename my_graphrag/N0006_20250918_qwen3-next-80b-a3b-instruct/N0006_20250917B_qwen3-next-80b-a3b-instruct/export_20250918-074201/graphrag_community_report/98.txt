**Title: Chaoguo Zhuoze and Shengguo in the Path of Spiritual Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True spiritual maturity is defined not by the attainment of elevated states, but by the freedom from clinging to them. The practice of deep concentration enables direct perception of impermanence and the absence of inherent self, which naturally dissolves attachment—even to spiritual achievements. Those who remain equanimous toward both experience and attainment, without grasping or rejecting, are recognized as genuinely liberated.

**Perfected Concentration Reveals the Illusory Nature of Experience**  
When deep stillness is fully developed, the mind observes all phenomena—thoughts, emotions, meditative states—as transient and lacking fixed identity. This direct seeing, not conceptual understanding, dismantles the tendency to identify with or possess any experience, whether ordinary or sublime.

**Spiritual Attainments Become Obstacles When Clung To**  
Enlightenment, liberation, and other sacred results are not to be pursued as endpoints. Holding onto them as milestones of success reinforces the ego’s narrative of progression, transforming spiritual goals into objects of desire. The very act of clinging to attainment negates its purpose.

**Liberation Is Measured by Equanimity, Not Achievement**  
A being is truly awakened not by the depth of their experiences or the titles they receive, but by their unshakable non-attachment to all states—both desirable and undesirable. Even the highest meditative absorptions are relinquished without resistance or longing, revealing freedom beyond any condition.
