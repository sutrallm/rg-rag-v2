**Title: The True Practitioner: Freedom Beyond Spiritual Attainments**

**Summary:**  
A true practitioner achieves liberation not through external achievements or mystical experiences, but by living with unwavering mindfulness, humility, and non-attachment—even to spiritual progress. They remain undisturbed by adversity, unswayed by prosperity, and free from the need for validation. Clinging to spiritual states, such as deep concentration or visions, creates a subtle barrier to further growth. True freedom lies in persistent, grounded practice without self-satisfaction or pride.

**Non-Attachment Extends to Spiritual Achievements**  
Even progress in meditation, such as heightened concentration or visionary experiences, must not be clung to. Attachment to these states—no matter how elevated—becomes a hidden obstacle, preventing deeper liberation. Letting go of spiritual milestones is as essential as releasing worldly desires.

**Freedom Is Demonstrated in Daily Conduct**  
The hallmark of this path is not extraordinary feats, but consistent presence in ordinary life. A true practitioner remains calm under hardship, free from arrogance in success, and untouched by the urge to prove or display their state. Their embodiment of awareness is quiet, unassuming, and rooted in action rather than attainment.

**No Distinction Between Practitioner and Saint**  
In essence, the terms are interchangeable: both refer to an individual who lives freed from mental bondage, detached from outcomes, and unmoved by internal or external reactions. This equivalence underscores that liberation is not reserved for the rare few but is realized through the quality of one’s daily presence.

**Self-Satisfaction Undermines the Path**  
When practice becomes a source of pride or when experiences are dwelled upon with awe, the mind recoils from true freedom. The path is sustained not by accomplishments, but by continuous grounding—refusing to rest in what has been reached, and never seeking approval from self or others.
