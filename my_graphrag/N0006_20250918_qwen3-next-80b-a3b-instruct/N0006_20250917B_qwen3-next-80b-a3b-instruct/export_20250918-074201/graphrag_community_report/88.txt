Title: 舍離與虛空：解脫之道的本質

Summary:  
解脫 stems from the act of relinquishment, not from acquisition. True freedom is achieved when the mind is free from attachment, likened to the boundless expanse of empty space—unobstructed, unclinging, and undisturbed by gain or loss. The state of complete liberation is defined not by what is attained, but by what is released.

—

No Seeking, No Gain: The Essence of Liberation  
Liberation is not found in acquiring anything. It is fully realized only when the mind ceases to pursue or possess. The absence of desire and the absence of attachment to outcomes constitute the core of this state, where fulfillment arises from non-craving rather than from attainment.

The Mind as Empty Space  
When attachment is relinquished, the mind becomes like empty space—vast, unbounded, and unimpeded by barriers. This metaphor describes a condition in which thoughts, emotions, and experiences move freely without leaving traces or creating clinging. The nature of freedom here is not an active state but the absence of obstruction.

Letting Go as the Sole Path  
Freedom from bondage cannot be reached through accumulation, effort, or achievement. The only way to arrive at this unshackled condition is through renunciation. Anything held onto—whether desire, identity, or outcome—creates a tie that prevents the full experience of unconditioned release.
