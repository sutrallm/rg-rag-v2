**Title: Wulizheng, Chanding, and Shengzhe: The Nature of Unattached Wisdom**

**Summary**  
True freedom from attachment arises when one perceives all experiences—meditative states, bliss, emptiness, and supernatural powers—as impermanent, selfless, and unreal. This perception, cultivated through stillness and non-striving, dissolves clinging to identity and spiritual achievements. Those who embody this understanding no longer pursue or cling to any state, allowing the mind to rest naturally in clarity.

**The illusory nature of all experience removes fear and attachment**  
All phenomena, including mental constructs and spiritual experiences, are understood as unreal or not truly existent. Recognizing this illusory quality eliminates the tendency to grasp at or resist any sensation or state, thereby dissolving both attachment and the fear that accompanies it.

**Stillness without wisdom leads to attachment to meditative states**  
Meditative stillness alone is insufficient; without the recognition of impermanence and selflessness, one may become attached to the peace, bliss, or powers arising in meditation. True freedom requires stillness paired with clear perception of illusion.

**Wisdom manifests as freedom from clinging to spiritual achievements**  
Those who embody this understanding are free not only from worldly attachments but also from attachment to advanced spiritual experiences—such as emptiness or bliss—because they see these as transient and without inherent self. This freedom defines their presence and action.

**The absence of a permanent self dissolves identity and clinging**  
All experiences are perceived without a fixed center or enduring identity. This realization undoes the basis of clinging, as there is no “self” to protect, maintain, or achieve anything through spiritual practice.

**Transcendent wisdom naturally leads to non-striving**  
When all phenomena are seen as impermanent and unreal, the drive to attain, hold, or become something specific ceases. The mind, no longer pulling toward goals or outcomes, settles into effortless clarity.

**Impermanence is the direct pathway to unattached awareness**  
The foundational clarity that enables release from attachment comes not from belief or effort, but from direct observation of the constant change inherent in every phenomenon—sensations, thoughts, and experiences alike.

**Illusoriness is the bedrock of liberation from all experiences**  
The recognition that nothing—whether subtle or profound—is ultimately real forms the basis for complete release. Without this understanding, even the highest spiritual states become traps.
