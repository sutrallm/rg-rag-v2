**Title: The Shengzhe: Liberation Through the Eradication of the Three Poisons and the Realization of Non-Self**

**Summary:**  
The Shengzhe, or sage, embodies complete freedom from suffering and the cycle of rebirth by fully eradicating greed, hatred, and delusion—the three root afflictions that bind all sentient beings. This liberation is not attained through external rituals, divine intervention, or supernatural powers, but through the internal cultivation of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and profound wisdom. Central to this transformation is the direct realization of non-self and emptiness, which uproots the illusion of a permanent "I" and dissolves all clinging. Continuous mindfulness and unwavering diligent effort sustain this state, allowing the Shengzhe to dwell in unshakable tranquility, unaffected by pleasure, pain, praise, or blame. Their freedom is marked by the absence of attachment—not only to worldly desires but even to spiritual attainments—and is recognized even by divine beings still entangled in samsara.

---

**Moral Discipline Sustains Mental Clarity**  
Ethical conduct forms the essential foundation for all spiritual progress. Without moral purity, the mind remains agitated and incapable of achieving stable concentration. The Shengzhe upholds ethical precepts not as rigid rules, but as the natural expression of an uncluttered mind. This discipline creates the conditions for meditative depth and clear insight, ensuring that mental energy is not dissipated by unwholesome actions or emotional reactivity. It is the first and irreplaceable step in the threefold training that leads to liberation.

**Meditative Concentration Enables Direct Perception**  
Deep, unwavering concentration calms the mind’s turbulence, allowing unobstructed observation of reality as it is. The Shengzhe embodies perfected meditative stillness, remaining unmoved by internal thoughts or external stimuli. This state does not produce mystical experiences but prepares the mind to see clearly: the arising and passing of all phenomena, their impermanence, and lack of inherent self. Concentration weakens afflictions by reducing reactivity and creates the mental space where genuine wisdom can take root.

**Wisdom Dissolves the Illusion of Self**  
True wisdom is not theoretical knowledge but the direct, non-conceptual realization that all phenomena—including the sense of “I”—are impermanent, interdependent, and empty of intrinsic existence. This insight dismantles the foundational delusion of a permanent self, which is the source of all craving, aversion, and suffering. When this realization is complete, attachment to “mine,” to achievements, to even liberation itself, dissolves spontaneously. Wisdom is the force that turns observation into liberation.

**Eradication of the Three Poisons Defines Liberation**  
The complete absence of greed, hatred, and delusion is not merely a moral achievement but the defining condition of freedom. These three root afflictions, arising from ignorance of reality’s true nature, fuel the cycle of rebirth and perpetuate suffering. The Shengzhe has extinguished them at their source, not through suppression but through the clarity of insight. Their mind is no longer driven by desire, resistance, or confusion—making their existence free from internal conflict and external dependency.

**Non-Attachment Is the Natural Outcome of Insight**  
Freedom is not the absence of experience but the absence of clinging to it. The Shengzhe does not reject pleasure or pain, fame or obscurity, meditative bliss or ordinary moments. They meet all phenomena with equanimity because they see their transient, conditioned nature. Even the pursuit of enlightenment is not clung to, as attachment to attainment would reestablish the ego. This non-attachment is not indifference, but the profound release that comes when nothing is seen as worth grasping or pushing away.

**Continuous Mindfulness Sustains Liberation**  
Liberation is not a static destination but a dynamic, continuous awareness of the present moment. The Shengzhe maintains unbroken mindfulness—day and night—in every action, thought, and sensation. This sustained attention prevents the return of delusion by catching subtle forms of craving and aversion before they solidify. Mindfulness is not a technique to be practiced intermittently but the very fabric of their being, ensuring that wisdom remains alive and active in all circumstances.

**Relentless Effort Prevents Regression**  
Spiritual awakening is not the result of occasional devotion or fleeting inspiration but of unwavering, lifelong diligence. The Shengzhe embodies this relentless effort, refusing to succumb to laziness, complacency, or distraction. Without this continuous practice, the mind lapses back into ignorance and the patterns of suffering return. This diligence is the active force that protects insight, deepens concentration, and ensures moral integrity are never compromised.

**Liberation Is Internal, Not External**  
Sacred places, rituals, prayers, deities, or mystical experiences hold no inherent power to free the mind. The Shengzhe transcends samsara not by appealing to external forces but by transforming the mind from within. Their freedom arises solely from seeing the truth of impermanence and non-self. Even the figure of the Buddha serves only as a guide—the actual work of liberation belongs to the individual’s own practice and insight.

**The Union of Wisdom and Compassion Completes the Path**  
True freedom is not cold or detached. The Shengzhe’s wisdom, which perceives the suffering inherent in samsara, naturally gives rise to compassion—untainted by attachment or the urge to fix. This compassion arises without clinging to outcomes, without the need to be seen as helpful, and without the illusion of a separate self acting upon others. It is the balanced expression of seeing reality clearly while responding with kindness, free from both aversion and exploitation.

**Transcendence of Rebirth Is the Result of Root-Cause Elimination**  
The cycle of birth, aging, sickness, and death does not end through ritual worship, accumulation of merit, or celestial favor. It ends when ignorance is fully dispelled, craving is utterly extinguished, and the sense of a separate self is seen through. The Shengzhe has broken this cycle not by fleeing it, but by understanding its mechanisms and letting go of its fuel. Their presence alone—calm, clear, and unattached—demonstrates that liberation is possible here and now, within the ordinary flow of mind and body.
