**Title: The Saintly One’s Path Beyond the Cycle of Birth and Death**

**Summary:**  
The Saintly One achieves total liberation by dismantling the core mechanisms of suffering: greed, hatred, and delusion. This liberation is rooted in the direct realization that the sense of “I” and “mine” is an illusion, and that thoughts, though vivid, are impermanent and self-less. Through non-attachment and mindful observation in daily life, the Saintly One cuts through ignorance and transcends rebirth. Chan Ding serves as the meditative method that facilitates this transformation, sustained by the consistent practice of observing mental phenomena without identification or resistance.

**The Three Root Afflictions Are the Source of Bondage**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are identified as the primary forces trapping beings in the continuous cycle of rebirth. Their complete eradication by the Saintly One is not merely a moral achievement but the decisive condition for breaking free. These afflictions are not isolated emotions but deep-seated drivers that reinforce the illusion of self and perpetuate clinging.

**Non-Attachment Is the Foundation of Inner Freedom**  
True ease and stability arise not from changing external circumstances, but from releasing the inner habit of clinging to or resisting experience. This attitude is not passive indifference but an active non-reaction that allows the mind to rest in its natural state. It is the essential quality that enables Chan Ding to function as a liberating tool rather than a mere technique.

**The Illusion of Self Is the Root of Samsara**  
The conviction that there is a permanent, owning “I” is the fundamental error sustaining rebirth. When the Saintly One realizes that thoughts, feelings, and identities are without a lasting owner, the structure of self-clinging collapses. This realization—manifestation of wisdom of no-self—is not intellectual but experiential, dismantling the very mechanism that generates suffering.

**Impermanence of Thoughts Unlocks the Wisdom of No-Self**  
All mental activity, regardless of its content, is transient and lacks internal control. Recognizing this allows one to observe thoughts without entanglement. This observation reveals that the “I” claiming ownership of thoughts is itself just another fleeting pattern, reinforcing the breakdown of self-identification and deepening the dissolution of attachment.

**Awake Daily Practice Sustains Liberation in Ordinary Life**  
Liberation is not confined to meditation hours but is cultivated through continuous mindfulness in eating, walking, and sensing thoughts. By observing these actions without suppression or indulgence, the practitioner embeds non-attachment into daily behavior. This practice depends on recognizing the fleeting nature of thoughts, ensuring mindfulness remains genuine and undistorted by identification.

**Chan Ding Is the Meditative Path to Transcendence**  
Through Chan Ding, the Saintly One directly interrupts the cycle of birth and death. This practice is not goal-oriented concentration but a penetrating stillness that severs the roots of attachment and ignorance. Its effectiveness depends entirely on the presence of non-attachment and the applied understanding of thought’s impermanence and the absence of self.
