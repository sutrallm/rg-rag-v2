**Title: Jingjin, Chanding, and Mindfulness as the Path to Liberation for the Shengzhe**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved only through unceasing, disciplined effort—Jingjin—sustained mindfulness in every moment, and deep meditative concentration (Chanding). These elements are interdependent: Jingjin provides the driving force to maintain mindfulness, which in turn stabilizes Chanding; Chanding creates the mental clarity necessary for wisdom to arise, dispelling ignorance, craving, and aversion. The Shengzhe, or enlightened beings, embody this triad as an unbroken, natural state—undaunted by external or internal disturbances, free from clinging to meditative experiences, and continually aware in all activities. Ritual, speculation, or passive relaxation are insufficient; true practice is found only in the persistent, non-attached observation of the present moment.

**Continuous Diligent Effort (Jingjin) Is the Sole Path to Liberation**  
The unwavering, non-lazy, and relentless cultivation of practice—Jingjin—is not optional but essential. It is the active force that cuts through ignorance, laziness, complacency, and habitual attachment. Without it, even correct understanding remains inert, and the mind readily regresses into samsaric patterns. Jingjin sustains every other practice; it is the very fuel that prevents spiritual decline, counters sloth, and enables the mind to remain aligned with the path. It is not intermittent devotion but a lifelong commitment expressed through daily, moment-to-moment vigilance. To falter in effort is to invite entanglement in desire and delusion; to persist is to dismantle the roots of suffering.

**Meditative Concentration (Chanding) Provides the Stable Ground for Wisdom**  
Chanding is the deep, unshakable calm achieved not through passive stillness but through sustained effort and mindfulness. It is not an end in itself, nor is it to be clung to as a state of bliss or spiritual achievement. Rather, it is the necessary foundation upon which true insight arises. Only when the mind is concentrated, free from distraction and emotional reactivity, can the nature of phenomena—impermanence, non-self, and unsatisfactoriness—be directly perceived. Chanding alone cannot liberate; it must be illuminated by wisdom. Yet without Chanding, wisdom remains theoretical, fragmented, and unable to uproot delusion. It is the stabilizing force that transforms fleeting awareness into penetrating insight.

**Mindfulness Is the Living Practice That Sustains Effort and Concentration**  
Mindfulness is not a technique for brief retreats but the continuous, non-judgmental awareness of breath, sensation, thought, and environment in all activities—walking, eating, speaking, sitting. It is the practical mechanism that anchors effort and sustains concentration. Without it, Jingjin becomes mechanical, and Chanding collapses into distraction or dullness. Mindfulness prevents the mind from slipping into forgetfulness, attachment, or aversion by holding attention firmly in the present. It is the bridge between formal meditation and daily life, making spiritual practice inseparable from ordinary existence. True mindfulness uncovers the illusory nature of self, dissolves craving, and allows non-attachment to arise naturally—not through suppression, but through clear, steady observation.

**The Shengzhe Embodies the Fusion of Effort, Concentration, and Awareness**  
The enlightened being—the Shengzhe—is defined not by external signs, titles, or rituals, but by the uninterrupted presence of Jingjin, Chanding, and mindfulness. Their mind is still as calm water, undisturbed by internal afflictions or external conditions. This state is not attained through sporadic achievement but through relentless, consistent practice over time. They do not cling to meditative peace, nor do they seek spiritual status. Their conduct, speech, and awareness are aligned in every moment, reflecting complete freedom from greed, anger, and ignorance. Their stillness is not passive—it is the dynamic result of ceaseless, wise effort.

**Non-Attachment Is the Natural Outcome of Authentic Practice**  
True progress is marked not by the acquisition of pleasant states, but by the unraveling of clinging—even to meditation itself. Chanding without non-attachment becomes spiritual materialism; Jingjin without wisdom becomes ego-driven striving. The path demands release from all forms of grasping: to pleasure, to peace, to achievement. Through sustained mindfulness, one observes the arising and passing of all phenomena without identification. This seeing, not forcing, dissolves attachment. Liberation is not the attainment of a new state, but the cessation of clinging to all conditioned experiences—mental, sensory, and meditative. The mind, no longer bound by craving or aversion, abides in its natural purity.
