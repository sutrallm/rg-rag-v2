**Title: Attachment and Liberation in Spiritual Practice**

**Summary:**  
True freedom from suffering requires the complete abandonment of all forms of attachment—including to meditative states, spiritual achievements, pleasant mental experiences, and supernormal abilities. Attachment arises from the belief in a permanent self and is triggered by sense objects when perceived without mindful awareness. The pathways to liberation involve recognizing impermanence, realizing non-self, and cultivating equanimity through practices like chanding. Even desirable spiritual experiences become obstacles if clung to, and only through non-attachment can lasting freedom be achieved.

—

**Attachment to Spiritual Experiences Hinders Liberation**  
Meditative joy, meaningful achievements in practice, and supernormal powers, though naturally arising and beneficial in development, become barriers when identified with or pursued as endpoints. Clinging to these experiences reinforces the illusion of a self that possesses or owns them, preventing deeper realization. True liberation requires letting go of these states not because they are undesirable, but because attachment to them sustains suffering.

**Sense Objects Seed Craving Without Mindfulness**  
External stimuli such as sights, sounds, and smells activate craving or aversion when encountered without mindful awareness. This reactive pattern forms the basis of attachment and suffering. The absence of mindful observation allows the mind to grasp at sensory experiences, perpetuating cycles of desire and dissatisfaction.

**Non-Attachment Is the Essential Antidote**  
Attachment cannot be subdued through partial effort or temporary restraint. Its dissolution requires the complete presence of non-attachment—the absence of clinging to any phenomenon, whether pleasurable, spiritual, or material. One cannot maintain attachment while progressing toward freedom; the two states are mutually exclusive.

**Belief in a Permanent Self Is the Root of Clinging**  
The foundation of all attachment is the mistaken belief in a fixed, enduring identity. When this belief is understood as illusory—through direct realization of non-self—the basis for ownership, possession, and identification dissolves. Without the notion of “mine” or “self,” attachment loses its power to bind.

**Impermanence Reveals the Futility of Clinging**  
The recognition that all phenomena—inclusive of meditative bliss, spiritual gains, and sensory pleasures—are fleeting undermines the impulse to hold on. Seeing clearly that nothing lasts leads naturally to release. This awareness is not theoretical but experiential, and it is the active weakening of attachment through direct perception.

**Chanding Cultivates Equanimity Through Non-Clarity**  
Chanding enables distance from attachment by training the mind to observe without grasping or rejecting. It is not achieved by pursuing the pleasant states it may produce, but through steadiness in awareness regardless of arising conditions. True chanding arises when even the joy of practice is no longer clung to as a sign of progress.
