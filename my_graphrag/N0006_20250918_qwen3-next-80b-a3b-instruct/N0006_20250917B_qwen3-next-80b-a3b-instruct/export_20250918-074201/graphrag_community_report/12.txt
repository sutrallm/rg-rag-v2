**Title: Shengjie and Liu Huan: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of rebirth is achieved only through unwavering diligent effort, the dissolution of self-centered attachment, and the direct realization of emptiness and non-self. The fully realized being has eradicated all defilements and rests in unmoving tranquility, having transcended suffering by abandoning craving and ignorance. Continuous mindful practice and wisdom are not auxiliary but essential and irreplaceable requisites for breaking free from endless rebirth.

**Chanding as the Foundation for Calm and Release**  
The practice of calming the mind through Chanding reduces attachment and directly aligns the practitioner with liberation. It is not a preliminary exercise but a necessary mechanism for weakening the psychological grip of desire and aversion, enabling deeper spiritual progress.

**The Shengzhe as the Embodiment of Transcendence**  
The Shengzhe represents the complete end of suffering — free from all defilements, unmoved by worldly conditions, and no longer subject to rebirth. Their state is not symbolic but the tangible outcome of sustained ethical discipline and wisdom, demonstrating that liberation is a realizable condition.

**Non-Self as the Core of Liberation**  
Freedom from the cycle of rebirth is impossible without relinquishing all identification with “I” and “mine.” The conceptual clinging to self is the root of craving and ignorance; its dissolution is not optional but the defining condition of enlightenment.

**Jingjin as the Indispensable Path**  
Only through relentless, continuous effort can one escape the cycle of rebirth. Spiritual freedom does not arise from intermittent practice or passive reflection; it demands persistent, unwavering dedication that sustains progress beyond temporary states.

**Chaoshi as the Direct Remover of Attachment**  
Wisdom that perceives emptiness and non-self does not merely inform understanding — it actively dismantles the mental structures that sustain attachment. This seeing is not intellectual but transformative, severing the roots of suffering at their source.

**Liu Huan as the Cycle Targeted by All Practice**  
Every spiritual discipline, from mindfulness to ethical conduct, is oriented toward breaking the endless round of existence. Liberation is defined in relation to this cycle — its presence defines the problem; its cessation defines the solution.
