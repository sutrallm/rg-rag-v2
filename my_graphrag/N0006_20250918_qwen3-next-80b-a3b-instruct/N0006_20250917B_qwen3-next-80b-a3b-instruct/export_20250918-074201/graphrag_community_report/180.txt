**Title: Chenhen, Tanyu, and Yuchi: The Path to the Shengler’s Liberation**

**Summary:**  
The three sources of suffering—Chenhen (hatred), Tanyu (greed), and Yuchi (delusion)—are overcome through Chanding, a practice of mindful awareness that transforms emotional reactivity, reduces craving, and reveals the true nature of reality. The Shengler, having fully transcended these three, embodies equanimity, non-attachment, and clear perception, serving as a model of inner freedom and spiritual refuge.

**Chenhen is dissolved through non-reactive observation**  
Hatred, when observed without identification during Chanding, loses its grip on the mind. Its frequency and intensity diminish as reactions are no longer reinforced by identification, leading to a stable state of equanimity. The Shengler no longer experiences hatred, having transformed reactive impulses into calm presence.

**Tanyu is subdued by mental stillness and awareness**  
Greed arises from craving driven by mental agitation. Chanding calms the mind, weakening the momentum of desire. Through sustained awareness, attachment softens, and the Shengler exhibits complete non-attachment, no longer bound by the pursuit of fleeting satisfaction.

**Yuchi is dispelled by direct perception of reality**  
Delusion stems from misperceiving existence as permanent, self-contained, or inherently satisfying. Chanding cultivates clear seeing, allowing phenomena to be recognized as impermanent, empty, and selfless. The Shengler perceives all things without distortion, having extinguished ignorance at its root.
