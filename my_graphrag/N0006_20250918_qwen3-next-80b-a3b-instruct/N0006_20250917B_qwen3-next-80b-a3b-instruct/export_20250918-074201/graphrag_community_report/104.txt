**Title: Wuzhu, Wuchang, and Jiefang: The Path of Non-Attachment and Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (Jiefang) is attained not through rituals or distant goals, but through present-moment awareness and complete non-attachment (Wuzhu). This non-attachment arises from the direct observation of impermanence (Wuchang) and the absence of an inherent self (Wuwo), both rooted in dependent origination (Yuanqi). Daily mindful practice (Richang xinxiang) and mindful observation (Yinguan) are the practical methods through which this understanding is cultivated, transforming ordinary actions into pathways to freedom. Meditative stillness (Chanding) supports this process but is incomplete without wisdom—without recognizing the illusory nature of all experiences, even blissful or enlightened states, attachment persists. The fully liberated (Shengzhe) embody this wisdom, transcending all clinging, including to spiritual attainments, and live in unclouded awareness.

**Observation of Impermanence as the Foundation of Liberation**  
All phenomena—including wealth, emotions, the body, and meditative states—are constantly arising and ceasing due to causes and conditions. Recognizing this constant change (Wuchang) dismantles the illusion of permanence, which underlies clinging and fear. This understanding is not passive acceptance but an active, moment-to-moment observation that reveals the unsatisfactory nature of conditioned existence. It is the essential gateway to releasing attachment, forming the basis for all further realization, including non-self and liberation.

**Non-Self as the Natural Consequence of Impermanence**  
Since all experiences and aggregates are in perpetual flux, no enduring, independent self can be found. The realization that there is no fixed “I” (Wuwo) arises directly from seeing impermanence. This insight dismantles ego-based clinging, as there is no permanent entity to protect, possess, or advance. Without a perceived self, craving and aversion lose their ground, and the mind naturally lets go. This understanding is inseparable from impermanence—neither can be fully realized without the other.

**Meditative Stillness Without Wisdom Is Incomplete**  
Cultivating inner stillness (Chanding) provides the mental clarity needed to observe phenomena without distraction. However, stillness alone risks attachment to calm, bliss, or psychic experiences. True meditative stability is only complete when it is accompanied by the clear recognition that all states—however refined—are transient and without inherent substance. Chanding must serve wisdom; wisdom must inform stillness. Their union prevents spiritual clinging and ensures meditation leads beyond retention to release.

**Daily Mindful Practice as the Living Expression of Liberation**  
Liberation is not confined to formal meditation or retreats. Continuous awareness in everyday activities—walking, breathing, thinking—called Richang xinxiang, transforms routine life into a sustained practice of mindful observation (Yinguan). This is the most accessible and sustainable path to realizing impermanence and non-self in real time. Through this, mundane actions become vehicles for insight, dissolving the separation between sacred and ordinary, and making liberation an immediate, lived condition, not a future achievement.

**Transcending All Attachment, Including Spiritual Attainments**  
True freedom requires letting go not only of worldly desires but also of meditative bliss, emptiness, psychic powers, and even the concept of liberation itself. Clinging to any experience, no matter how elevated, reinforces the illusion of self and perpetuates the cycle of suffering. The liberated (Shengzhe) exhibit this fully: they observe all mental states with equanimity, uninterested in outcomes, unattached to progress, and unmoved by novelty or profundity. This is the hallmark of wisdom—seeing all phenomena as unreal (Wulizheng) and without self, and responding with neither grasping nor rejection.

**Dependent Origination as the Underlying Structure of Reality**  
All phenomena emerge only through interdependent causes and conditions—nothing exists independently. This principle (Yuanqi) explains why impermanence and non-self are universal truths: if nothing arises on its own, nothing can possess a fixed identity or lasting essence. Recognizing this undermines all notions of inherent reality, dissolving the foundations of attachment. Seeing reality as dependently originated is not abstract philosophy but the direct perception that enables non-attachment and freedom from delusion.

**Wisdom as the Direct Cause of Liberation**  
The path to freedom (Jiefang) does not rely on external worship, rituals, or supernatural intervention. It arises solely from clear seeing—Chaoshi—of impermanence, suffering, and non-self. This wisdom dissolves ignorance (Wuming), the root cause of the cycle of rebirth (Siwang lunhuan). Liberation is not a destination reached after years of effort; it is the present-moment condition of unclouded awareness that emerges when clinging ceases. Diligent, consistent practice (Jingjin) sustains this awareness, preventing regression and ensuring that insight deepens into unshakable freedom.
