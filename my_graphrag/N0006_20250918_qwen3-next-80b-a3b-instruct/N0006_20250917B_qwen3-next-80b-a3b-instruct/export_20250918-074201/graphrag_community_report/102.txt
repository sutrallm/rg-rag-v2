**Title: Shengzhe: The Embodied State of Liberation Through Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary**  
The Shengzhe, or saintly being, is defined by the total extinction of greed, hatred, and delusion—the three root afflictions binding sentient beings to cyclic existence. This complete eradication results in a state of unmoving tranquility, continuous mindfulness, and non-attachment to all experiences, including spiritual attainments. Liberation is not achieved through external rewards, supernatural powers, or ritual observance, but solely through relentless disciplined practice, moral purity, and direct insight into impermanence, non-self, and emptiness. The Shengzhe abides in inner stillness and purity, unaffected by praise or blame, pleasure or pain, and is revered even by divine beings. Their existence is the living expression of wisdom and compassion unified, free from ego, craving, and the illusion of control.

---

**Eradication of the Three Poisons Defines Liberation**  
The complete uprooting of greed, hatred, and delusion—referred to across multiple terms as Tan Chen Chi, Si Du, San Du, Qiling, and Dai Zhi—is the singular criterion that distinguishes the Shengzhe from all other beings. These afflictions are not merely suppressed but entirely extinguished, eliminating the very foundation of samsara. Their removal leads to the cessation of suffering, the end of rebirth, and the realization of nirvana. No spiritual achievement, meditative state, or external recognition can substitute for this eradication; it is the indispensable core of enlightenment.

**Non-Attachment as the Natural Outcome of Wisdom**  
The Shengzhe exhibits profound non-attachment—not as a forced restraint but as the spontaneous result of realizing impermanence, non-self, and emptiness. This includes freedom from clinging to sensory pleasures, worldly rewards, spiritual experiences, meditative bliss, and even the concept of liberation itself. The absence of grasping or aversion allows the mind to remain undisturbed by internal or external conditions, manifesting as inner stability, quietude, and purity. Such non-attachment extends to all phenomena, including the notion of “I” and “mine,” undermining the ego’s foundation.

**Continuous Mindfulness and Moral Discipline as the Path**  
The Shengzhe maintains unwavering mindfulness (nian) and moral purity (jie, jielv) as constant, integrated practices, not transient efforts. This continuous awareness, upheld day and night, enables precise observation of mental states and prevents regression into ignorance. Moral discipline is not a preliminary step but an essential quality woven into their being, forming the basis for meditative concentration (chanding) and wisdom (zhihui). Without this relentless, lifelong commitment (jingjin), the purification of the mind and transcendence of afflictions are unattainable.

**Perfect Equanimity Arises from Realization of Non-Self**  
The Shengzhe has fully transcended the illusion of self (wuwo), dissolving all ego-clinging and identity-based suffering. This realization dismantles the source of greed, hatred, and delusion, which arise from the mistaken sense of a separate, enduring “I.” As a result, they remain eternally unmoved by conditions—neither elated by praise nor afflicted by blame. Their tranquility (qingjing, jingjian) is not a cultivated mood but the natural expression of direct insight into the conditioned, transient nature of all phenomena, including the five aggregates.

**Wisdom and Compassion Intertwine as the Expression of Enlightenment**  
The liberated state is not cold detachment but the harmonious union of profound wisdom and boundless compassion. Wisdom perceives suffering without attachment; compassion motivates ethical action without delusion. One without the other leads to imbalance—attachment without wisdom, or indifference without compassion. For the Shengzhe, these qualities are inseparable, arising together from the clear perception of reality and the absence of self-centeredness, resulting in effortless, unconditioned ethically aligned conduct.

**Liberation from Cyclic Existence is the Sole Goal**  
The ultimate aim of cultivation is not longevity, divine favor, miraculous powers, or future blessings, but the complete cessation of the cycle of birth, aging, sickness, and death (samsara). This is achieved only through the destruction of ignorance (wuming) and craving (tan), the twin roots of rebirth. The Shengzhe has severed these roots through direct insight, thereby breaking the chain of dependent origination. Their liberation (jietuo, jiefu) is not conceptual but experiential—a state of unconditioned freedom from all suffering and its causes.

**Divine Reverence Honors the Unattached Sage**  
Even celestial beings, who occupy elevated realms within samsara, hold the Shengzhe in reverence. This reverence is not for power, charisma, or miraculous feats, but for the Shengzhe’s complete transcendence of attachment—especially to spiritual achievements. Their quiet, unremarkable life of moral clarity and inner stillness stands in stark contrast to the conditioned existence of even the most powerful divine entities, demonstrating that true superiority lies not in status, but in the total freedom from clinging.
