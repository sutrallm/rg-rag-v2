**Title: Jingjin as the Antidote to Wangmin in the Cycle of Samsara**

**Summary**  
The text establishes that suffering arises from delusion and ignorance, which fuel attachment and aversion, sustaining the cycle of rebirth. The only direct means to interrupt this cycle is through constant mindfulness and diligent effort, embodied by Jingjin, which counteracts Wangmin. Without active attention to body and mind in every moment, habitual tendencies reinforce ignorance, leading to continued entrapment.

**Constant Mindfulness Breaks the Flow of Ignorance**  
Attentiveness in every moment directs awareness toward the body and mind, disrupting the automatic patterns that sustain delusion. This presence prevents ignorance from taking hold, acting as a continuous restraint against the habitual tendencies that bind one to samsara.

**Jingjin Counteracts Wangmin Through Diligent Effort**  
Wangmin thrives in the absence of persistent vigilance. Jingjin, as the active practice of unwavering attention and non-idleness, serves as its direct opposite. Only through sustained effort can one resist the pull of delusion and avoid falling back into the cycle driven by unconscious patterns.

**Delusion and Ignorance Sustain the Cycle of Samsara**  
The root of recurring suffering lies in distorted perception and lack of awareness, which generate attachment and aversion. These mental states perpetuate rebirth not through external forces, but through internal, unexamined habituations that go unchallenged without mindful intervention.
