**Title: Qingjing and Tanchendu in the Liberation of Shengzhe**

**Summary**  
Shengzhe attain a state of inner purity and stillness—Qingjing—by fully eradicating the three root afflictions known as Tanchendu: greed, hatred, and delusion. This eradication is not merely a cessation of negative states but the direct cause of lasting clarity and peace. Qingjing is not an external achievement but the natural outcome of Tanchendu’s removal, and its presence defines the liberated condition of Shengzhe.

**Qingjing Arises Only After the Complete Elimination of Tanchendu**  
The state of inner clarity and peace, known as Qingjing, does not emerge independently or through external means. It is consistently described as the direct and natural consequence of purging greed, hatred, and delusion. When these three afflictions are fully uprooted, stillness and clarity appear spontaneously, indicating that their removal is both necessary and sufficient for this transformation.

**Shengzhe Are Defined by the Absence of Tanchendu and the Presence of Qingjing**  
Shengzhe are not distinguished by rituals, titles, or external behaviors, but by their internal condition: the total absence of greed, hatred, and delusion, and the full presence of inner purity and stillness. These two conditions are inseparable—Tanchendu’s eradication is the defining quality of their liberation, and Qingjing is its visible, lived expression.

**Tanchendu Are the Fundamental Roots of Suffering, and Their Eradication Is the Path to Liberation**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are not merely obstacles but the foundational causes of bondage and suffering. To achieve true liberation, these three must be completely extinguished. Their presence sustains cycles of disturbance; their absence is what enables the enduring peace of Qingjing, confirming that liberation is a process of inner purification, not external attainment.
