**Title: The Path of Spiritual Liberation Through Moral Discipline, Concentration, and Discernment**

**Summary:**  
Moral discipline forms the foundational stage of spiritual practice, enabling the development of deep concentration, which in turn supports the emergence of discerning wisdom. This wisdom, the culmination of the process, dissolves attachment, aversion, and delusion, leading to liberation. The three stages—moral discipline, concentration, and discerning wisdom—are sequential and interdependent, representing different expressions of a unified path to freedom.

**Moral Discipline as the Foundation**  
The practice begins with moral discipline, which establishes the necessary conditions for mental stability and ethical clarity. Without this initial stage, the subsequent development of focused awareness cannot take root.

**Concentration as the Intermediate Stage**  
Following moral discipline, concentration arises naturally as the mind becomes steadier and less distracted. This focused state is not an end in itself but serves as the essential preparation for deeper discernment.

**Discerning Wisdom as the Culmination**  
The highest stage is discerning wisdom, which arises only upon the stabilization of moral conduct and focused attention. This wisdom enables direct recognition of reality as it is, resulting in the complete dissolution of craving, aversion, and ignorance, and the attainment of unshakable freedom.

**Unity of the Path**  
The three stages—moral discipline, concentration, and discerning wisdom—are not separate endeavors but sequential dimensions of a single transformative process. Each phase is conditionally dependent on the one before it, and together they express one unified movement toward liberation.
