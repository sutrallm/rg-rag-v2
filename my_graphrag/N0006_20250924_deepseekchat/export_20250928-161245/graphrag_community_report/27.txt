**Title:** The Nature of Impermanence in Sensations and Attachment

**Summary:**  
This report explores the impermanent and interdependent nature of sensations (感受), a component of the five aggregates (五蘊), and the importance of observing their essence to eliminate attachment and suffering. Sensations arise dependent on sensory faculties and external conditions, emphasizing their transient and non-self characteristics.

**The Impermanence of Sensations**  
Sensations are identified as impermanent and without a permanent self. Their fleeting nature underscores the importance of recognizing their transient quality to avoid attachment.

**Sensations as Dependent Phenomena**  
Sensations arise through the interaction of sensory faculties (根) and external objects (境), illustrating their dependent origination. Understanding this interdependence helps in seeing sensations as conditioned and not inherently existent.

**Observation of Sensations to Eliminate Attachment**  
Observing the essence of sensations during meditation is crucial for releasing attachment and dispelling afflictions. This practice aligns with the broader goal of understanding the five aggregates to reduce clinging.

**Sensations as Part of the Five Aggregates**  
As one of the five aggregates, sensations play a key role in shaping perception and experience. Examining their nature is essential for breaking free from fixation on them and other aspects of the aggregates.
