**Title: The Path to Inner Stillness and Liberation: Practices and States of the Practitioner and the Liberated One**

**Summary**  
The text outlines a coherent progression from initial practice to ultimate liberation, centered on the cultivation of inner stillness through mindfulness and meditation. It describes how sustained practice leads to a state of mind that is unshaken by external conditions, free from attachment, and ultimately liberated from suffering and the cycle of birth and death. Key practices include regulating the senses, observing breath and thoughts, and maintaining non-attachment to experiences—even those arising in deep meditation. The liberated individual embodies this stillness and non-attachment in all activities, reflecting a mind clear as a mirror and vast as space, untouched by craving, aversion, or self-identification.

---

**Cultivation of Inner Stillness Through Practice**  
Inner stillness arises from consistent practice that includes regulating the five senses and gathering the six consciousnesses to prevent the mind from scattering outward. This is achieved through observing breath and thoughts without suppression or pursuit, allowing the mind to settle naturally into clarity. The state is not inert but dynamically aware, marked by freedom from emotional turbulence and external influence. Solitude supports this cultivation by reducing sensory distractions and creating conditions for sustained observation.

**Mindfulness as the Foundation of Stability**  
Mindfulness is the continuous practice of guarding the senses and maintaining awareness without being pulled by external stimuli or internal emotions. It enables the practitioner to remain steady amid movement and not cling to stillness when it arises. This quality becomes the natural condition of the liberated individual, who remains undisturbed in all postures and activities. Mindfulness is not a technique to achieve a goal but the ongoing presence that allows stillness and non-attachment to unfold.

**The State of Non-Attachment in Action**  
Non-attachment in action—referred to as “acting without abiding”—is the hallmark of advanced practice and liberation. It involves not clinging to any experience, including meditative bliss or psychic powers, and not rejecting discomfort or difficulty. The practitioner neither seeks nor avoids states, maintaining equanimity toward all sensations and conditions. This state is characterized by neither grasping nor rejecting, neither welcoming nor resisting, and is expressed as a mind that reflects all phenomena without clinging, like a mirror or open space.

**The Liberated One: Characteristics and Expression**  
The liberated individual has completely severed mental afflictions and is no longer bound by birth and death. Their mind is clear as a mirror, reflecting reality without distortion or attachment, and vast as space,包容而不执, encompassing all without clinging. They are free from pride in achievement, unaffected by praise or blame, and unburdened by the notion of “I am practicing” or “I have attained.” Their actions, whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, are rooted in this unshakable stillness and non-attachment.

**Wisdom as the Catalyst for Liberation**  
Wisdom, cultivated alongside meditation, enables the realization of impermanence and non-self. This insight dissolves the knots of attachment and aversion, leading to a mind free from obstruction. The liberated one’s freedom is not passive but active—fully engaged with the world yet untouched by it. Wisdom and meditation are interdependent: meditation stabilizes the mind to allow wisdom to arise, and wisdom deepens the quality of meditation, culminating in complete liberation.

**The Interdependence of Stillness, Meditation, and Liberation**  
Meditation is the primary means to develop inner stillness, which in turn supports the emergence of wisdom and liberation. Stillness is both the result of meditation and the condition that allows deeper meditation to unfold. Liberation is the ultimate fruit of this process, marked by the complete cessation of suffering and the full expression of non-attachment in daily life. The three—meditation, stillness, and liberation—are not separate stages but mutually reinforcing aspects of a unified path.

**The Symbolism of Mirror and Space**  
The mind of the liberated one is repeatedly compared to a mirror and to space. The mirror reflects without distortion or retention; the space contains all without resistance or ownership. These metaphors convey a state of pure awareness—clear, unobstructed, and free from clinging. They signify the absence of self-referential thought, the dissolution of boundaries between subject and object, and the full realization of freedom from all forms of entanglement.

**The Role of Non-Attachment to Spiritual Experiences**  
Even spiritual attainments, such as meditative joy or supernatural abilities, are not to be clung to. The practitioner who abides in non-attachment does not become proud of progress or discouraged by setbacks. They recognize that any experience, pleasant or unpleasant, is impermanent and not to be taken as identity. This attitude prevents regression and sustains progress toward complete liberation.

**The Natural State Beyond Effort**  
Inner stillness is not forced or manufactured but emerges naturally when the mind is no longer driven by craving or aversion. It is not a state of emptiness or numbness but of vivid, unobstructed awareness. The liberated one does not strive to maintain this state because it is their natural condition. They act without intention to achieve, rest without clinging to peace, and respond without reacting to conditions.

**The Liberation from the Illusion of Self**  
The liberated one has transcended the illusion of a separate self. They do not identify with the body, mind, or any experience as “mine.” This absence of self-identification is the root of their freedom from suffering. It allows them to engage fully with life without being bound by personal gain, loss, or status. Their actions are spontaneous, appropriate, and free from the burden of self-concern.

**The Integration of Practice into Daily Life**  
Liberation is not confined to meditation or retreat. It is expressed in every moment—walking, speaking, eating, resting. The liberated one moves through the world with full presence, undisturbed by external events or internal fluctuations. Their life is a continuous demonstration of non-attachment, clarity, and compassion, grounded in the unshakable stillness of the mind.
