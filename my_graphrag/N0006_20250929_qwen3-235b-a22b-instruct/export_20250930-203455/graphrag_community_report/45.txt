**Title: The True Aim of Practice: Liberation Beyond Fortune and Supernatural Powers**

**Summary:**  
The core purpose of spiritual practice is not to gain worldly benefits, supernatural abilities, or temporary fortune, but to achieve complete liberation through the eradication of ignorance, attachment, and the three poisons — greed, hatred, and delusion. The ultimate goal is the attainment of Nirvana, a state of permanent freedom from suffering and the cycle of rebirth. Any pursuit that diverts from this objective — such as seeking fortune or supernatural powers — is considered a deviation from the correct path. All virtuous actions serve this singular end.

**Eradication of Ignorance and Attachment as the Core Objective**  
The practice is fundamentally directed toward eliminating ignorance and attachment. These are identified as root causes of suffering and the cycle of rebirth. The goal is not to accumulate merit or favorable conditions, but to attain a state of purity and freedom by uprooting these mental afflictions.

**Liberation as the Ultimate Destination**  
The final aim of practice is liberation — the permanent cessation of greed, hatred, and delusion, leading to the state of Nirvana. This state represents the end of suffering and the transcendence of the cycle of birth and death. It is described as the true and lasting source of peace and happiness.

**Rejection of Fortune as a Primary Goal**  
Seeking fortune or worldly rewards is explicitly rejected as a valid aim of practice. Such pursuits are seen as misaligned with the true purpose and lead away from the correct path. The text repeatedly emphasizes that fortune is not the endpoint, and its pursuit undermines the integrity of spiritual development.

**Avoidance of Supernatural Powers**  
The pursuit of supernatural abilities is considered a distraction from the authentic path. Engaging in practice for the sake of gaining powers is viewed as a deviation that prevents the practitioner from reaching the ultimate goal of liberation.

**Adherence to the Correct Path Through Persistent Effort**  
Those who practice diligently follow the teachings of the Buddha, maintaining consistent effort to avoid falling under the influence of the cycle of rebirth. The correct path is defined by its alignment with the goal of ending suffering and attaining Nirvana.

**All Virtuous Actions Serve the Ultimate Goal**  
Every good deed and moral action undertaken in practice is instrumental in supporting the singular objective: the complete eradication of mental defilements and the realization of liberation. No virtuous act exists independently of this overarching purpose.

**The Three Poisons as Primary Obstacles**  
The practice is oriented toward the destruction of the three poisons — greed, hatred, and delusion — which are identified as the fundamental barriers to liberation. Overcoming these is not a secondary concern but the central mission of spiritual discipline.
