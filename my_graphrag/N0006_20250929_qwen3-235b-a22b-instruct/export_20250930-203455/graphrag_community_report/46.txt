**Title: Standards for Validating Spiritual Progress: Focus on Inner Transformation Over Supernatural Phenomena**

**Summary:**

Spiritual progress should be evaluated by measurable internal changes — specifically, reduced mental afflictions, stabilized mind, and increased clarity of wisdom — rather than by supernatural experiences or感应 (spiritual sensations). Attachment to such phenomena leads to new forms of clinging and distracts from genuine practice. These internal transformations reflect authentic advancement, including emotional resilience in all circumstances and deeper understanding of core truths like impermanence, suffering, and non-self. Pursuit of supernatural signs also carries risk of disturbance and is explicitly discouraged.

---

**Reduced Mental Afflictions as Core Indicator**

The primary measure of spiritual progress is the extent to which mental disturbances — such as agitation, attachment, or aversion — diminish. This reduction signals effective practice and is the most reliable gauge of inner development. If afflictions persist or intensify, it suggests the practice is not yielding its intended effect.

**Stabilized Mind Reflects Depth of Practice**

A calm and steady mind, especially under varying conditions, demonstrates the maturation of meditation and wisdom. This stability is not passive but active resilience — the ability to remain unaffected by external events, without generating greed or anger. It confirms the practitioner’s growing mastery over reactive patterns.

**Clarity of Wisdom Marks Deepening Understanding**

Increased mental clarity signifies advancement in insight, particularly regarding fundamental realities: impermanence, suffering, and the absence of a fixed self. This clarity is not theoretical but experiential — it manifests as a shift in perception and behavior, allowing the practitioner to navigate life with greater discernment and freedom.

**Supernatural Experiences Are Not Valid Measures**

Phenomena such as感应 (spiritual sensations or mystical experiences) do not indicate genuine progress. Relying on them as benchmarks misdirects effort and fosters attachment, which contradicts the aim of liberation. These experiences are neither necessary nor sufficient for evaluating spiritual development.

**Attachment to Sensations Undermines True Practice**

When practitioners fixate on感应, they create new forms of clinging that obstruct the path. This fixation diverts attention from inner transformation and reinforces egoic patterns. True progress requires letting go of such attachments, not cultivating them.

**Pursuit of Supernatural Powers Invites Disturbance**

Seeking extraordinary abilities or sensations invites disruptive influences during meditation. This is a known hazard in disciplined practice and must be avoided. The goal is not to acquire powers but to dissolve delusion and cultivate peace.

**Resilience Across Conditions Demonstrates Liberation**

The ability to remain unmoved by favorable or adverse circumstances — without generating craving or aversion — is a tangible sign of inner freedom. This resilience is not suppression but natural equanimity, emerging from deep insight and sustained practice. It confirms the practitioner’s movement toward lasting liberation.
