**Title: The Path to Liberation: How Practitioners Transcend Samsara Through the Eradication of Greed, Hatred, and Delusion**

**Summary:**  
Practitioners aim to permanently escape the cycle of birth and death by eliminating greed, hatred, and delusion — the root causes of suffering and cyclic existence. This liberation is achieved through disciplined practice, including ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom, which together dismantle the mental afflictions that bind beings to samsara. Once these afflictions are fully eradicated, practitioners no longer experience the pull of karma, attain a state of unconditioned freedom, and dwell in enduring peace. The ultimate goal is not merely to avoid suffering but to realize a mind completely untainted by external conditions, free from all forms of attachment and delusion.

---

**Eradication of Greed, Hatred, and Delusion as the Core Mechanism for Liberation**  
The cycle of birth and death is sustained by greed, hatred, and delusion. These three afflictions are the fundamental causes that bind beings to repeated existence. Practitioners must completely eliminate them to break free from this cycle. Their removal is not partial or temporary; it is total and irreversible. Only when these afflictions are extinguished does the practitioner cease to be subject to the forces of karma and the suffering of cyclic existence.

**Karma as the Driving Force Behind Rebirth and the Necessity of Ethical Conduct**  
Karma determines the direction and outcome of a practitioner’s existence. It is the causal force that propels beings through successive lives. To avoid negative outcomes and support progress toward liberation, practitioners must act with care, cultivating wholesome actions and avoiding harmful ones. Ethical discipline — maintaining purity in body, speech, and mind — is essential to generate the necessary conditions for spiritual advancement and to prevent being drawn further into the cycle.

**Wisdom and Meditation as Tools to Dissolve Ignorance and Afflictions**  
Wisdom arises from direct insight into the nature of reality, particularly the impermanence of all phenomena and the absence of inherent self. This wisdom dismantles ignorance, which is the root of all suffering and the foundation of the three afflictions. Meditation supports this process by calming the mind, removing mental obstacles such as the five hindrances, and enabling deeper insight. Together, wisdom and meditation allow practitioners to see through delusion and gradually sever the ties that bind them to samsara.

**The State of Liberation: Freedom from Karma, Afflictions, and Conditioned Existence**  
Liberation is the complete cessation of all mental afflictions and the end of karmic bondage. In this state, the practitioner no longer experiences the pull of karma, nor is the mind disturbed or stained by external conditions. The liberated mind is described as clear, still, and unattached — like a mirror that reflects without retaining impressions. This state is not merely the absence of suffering but the realization of ultimate freedom, characterized by enduring peace and self-mastery.

**The Role of Persistent Effort in Sustaining Spiritual Progress**  
Sustained and diligent practice is indispensable for achieving liberation. Without continuous effort, practitioners risk being drawn back into the cycle of birth and death. Persistent application of ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom ensures that progress is maintained and that the conditions for final liberation are fully realized. This effort must be unwavering, as even temporary lapse can impede the path to complete freedom.

**The Ultimate Achievement: Unconditioned Freedom and the Cessation of All Attachment**  
The highest state of liberation includes the complete dissolution of not only greed, hatred, and delusion, but also of the clinging to self and phenomena — known as self-view and views of existence. In this unconditioned state, the mind is entirely free from all forms of attachment and conceptual fixation. It is not influenced by external conditions, does not generate new karma, and remains permanently at peace. This is the final goal of the path: a state of absolute autonomy and purity, beyond all cycles of suffering and rebirth.
