**Title: The Role of Anattā Practice in Liberation Through Five Aggregates Observation**

**Summary:**  
This report details how practitioners achieve liberation by observing the five aggregates (form, feeling, perception, mental formations, volition, and consciousness) through the lens of anattā (non-self). The core method involves recognizing that these aggregates are impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of inherent self. This observation dismantles attachment to “I” and “mine,” thereby eliminating greed, aversion, fear, and pride. The practice leads to mental freedom, cessation of suffering, and realization of emptiness. It is supported by mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom, and is grounded in the Three Marks of Existence: impermanence, suffering, and non-self. Liberation is attained when the illusion of self dissolves, allowing the mind to rest in non-abiding, free from clinging to internal or external phenomena.

---

**Observation of the Five Aggregates as the Foundation for Non-Self Realization**  
The five aggregates—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are the primary objects of observation in the practice of non-self. Practitioners examine these aggregates as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and empty of inherent existence. By continuously observing their arising and ceasing, practitioners recognize that no enduring “self” exists within them. This direct observation dismantles the mistaken belief that the aggregates constitute a permanent, independent “I” or “mine.” As this illusion fades, attachment to body, mind, and possessions dissolves, leading to the cessation of suffering rooted in self-attachment.

---

**Non-Self Practice as the Core Method for Eliminating Suffering**  
The practice of non-self directly targets the root of suffering: attachment to “I” and “mine.” By observing the five aggregates as empty and impermanent, practitioners break the grip of self-view and self-attachment. This results in the natural dissolution of greed, aversion, fear, and pride. The practice also removes the basis for clinging to outcomes, status, or experiences, allowing the mind to remain undisturbed by gains or losses. As attachment fades, suffering ceases, and the practitioner experiences inner freedom and peace.

---

**Integration of Mindfulness, Concentration, and Wisdom in Non-Self Practice**  
Mindfulness is the essential foundation for practicing non-self. Only through mindful observation can practitioners accurately perceive the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of the five aggregates. Concentration provides the stable mental state necessary to sustain this observation. Through deep concentration, practitioners can directly perceive the empty nature of the aggregates, which deepens insight. Wisdom arises from this direct perception, allowing practitioners to see through the illusion of self and realize the truth of non-self. The interplay of mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom is indispensable for the full realization of non-self.

---

**Non-Self Practice as the Path to Emptiness and Liberation**  
The ultimate aim of non-self practice is the realization of emptiness—the understanding that all phenomena, including the self, arise dependently and lack inherent existence. By observing the five aggregates as empty, practitioners directly experience this truth. This realization dissolves the illusion of a separate self and the attachments that stem from it. As a result, the mind no longer clings to internal or external phenomena and rests in a state of non-abiding. This state is characterized by freedom from all forms of bondage, including the cycle of birth and death. Liberation is achieved when the practitioner fully embodies this understanding and lives in alignment with it.

---

**Non-Self Practice and the Three Marks of Existence**  
The practice of non-self is inseparable from the Three Marks of Existence: impermanence, suffering, and non-self. Observing the five aggregates as impermanent undermines the belief in a permanent self. Recognizing their unsatisfactory nature reveals the futility of clinging to them. Seeing their selfless nature directly dismantles the illusion of “I” and “mine.” Together, these observations form the basis for wisdom and liberation. Practitioners who align their practice with these three marks avoid spiritual pitfalls and progress steadily toward freedom.

---

**Psychological and Behavioral Effects of Non-Self Realization**  
As non-self practice matures, practitioners experience profound psychological changes. The mind becomes stable and undisturbed by external circumstances, no longer reacting with greed, aversion, or fear. They no longer seek personal gain or validation, and are free from attachment to spiritual experiences or supernatural abilities. Their actions become naturally aligned with ethical conduct, as the motivation rooted in self-interest dissolves. Compassion arises naturally, as the practitioner sees all beings as equally devoid of inherent self. The mind rests in equanimity, free from extremes, and responds to life with clarity and balance.

---

**Non-Self Practice as the Culmination of Wisdom and the Essence of Liberation**  
Non-self practice is not merely a technique but the very essence of wisdom and liberation. It is the means by which practitioners directly perceive the true nature of reality: that all phenomena, including the self, are empty, impermanent, and interdependent. This perception transforms the mind, dissolving ignorance and the afflictions that arise from it. The practitioner no longer clings to any concept of self or other, and lives in harmony with the way things truly are. Liberation is not an external achievement but the natural outcome of seeing through the illusion of self. In this state, there is no “one” who is liberated, no “thing” to attain, and no “place” to reach—only the unobstructed flow of awareness, free from all clinging.
