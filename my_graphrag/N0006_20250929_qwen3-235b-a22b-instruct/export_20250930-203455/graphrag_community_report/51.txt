Title: Wisdom, Impermanence, and Psychological Transformation in Spiritual Practice

Summary:  
The text describes how wisdom and the direct observation of impermanence lead to psychological shifts that reduce attachment and craving. Practitioners who cultivate wisdom remain balanced, neither clinging to experiences nor rejecting liberation. Observing impermanence truthfully triggers a natural detachment from worldly pleasures, which in turn fosters a stable inner state free from pursuit of illusory satisfaction.

Wisdom Supports Non-Attachment Without Rejection  
Wisdom enables practitioners to remain unattached to any experiential state. This includes not developing aversion even toward the goal of liberation. The result is a stable, balanced presence grounded in the middle way, avoiding extremes of clinging or aversion.

Observing Impermanence Triggers Detachment from Worldly Pleasures  
When impermanence is observed as it truly is, it naturally leads to a letting go of desire. This detachment arises not from force or suppression, but from a clear recognition that worldly pleasures are transient and ultimately unsatisfying.

Detachment Arises as a Consequence of Truthful Observation  
The psychological shift away from chasing illusory happiness is a direct outcome of seeing impermanence without distortion. This shift is not imposed externally but emerges internally through accurate perception, leading to a sustained disengagement from superficial gratifications.
