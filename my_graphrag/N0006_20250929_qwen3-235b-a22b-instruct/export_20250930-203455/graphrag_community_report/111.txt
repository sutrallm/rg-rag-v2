**Title: The Practice of Formless Cultivation in Spiritual Liberation**

**Summary:**  
This report outlines the core principles of formless cultivation as a method for spiritual liberation. It emphasizes the rejection of attachment to external forms, postures, or supernatural phenomena, and instead prioritizes internal mental transformation. The practice is presented as essential for entering the state of non-action and achieving true liberation.

---

**Rejection of Attachment to External Forms**  
Formless cultivation requires practitioners to avoid clinging to physical forms or meditative states. This detachment is not a rejection of practice itself, but a shift away from mistaking the method for the goal. The focus is on inner realization rather than outward appearance or ritual.

**Prioritization of Mental Transformation Over Physical Posture**  
The practice places primary importance on the transformation of the mind rather than adherence to specific physical postures, such as sitting meditation. The posture is not inherently significant; what matters is the internal state cultivated through it.

**Avoidance of Supernatural Goals**  
Practitioners are cautioned against pursuing or valuing supernatural abilities or experiences as objectives of their practice. Such pursuits are seen as distractions that reinforce attachment and hinder the path to liberation.

**Entry into Non-Action Through Non-Attachment**  
The ultimate aim of formless cultivation is to enter a state of non-action — a condition free from effort, striving, or conceptualization. This is achieved by consistently releasing attachment to forms, experiences, and outcomes, thereby aligning with the natural flow of existence.

**Liberation as the Outcome of Non-Attachment**  
True liberation is described as the direct result of sustained non-attachment to forms and states. It is not attained through accumulation or achievement, but through the dissolution of clinging — allowing the practitioner to realize their inherent freedom.
