**Title: Wisdom, Liberation, and the Practice of Non-Self in the Path of the Practitioner**

**Summary:**

The practitioner’s path centers on the cultivation of wisdom through meditation and direct observation of reality. Wisdom arises naturally when the mind is clear, focused, and free from attachment. It enables the practitioner to see the true nature of phenomena — impermanence, non-self, and suffering — thereby dissolving ignorance and self-attachment. This wisdom is inseparable from liberation; it is both the means and the result of the path. It must be balanced with ethical conduct and mental stability (concentration), forming a unified practice. Without wisdom, liberation is impossible. The practitioner’s liberation is achieved by directly realizing non-self, which dismantles the root of suffering and breaks the cycle of rebirth. This realization is supported by sustained observation of impermanence and the five aggregates, leading to freedom from clinging, fear, and desire. The fully realized being embodies this wisdom, having completely extinguished greed, hatred, and delusion, and lives in unshakable peace and purity.

---

**Wisdom Arises from Clear, Focused, and Unattached Mind**

When the practitioner’s mind is purified, concentrated, and free from clinging, wisdom naturally emerges. This wisdom is not acquired through external teaching or conceptual reasoning, but through direct inner realization. It allows the practitioner to see things as they are — impermanent, without a fixed self, and inherently unsatisfactory. This seeing dissolves ignorance and the illusion of a permanent self, thereby cutting off the root of suffering. Wisdom is not a static state but a dynamic capacity that deepens with continued practice, enabling the practitioner to remain unshaken by changing conditions and to transcend all forms of attachment.

**Liberation Depends on the Direct Realization of Non-Self**

The core of liberation is the direct experience of non-self. Only by seeing through the illusion of “I” and “mine” can the practitioner sever the root of suffering and break free from the cycle of birth and death. This realization is not intellectual but experiential, arising from sustained observation of the five aggregates — form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness — as impermanent, empty, and devoid of inherent existence. The practitioner who fully realizes non-self no longer clings to any identity or境界 (state), and thus attains freedom from all forms of bondage. This realization is the defining mark of liberation and the foundation of true peace.

**Impermanence Is the Foundation for Wisdom and Liberation**

Observing impermanence is essential to the practitioner’s progress. By seeing that all phenomena arise and pass away, the practitioner weakens attachment to fixed identities, outcomes, or states of being. This observation reduces greed and fear, allowing the mind to rest in stability and equanimity. Impermanence is not merely a concept to be understood but a reality to be directly witnessed in every moment. This direct seeing leads to dispassion, which in turn fuels the path toward liberation. Without seeing impermanence, the practitioner remains trapped in the illusion of permanence and self, unable to break free from suffering.

**Wisdom and Concentration Are Mutually Dependent**

Wisdom and mental concentration are inseparable. Concentration provides the stable, clear, and focused mind necessary for wisdom to arise. Without concentration, the mind is scattered and unable to penetrate the true nature of reality. Conversely, wisdom guides concentration, preventing it from becoming stagnant or falling into mere absorption without insight. When concentration and wisdom are balanced, the practitioner avoids the pitfalls of empty stillness or intellectual speculation. Together, they form the path of “concentration leading to wisdom,” which culminates in full liberation. Neither can achieve the goal alone; both are indispensable.

**Wisdom Is the Ultimate Tool for Ending Suffering**

All suffering stems from ignorance and attachment. Wisdom is the only force capable of uprooting these causes. It does so by directly seeing the emptiness and impermanence of all phenomena, including the self. This seeing dissolves the illusions that give rise to craving, aversion, and delusion. Wisdom does not suppress or avoid suffering; it dissolves its root by revealing its true nature. As wisdom deepens, the three poisons — greed, hatred, and delusion — naturally fade away, like dew under the morning sun. The practitioner who relies on wisdom does not need to struggle against suffering; they simply see through it and are free.

**The Fully Realized Being Embodies Complete Wisdom and Liberation**

The fully realized being has completely eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion through the power of wisdom. They see all phenomena — including impermanence, non-self, and emptiness — with perfect clarity. This insight liberates them from all forms of attachment, including attachment to spiritual states or achievements. Their mind is utterly free, peaceful, and unshakable. Their presence radiates purity and stillness, naturally commanding reverence. Their state is not a result of external conditions but of complete inner transformation through the direct realization of truth. They are the living embodiment of wisdom and liberation.

**The Path Requires Letting Go of All Attachments, Including Spiritual Goals**

The practitioner must not cling to any achievement, state, or outcome — even spiritual ones. The pursuit of liberation must not become a new form of attachment. True progress occurs when the practitioner observes without grasping, whether in meditation or daily life. The goal is not to attain a special state but to see things as they are and let go of all clinging. Even the desire for enlightenment must be released, for it too can become a fetter. Only when all attachments — including those to spiritual success — are relinquished does true freedom arise.

**Wisdom and Liberation Are One and the Same**

Wisdom is not a tool that leads to liberation; it is liberation itself. The moment wisdom fully arises, ignorance is dispelled, and the mind is free. Liberation is not a future state to be reached but the immediate result of seeing reality clearly. Wisdom and liberation are inseparable — one cannot exist without the other. The practitioner who realizes wisdom has already attained liberation; the one who is liberated embodies wisdom. This unity is the heart of the path: to see clearly is to be free.
