**Title: The Path to Liberation and Nirvana: Practices, States, and Ultimate Realization of the Practitioner**

**Summary**

The practitioner’s journey centers on liberation and Nirvana as inseparable ultimate goals, achieved through disciplined practice of moral conduct, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Progress involves systematically dismantling greed, hatred, delusion, and all forms of attachment—including attachment to spiritual methods themselves. The culmination is a state of unshakable inner freedom, free from suffering, conceptual thinking, and external influence. This state is not theoretical but experiential, realized directly through practice, and marked by tranquility, luminosity, and non-attachment to outcomes. True freedom is expressed in equanimity toward praise or blame, and in the complete cessation of inner disturbance.

---

**The Ultimate Goal Is Liberation and Nirvana**

Liberation and Nirvana are presented as identical in essence—the final state attained when all afflictions, ignorance, and clinging are extinguished. This state is characterized by the absence of suffering, the dissolution of self and phenomena as fixed entities, and the realization of a timeless, unconditioned peace. It is not a destination reached externally but an internal transformation that transcends birth and death.

**The Method: Moral Conduct, Concentration, and Wisdom**

The path follows a structured progression: ethical discipline establishes a stable foundation, meditative concentration calms and unifies the mind, and wisdom arises from direct observation, leading to the dissolution of delusion. These three elements are interdependent and must be cultivated in sequence to progressively weaken and ultimately eradicate the root causes of suffering.

**The Obstacles: Greed, Hatred, Delusion, and Attachment**

The primary impediments to liberation are the three poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—and their extensions: ignorance, self-attachment, and attachment to views or methods. Even spiritual achievements must not be clung to, as attachment to results or practices themselves becomes a new form of bondage. The practitioner must continually release all forms of clinging, including attachment to the idea of liberation.

**The Nature of the Realized State: Freedom, Equanimity, and Non-Attachment**

The liberated state is marked by complete inner freedom—unaffected by external circumstances, internal thoughts, or social judgments. The practitioner remains unmoved by gain or loss, praise or blame, and experiences a natural luminosity and ease. This state is not passive but dynamically present, expressed as “no-abiding mind” and “spontaneous responsiveness without clinging.”

**The Necessity of Direct Experience**

Liberation cannot be conveyed through words, logic, or conceptual thinking. It must be personally realized through sustained practice. The practitioner does not infer or deduce the truth of liberation but directly experiences it as the cessation of mental agitation and the arising of innate clarity. This direct knowing is the only valid means of understanding reality.

**The Gradual Process: From Thinning Afflictions to Complete Cessation**

Progress is incremental. As the practitioner advances through stages—from initial insight to full realization—afflictions diminish, the mind becomes increasingly clear and still, and attachment to phenomena fades. Even partial realizations, such as the attainment of the first through fourth fruits, must not be clung to; true liberation is defined by the absence of any residue of attachment or self-reference.

**The Final State: Natural Purity and Effortless Peace**

At culmination, the mind returns to its original, unconditioned nature—serene, luminous, and beyond the need for practice or attainment. There is no sense of “having achieved” or “being liberated”; the distinction between practitioner and liberation dissolves. This is the state of “no practice, no attainment,” where peace is not cultivated but revealed as the fundamental condition of being.

**The Rejection of Worldly Motivations**

The practitioner must not be driven by fame, recognition, or material gain. These are distractions that reinforce the very attachments the path seeks to dissolve. The sole valid motivation is the complete eradication of suffering and the realization of Nirvana.

**The Expression of True Freedom**

True freedom is not the ability to act without constraint but the absence of internal disturbance. It is expressed in the practitioner’s unshakable equanimity, their ability to remain undisturbed by external events or internal fluctuations, and their spontaneous, non-attached engagement with the world. This is the hallmark of one who has fully realized liberation.
