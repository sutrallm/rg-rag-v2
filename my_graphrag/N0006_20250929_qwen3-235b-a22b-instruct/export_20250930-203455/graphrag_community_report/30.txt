**Title: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment: Understanding the Practice of “No Abiding, Yet Mind Arises”**

**Summary**  
The practice of “no abiding, yet mind arises” represents the highest stage of meditative discipline and wisdom cultivation. It requires complete non-attachment to all experiences—including emptiness, joy, tranquility, and even virtuous states—while allowing the mind to naturally express clarity, compassion, and wisdom. This state mirrors the mind as a clear mirror or open sky: reflecting all phenomena without clinging, rejecting, or retaining them. It is the embodiment of full liberation and ultimate freedom, achieved through purified consciousness and refined discernment.

---

**Non-Attachment as the Core of the Practice**  
The central requirement of this state is the absence of clinging to any mental or meditative condition. Whether encountering emptiness, joy, tranquility, or even virtuous states, the practitioner must not grasp or dwell upon them. This non-attachment is not passive indifference but active freedom from fixation, enabling the mind to remain unobstructed and responsive.

**The Mind as a Mirror Reflecting Without Retention**  
The mind in this state functions like a clear mirror: it perceives and reflects all phenomena without distortion or residue. This metaphor underscores the capacity to engage with reality fully while remaining untouched by attachment. It is a dynamic equilibrium—aware, responsive, yet unburdened.

**Freedom from All Meditative States**  
Even profound meditative experiences such as emptiness, lightness, or bliss must not be clung to. The practice demands that such states be acknowledged and passed through without attachment, ensuring that the practitioner does not become ensnared by the very experiences that may arise from deep concentration.

**Wisdom Manifesting Naturally in Non-Abiding**  
From the foundation of non-attachment, the mind naturally gives rise to qualities such as clarity, compassion, and wisdom. These are not forced or cultivated through effort but emerge spontaneously when the mind is free from fixation. This natural arising is the culmination of the wisdom aspect of training, reflecting the highest level of discernment.

**Liberation as the Embodiment of Ultimate Freedom**  
This state is described as the full realization of liberation—not as an abstract concept but as a lived condition of complete ease and freedom. It is the manifestation of true nature, unobstructed by clinging, and is synonymous with the deepest peace and unshakable stability.

**Purification of Consciousness as the Prerequisite**  
Achieving this state requires prior refinement of the mind. Only after consciousness has been purified can one naturally reside in non-abiding while allowing the mind to arise with purity and responsiveness. This purification is the necessary groundwork for the spontaneous expression of wisdom and compassion.

**The Integration of Non-Abiding and Active Mind**  
The practice is not merely about stillness or detachment but about the harmonious integration of non-abiding with the active, responsive mind. It is a state in which the mind is simultaneously free from fixation and fully engaged with reality—expressing wisdom and compassion without effort or strain.
