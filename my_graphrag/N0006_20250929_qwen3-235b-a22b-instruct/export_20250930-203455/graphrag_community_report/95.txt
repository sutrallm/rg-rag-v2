**Title: Zen Practice and the Symbolism of the Serene Jungle for Inner Peace**

**Summary:**  
The text consistently uses the imagery of wild animals—particularly deer, rabbits, and beasts—moving peacefully through a jungle free of insects to represent the mental state achieved through Zen practice. This metaphor illustrates how disciplined meditation and mindfulness allow practitioners to detach from external distractions and internal disturbances, resulting in a calm, stable, and liberated mind. The jungle symbolizes the environment of practice, and the absence of insects signifies freedom from烦恼 (disturbances, troubles, or afflictions). The repeated use of this analogy across variations reinforces its centrality to conveying the transformative effect of Zen on mental clarity and emotional equilibrium.

**Wild Animals in the Insect-Free Jungle Reflect Mental Liberation Through Meditation**  
The consistent depiction of deer, rabbits, and beasts walking without disturbance in a jungle devoid of insects serves as a direct metaphor for the practitioner’s mind after engaging in Zen meditation. The animals’ untroubled movement signifies a state of inner peace, stability, and freedom from agitation. This condition is explicitly linked to the practice of禅定 (meditative concentration), which enables the mind to settle and remain unaffected by external or internal disturbances.

**The Jungle as a Symbol of the Ideal Practice Environment**  
The jungle is repeatedly described as the setting where the animals move freely and safely, indicating that it represents the environment in which Zen practice occurs. Its freedom from insects underscores its purity and suitability for cultivating mental stillness. The absence of insects is not merely physical but symbolic of the removal of distractions,烦恼 (troubles or afflictions), and杂念 (mental clutter) that hinder spiritual progress.

**Insects Represent External and Internal Disturbances**  
Mosquitoes and other insects are consistently used as symbols for the sources of mental unrest—both external conditions and internal thoughts that agitate the mind. The image of animals moving safely through a jungle without insects highlights the effectiveness of Zen practice in neutralizing these disturbances. The liberation from such disturbances is portrayed as the core benefit of sustained meditation and mindfulness.

**Mindfulness and Meditation Yield Identical States of Calm**  
The text equates the effects of禅定 (meditative concentration) and正念 (mindfulness) by using the same jungle-and-animal metaphor for both. Whether through focused meditation or sustained awareness, the outcome is the same: a mind that is tranquil, centered, and unshaken by surrounding chaos. This suggests that while the methods may differ, their purpose and result converge in achieving mental serenity.

**Repetition of the Metaphor Reinforces the Centrality of Peaceful Mental States**  
The repeated use of the jungle-animal metaphor across multiple variations underscores its importance in conveying the essence of Zen practice. Each iteration emphasizes different aspects—freedom from烦恼, the role of the environment, the nature of disturbances—but all converge on the same outcome: a mind that is calm, stable, and liberated through disciplined practice. This repetition serves to anchor the reader’s understanding in the tangible image of serene movement through a pure, undisturbed space.
