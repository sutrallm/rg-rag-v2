**Title: The Interdependence of Moral Conduct, Concentration, and Wisdom in Spiritual Practice**

**Summary:**  
Moral conduct, mental concentration, and wisdom form a sequential and interdependent foundation for spiritual development. Moral conduct must be established first, as it enables stable concentration, which in turn allows for the emergence of wisdom. Without moral purity, neither concentration nor wisdom can fully develop. Each stage supports the next, creating a necessary progression for realizing the true nature of phenomena.

**Moral Conduct as the Foundational Requirement**  
Moral conduct must be established at the outset of practice. It serves as the essential prerequisite for developing mental concentration and wisdom. Without purity in moral behavior, the mind cannot settle into the stability required for deeper contemplation or insight.

**Concentration as the Bridge to Wisdom**  
Mental concentration, when firmly established, allows the mind to remain undistracted. This undisturbed state is necessary to directly perceive the true nature of all phenomena. Concentration is not an end in itself but a means to enable the arising of wisdom.

**The Sequential and Interdependent Relationship**  
The three elements—moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom—are inseparably linked. Moral conduct supports concentration, and concentration supports wisdom. Each stage must be cultivated in order, and the strength of each depends on the integrity of the preceding stage. This progression is essential for the complete eradication of mental afflictions and the realization of tranquility.
