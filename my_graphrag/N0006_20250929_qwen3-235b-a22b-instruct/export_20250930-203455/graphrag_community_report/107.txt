**Title: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment: Key Realizations of the Practitioner**

**Summary:**  
The practitioner achieves true liberation by cultivating non-attachment to all states, results, and conceptual frameworks. Liberation is not attained through clinging to meditative experiences, spiritual achievements, or even virtuous practices, but through the realization of three essential gateways: non-self, formlessness, and non-aspiration. The core state of liberation is described as “non-abiding,” where the mind remains free, unbound, and as vast as space, neither clinging to nor rejecting any experience. This state is not a passive void but an active, clear, and compassionate awareness that arises naturally when all forms of attachment cease. The ultimate goal is not the acquisition of any境界 or成果, but the complete release from all forms of clinging — including the desire for liberation itself.

---

**Non-Abiding as the Core State of Liberation**  
The mind achieves true freedom when it does not settle on or cling to any internal or external state, including meditative experiences, spiritual attainments, or even the concept of self. This state of non-abiding is not a lack of activity but a dynamic, unobstructed awareness that functions like a clear mirror — reflecting all phenomena without retention or distortion. It is the culmination of practice, where the practitioner no longer seeks or rejects any condition, and thus remains continuously at ease.

**The Three Gateways to Complete Liberation**  
Liberation is fully realized only when the practitioner directly experiences the three gateways: non-self, formlessness, and non-aspiration. Non-self dissolves the illusion of a fixed, independent identity. Formlessness transcends attachment to appearances, names, or conceptual structures. Non-aspiration releases all striving, desire, and expectation, including the wish for enlightenment or peace. Together, these three gateways constitute the complete dismantling of all forms of clinging, leading to the ultimate state of freedom.

**Non-Attachment to Meditation Experiences**  
Even profound meditative states — such as emptiness, clarity, joy, or stillness — must not be clung to. Attachment to these experiences, however refined, becomes an obstacle to liberation. The practitioner must remain unattached to the very experiences that arise during practice, including the sense of peace or bliss, to avoid mistaking temporary states for final realization. True meditation成就 is not measured by the depth of experience, but by the absence of attachment to it.

**Wisdom as the Means to Transcend Form and Desire**  
Wisdom enables the practitioner to directly perceive the absence of inherent form in all phenomena and to release all desires and aspirations. This wisdom is not intellectual but experiential — it arises when the mind is purified and no longer caught in dualistic thinking. It allows the practitioner to see reality as it is — empty of fixed identity, free from conceptual overlay, and unbound by craving.

**The Mind’s Natural State is Freedom**  
When the mind ceases to grasp at internal or external objects, it naturally settles into a state of effortless ease. This is not a state induced by effort, but one revealed when all conditions of attachment are removed. The mind becomes like space — unobstructed, accommodating all phenomena without being affected by them. This natural state is the essence of liberation, and it is accessible to any practitioner who relinquishes clinging.

**The Role of Practice in Cultivating Non-Abiding**  
Practices such as non-self contemplation, formless observation, and non-aspiration are not ends in themselves but methods to dismantle attachment. When practiced correctly, they lead the mind to spontaneously rest in non-abiding. The practitioner does not force the mind into stillness, but allows it to settle by releasing all forms of clinging — including the desire to achieve or maintain any particular state.

**Liberation as the Absence of All Clinging**  
The defining characteristic of one who is liberated is the complete absence of attachment — not only to worldly objects or desires, but also to spiritual goals, meditative states, or even the notion of liberation. This non-clinging is not indifference, but a profound freedom that allows the mind to respond to life with clarity, compassion, and wisdom. It is the ultimate expression of peace and autonomy.

**The Ultimate Goal is Not Attainment, but Release**  
True achievement is not the acquisition of any境界 or成果, but the complete letting go of the need to acquire anything. The practitioner who seeks liberation must also release the desire for liberation. Only when all forms of seeking — including spiritual seeking — are abandoned, does the mind fully enter the state of non-abiding, which is itself the essence of liberation.
