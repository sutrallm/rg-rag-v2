**Title: Four Noble Truths as the Core Framework for Liberation: Guidance from the Buddha’s Teachings**

**Summary:**

The Four Noble Truths—suffering, origin of suffering, cessation of suffering, and the path leading to cessation—form the essential structure guiding practitioners toward liberation. Their practice is not theoretical but demands direct personal experience. Mastery of these truths enables the complete eradication of suffering and its causes, leading to freedom from cyclic existence. The framework integrates wisdom, ethical conduct, and mental discipline, and serves as the foundation for right understanding, mindfulness, and balanced living. Without accurate comprehension and application of the Four Noble Truths, true liberation cannot be achieved.

---

**Suffering and Its Origin Must Be Directly Observed**

Practitioners must clearly recognize suffering and its root causes—particularly craving and clinging—as outlined in the first two truths. This recognition is not abstract but requires personal observation of the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all phenomena. Without this direct seeing, the cycle of suffering continues.

**Cessation and the Path Are Achievable Through Practice**

The third and fourth truths—cessation of suffering and the path leading to it—are not mere ideals but attainable realities. Those who fully engage with the path, especially through ethical conduct, mental discipline, and wisdom, can realize the cessation of suffering. This outcome is verifiable through personal experience and is the definitive mark of liberation.

**Wisdom Arises from Understanding the Four Truths**

True wisdom emerges from correctly understanding the Four Noble Truths. This includes seeing impermanence, non-self, and the futility of attachment. Such insight directly undermines delusion and ignorance, which are the underlying causes of suffering. Without this wisdom, liberation remains out of reach.

**Right View Is Rooted in the Four Noble Truths**

Right view, the first factor of the Noble Eightfold Path, is built upon a correct understanding of the Four Noble Truths. It serves as the foundation for all other aspects of practice. Misunderstanding these truths leads practitioners astray, while accurate comprehension ensures alignment with the path to liberation.

**Mindfulness and Observation Support Truth Realization**

Practitioners who apply mindfulness observe suffering, its causes, its cessation, and the path with clarity. This observation is not passive but active, preventing the mind from being carried away by delusion or distraction. Such practice sustains progress toward liberation.

**The Threefold Training Is Guided by the Four Truths**

Ethical conduct (sila), mental discipline (samadhi), and wisdom (pañña) are cultivated in sequence, each step supported by the framework of the Four Noble Truths. This training leads to the complete stilling of suffering and the full realization of the path.

**Personal Experience Is Required for Liberation**

Liberation cannot be attained through intellectual knowledge alone. It demands direct, experiential realization of the Four Noble Truths. Only through personal practice—observing, understanding, and applying these truths—can one uproot the causes of suffering and attain freedom.

**Impermanence and Non-Self Undermine the Root of Suffering**

Recognizing impermanence and the absence of a permanent self is essential to dismantling the illusion of “I” and “mine,” which are the core of suffering and its origin. This understanding directly weakens attachment and craving, paving the way for cessation.

**Balance Is Integral to the Path**

The practice of the Four Noble Truths requires moderation—not indulgence nor extreme asceticism. Like tuning a stringed instrument, the path is most effective when applied with appropriate effort and equilibrium, leading to the cessation of suffering.

**Liberation Is Marked by Inner Freedom**

True liberation, as described in the context of the Four Noble Truths, manifests as inner freedom—unmoved by external conditions, free from craving and aversion. This state is the direct result of fully realizing the third and fourth truths through sustained practice.

**The Buddha’s Core Teaching Is the Four Noble Truths**

All of the Buddha’s teachings are structured around the Four Noble Truths. They serve as the central framework for understanding suffering and its end. Any deviation from this structure risks misalignment with the path to liberation. The truths are not merely introductory but remain the guiding principle throughout the entire journey.
