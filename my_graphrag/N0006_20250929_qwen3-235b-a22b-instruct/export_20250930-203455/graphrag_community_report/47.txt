**Title: Attitudes and Self-Examination for Sustained Spiritual Progress**

**Summary:**  
Sustained spiritual progress requires unwavering commitment during adversity, consistent effort to accumulate merit, and vigilant self-examination across both favorable and unfavorable conditions. True progress is marked by emotional stability in hardship and absence of attachment in ease. Persistent self-reflection on habitual tendencies, reactions to adversity, and behavior in comfort is essential to maintain alertness and avoid regression.

**Unwavering Commitment Amid Adversity**  
When faced with hardship, a practitioner must not waver or lose confidence. Reliance on correct teachings and continuous, diligent effort are indispensable for attaining spiritual results. This steadfastness ensures momentum is preserved even under pressure.

**Emotional Stability as a Marker of Progress**  
Genuine spiritual advancement is demonstrated by the ability to remain calm and composed during difficult circumstances. This composure signals the early stages of liberation, indicating that inner disturbances are diminishing.

**Non-Attachment in Favorable Conditions**  
True spiritual growth is also revealed by the absence of clinging or craving when circumstances are favorable. Remaining unattached during ease reflects a maturing detachment from external conditions, another early sign of liberation.

**Self-Examination to Counter Habitual Patterns**  
Practitioners must regularly examine whether they are still driven by ingrained habits. This self-awareness is critical to prevent unconscious behaviors from undermining progress and to sustain mindful awareness.

**Monitoring Reactions to Adversity**  
Regular self-assessment is required to determine whether one regresses or loses resolve when confronted with challenges. This vigilance helps maintain momentum and prevents setbacks from eroding spiritual development.

**Monitoring Behavior in Ease**  
Equally important is examining whether one becomes complacent or negligent during favorable conditions. Maintaining discipline and alertness in comfort prevents spiritual decline and reinforces consistent practice.
