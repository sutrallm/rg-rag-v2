**Title: True Progress in Spiritual Practice: Focus on Inner Transformation, Not Supernatural Powers**

**Summary:**  
The core of spiritual practice lies in reducing inner afflictions—greed, hatred, delusion—and cultivating wisdom, compassion, and inner peace. Supernatural abilities, while sometimes arising during practice, are not goals, benchmarks, or signs of genuine progress. Attachment to such powers diverts practitioners from the true path of liberation, potentially leading to delusion or spiritual harm. Real advancement is measured by internal changes: diminished烦恼 (disturbances), increased calm, clearer awareness, natural compassion, and ethical conduct. These qualities emerge organically through consistent practice, not through pursuit of external phenomena.

---

**Inner Transformation, Not Supernatural Powers, Defines True Progress**

The fundamental aim of spiritual practice is to eliminate ignorance and afflictions, leading to inner purity and liberation. Supernatural abilities may appear as incidental byproducts during practice but are never the target or measure of success. Fixation on such powers distracts from the essential work of dissolving greed, hatred, and delusion, and may even create new attachments that obstruct liberation.

---

**Genuine Results Manifest as Reduced Afflictions and Increased Calm**

True progress is evident in decreased emotional turbulence, greater mental stability, and a naturally peaceful mind. These internal shifts—such as reduced reactivity to external conditions, sustained equanimity in both favorable and adverse circumstances, and freedom from craving—are reliable indicators of advancement. They reflect the deepening of wisdom and the weakening of ego-driven impulses.

---

**Wisdom and Compassion Are the Core Markers of Achievement**

Growth in wisdom—seen as clearer perception of reality and insight into impermanence and non-self—directly correlates with spiritual progress. Compassion, which arises naturally as afflictions lessen, is not manufactured but flows from an unburdened, awakened heart. These qualities are inseparable from true practice and serve as authentic benchmarks, not external displays or mystical experiences.

---

**Ethical Conduct and Mindful Awareness Are Natural Outcomes**

As practice deepens, behavior becomes naturally aligned with ethical principles, marked by purity of action and presence of mind. This is not forced compliance but the spontaneous expression of an inner transformation. Mindfulness supports this by anchoring awareness in the present, preventing attachment to supernatural phenomena or external validation.

---

**Supernatural Abilities Are Not Only Irrelevant—They Are Potentially Harmful**

Pursuing or clinging to supernatural powers is repeatedly warned against as a deviation from the true path. Such pursuit can lead to spiritual confusion, arrogance, or even entanglement with harmful influences. Even if such abilities arise, they are impermanent, conditioned by karma, and ultimately irrelevant to the goal of liberation. True practitioners, including those who may possess such powers, remain unattached to them.

---

**Liberation Is the Ultimate Goal, Beyond All Phenomena**

The final aim of practice is complete freedom from suffering, achieved through the total eradication of ignorance and afflictions. This state—Nirvana—is not dependent on or defined by any supernatural ability or altered state. It is characterized by unshakable peace, non-attachment, and the absence of ego. Any focus on powers or experiences, no matter how extraordinary, distracts from this ultimate objective.

---

**Self-Assessment Through Internal Change Is the Only Valid Standard**

Progress must be evaluated internally: by observing whether greed, hatred, and delusion have lessened; whether the mind is more settled and aware; whether compassion and wisdom are growing. External signs,感应 (sensory experiences), or supernatural events are unreliable and misleading. Authentic practice is verified through personal experience, not external validation or public display.

---

**Cultivating Non-Attachment and Equanimity Is Essential**

Practitioners must avoid extremes and maintain balance, recognizing that all phenomena—including supernatural abilities—are impermanent and not self. This understanding, rooted in the insights of impermanence and non-self, prevents attachment to any experience, including those that appear extraordinary. True freedom arises from letting go, not from acquiring special powers.

---

**The Saintly State Is Marked by Inner Purity, Not External Power**

Those who have achieved profound realization may possess supernatural abilities, but they do not value or display them. Their true distinction lies in the complete cessation of afflictions, the stability of inner peace, and the natural expression of compassion and wisdom. The spiritual community honors such individuals not for their powers, but for their freedom from suffering and their embodiment of truth.

---

**Supernatural Phenomena Are Not Evidence of Achievement**

No amount of external signs, mystical experiences, or perceived powers can substitute for the internal transformation that defines genuine spiritual progress. These phenomena are neither necessary nor sufficient indicators of advancement. The only meaningful measure is the degree to which the practitioner’s mind is free from disturbance, rooted in clarity, and expressed through ethical, compassionate action.
