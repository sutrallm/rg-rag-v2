**Title: The Path to Liberation: How Practitioners Overcome Greed, Hatred, and Delusion**

**Summary**  
Practitioners aim to eliminate greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering and cyclic existence. These mental afflictions stem from attachment to self and possessions and manifest as persistent psychological obstacles. Through disciplined practice grounded in ethical conduct, mental focus, and wisdom—particularly via the Eightfold Path and meditative observation—practitioners cultivate awareness of impermanence, suffering, and non-self. This enables them to recognize, contain, and ultimately extinguish these afflictions. Consistent mindfulness and insight are essential to prevent recurrence and maintain inner stability, leading to complete liberation from suffering.

---

**Root Cause of Suffering Lies in Attachment to Self and Possessions**  
The mental afflictions practitioners seek to overcome arise from clinging to the notion of “I” and “mine.” These attachments manifest as greed, hatred, and delusion, which are not superficial distractions but fundamental barriers to progress. Their persistence directly fuels suffering and perpetuates the cycle of existence. Addressing them requires dismantling the underlying belief in a permanent, independent self.

**Methodology for Overcoming Afflictions Involves Ethical Discipline, Mental Focus, and Wisdom**  
Practitioners employ a structured framework: ethical conduct (sila), mental concentration (samadhi), and wisdom (panna). These are operationalized through the Eightfold Path—right view, intention, effort, speech, action, livelihood, mindfulness, and concentration. Together, they form an integrated system for observing the mind, recognizing the arising and passing of mental states, and cultivating clarity about the transient, unsatisfactory, and impersonal nature of experience.

**Continuous Observation of Mental and Physical Phenomena Is Essential**  
Through sustained meditation and mindful awareness, practitioners directly observe the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of the five aggregates (form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness). This direct experience allows them to see how afflictions arise and dissolve, enabling timely intervention before they take root. Without this vigilant observation, sensory experiences easily trigger attachment and aversion.

**Prevention and Immediate Response Are Key to Maintaining Inner Stability**  
The practitioner’s daily stance must be one of guarding the mind. When afflictions arise, immediate recognition and observation are required. Applying mindfulness and wisdom at the moment of emergence prevents escalation and reinforces the mind’s capacity to remain undisturbed. This proactive approach ensures that the mind remains anchored in clarity and does not drift into habitual reactive patterns.

**Ultimate Liberation Requires Complete Eradication of Greed, Hatred, and Delusion**  
The final goal is not mere suppression but total eradication of the three poisons. This is achieved through deep insight that dissolves both self-attachment and attachment to phenomena. As these root afflictions are extinguished, the mind becomes permanently calm, free from disturbance, and liberated from the cycle of suffering. The result is a state of unshakable peace and unrestricted freedom.
