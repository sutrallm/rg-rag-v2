**Title: The Path to Ultimate Liberation: Understanding the Three Gates of Emptiness Through the Practices of Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**  
This report outlines the essential conditions and experiential states required for ultimate liberation as described in the text. The core of liberation is achieved through the realization of three interrelated states: non-self (无我), formlessness (无相), and non-aspiration (无愿). These are not theoretical concepts but direct experiential realizations that must be personally verified. The state of non-abiding (无住) serves as both the method and the result — a mind free from attachment to any境 (state, object, or outcome), including meditative experiences, self-concept, or spiritual attainment. Liberation is not a static condition but an active, unconditioned freedom maintained through continuous non-attachment. The ultimate goal is the complete integration of these three states, which together constitute the final, unobstructed freedom of nirvana.

---

**Non-Abiding as the Core State of Liberation**

The mind that does not abide in any境 — whether meditative states, spiritual results, or self-identity — is the defining characteristic of true liberation. This state is not passive emptiness but active freedom: the mind remains unbound while fully engaged. It is achieved through consistent non-attachment to all phenomena, including the experience of joy or emptiness during meditation. The practitioner does not reject these experiences but does not cling to them. This non-abiding is the culmination of meditation practice and the hallmark of one who has severed all afflictions. It is the natural resting state of the mind once all clinging has ceased.

**Formlessness as the Liberation from Perceptual Fixation**

Liberation requires the direct realization that all phenomena lack inherent, fixed form. This is not a conceptual negation but a lived experience where the mind no longer perceives or reacts to things as having permanent, independent existence. This realization dismantles attachment to appearances — whether physical, mental, or spiritual — and allows the mind to remain clear and undisturbed. It is one of the three essential gates to liberation and is inseparable from the state of non-abiding. Without transcending form, the mind remains bound by perception and reaction.

**Non-Aspiration as the Liberation from Desire and Effort**

True freedom is attained when the mind no longer seeks, desires, or clings to any outcome — including spiritual progress or enlightenment itself. This state, known as non-aspiration, is not passivity but the complete absence of craving or aversion toward any境. It is the natural result of severing the root of self-attachment and the clinging to external conditions. It is one of the three essential gates to liberation and represents the final release from all forms of striving. In this state, action arises spontaneously without attachment to result, and the mind remains effortlessly at peace.

**The Three Gates as the Complete Realization of Liberation**

The realization of non-self, formlessness, and non-aspiration together constitute the complete and final state of liberation. Each gate addresses a different dimension of clinging: non-self dissolves the illusion of a permanent “I,” formlessness dissolves attachment to appearances, and non-aspiration dissolves attachment to outcomes or desires. These are not sequential stages but interdependent realizations that must be fully integrated. The practitioner who has realized all three is said to have attained ultimate nirvana — a state of complete freedom, unobstructed awareness, and spontaneous action without attachment.

**Wisdom as the Means to Realize Formlessness and Non-Aspiration**

Wisdom is the faculty that directly realizes formlessness and non-aspiration. It is not intellectual understanding but direct perception that all phenomena are empty of inherent existence and that all striving is ultimately futile. This wisdom arises through consistent practice of non-attachment and is the necessary condition for entering the state of liberation. Without this wisdom, meditation and ethical conduct alone cannot lead to final freedom.

**The Role of Non-Self Practice in Achieving Non-Abiding**

The practice of observing the absence of a permanent self leads naturally to the state of non-abiding. When the mind no longer clings to the notion of “I” or “mine,” it ceases to grasp at internal or external phenomena. This practice dissolves the root of attachment and allows the mind to rest in its natural state — free, open, and unconditioned. It is not a technique to be forced but a realization that emerges when clinging is relinquished.

**Non-Abiding as the Essence of the Path to Enlightenment**

The path to enlightenment is defined by non-abiding — the refusal to cling to any teaching, method, or experience as ultimate. The practitioner does not reject the Dharma but does not become attached to it. The mind remains as open as space, unobstructed by concepts or achievements. This is the highest expression of wisdom and compassion: acting without attachment, teaching without ownership, and living without clinging. It is the state in which liberation is not sought but naturally present.
