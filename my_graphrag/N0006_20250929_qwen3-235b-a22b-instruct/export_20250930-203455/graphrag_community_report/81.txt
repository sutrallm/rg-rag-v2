**Title: Progress Metrics for Practitioners: Focus on Inner Transformation**

**Summary:**  
Practitioners evaluate their advancement not through external markers but through measurable internal shifts. Key indicators include the reduction of greed and anger, the cultivation of compassion, heightened awareness, and diminished attachment. These inner changes constitute the primary and authentic measure of progress in practice.

**Inner Shifts Define Advancement**  
Progress is assessed by observable changes in mental and emotional patterns. Specifically, practitioners look for decreases in greed and anger, increases in compassion, greater clarity of awareness, and reduced clinging to outcomes or identities. These are not abstract ideals but concrete benchmarks used to gauge personal development.

**Core Standard: Transformation of Mental Habits**  
The central criterion for evaluating growth is the transformation of habitual mental tendencies. This includes the diminishing of reactive emotions like greed and anger, alongside the active nurturing of positive qualities such as compassion. This standard prioritizes internal realignment over external achievements or rituals.

**Authentic Progress Lies in Behavioral Change**  
True advancement is not measured by external milestones but by the tangible evolution of one’s conduct and emotional responses. The emphasis is on consistent, observable shifts in how one relates to desires, aversions, and attachments — indicating that meaningful growth is rooted in daily lived experience.
