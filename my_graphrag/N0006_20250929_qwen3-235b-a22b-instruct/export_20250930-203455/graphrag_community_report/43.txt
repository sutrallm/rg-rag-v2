**Title: Lightness and Missteps in Meditation: Guidance for Practitioners on Avoiding Attachment to States**

**Summary:**  
Meditation practitioners risk straying from the path to liberation when they attach to experiences such as lightness, concentration, or perceived spiritual states. These attachments—whether to comfort,境界 (states), or supernatural abilities—obscure the core purpose of meditation: ending suffering through non-attachment and clear seeing. Lightness, while a beneficial and verifiable stage in practice, must not be mistaken for final attainment. True progress requires maintaining awareness without clinging, even when experiencing ease or stillness. Persistent mindfulness and the cultivation of wisdom, not the pursuit of pleasant states, are essential to avoid stagnation and ensure continued advancement.

---

**Attachment to Experiences Hinders Liberation**

When practitioners fixate on meditative states, sensations, or the feeling of lightness, they divert attention from the fundamental aim of meditation: dismantling delusion and ending suffering. Such attachment transforms temporary conditions into goals, blocking the emergence of wisdom and reinforcing the very mental patterns meditation seeks to dissolve. This misdirection prevents genuine insight and delays progress toward freedom.

---

**Lightness Is a Supportive Stage, Not the Goal**

Lightness arises naturally in early meditation as a sign of mental and physical relaxation. It supports deeper concentration and prepares the ground for wisdom to emerge. However, mistaking this state for liberation or final achievement leads to stagnation. Lightness is transient and functional—it aids practice but must not be clung to. Its value lies in its role as a stepping stone, not as an endpoint.

---

**Pursuit of Supernatural Abilities Diverts from True Practice**

Seeking or valuing mystical powers as a measure of spiritual progress is a deviation from the path. These abilities, if pursued, distract from the essential work of understanding impermanence, non-self, and the nature of suffering. True practice is rooted in clear observation and letting go, not in acquiring extraordinary experiences or validating one’s progress through external or internal phenomena.

---

**Mindfulness Must Remain Unbroken, Even in Comfort**

Maintaining continuous awareness without grasping or rejecting experiences is critical. When lightness or other pleasant states arise, the practitioner must observe them without attachment or aversion. Any attempt to hold onto or enhance such states undermines mindfulness and weakens the capacity for insight. The practice must remain grounded in neutrality and presence, regardless of the quality of experience.

---

**Attachment to Any State, Including Lightness, Is a Form of Clinging**

The root of meditation missteps is attachment—even to states traditionally seen as positive. Whether it is clinging to comfort, stillness, or perceived spiritual progress, such attachment reinforces the illusion of a self that possesses or controls experience. To counter this, practitioners must cultivate awareness of impermanence and apply mindfulness consistently, ensuring that no state becomes a refuge or object of desire.

---

**Lightness Helps Overcome Obstacles, But Only When Not Clung To**

Lightness serves as a useful tool in overcoming common meditation hindrances such as dullness or restlessness. When experienced without attachment, it supports mental clarity and stability. However, when it becomes an object of desire or a measure of success, it turns into a hindrance itself. Its effectiveness depends entirely on the practitioner’s ability to remain unattached and focused on the deeper purpose of practice.

---

**The Core of Meditation Is Non-Attachment and Clear Seeing**

True meditation is defined by its commitment to observing reality as it is, without seeking or rejecting any experience. Whether in states of ease or discomfort, the practitioner must remain grounded in awareness, free from the impulse to grasp or push away. This non-attached observation is the only reliable path to dismantling ignorance and achieving liberation. Any deviation—whether toward pleasure, power, or perceived spiritual attainment—leads away from this goal.
