**Title: The Interdependent Path of戒、定、慧 in Achieving Liberation**

**Summary:**

The path to liberation is structured around three interdependent practices: ethical conduct (戒), mental stability (定), and wisdom (慧). Ethical conduct forms the foundational basis, enabling the development of mental stability. Mental stability, in turn, serves as the necessary condition for the arising of wisdom. Wisdom, as the culmination, directly leads to the complete eradication of ignorance and suffering, resulting in liberation. Liberation is characterized by inner freedom — an unshakable state untouched by external conditions, free from greed, hatred, and delusion. This entire process is grounded in the understanding of dependent origination, which reveals the impermanent, conditioned nature of all phenomena. Practices such as mindfulness of body, feelings, mind, and mental objects support each stage, reinforcing ethical conduct, stabilizing the mind, and cultivating wisdom. The Buddha’s teachings emphasize this sequential and integrated progression, with balance and right view guiding each step.

---

**Ethical Conduct as the Foundation**

Ethical conduct is the initial and essential basis for all further progress. Without purity in action and restraint of the senses, mental stability cannot be established. It prevents the arising of defilements and creates the necessary conditions for the mind to settle. This foundational role is consistent across all practitioners — whether beginners, diligent practitioners, or realized beings — and is indispensable for the subsequent development of concentration and wisdom.

**Mental Stability as the Bridge**

Mental stability is the pivotal stage connecting ethical conduct and wisdom. It is not an end in itself but a necessary condition for the emergence of insight. A stable mind is free from distraction and agitation, enabling clear observation of phenomena. This stability allows practitioners to see impermanence, suffering, and non-self, which are essential for wisdom to arise. Without this intermediate stage, wisdom cannot develop, and liberation remains unattainable.

**Wisdom as the Culmination**

Wisdom is the ultimate goal and the direct cause of liberation. It arises from a stable mind and is grounded in ethical conduct. Wisdom fully penetrates the true nature of reality — seeing all phenomena as impermanent, conditioned, and devoid of inherent self. This understanding severs the roots of ignorance, craving, and attachment, leading to the complete cessation of suffering. Wisdom is not merely intellectual knowledge but a direct, experiential realization that transforms the practitioner’s entire being.

**Liberation as the Result of Wisdom**

Liberation is the direct outcome of wisdom. It is not a state achieved through external means or rituals but through the complete eradication of ignorance and the defilements that stem from it. Liberation is characterized by inner freedom — a state of unshakable peace, untouched by external conditions, free from greed, hatred, and delusion. This freedom is not passive but active: the mind remains fully aware and responsive without being controlled by impulses or reactions.

**The Role of Dependent Origination**

The entire path is grounded in the understanding of dependent origination — the principle that all phenomena arise and cease due to causes and conditions. This understanding underlies ethical conduct, mental stability, and wisdom. It reveals the impermanent, conditioned nature of all experiences, thereby dismantling the illusion of permanence and self. This insight directly counters ignorance, the root of suffering, and enables the practitioner to break free from the cycle of rebirth.

**Mindfulness Practices as Practical Tools**

Mindfulness of body, feelings, mind, and mental objects is the concrete method through which ethical conduct, mental stability, and wisdom are cultivated. By observing the arising and passing of phenomena without attachment, practitioners develop the ability to remain present, avoid distraction, and see reality as it is. This practice supports the stabilization of the mind, reinforces ethical behavior, and directly leads to the arising of wisdom. It is also the means by which the Four Noble Truths are realized in direct experience.

**Inner Freedom as the Manifestation of Liberation**

Inner freedom is the lived expression of liberation. It is not a state of withdrawal or indifference but of full presence without reactivity. The liberated mind is not moved by pleasure or pain, praise or blame, gain or loss. It is free from the three poisons — greed, hatred, and delusion — and remains equanimous in all circumstances. This freedom is the result of perfected wisdom and is inseparable from the complete eradication of ignorance.

**Balance as a Guiding Principle**

The path requires balanced effort — neither excessive nor deficient. Just as a musical instrument must be tuned neither too tight nor too loose, the mind must be neither overly rigid nor lax. This balance supports the development of mental stability, prevents burnout or complacency, and creates the optimal conditions for wisdom to arise. It is an expression of the principle of dependent origination, recognizing that extremes lead to suffering, while the middle way leads to peace.

**The Interdependence of the Three Practices**

The three practices — ethical conduct, mental stability, and wisdom — are not sequential steps to be completed one after another, but interdependent dimensions that reinforce each other. Ethical conduct supports mental stability, which supports wisdom. Wisdom, in turn, deepens ethical conduct and stabilizes the mind. This dynamic interplay ensures that progress in one area naturally supports progress in the others, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that leads inevitably to liberation.
