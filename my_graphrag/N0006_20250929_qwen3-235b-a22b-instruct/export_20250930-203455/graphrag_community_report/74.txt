**Title: The Role of Vipassanā in Buddhist Practice: Key Functions and Outcomes**

**Summary**  
Vipassanā serves as the central method in Buddhist meditation, enabling practitioners to directly observe bodily and mental phenomena with mindfulness. Its consistent practice weakens afflictive mental states, preventing their unchecked spread. As Vipassanā deepens, it facilitates transcendence of conceptual thinking and dualistic perception, leading to direct realization of ultimate reality and the attainment of non-arising endurance.

**Core Function of Vipassanā**  
Vipassanā is the principal method in meditation practice. It involves sustained attention to present-moment bodily and mental phenomena, observing them as they truly are without distortion or interpretation. This direct observation forms the foundation of the practice.

**Impact on Mental Afflictions**  
Without regular engagement in Vipassanā, afflictive mental states grow unchecked, spreading like wildfire. When practiced consistently, Vipassanā actively dismantles the power of these afflictions, reducing their influence and preventing their proliferation.

**Progressive Transformation Through Deepening Practice**  
As Vipassanā matures, practitioners move beyond conceptual frameworks and dualistic distinctions. This shift allows direct access to the nature of reality, culminating in the realization of non-arising endurance — a stable, unshakable understanding of phenomena as they are, beyond conditioned existence.
