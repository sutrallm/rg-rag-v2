**Title: Seven Factors of Enlightenment in Practice: Path to Nirvana**

**Summary:**  
The practice of the seven factors of enlightenment directly leads to the realization of Nirvana. Key factors — discernment of truth, joyful progress, equanimity, and physical ease — actively support the development of wisdom. These elements are cultivated through meditation and are essential for sustained spiritual advancement. Each factor addresses a specific mental or emotional condition necessary for liberation.

**Discernment of Truth Guides Correct Practice**  
The practitioner cultivates the ability to distinguish true teachings from false ones using wisdom. This discernment ensures alignment with the correct path and prevents deviation. It is a foundational element that informs all other practices.

**Joy Sustains Momentum in Practice**  
Joy arises naturally from progress in meditation or from the experience of inner peace. This emotional upliftment encourages continued effort and prevents discouragement. It is not external happiness but an internal response to spiritual growth.

**Equanimity Enables Non-Attachment**  
Equanimity is cultivated as a state of balanced awareness — neither clinging to pleasant experiences nor rejecting unpleasant ones. This mental posture allows the practitioner to remain steady amid changing conditions, fostering deeper insight and freedom from fixation.

**Physical Ease Supports Mental Clarity**  
Lightness and comfort in body and mind are integral to the practice. This state reduces tension and distraction, creating optimal conditions for sustained attention and deeper meditative absorption.

**Wisdom Develops Through Integrated Practice**  
The factors of discernment, effort, joy, ease, and equanimity directly nourish the growth of wisdom. Their combined cultivation marks critical stages toward the full maturation of understanding, which is necessary for the ultimate goal.

**Meditation Is the Context for Cultivating All Seven Factors**  
All seven factors are developed within the framework of meditation. Their practice is not theoretical but experiential, rooted in direct observation and adjustment of mental states during seated or mindful practice.

**Direct Path to Nirvana**  
The systematic cultivation of the seven factors is presented as the direct method to attain Nirvana. No intermediary steps or external conditions are described as necessary — the practice itself, when correctly applied, leads to liberation.
