**Title**  
Ananda, Puṇḍara, and the Monastic Structure of the Buddha’s Circle  

**Executive Summary**  
The Buddhist community surrounding the Buddha displays a clear hierarchy of teacher‑disciple relations, functional roles, and geographic anchors. Ananda serves as the primary conduit of the Buddha’s teachings, acting as teacher, mediator, and supporter of fellow monks. Puṇḍara, a novice under Gu Shōshārīlīf, exemplifies the master‑servant dynamic and the chain of support extending from hand‑to‑hand. The collective assembly of monks concentrates their practice in specific forests and gardens, while the wider social sphere—illustrated by the figure “世界”—extends spiritual instruction beyond the immediate circle. Together, these elements reveal a tightly knit, multi‑layered community that prioritizes breath meditation, the Four Establishments, and the Seven Spiritual Faculties as core pedagogical pillars.  

**Key Observations**  

1. **Ananda’s Dual Role as Teacher and Alms Mediator**  
   Ananda attends the Buddha’s residence, receives teachings, and later facilitates the transfer of an alms bowl to the novice Puṇḍara. He also delivers comprehensive instruction on breath meditation, the Four Establishments, the Seven Spiritual Faculties, and liberation. Thus, Ananda functions both as a primary receiver of the Buddha’s doctrine and as the intermediary through whom the teachings are passed to other monks.

2. **Puṇḍara’s Master‑Servant Relationship with Gu Shōshārīlīf**  
   Being a novice, Puṇḍara serves Gu Shōshārīlīf, assisting with alms and attending the Buddha’s sermons. This relationship illustrates the hierarchical support structure within the monastic body, where experienced monks guide less seasoned attendants through daily practice and infrastructure.

3. **Support Network Among Attendants**  
   Puṇḍara’s delivery of an alms bowl to Ananda and the mutual mention of each other’s roles highlight a reciprocal aid system. Attendants support one another, ensuring the smooth functioning of alms distribution and the continuity of instruction.

4. **Geographic Anchors for Instruction and Practice**  
   Key sites include the Buddha’s residence, the tower where sermons are delivered, the forest of 一奢能伽羅, 祇樹林給孤獨園, and the village of Mōjépí‑Narah. These locations serve as physical markers for gathering, teaching, and meditation, reinforcing a spatial dimension to the community’s practices.

5. **Expansion of the Teacher Lineage Beyond the Immediate Circle**  
   The figure “世界” acts as a teacher and mentor to the disciple 55ac, offering instruction at the tower. This extension demonstrates an external layer of spiritual guidance that interacts with the Buddha’s internal community, thereby broadening the doctrinal reach.

6. **Emphasis on Breath Meditation and Progressive Stages**  
   Ananda repeatedly expounds on breath meditation (入口法禪) and its role in generating the Four Establishments. He also explains techniques such as 入出息念定, thereby stressing the importance of precise meditative practice as a foundation for the path to liberation.

7. **Integration of the Seven Spiritual Faculties in Practical Teaching**  
   By outlining the development of the Seven Spiritual Faculties through diligent practice, Ananda connects theoretical doctrine with actionable training, ensuring monks internalize these faculties as tangible skills.

8. **Collective Monk Body as a Structured Community**  
   The reference to 宗眾 (比丘眾) showcasing a collective body of individual monks indicates an organized monastic assembly where instruction, practice, and communal life are collectively coordinated.  

---

These findings detail how central figures facilitate doctrinal transmission, how hierarchical and supportive structures maintain community cohesion, and how specific practices and locations underpin the spiritual life of the Buddha’s monastic tradition.
