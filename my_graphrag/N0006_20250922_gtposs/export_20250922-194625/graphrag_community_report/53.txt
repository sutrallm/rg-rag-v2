**Title**  
Breath Meditation (入出息念定) and Its Role in Buddhist Spiritual Development  

**Executive Summary**  
The practice of consciously controlling breath in and out, known as 入出息念定, is identified as the fundamental technique for achieving the Four Establishments and the Seven Spiritual Faculties. It is also the means by which monks dissolve karmic bonds and worldly attachments, thereby severing the causal links that bind them. The monastic community, especially the bhikkhu class, routinely engages in this meditation. The renowned monk 阿難 specifically expounds on its method and benefits, highlighting its importance for spiritual progress.

**Key Findings**

*Foundational Practice for Core Spiritual Achievements*  
- The controlled inhalation and exhalation meditation is described as the primary practice that leads to the attainment of both the Four Establishments (The Four Foundations of Mindfulness) and the Seven Spiritual Faculties (such as mindfulness, energy, joy, tranquility, concentration, equanimity, and wisdom).  
- This establishes the technique as the cornerstone of the broader spiritual curriculum in the monastic setting.

*Mechanism for Dissolving Karmic Bonds*  
- The meditative focus on breath is linked directly to the dissolution of karmic bonds and worldly attachments.  
- By practicing 入出息念定, practitioners experience the dissolution (“斷結”) of the causal chain that sustains karmic conditioning, implying a profound shift in the underlying causal dynamics of their experience.

*Monastic Commitment to the Practice*  
- The term “比丘” refers to monks who regularly practice this specific breath meditation technique.  
- This regularity underscores the technique’s institutionalization within the monastic rule and its recognition as essential for spiritual development.

*Ānan’s Exposition on Technique and Benefit*  
- The senior monk 阿難 provides a detailed explanation of the method and its effects, indicating a pedagogical role that reinforces the meditation’s centrality in monastic training.  
- His teachings serve as a conduit for disseminating the practice among new and experienced monks alike.

*Congruence Between Practice and Result*  
- The phrase “修習入出息念定帶來斷結，即斷除因緣與結” explicitly links the practice to the removal of causal conditions and bonds.  
- This reinforces the direct cause‑effect relationship claimed in the text, demonstrating that disciplined breath meditation produces tangible transformation within the practitioner’s karma and attachment structure.

These observations collectively highlight how breath meditation is not merely an adjunct practice but is foundational to the spiritual architecture and the transformative objectives of the Buddhist monastic tradition.
