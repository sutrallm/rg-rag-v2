**Title**  
六器 Foundations: Naming, Form, and Contact in Buddha's Teaching  

**Summary**  
Buddha’s description of the six sense bases (六器) highlights a tightly interwoven structure in which naming (名) and form (色) together condition each sense location. Consciousness (識) is identified as the key conditioning factor for naming and form. Together, these six places serve as the groundwork for sensory contact (觸). The synthesis of naming and form not only shapes each sense base but also delineates the six sensory locations (六處), underscoring the integral role of these conditions in the experience of contact.

---  

**Foundational Structure of the Six Sense Bases**  
The six sense bases are listed as: body, sense‑motives, perception, feeling, consciousness, and mental activities. Each represents a distinct yet interrelated aspect of bodily or mental experience, collectively offering a comprehensive map of how consciousness operates.

**Conditions Set by Naming and Form**  
Naming (名) and form (色) are presented as conditions that define and shape the six locations. Their joint application determines the specific characteristics of each sense base, guiding how experiences are designated and experienced.

**Consciousness as a Conditioning Factor**  
Consciousness (識) is singled out as a conditioning influence on both naming and form. This suggests that without consciousness, naming and form would lack a cohesive structure, illustrating the centrality of conscious awareness in constructing meaning.

**Six Places as the Base for Contact**  
The six sense locations together constitute the foundation for sensory contact (觸). This establishes contact as an emergent property that arises from the proper functioning of each sense base under the influence of naming, form, and consciousness.

**Dual Role of Naming and Form in Sensory Localization**  
The combined action of naming and form not only governs the six sense bases but also results in the six sensory locations (六處). This dual role indicates that the processes that define and label experiences simultaneously give rise to the specific sites where contact can occur.
