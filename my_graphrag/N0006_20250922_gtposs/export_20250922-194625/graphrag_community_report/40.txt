**Title**  
Sensory Foundations and Impermanence in the Five‑Element Framework  

**Summary**  
The passage outlines how five sensory phenomena—taste (味), law or teaching (法), fragrance (香), sound (聲), and contact (觸)—are viewed as inherently transient and capable of producing aversion when perceived unfavorably. These phenomena are linked to foundational taste roots and are integral to a fundamental life‑generating process (生之法). Six sensory bases (六器) are identified—body, sense motives, perception, feeling, consciousness, and mental activities—which together form the infrastructure for contact. Naming and form (名色) produce these six sensory places, and consciousness (意識) conditions naming and form, reinforcing the cyclical nature of sensory experience. This conceptual framework emphasizes impermanence across all sensory interactions, underscoring the need for mindful engagement in contexts where sensory influence matters.  

**Taste (味) and Its Impermanent Nature**  
Taste is described as a sensory experience that is transient and can trigger discomfort or aversion when undesirable. It is connected to the five foundational taste roots and considered a fundamental component shaping life’s path.  

**Law of Causation (法) and Impermanence**  
The character 法 represents “law” or teachings and is seen as an expression of causal relations. It is also regarded as an impermanent phenomenon that, when perceived in unfavorable or unskillful ways, becomes a source of aversion.  

**Fragrance (香) as a Mutable Sensory Object**  
香 denotes a sense of fragrance or smell. Like other senses, it is inherently impermanent and can elicit aversion if the scent is bad. It is part of life experience and emphasizes the immediate, mutable nature of sensory perception.  

**Sound (聲) and Transient Perception**  
聲, the sensory element of sound, shares the impermanent quality with other sensory objects. Its unfavourable perception can evoke aversion.  

**Contact (觸) as a Core Sensory Process**  
觸 is defined as the sensation arising from body contact and is considered a part of 生之法. Contact enables the reception of experience and, together with naming and form, contributes to the formation of sensory objects.  

**Six Fundamental Sense Bases (六器)**  
The six places – body, sense motives, perception, feeling, consciousness, and mental activities – are identified as the primary locations or sense bases forming the structural foundation for all sensory processes.  
- Body, ear, nose, and mind are listed as components of 生之法.  
- Eye recognition (眼識) and eye contact (眼触) are highlighted as essential visual mechanisms within this framework.  

**Naming and Form (名色) Generating Six Sensory Places**  
The act of naming and giving form is said to produce the six sensory locations, indicating a cognitive and linguistic component in the construction of sensory experience.  

**Consciousness (意識) as Regulator of Naming and Form**  
意識 is portrayed as an active recognition process created by the mind. It is a vital part of life’s energy, classified within the six senses as “informed sense” and is intimately connected with naming and form.  

**Eye Recognition (眼識) and Color Interaction**  
眼識 is depicted as the visual identification process that relies on the interaction between vision and color. It is considered a part of the life‑generating process and underscores the centrality of vision in sensory recognition.  

**Body–Sense Integration**  
Each sense organ (eye, ear, nose, body, mind) is described as a component of 生之法, illustrating that sensory reception and bodily contact collectively constitute the conditions for life.  

**Interwoven Nature of Sensory Phenomena in Life Conditions**  
All sensory phenomena—taste, law, fragrance, sound, and contact—are linked through their impermanent character. They collectively illustrate a broader doctrine that positions sensory experience as mutable, aversive in unskillful perception, and fundamental to the formation of life’s regulatory mechanisms.
