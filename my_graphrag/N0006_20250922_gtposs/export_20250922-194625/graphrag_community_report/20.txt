**Title**  
Monastic Discipline of the Four Right Efforts and Their Practical Expressions  

---

### Executive Summary  
The Buddhist monastic community follows a clearly defined path of practice known as the Four Right Efforts. Each effort targets a specific stage of unwholesome and wholesome actions, enabling monks to eradicate impediments (the five upper hindrances) and progress toward Nirvana. Central to this discipline is the active cultivation (修習) performed by each monk (比丘) and the complementary actions—expanding diligence (發勤), mind cultivation (策心), persistent effort (精進), and aspirational intention (起志欲). The practice is taught by revered teachers such as 世尊 and Wu Bi En, and is performed collectively by the monk body (諸比丘). The path is described with evocative metaphors including the flow of a river (恒河) and a journey toward the east, symbolizing continuous purification and a forward movement toward enlightenment.

---

### Four Right Effort Components  
Monks engage in four sequential activities:  
1. **Prevention of potential unwholesome actions** – guarding against harms that have yet to arise.  
2. **Disruption of existing unwholesome actions** – breaking habits that are already present.  
3. **Cultivation of new wholesome actions** – fostering beneficial behaviors that have not yet manifested.  
4. **Sustenance of existing wholesome actions** – preserving and strengthening actions that are already cultivated.  

Each component addresses a different segment of moral development—prevention, corrective action, creation, and reinforcement.

---

### Monastic Practice Activities  
Beyond the four right efforts, monks perform four foundational actions that reinforce the training:  
- **Increasing effort (發勤)** – actively intensifying practice.  
- **Cultivating focused mind (策心)** – ensuring concentration during effort.  
- **Diligent exertion (精進)** – committing sustained, hardworking engagement.  
- **Aspirational intention (起志欲)** – setting clear, purposeful goals for spiritual progress.  

These actions are embedded within every attempt at one of the four right efforts.

---

### Collective Monastic Engagement  
Monks (比丘) practice 修習 individually and in unified groups (諸比丘). The communal aspect strengthens accountability, provides mutual encouragement, and establishes a shared framework for progressing through the four right efforts together toward enlightenment.

---

### Ultimate Objective: Liberation to Nirvana  
The Four Right Efforts are portrayed as the definitive path leading monks toward the ultimate goal of Nirvana (涅槃). Each effort aligns with the strategic removal of the five upper hindrances, thereby clearing the way for complete liberation.

---

### Metaphorical Illustrations Guiding Practice  
The practice is enriched with symbolic descriptions:  
- **Eastward direction (東)** – representing movement toward enlightenment.  
- **Flowing river (恒河)** – depicting continuous effort, unbroken by obstacles.  
- **Descriptions in the “瀑流品”** – elaborating on streams of contemplation and the gradual emergence of clear insight.  

These images help internalize the concepts and provide a visual map of the journey.

---

### Engagement with the Five Upper Hindrances  
The Four Right Efforts confront and overcome the five upper hindrances: lust, ill-will, confusion, restlessness, and a sense of superiority. By applying prevention, disruption, cultivation, and sustenance strategies, monks systematically remove these hindrances from their consciousness.

---

### Instructional Transmission by Esteemed Teachers  
- **世尊** emphasizes the technique and benefits of the Four Right Efforts.  
- **悟比恩** (also called 四正禪) delivers specialized teachings for the practitioners.  

Their guidance authenticates the practice and provides doctrinal authority for adherence within the monastic community.
