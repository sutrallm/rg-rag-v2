**Title**  
Section Eight Meditation Themes for 比丘: Sequence, Themes, and Concluding Hymn  

**Summary**  
The text outlines a structured practice for monks (比丘) that centers on ten meditation themes organized into a specific order. Each theme is delivered as a contemplative focus (“想”) directed at monastic listeners. The practice culminates in a concluding hymn that recaps all themes, emphasizing the primacy of the extinction‑thought practice (滅). The arrangement reflects an intentional pedagogical design: starting with basic defilements and moving toward deeper realizations, culminating in the cessation of desire and attachment.  

**Themes and Their Placement**  

1. **Contemplation of Impurity (不淨想)**  
   – First theme; introduces the negative mental state foundational for further reflection.  

2. **Contemplation of Death (死想)**  
   – Second theme; establishes mortality as a central meditation subject.  

3. **Contemplation of Disobedience (食違逆想)**  
   – Third theme; addresses moral violations that impede progress.  

4. **Contemplation of “Cannot Enjoy” (不可樂想)**  
   – Fourth theme; highlights how worldly pleasures conflict with spiritual practice.  

5. **Contemplation of Impermanence (無常想)**  
   – Fifth theme; underscores the transient nature of experiences.  

6. **Contemplation of Suffering and Impermanence (無常苦想)**  
   – Sixth theme; links impermanence with the arising of suffering.  

7. **Contemplation of Suffering and Non‑self (苦無我想)**  
   – Seventh theme; connects suffering to the absence of a permanent self.  

8. **Contemplation of Cessation (斷想)**  
   – Eighth theme; focuses on halting defilement.  

9. **Contemplation of Detachment from Desire (離貪想)**  
   – Ninth theme; addresses the removal of craving.  

10. **Contemplation of Extinction (滅想)**  
    – Tenth theme; considered the core practice of Section 八 and represents the final cessation of suffering.  

**Monastic Target of the Practice**  
All contemplations are framed as "比丘 engage in…" indicating that the meditation instructions are specifically directed at monks, reinforcing their role as practitioners of these themes.  

**Concluding Hymn (攝頌)**  
The hymn called “攝頌” appears at the end of Section 八 and serves to summarize the ten meditation themes. Its placement as the concluding element signals that the thematic sequence is meant to be remembered collectively, reinforcing the interconnectedness of the practices.  

**Relationship Between Themes and Meditations**  
Each theme (e.g., 不淨, 死, 違逆) is explicitly paired with its corresponding meditation term (不淨想, 死想, 食違逆想). This direct mapping ensures consistency between the conceptual focus and the meditative practice, guiding monks through a coherent contemplative process.  

**Central Role of Extinction Thoughts**  
The final theme, 滅想, is identified as the core practice of Section 八. Its designation as the tenth meditation and the final recurrent focus underlines its importance as the culmination of the contemplative path described in the text.
