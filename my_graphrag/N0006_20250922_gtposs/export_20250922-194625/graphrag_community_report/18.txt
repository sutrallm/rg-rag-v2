**Title**  
Five Aggregates, Impermanence, and Attachment: Buddhist Teaching Analysis  

**Summary**  
The passage examines the five aggregates—form (色), feeling (受), perception (想), mental formations (行), and consciousness (識)—and their roles in Buddhist doctrines of impermanence (無常), non‑self (無我), suffering (dukkha), and liberation. Each aggregate is shown to be impermanent, non‑self, and a source of suffering when attached to. Relationships among the aggregates are explicit: action (行) generates consciousness (識); consciousness conditions naming and form; form (色) engenders desire, craving, joy, and attachment. Texts such as 煻煨 and 攝頌 emphasize observing these aggregates together, cutting desire in the contexts of impermanence and non‑self, and interrogating the nature of each aggregate. The instructions of key teachers—世界, 世尊, 尊者羅睺羅—highlight the binding power of the aggregates and the necessity of their examination to progress toward liberation.  

**Key Observations**

**Core Properties of the Five Aggregates**  
- Every aggregate—色, 受, 想, 行, 識—is declared impermanent and non‑self.  
- Each is a potential source of suffering when a practitioner clings to it.  
- The first aggregate, color (色), represents tangible physical form and is the primary object of attachment.  

**Inter‑Aggregate Causation and Dependency**  
- Action (行) generates consciousness (識).  
- Consciousness conditions naming (名) and physical form (色).  
- Color (色) gives rise to emotional states such as love (欲), greed (貪), joy (喜), heart (心), and desire (愛).  
- Sensory objects (色, 想, 受) together demonstrate impermanence as part of the analysis of impermanence.  

**Teaching Emphasis on Observation and Cutting Desire**  
- The 煻煨 teachings instruct students (諸比丘, 善男子, 聖弟子) to observe each aggregate and to cut desire in relation to both impermanence and non‑self.  
- The practice of examining aggregates together is a recurring method across the texts.  
- Sections within the larger work allocate themes: 無常 appears as the fifth or fourth theme; 無我 as the seventh theme; both reside in section 八 of the *Second Book of Arhants*.  

**Boundaries Set by the Bindings of the Five Aggregates**  
- The binding force ('binding force that issues from the five aggregates') must be eliminated for liberation.  
- The concept of 有綱 is explained as arising from multiple aggregates—受, 想, 色, 行, 識—each reflecting a binding aspect of sensory objects.  

**Magical Manifestations and Negative Aspects**  
- The negative aspect of magic (魔) is linked to perception (想), form (色), mental formations (行), and consciousness (識).  
- These manifestations highlight how attachment to each aggregate can translate into negative mental and external conditions.  

**Aversion as a Diagnostic Indicator**  
- Cultures of aversion are noted: practitioners and priests (善男子, 聖弟子) exhibit aversion toward 色, 想, 行, and 識, indicating recognition of these aggregates as sources of suffering.  

**Cross‑Sectional Requirements for Liberation**  
- Both impermanence and non‑self are underscored in every section, reinforcing that observation, aversion, and cutting of desire in these contexts are essential practices.  
- The combination of these teachings culminates in the dissolution of the binding force and the attainment of liberation.
