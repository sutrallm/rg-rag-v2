**Title**  
比牙 Practice and Buddhist Teachings at Sarnath and Sravasti  

**Executive Summary**  
The text outlines the distinctive role of 比牙, a practitioner who may be both a resident monk (比丘) and a lay devotee (居士). 比牙 must observe strict precepts, cultivate mind‑cultivation techniques such as the Four Mindfulnesses and Seven Concentrations, and maintain vigilance against worldly attachments. Central to its practice is the systematic mastery of the five roots (五根), whose control leads to a pure, untainted state (清净) and the attainment of Arhatship. Renunciation (坤) and extinction (灭灭) form the core of the meditative discipline, while the Buddha’s instruction—delivered at venues such as the Sarnath garden and the city of 曹寺城—provides the doctrinal framework. Mara is portrayed as the personification of worldly temptation, illustrating the moral and spiritual challenges faced by the monastic community. The overarching aim of these practices is the cessation of suffering, culminating in the complete liberation of the self.  

**Key Monastic Identity of 比牙**  
比牙 combines the functions of a resident monk and a devoted layperson. Whether residing in a forest, temple, or temporary dwelling chosen by the Buddha, he adheres to the precepts, engages in study and discussion of the Dharma, and practices meditation centered on the Four Mindfulnesses and the Seven Concentrations. His dual status underscores the inclusive nature of the monastic path, allowing both full‑time and lay‑faithful practitioners to pursue the same objectives.  

**Centrality of the Five Roots**  
The survey repeatedly highlights the Five Roots—sensual desire, ignorance, attachment, aversion, and pride—as the primal obstacles to liberation. Proficiency over these roots is written as the prerequisite for attaining the state of Cleanliness (清净) and for progressing toward Arhatship (阿罗汉). The notion that mastery of the five roots opens the path to pre‑stream (预流) enlightenment and ultimate Nirvana is established.  

**Renunciation and Extinction Practices**  
Renunciation (坤) and extinction (灭灭) are presented as integral features of the meditation program. Tools such as 斨覺服 support the seven mind‑concentrations, particularly the renunciation component, by encouraging students to relinquish attachments. The text positions extinction as the final aim, describing it as the complete annihilation of suffering and defilements through disciplined practice.  

**Buddha’s Role as Teacher**  
The Buddha, referred to as 世界, functions as the anchor of doctrinal instruction. He addresses the collective of monks (诸比丘) and provides guidance on the Eightfold Path, the Four Right Efforts, and the four divine feet. His teachings take place in specific settings: the Sarnath garden, the city of 曹寺城, and the broader Sravasti region. In each venue, he delivers sermons, answers questions, and reinforces meditation techniques, establishing a continuous cycle of revelation and application.  

**Mara’s Influence**  
Mara is portrayed as the antagonist who lures monks toward worldly gain. By enticing them with wealth, patronage, and reputation, Mara fosters moral decline and obstructs spiritual progress. His presence serves as a reminder of the ongoing need for vigilance and renunciation among practitioners.  

**Collective Monastic Structures**  
The terminology distinguishes between individual monks (比丘, 比祈) and their collective body (比丘眾, 典比祈). These terms emphasize that while each monk practices individually, they participate in communal gatherings for instruction and practice. This collective dimension is important for understanding the social dynamics within the sangha.  

**Locations of Instruction and Practice**  
Key venues identified include:  
- Sarnath garden, where the Buddha dwells and teaches.  
- 曹寺城, a city that hosts the monks and serves as a deputy assembly point.  
- Sravasti (Sravaka City), another location where disciples listen, practice, and inhabit the teaching environment.  

These sites frame the geographical and cultural backdrop of the doctrinal transmission.  

**Goal of the Path**  
Each monastic practice—whether the Four Right Efforts (四正勤), the contemplation of extinction thoughts, or the meditation on the Five Roots—is geared toward a single objective: the eradication of suffering and the attainment of a liberated, pure, and irreconcilable state. Mastery of these disciplines leads the practitioner to the state of Nirvana (涂佛) and, ultimately, that of Arhat.  

**Conclusion**  
From the dual identity of 比牙 to the rigorous focus on the five roots, renunciation, and extinction, the text depicts a well‑structured monastic path supervised by the Buddha. The challenges posed by Mara, the support of communal gathering, and the guidance delivered across key locations collectively map a comprehensive road from disciplined practice to ultimate liberation.
