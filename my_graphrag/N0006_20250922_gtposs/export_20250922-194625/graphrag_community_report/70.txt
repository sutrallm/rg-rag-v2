**Title**  
Sumeru Pool and the Fourfold Army: Symbolic Layers in Buddhist Narrative  

**Executive Summary**  
- The Buddha’s sighting of the fourfold army entering reeds at the Sumeru Pool is deliberately framed as a metaphor for cause‑and‑effect teachings.  
- An ordinary male observer records the event, reflects on its chaotic nature, and later publicizes his experience, illustrating the transmission of insight from experience to community.  
- The narrative incorporates a celestial conflict in which heavenly beings triumph over Asuras, highlighting moral and cosmological themes.  
- Reeds serve as the tangible medium through which the army’s passage is described, grounding abstract concepts in physical detail.  

**Observational Dynamics and Didactic Metaphor**  
The Buddha witnesses the fourfold army infiltrating reeds at the Sumeru Pool and uses this imagery to craft a teaching about how causes produce effects. The scene functions as a concrete illustration that readers can visualize and relate to theoretical principles, thereby enhancing comprehension of karma and causation.  

**Human Witness and Communal Transmission**  
A male resident of the capital travels to the pool, observes the army’s entry, and later reports the event to a broader audience, describing the confusion it induces. This sequence demonstrates how personal experience is validated through description and subsequently shared, reinforcing communal learning and the spread of philosophical ideas.  

**Celestial Conflict as Moral Commentary**  
The text records a battle between heavenly beings and Asuras, with the heavens emerging victorious. This episode underscores themes of virtue prevailing over conflict and serves to contextualize earthly phenomena within a larger cosmological framework. The designation of a teacher as “無上正等雅者” further marks the authority and purity associated with heavenly realms.  

**Material Grounds of the Metaphor**  
Reeds situated along the Sumeru Pool’s shore provide a physical entry point for the army, allowing the metaphor to be anchored in a tangible element. The reeds’ role as the army’s chosen path emphasizes how physical objects can symbolize the flow of forces and the unfolding of events.  

**Public Declaration of Experience**  
After witnessing the army, the male observer approaches the populace and explains both the maddening aspect of the sight—“狂亂”—and the perception that the worldlessness he observed deviated from ordinary experience. This public discourse serves to align personal insight with communal understanding and to prompt reflection on the nature of worldly phenomena.
