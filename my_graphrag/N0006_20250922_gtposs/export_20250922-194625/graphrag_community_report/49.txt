**Title**  
Mapping the Eight Wrong Conduct Factors in Buddhist Teaching  

**Summary**  
The text presents a clear delineation of the eight elements that compose Wrong Conduct, each named in both English and Chinese. The factors—Wrong Livelihood, Wrong Concentration, Wrong Mindfulness, Wrong Intention, Wrong Action, Wrong Effort, Wrong View, and Wrong Speech—are described as the antithesis of the Eightfold Path. The Chinese terms (邪命, 邪定, 邪念, 邵思惟, 邵業, 邵精進, 邵見, 邵語) directly correspond to their English counterparts, indicating a parallel structure across linguistic traditions.

**Key Findings**

*Components of Wrong Conduct*  
The eight listed factors represent distinct aspects of conduct that diverge from the recommended ethical and mental discipline. Each factor is presented as a separate dimension, underscoring the comprehensive nature of the concept of Wrong Conduct.

*Relationship with the Eightfold Path*  
Wrong Conduct is explicitly framed as the opposite of the Eightfold Path, suggesting that avoiding each of these eight factors is essential for following the path correctly. This inverse relationship highlights the centrality of these factors in ethical instruction.

*Bilingual Equivalence*  
The text pairs each English term with a Chinese equivalent, demonstrating consistent terminology across languages. This parallelism can aid cross-cultural study and translation of teachings on moral conduct.

*Oppositional Nature of Wrong Conduct*  
The repeated use of the term “opposite” (邪行 is opposite of 正行) reinforces the notion that Wrong Conduct is not merely a set of negative behaviors but a systematic counterpart to the path of virtue. This framing emphasizes the moral urgency of steering away from the eight negative factors.
