**Title**  
Indra’s Mediation in the Hall of Right Dharma: Devas, Yaksha, and Asura Interactions  

**Summary**  
Indra (also referred to as 天帝釋) acts as a central mediator between the heavenly beings, a satiety‑named yaksha, and an ailing Asura king. The narrative unfolds in the Hall of Right Dharma, where Indra forgives the mocking devas, delivers a verse, and employs rhetoric that transforms a previously ugly yaksha into a beautiful form. Monks (比丘) observe this exchange, while the heavens (忉利之諸天) witness the act of forgiveness. The pattern demonstrates that Indra’s discourse not only restores harmony among celestial entities but also offers healing and transformation, signaling a model of conflict resolution and moral instruction.

**Key Domains**

**Central Mediator**  
- Indra visits the Buddha (世尊), the yaksha, and the Asura king, establishing direct communication with each group.  
- He relocates the devas to the Hall of Right Dharma, the same location where he later forgives them, showing a deliberate venue for reconciliation and instruction.  

**Transformation Through Rhetoric**  
- The yaksha, described as small and ugly, is mocked by the devas.  
- Indra’s rhetoric turns the yaksha into a beautiful form, illustrating the power of speech to alter perception and status.  

**Healing Interaction**  
- The Asura king, ill and in need of cure, is personally visited by Indra, who offers healing and advice.  
- This encounter underscores Indra’s role as a healer, not only of reputational wounds but also physical ailments.  

**Observation by Monks**  
- Dedicated monks (比丘) listen to, observe, and attend the Hall of Right Dharma, highlighting a layer of spiritual oversight.  
- Their presence indicates that the proceedings produce teachings relevant to the monastic community.  

**Forgiveness and Fellowship**  
- Devas mock the yaksha but later seek forgiveness in the Hall of Right Dharma.  
- Indra confronts these devas, then forgives them, demonstrating a cycle of blame, repentance, and reconciliation.  

**Audience and Setting**  
- The Hall of Right Dharma becomes a focal point where heavens (忉利之諸天), devas, yaksha, and Asura converge, reinforcing a shared space for moral adjudication.  
- The repeated mention of Indra’s residence and activities within this hall underlines its significance as a locus of authority and transformation.  

These observations collectively provide a detailed map of Indra’s diplomatic, therapeutic, and moral influence among celestial and semi‑divine figures, offering a framework that could guide broader strategies of conflict resolution, healing practices, and instructional discourse.
