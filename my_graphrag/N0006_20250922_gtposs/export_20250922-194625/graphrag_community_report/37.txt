**Title:**  
HAIPIN Chapters 2 and 3: Ocean Metaphor, Sensory Emphasis, and Monastic Practice  

**Summary**  
The second and third chapters of *HAIPIN* both employ the ocean as a central metaphor to convey Buddhist doctrine. Chapter 2 focuses on sensory perception through this imagery, while Chapter 3 explores the dynamic flows of the sea and how monks perceive them. In both sections, the teachings originate from Buddha and are transmitted to monks, who receive and practise them. These elements together form a coherent pedagogical structure that links metaphor, source, and practitioner.  

**Ocean‑Based Symbolism**  
Both Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 present the ocean as a metaphor for the teachings. Chapter 2 uses this image primarily to illustrate sensory perception, while Chapter 3 expands the metaphor to emphasize the various flows within the sea and how monks experience these dynamics. The recurring use of salt‑water imagery provides a unified framework for expressing complex doctrinal ideas in terms that are easy to grasp.  

**Doctrinal Source**  
The doctrines referenced in the two chapters are identified as derived from Buddha. This establishes an authoritative foundation for the teachings and situates the work within the broader context of Buddhist scripture. By explicitly naming Buddha as the source, the text reinforces the credibility of the instruction offered.  

**Monastic Reception and Practice**  
Monks are portrayed as the recipients and practitioners of the teachings from a teacher. The narrative underscores the community aspect of Buddhist learning: the teacher imparts doctrine, monks receive it, and they engage in practice. This emphasis on communal learning highlights the practical application of the outlined sensitivities and oceanic metaphors.
