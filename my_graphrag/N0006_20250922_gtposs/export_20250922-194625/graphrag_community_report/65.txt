**Title**  
Buddha’s Doctrines on the Four Divine Feet, Pre‑Stream State, and Arhatship in the Shakya Realm  

**Executive Summary**  
The text charts the Buddha’s systematic instruction to monks and lay followers across multiple Shakya cities—Kapilavastu, Shravasti, Parana, Sivaka, and Nigra Garden—emphasizing the Four Divine Feet as progressive meditative ladders toward liberation. The Buddha repeatedly assigns monks to sit in the pillar‑tower, where he delivers the Pre‑Stream Book, a compilation of teachings that outline the Four Laws (impermanence, suffering, no‑self, and the process of eliminating desire, greed, ignorance, and hatred). The doctrine connects mastery of these stages with the attainment of the pre‑stream (stream‑enterer) and, ultimately, Arhatship. Key disciples such as Mahākassapa, Sāriputta, 具壽羅陀, and a lay follower, 優婆塞達摩提那, receive personalized explanations of the nature of phenomena, the arhat, and the pre‑stream state. The narrative repeatedly stresses that ignorance, greed, hatred, and desire arise from the Five Aggregates and must be cut by practitioners in order to achieve liberation.

---

### Four Divine Feet as Progressive Milestones  
The Buddha’s teaching enumerates four distinct stages—勤 (diligence), 欲 (desire), 心 (mind), and 觀 (contemplation)—each representing a level of mastery in śamatha practice. Each stage is described as a “divine foot” (神足) that practitioners acquire through focused meditation, culminating in the completion of the Four Laws.

### Pre‑Stream Book as a Curriculum  
The Pre‑Stream Book is organized into numbered sections that systematically cover the nature of the self, reality, and the Five Aggregates. It serves as the core curriculum for monks attending the pillar‑tower, reinforcing the progression toward the pre‑stream state.

### Monastic Study of the Five Aggregates  
Both monks (*砂壤*) and Brahmins (*稻竹山*) are shown to study the Five Aggregates. Their lack of deep understanding at first, followed by gradual revelation, illustrates the necessity of direct insight into color, sensation, thought, conduct, and consciousness for liberation.

### Elimination of Desire, Greed, and Suffering  
The Buddha instructs monks to cut the “欲” and “貪” arising from the śkandhas. He explains that desire and greed are the primary binding factors for *眾生* (conditioned beings). The elimination of these afflictions, along with ignorance (*愚癡*) and hatred (*瞋恚*), is presented as the path to *所徧知* (absolute knowledge) and the attainment of Arhatship.

### Role of the Pillar‑Tower Setting  
The pillar‑tower is repeatedly cited as the focal point where the Buddha directs monks to sit and delivers discourse on the Four Divine Feet. Its physical presence signifies the integrated practice of meditation, study, and direct teaching necessary for progressive insight.

### Key Discipleship Narratives  
- **具壽羅陀** and **婆羅門** receive specific explanations of **魔**, **阿羅漢**, **預流**, **眾生**, **有綱**, **所徧知**, **五取蘊**, **欲貪**, and the Four Laws.  
- **Mahākassapa** and **Sāriputta** are noted as healthy companions of the Buddha within the text, signalling their role in early transmissions.  
- **優婆塞達摩提那** and **MAHA MAN** engage directly with the Buddha during illness discourses, exemplifying lay participation in the monastic instruction tradition.

### Geographic Dispersion of Teachings  
The Buddha’s residence and preaching occur in distinct Shakya locations:  
- Kapilavastu (city, rain shelter, Nigra Garden)  
- Shravasti (Suvā ward, rain shelter)  
- Parana (Lacune Garden)  
- Sivaka (city)  

Each site hosts specific disciples or lay followers who seek instruction, demonstrating the broad geographic reach of the Buddha’s doctrinal dissemination.

### Progression to Arhatship  
The text consistently links mastery of the Four Laws and the Four Divine Feet with the pre‑stream state, and the latter with eventual Arhatship. It notes that “沙門” who fully understand the gathering, extinguishing, taste, afflictive cause, and liberation of the six sense bases are referred to as *阿羅漢*.  

### Practical Methods for Illness and Relief  
Four relief/termination methods (*四蘇息法*) are discussed during the illness discourse, highlighting the Buddha’s integrated approach to health, doctrine, and practical guidance.  

### Conclusion  
The narrative presents a comprehensive framework where meditation, doctrinal study, and direct guidance converge to eliminate desire, greed, ignorance, and hatred. Mastery of the Four Divine Feet, studied within the Shakya tradition across multiple cities, prepares practitioners for the pre‑stream state and ultimately for Arhatship. The Buddha’s engagement with disciples, both monastic and lay, demonstrates a systematic curriculum that remains consistent across geographic settings.
