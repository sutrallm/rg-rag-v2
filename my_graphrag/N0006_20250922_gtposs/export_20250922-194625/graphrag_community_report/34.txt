**Title:  
Arhatship Attainment and Teaching Dynamics in the Buddhist Narrative**

**Summary**  
The text presents a series of encounters between the Buddha and several disciples, most notably 具壽羅陀, 具壽修羅陀, and others. Each disciple receives instruction on core Buddhist principles—such as the nature of desire (欲貪), forces (力), and conditioned existence (世生)—and in turn attains Arhatship. The narrative repeatedly emphasizes the requirements for becoming an Arhat: full realization of the teachings, eradication of desire and greed, and detailed understanding of the six senses and their extinction. The Buddha is portrayed as the primary teacher who imparts the necessary knowledge, after which disciples are recognized as fully enlightened saints (阿羅漢/阿佛罗哈).  

**Findings**  

*Definition and Role of 阿佛罗哈*  
- 阿佛罗哈 refers to a fully enlightened saint who has completely realized the Buddha’s teachings, including the impermanence, suffering, and non‑self.  
- This status denotes the final eradication of all afflictive cravings, enabling liberation from the cycle of birth and death.  
- Within the text, 阿佛罗哈 serves as a model for practitioners, illustrating how insight translated into action leads to Arhatship.  

*Path to Arhatship through Direct Instruction*  
- Disciples such as 具壽羅陀, 具壽修羅陀, and 具菽赫鼻 attend the Buddha’s teachings and ask specific questions about forces, the nature of desire, and the qualities of beings.  
- After receiving answers, each is recorded as attaining Arhatship at the conclusion of the narrative.  
- The process demonstrates that sincere inquiry followed by Buddha‑supplied instruction is sufficient for reaching the state of an Arhat.  

*Common Themes Among Disciples' Inquiries*  
- Recurrent questions focus on desire (欲貪), the nature of beings (眾生), and the conditions for true understanding.  
- The Buddha’s responses consistently emphasize the necessity of eliminating desire, grasping the six senses, and recognizing the extinction of suffering.  
- These themes underline the universal conditions required for liberation across different disciples.  

*Conditions for Recognizing an Arhat*  
- Full knowledge of the six senses, their extinction, and the qualities that lead to liberation.  
- Complete annihilation of desire and greed.  
- Establishment of a pure conduct (梵行).  
- When a disciple meets these conditions, the narrative treats them as an Arhat.  

*Repetition of Successful Attainment among Disciples*  
- The text records multiple disciples—具壽羅陀, 具壽修羅陀, and others—each meeting with the Buddha, receiving instruction, and ultimately being declared Arhat.  
- This pattern indicates that the Buddha’s teaching method consistently produces the same outcome for earnest seekers.  

These points highlight that the Buddha’s dialogues, the disciples’ earnest questions, and the clarified teachings together form a reliable framework for attaining complete liberation.
