**Title**  
Right Mindfulness Across Buddhist Practice Frameworks  

**Executive Summary**  
Right Mindfulness, translated as “正悟” or “正念”, serves as a foundational element in key Buddhist teachings. It appears explicitly within the Noble Eightfold Path and Right Conduct, and it functions as a conscious observation technique that encourages intentional non‑action. The practice is positioned as the root of meditation, guiding practitioners to eliminate distractions. Additionally, Right Mindfulness is employed in the conduct guidance of Right Law to preserve the precepts and to analyze the internal causes underlying each law. These roles underscore its importance as both a cognitive and ethical tool within the tradition.  

---

### Role in the Noble Eightfold Path  
The Noble Eightfold Path lists Right Mindfulness as one of its eight factors, signifying its status as a core component of the path toward liberation.

### Function within Right Conduct  
Right Conduct includes Right Mindfulness as an essential factor, linking mindful observation directly to ethical behavior and disciplined living.

### Foundation for Meditative Cleansing  
Meditation principles emphasize using Right Mindfulness to purge mental clutter, establishing it as the fundamental method for achieving a clear and focused mind.

### Tool for Upholding Precepts  
Guidance on Right Law stresses that Right Mindfulness supports the maintenance of precepts, showing how mindful awareness reinforces moral discipline.

### Basis for Precept Enforcement  
The conduct guidance of Right Law is grounded in the execution of precepts, indicating that Right Mindfulness not only observes but also enforces ethical norms.

### Emphasis on Inner Causes  
The conduct guidance highlights the internal causes of each law, implying that Right Mindfulness facilitates the recognition of these underlying factors and aids in their transformation.
