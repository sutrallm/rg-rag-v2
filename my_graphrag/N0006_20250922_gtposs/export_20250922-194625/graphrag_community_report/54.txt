**Title**  
Five Skandhas and the Path to Liberation: 受 ・ 色 ・ 行 ・ 想 ・ 识  

**Summary**  
The text outlines the nature of the five aggregates—feeling (受), form (色), action (行), thought (想), and consciousness (识)—and their roles in Buddhist practice. Each aggregate is characterized by impermanence and non‑self, and each is a seed of craving, attachment, and suffering. The teachings emphasize that attachment to these aggregates binds one to the cycle of rebirth, while detachment fosters liberation. Practitioners (善男子, 圣弟子, 诸比丘) cultivate aversion toward the aggregates, cut desire in relation to impermanence (无常) and self‑lessness (无我), and study the aggregates as part of the “煻煨” instruction. Demon (魔) narratives describe how negative manifestations of the aggregates ensnare the mind. The doctrine of “有綱” shows how feeling, form, thought, action, and consciousness give rise to binding phenomena. Overall, the report highlights the doctrinal framework that views all aggregates as impermanent, non‑self, and sources of suffering, guiding practitioners toward the cessation of attachment.

**Key Takeaways**  
- The five aggregates function simultaneously as the tangible experience of life and as instruments fostering attachment.  
- Buddhist practice centers on recognizing their impermanent and non‑self nature, thereby cultivating aversion to attachment.  
- The doctrine of “有綱” illustrates how feeling, form, thought, action, and consciousness intertwine to produce binding phenomena.  
- Liberation is achieved by eliminating the binding force generated by the aggregates and by severing desire linked to impermanence and self‑lessness.
