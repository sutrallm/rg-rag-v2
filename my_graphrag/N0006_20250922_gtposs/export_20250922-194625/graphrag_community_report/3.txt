**Title**  
Buddha’s Geographical‑Discursive Footprint: Locations, Themes, and Dialogues  

**Summary**  
The text records the Buddha (世界) residing and teaching in multiple sites: the cities of Sivaka, Shelavasti, Kapilavastu, Shravasti, and the town of 青田鎮, as well as gardens within the Paran (Parana) country—Nigra Garden and Lacune Garden. In each venue, the Buddha addressed monks, disciple 与着弾欤, and lay followers, most notably 具壽羅陀 and 優婆塞達摩提那, with core doctrines of impermanence, suffering, no‑self, and the Four Laws. A recurring dialogue with 具壽羅陀 covered a range of concepts—魔, 阿羅漢, 預流, 眾生, 有綱, 所徧知, 五取蘊, 欲貪—illustrating a systematic exploration of Buddhist teachings. Additionally, Venerable 釋氏摩訶男 sought medical and doctrinal guidance at a garden site. These patterns reveal a structured approach to teaching both monastic and lay audiences across multiple locales.  

**Observations**  

1. **Geographical Distribution of Buddha’s Abodes**  
   The Buddha’s residences cover a mix of urban centers and garden settings:  
   - Urban: Sivaka City, Shelavasti, Kapilavastu (Shakya country), Shravasti, 青田鎮.  
   - Garden Venues: Nigra Garden (intonance on illness), Lacune Garden (parate on the Four Laws), garden in Paran country (parāma‑lesson).  
   Each location provided a platform for different audiences and instructional focuses.  

2. **Central Doctrinal Themes**  
   Repeated teachings on impermanence, suffering, and no‑self highlight these as foundational pillars. The Four Laws surface explicitly during the parama‑lesson in Lacune Garden, suggesting a doctrinal milestone.  

3. **Structured Dialogue with 具壽羅陀**  
   具壽羅陀 initiates an extensive Q&A sequence: the Buddha explicates the nature of 魔, 阿羅漢, 預流, 眾生, 有綱, 所徧知, 五取蘊, and 欲貪. This breadth indicates a comprehensive curriculum tailored to a lay disciple’s inquiries.  

4. **Engagement of Lay Followers**  
   The lay follower 優婆塞達摩提那 repeatedly appears as an active recipient of teachings at Buddha’s residences, demonstrating active lay participation in doctrinal transmission.  

5. **Consultation on Health and Doctrine**  
   Venerable 釋氏摩訶男 seeks the Buddha’s advice on illness while simultaneously exploring doctrinal matters, illustrating a dual role of medical and philosophical guidance within the same setting.  

6. **Pilgrimage and Parama-Lesson Context**  
   The garden in Paran country attracts pilgrims and hosts the parama‑lesson on the Four Laws, indicating a nexus of pilgrimage, community learning, and doctrinal emphasis.  

7. **Continuity Across Shakya Territory**  
   The Buddha’s presence in the Shakya country, which includes Kapilavastu, signifies a sustained teaching presence within a single cultural domain, reinforcing foundational teachings while reaching wider audiences.  

These patterns collectively outline a systematic, multi‑site teaching strategy that blends monastic and lay instruction, doctrinal depth, and contextual relevance (health, pilgrimage) across the Buddha’s service domain.
