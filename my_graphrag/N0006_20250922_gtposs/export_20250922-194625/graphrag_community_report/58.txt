**Title**  
Four Right Efforts (四正勤) and Monastic Practice: Symbolic Journey Toward Nirvana  

**Executive Summary**  
The text outlines the disciplined practice of the Four Right Efforts (四正勤), a central method in Buddhist monastic discipline for overcoming the five upper hindrances. Monks (比丘) are instructed to engage in four distinct actions—preventing, breaking, initiating, and sustaining actions—each linked to specific utterances that cultivate diligence, concentration, aspiration, and resilience. Symbolic imagery of the Ganges River (恆河) flowing eastward into the Sea (海) is repeatedly employed to illustrate the gradual progress from aspiration to liberation (Nirvana). Together, these elements form a coherent framework that guides monks from contemplation to the attainment of ultimate freedom.

**Structural Composition**  
The practice is divided into four deliberate efforts:  
1. **Prevention** (first effort): Stops the emergence of unwholesome actions not yet arisen.  
2. **Breaking** (second effort): Undoes existing unwholesome actions that have already come into existence.  
3. **Initiation** (third effort): Brings forth wholesome actions that have not yet arisen.  
4. **Sustaining** (fourth effort): Maintains, remembers, and nurtures wholesome actions already present.  
Each effort is explicitly associated with a technical label (e.g., 已生之善法, 已生之惡不善法, 未生之善法, 未生之惡不善法), underscoring the dual focus on what is present and what must be cultivated or eliminated.

**Directional Symbolism**  
The compass point **East (東)** represents the desired destination of the practitioner’s spiritual journey. Both the metaphorical river and the chant orient toward this direction, signifying the path toward **Nirvana (涅槃)**. The river’s journey—its flow, direction, and eventual emptying into the **Sea (海)**—mirrors the monk’s progressive attainment of liberation. This triptych of direction (East), element (River), and goal (Sea/Nirvana) provides a vivid, concrete roadmap embedded in the practice.

**Behavioral Components**  
Monastic conduct is further sharpened through detailed action descriptors:  
- **防止**: Avoid unwholesome offerings that have yet to arise.  
- **破除**: Eliminate the unwholesome established.  
- **实施**: Establish wholesome practices yet to be brought forth.  
- **維繫**: Protect and reinforce the established wholesome practices.  
These components are applied consistently across instruction, deriving from the text’s emphasis on cultivating a disciplined mind and preventing the repetition of lower hindrances.

**Practice Actions**  
Specific devotional terms are mandatorily invoked to support the Four Right Efforts:  
- **發勤 (Act to increase effort)**  
- **策心 (Cultivate and keep the mind focused)**  
- **精進 (Diligent exertion)**  
- **起志欲 (Intention and aspiration)**  
Each term reinforces a particular quality essential for maintaining momentum toward the ultimate goal. Their repeated mention indicates an integrated curriculum that marries mental, behavioral, and intentional facets.

**Metaphoric Representation**  
Throughout the passage, the flowing river narrative anchors the abstract doctrine in concrete imagery. The **Ganges River (恆河)** embodies the natural, unforced progression of the monk’s practice. The direction toward the **Sea** grossly parallels the culmination in **Nirvana**—a state beyond worldly attachments. The textual recurrent motifs of “flow toward east, lean east” serve to remind practitioners that their journey is continuous, directional, and goal‑oriented, much like a river’s inexorable course toward the sea.

This compilation of textual elements equips decision‑makers with a clear, structured understanding of how the Four Right Efforts are taught, practiced, and symbolically contextualized, revealing both the procedural rigor and the visionary aesthetic embedded in monastic discipline.
