**Title**  
Four Relief Methods in the Illness Dialogue of Venerable Shishi Mahaman  

**Summary**  
Venerable Shishi Mahaman consulted the Buddha regarding illness and doctrinal matters. The dialogue, set in the Buddha’s garden, centers on the four relief/termination methods (四蘇息法) that form the core of the teaching. The exchange highlights how Buddhist practice addresses both physical suffering and spiritual development, offering a holistic path to health and liberation.

**Interpretations**

**Role of Venerable Shishi Mahaman**  
The title “Venerable” signals that Shishi Mahaman was a respected practitioner. His approach to the Buddha reflects a tradition in which advanced monks seek direct instruction on both doctrinal understanding and practical remedies for suffering. The dialogue demonstrates his aspiration to integrate teachings for personal well‑being and deeper insight.

**Setting: The Buddha’s Garden**  
The Buddha’s garden is the specific scene of the conversation. This location conveys a metaphorical and literal space where the Buddha’s presence invites contemplation, healing, and the cessation of dissatisfaction. The garden setting emphasizes that the Buddha offers guidance not only in formal teachings but also in intimate, life‑directed encounters.

**Four Relief/Termination Methods (四蘇息法)**  
These four methods constitute the central teaching of the dialogue. They represent the primary means by which the Buddha equips a practitioner to alleviate illness. The discussion underlines that Buddhist health instruction is organized around concrete practices that help to quiet suffering and progress toward liberation.

**Link Between Illness and Doctrine**  
The conversation intertwines immediate concerns about illness with broader doctrinal insights. This dual focus illustrates a Buddhist perspective where bodily challenges are understood in their spiritual context, and cure is pursued through both physical and metaphysical practices. The integration points to a holistic approach in which alleviating sickness is inseparable from learning and applying core teachings.
