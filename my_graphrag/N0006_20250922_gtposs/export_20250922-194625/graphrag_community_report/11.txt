**Title**  
Cities of the Buddha’s Late‑Era Teachings – Wangdu, Wangzuo, and Wangshe

**Executive Summary**  
The late Buddha’s ministry was anchored in several key urban sites that served as residences, discussion forums, lecture halls, and monastic centers.  
- Wangdu Cheng was the main venue where the Buddha met sages, conducted teachings, and issued supplementary instructions, especially after the renewal of the Mara vow.  
- Within Wangdai‑Bai, the Kun Jing Fu Platform functioned as a garden‑monastery where teaching, study, and debate took place.  
- Wangzuo City emerged as a long‑term teaching base, hosting the Buddha’s meetings with King Dìpō and remaining a focal point even after the Buddha moved to the “King Saha” site.  
- Wangshe City attracted many disciples, was home to natural landmarks that shaped encounters, and hosted the Buddha for numerous teachings.  
- Rōhoro, a devoted follower, illustrates the personal inquiry process of disciples, asking detailed questions about the senses and aggregates.

These insights reveal how geography, social interactions, and natural environments co‑ordinated to sustain the Buddha’s late‑era instructional practice.

---

### Wangdu Cheng as a Multifunctional Teaching Hub  
Wangdu Cheng, also named Vashyaka 城, Cosambi, or Rājagaha, functioned as a central nexus where the Buddha resided, preached, and debated with a wide circle of sages and disciples. The city housed walls, gardens, and a preaching hall that accommodated the continuous delivery of sutras, Dharma, and practice instructions. The Buddha’s post‑Mara renewal of vows took place against this backdrop, underscoring the city’s role as a permanent instructional base.

### Kun Jing Fu Platform as a Center for Study and Debate  
Located inside Wangdai‑Bai, the Kun Jing Fu Platform appears as a garden or monastic retreat. Referred to as the “world’s dwelling place,” it routinely hosted the Buddha’s encounters with various disciples and lay people, especially women of the aristocracy. The platform acted as a venue for sermons, lectures, reading, and debate, repeatedly recorded in local chronicles as a place where the “world” delivered sermons and chaired meetings.

### Wangzuo City: A Permanent Teaching Site After the Mara Event  
During a dialogue with King Dìpō, the Buddha was present in Wangzuo City. Following the departure of Mara, he relocated to King Saha’s city for additional teaching but maintained a sustained presence in Wangzuo. There, a monastery was established, and continuous instruction for monks was sustained, solidifying the city’s status as a long‑term teaching center for the Buddha’s disciples.

### Wangshe City: Frequent Visits, Natural Features, and Teaching  
Wangshe City, home to the Buddha’s mountain cave Mount Yagu, attracted frequent visits from disciples such as Aśoka and the monk Rōhoro. The city also encompasses natural landmarks: the Indra‑Peak situated near Wangshe, the Ling‑Jiu‐Mountain inside the city, and a forest area. These features provide context for encounters and meetings, while the Buddha delivered teachings in venues like the Pali term “world dwelling in Wangshe.” The city served both as the Buddha’s residence and a vibrant center of instruction.

### Rōhoro’s Inquiry on Sensory Processes and Aggregates  
Rōhoro, a devoted disciple, sought clarification from the Buddha regarding the nature of sensory processes, systematically questioning all senses and their qualities. This exchange illustrates the Buddha’s method of addressing specific doctrinal questions within his student‑teacher interactions and highlights the depth of epistemological exploration during the late period.
