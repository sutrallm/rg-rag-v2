**Five Roots: Foundations and Hierarchical Recognition in Buddhist Practice**

**Executive Summary**  
The discussion clarifies the centrality of the Five Roots (五根) as the foundational mental factors for practitioners and delineates how mastery of these roots determines recognition among the ranks of Brahmins, monks, and ascetics. It also specifies the individual roots, including an explicitly described root named “准确根,” and shows how separate groups—‘婆罗门’, ‘沙门’, ‘比丘’, and ‘诸具壽’—interact with the doctrine to set criteria for spiritual authority.

---

### Core Doctrine of Five Roots  
The Five Roots constitute the essential mental framework that a practitioner must cultivate. They are described as the core “heart fields” or foundational factors that shape a monk’s inner spiritual state. No practitioner can be considered complete without a deep grounding in each of these five elements.

### Two Variants of the Root Listing  
The passage presents two sets of names for these roots:
1. 信根, 轻根, 欲根, 迷根, 嗔根  
2. 信根, 精进根, 慧根, 定根, 慧根 (the repetition of 慧根 suggests a textual error).  
Both lists underscore that the roots can be enumerated differently but always maintain the same significance.

### Full Enumeration of Roots  
Beyond the paired lists, the passage adds several individual roots that together form the Five Roots:
- 乐根  
- 苦根  
- 喜根  
- 忧根  
- 舍根  
- 精进根  
- 念根  
- 定根  
- 慧根  
- 信根  
These components cover a broad range of mental states, from joy and suffering to willful effort and concentration.

### Root “准确根” and Its Functions  
“准确根” is identified as one of the five primary roots. It is linked to an “irregular or uneven hunting power” and is said to sustain breath and the force of “Ai Hua.” This root embodies a fluctuating force that nurtures cultivation, representing both the power of Ai Hua and the strength of disciplined practice.

### Pathway to Being Labeled a ‘婆罗门’
Brahmins (婆罗门) are recognized only when they possess a thorough understanding of all five roots. Those who lack this depth are not granted the title, underscoring the importance of root mastery for spiritual authority in this context.

### Pathway to Being Labeled a ‘沙门’
A ‘沙门’ is a higher‑ranked monk who has surpassed the level of a simple ‘比丘’. Recognition as a valid ‘沙门’ likewise requires a profound grasp of the Five Roots; without it, a monk cannot assume this elevated status.

### Role of ‘比丘’ in Root Study  
The ‘比丘’ (Zen practitioner) engages directly with the Five Roots through study and practice. Their work forms the baseline of spiritual training from which higher titles derive.

### Evaluation by ‘诸具壽’
The collective of ascetics or long‑lived monks, known as ‘诸具壽’, serves as an evaluative body. They assess candidates for both Brahmin and monk titles, judging whether an individual demonstrates the necessary depth in understanding the Five Roots before conferring the respective status.
