**Title**  
Buddhist Conception of World and Truth

**Executive Summary**  
The passage distinguishes the fleeting, unsatisfactory world from a permanent, enlightened reality. The Buddha, addressed as 世尊, actively teaches monks about the world’s nature and personally seeks the true reality through contemplation. The true reality is explicitly separated from the impermanent suffering inherent in the world. These points illuminate how Buddhist doctrine frames reality, the role of the teacher, and the monks’ spiritual objectives.

**Distinction Between Impermanent and True Reality**  
The text defines the worldly realm, “世界,” as enduring only in the sense that it constantly changes and causes dissatisfaction. In contrast, “真實” represents a stable, enlightened comprehension that Buddhism aims to reveal. The clear separation highlights that the ultimate reality is not subject to impermanence or suffering.

**Role of the Buddha in Conveying Reality**  
世尊 is portrayed as both an educator and seeker. He imparts the nature of the world to monks, guiding them through its characteristics, while simultaneously searching for the genuine reality through his own contemplation. This dual function underscores the Buddha’s responsibility in shaping doctrinal understanding and inspiring personal spiritual inquiry.

**Monastic Pursuit of Truth**  
The passage indicates that monks receive instruction on the world’s nature and, through contemplation, are directed toward recognizing true reality. This process shows that the monastic path involves learning about the worldly conditions and actively pursuing the enlightenment that transcends them.
