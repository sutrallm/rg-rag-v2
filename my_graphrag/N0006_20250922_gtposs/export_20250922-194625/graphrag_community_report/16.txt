**Title**  
Right Conduct Components of the Eightfold Path  

**Summary**  
The material describes Right Conduct (正行) as the practice of following the Eightfold Path. It lists the standard seven factors—Right Livelihood, Right Intention, Right Action, Right Effort, Right View, and Right Speech—and additionally includes Right Mindfulness and Right Concentration. The same factors are repeated in references to “八支聖道” and in the explicit inclusions under “正行,” emphasizing their essential role. The term “比丘” indicates that monks adhere to this conduct.  

---

### Component Listing Consistency  
The seven core factors of the Eightfold Path are repeatedly identified:  
- Right Livelihood (正命)  
- Right Intention (正思惟)  
- Right Action (正業)  
- Right Effort (正精進)  
- Right View (正見)  
- Right Speech (正語)  

These are cited both under “八支聖道” and in the “正行 includes the factor” statements, underscoring their fundamental status in the teaching.  

### Inclusion of Mindfulness and Concentration  
Right Mindfulness (正念) and Right Concentration (正定) appear only under “正行 includes the factor” listings. Their presence expands the conduct beyond the original seven, suggesting a broader interpretation that incorporates contemplative practices.  

### Repetition for Emphasis  
The repeated enumeration of each factor under “正行 includes the factor” serves to highlight each component individually. This repetition may function pedagogically, ensuring that practitioners remember and apply each aspect distinctly.  

### Monastic Observation  
The phrase “比丘 follow the right conduct” explicitly connects the practice to Buddhist monks, indicating that adherence to these factors is a hallmark of monastic discipline.  

### Unified Terminology  
Both the Chinese terms (“正行”, “八支聖道”) and the English translations (“Right Conduct”, “Eightfold Path”) are consistently used, reflecting a direct correspondence between linguistic representations and doctrinal content.
