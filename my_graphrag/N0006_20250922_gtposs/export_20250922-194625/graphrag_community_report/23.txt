**Title:  
Buddhist Monastic Practice and the Path to Arhat – Key Teachings from Canonical Texts**

---

### Executive Summary  
The canonical accounts depict a structured monastic culture where both ordained monks (比丘) and in‑house practitioners (比牙) observe strict precepts, meditate on the Four Mindfulnesss, and study the five roots of experience. Their teachings are delivered primarily by the Buddha (世尊) at sacred sites such as the Sarnath garden, the Sravasti city, and other courts. Central to their practice are the concepts of renunciation (坤, 斨覺服), extinction (灭, 滅), and the attainment of a pure state (清净) that culminates in Arhatship. Female monastics (比丘尼) participate alongside male monks in the same doctrinal framework, while advanced practitioners such as 沙门 and 婆罗门 are recognised only after profound mastery. These intertwined beliefs, practices, and settings shape the monks’ spiritual progression from pre‑stream to completion.

---

## Monastic Identity and Dual Role of 比牙  
比牙 serves as both a fully ordained monk and a household practitioner devoted to the Buddha’s teachings. Whether 烏成 or 常即居士, they listen, discuss, and practice the Dharma. They are regarded as 菩提众 alongside ordained monks, underscoring that the pursuit of liberation is shared across monastic and lay orientations.

## Core Practices and Attainment Goals  
- **Five Roots Mastery**: Observation and control of the sensory roots (pleasure, suffering, greed, illusion, etc.) leads to 彻净.  
- **Four Mindfulnesss (四念處)** and **Seven Mind Concentrations** are core meditation techniques integrated with renunciation and extinction.  
- **Eightfold Path** and **Four Right Efforts (四正勤)** form the behavioral framework.  
- **Extinction Training (灭灭, 滅)** focuses on annihilating attachment and achieving 完全 liberation.

Successful execution of these practices transitions a scholar from pre‑stream (预流) stage to Arhat (阿罗汉).

## Buddha’s Role and Teaching Settings  
- **Sarnath Garden**: The primary venue where 世尊 delivers sermons and answers questions from 比丘 and 比丘尼.  
- **曹寺城 (Sravasti)** and **菴羅園** host congregations where monks pursue instruction and reside during teaching periods.  
- **Discourse Themes**: Topics range from dragon mythic narratives (gestated, moist‑born, transmutation‑born) to elemental inquiries (egg, fire, resistance), illustrating moral lessons and doctrinal depth.

The Buddha frequently employs exemplars like 比牙 and creations such as Mara to illuminate doctrinal points.

## Renunciation and Extinction as Harbingers  
Renunciation is repeatedly highlighted through the term 坤 and through practices such as 斨覺服. These focus on relinquishing worldly attachments (wealth, reputation, patronage) and surrendering to the Dharma, facilitating the extinction of defilements (灭灭). The disciplined practice of renunciation is presented as the final turning point toward ultimate detachment.

## The Hierarchy of Monastic Advancement  
- **比丘**: The foundational monk engaging with the five roots.  
- **沙门** and **婆罗门**: Recognised only after profound understanding, evidencing a higher scholarly status.  
- **具壽** Group: Experienced monks who mentor novices (比丘) through discourse and instruction.

Such stratification underscores the importance of doctrinal depth before attaining higher titles.

## Female Monastic Participation  
比丘尼 hold the same central practices as their male counterparts, studying the Four Mindfulnesss and participating in sermons within spaces such as 舍衛城. Their inclusion reflects the equitable dissemination of the Dharma across genders.

## Thematic Emphasis of Dragon Allegories  
Monks inquire about dragons of various origins—gestated, moist‑born, transmutation‑born—during the Buddha’s teachings. These allegories serve to illustrate realization processes, the transformation of delusion into wisdom, and the containment of the mind’s untamed aspects.

## Collective Monastic Body and Individual Practice  
- **比丘众 / 典比祈** represent the collective community.  
- Individual monks (比丘) engage in daily **修習**, **修濟逸**, and **修滅想**, each anchoring the four right efforts.  
- The practice of breath meditation (**入出息念定**) and contemplation of various mental states (*不淨想*, *死想*, *食違逆想*, etc.) are routine methods to refine concentration and purify intention.

## The Role of the Mentor‑Disciple Relationship  
Experienced monks (具壽, 具壽差摩, 具壽闡陀, 具壽罗睺羅, etc.) conduct introductory and advanced talks with novices, supervising content and ensuring doctrinal fidelity. This mentorship safeguards the integrity of the teaching lineage.

## Ultimate Objective: No‑Self Awareness and Pure State  
Through discipline and the meticulous study of the five roots, a monk eventually reaches a state of 清净—a pure, unpolluted condition. Mastery of these roots effectively eliminates the self‑centered notions and produces the field necessary for culminating enlightenment.

---

**Key Takeaway**  
The Buddhist monastic tradition outlined here portrays a cohesive structure: a clarified set of duties, core meditation practices, clearly defined teaching environments, and a graded advancement system—all directed toward the ultimate goal of Arhatship through disciplined renunciation and extinction. This framework underscores the integration of doctrine, practice, and community life as essential ingredients for spiritual liberation.
