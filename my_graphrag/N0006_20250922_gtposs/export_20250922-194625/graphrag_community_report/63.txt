**Title**  
Waterfall Discourse Chant Analysis – Summary, Content and Thematic Connections  

**Summary**  
The chant “汰咽” and its companion “攝頌” serve as the final hymn of the Waterfall Discourse section. Their primary function is to synthesize the discourse’s core teachings—particularly the binding nature of craving, the application of right effort, and the practice of the Five Upper Divisions, Five Roots, and Four Right Diligences. The chants incorporate key doctrinal terms such as *妙欲* (refined desire), *无常*, *无我*, *苦*, and symbolic phrases like *瀑流* (waterfall) and *蓋* (cover). By embedding summary poems and evocative terminology, the chants provide performers with a concise, poetic reflection of the exposition and reinforce the path illustrated in chapter nine of the Buddhist scripture “Ganges Commentary.”  

---

### Role of the Concluding Hymn  
The chant appears at the end of the Waterfall Discourse section, functioning both as a summary of the teachings and as a collecting verse that reinforces and binds the message through melodic repetition. It encapsulates the chapter’s emphasis on how craving binds, why right effort is essential, and how both together lead toward liberation.  

### Dual Poetic Summary  
The chant contains two separate summary poems. The first emphasizes impermanence (*无常*) and non‑self (*无我*), while the second repeats the numerical pattern “二六一十二” to illustrate the structure of the discourse. These poems distill the four right diligences and the five upper divisions into accessible, rhythmic verses that can be easily reproduced.  

### Connection to Doctrinal Themes  
- **Refined Desires** – The chant references *妙欲* to highlight the nuanced form of craving discussed in the Waterfall Discourse.  
- **Binding Terminology** – Phrases such as *繫* (bind) and *軛取* (encumber) appear to stress the encarceration caused by craving.  
- **Sleep and Cover** – *隨眠* (following sleep) and *蓋* (cover) illustrate how craving blankets insight.  
- **Doctrine of Impermanence, Non‑Self, Suffering** – The chant explicitly invokes *无常*, *无我*, and *苦* to frame the human experience within the discourse’s philosophical grounding.  

### Structural and Linguistic Features  
The chant uses the key term *上下分* (upper and lower division) to denote the division of craving. It also incorporates *覆* (cover), *蘊* (accumulate), and *繫* to reinforce the mechanics of attachment. The textual structure mirrors that of the discourse, with compact, taut sentences that form a coherent whole and aid in memorization.  

### Alignment with Chapter Nine of “Ganges Commentary”  
Chapter nine explains the practice of the Seven Mind Concentrations and detachment through a river metaphor. The chants sit within this chapter, reciting the same central topics—habitual craving, right effort, and the path to Nirvana—while providing a condensed poetic summary that prisoners the river theme.  

### Use of Root Correspondence (根相應)  
The chants reference *根相應*, a method for explaining how various roots relate to one another. By echoing *根相應*, the chants align the discourse’s analytic framework with the conditioned development of mental states.  

### Subsequent Chapters and Sections Referenced  
The chant is also linked to classification chapters, excellence comparison sections, the four Bussa and four Listen sections, and the four giving‑benefit sections, illustrating the interconnectedness of Buddhist doctrines across the text.  

### Final Hymn Placement  
“攝頌” functions as the concluding hymn specifically of Section 八 and, like “汰咽,” is positioned within the Waterfall Discourse to encapsulate the subsection on overcoming hindrances through correct diligence.
