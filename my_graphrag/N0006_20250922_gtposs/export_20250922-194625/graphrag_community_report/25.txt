**Title**  
Five Mental Roots: Classification, Contrasts, and Spiritual Mastery  

**Summary**  
The material defines five distinct mental roots—乐根 (pleasure from bodily and mental sensations), 喜根 (pleasure from intellectual or spiritual delight), 忧根 (anxiety or grief), 舍根 (neutral non‑affection), and 苦根 (suffering from pain). Each root is identified by a characteristic self‑statement that a practitioner may utter when the root is activated. The text emphasizes that a practitioner (比丘) studies, learns, and masters each root in turn, culminating in complete liberation. Comparative relationships among the roots are highlighted: 乐根 and 喜根 are positive roots; 乐根 contrasts with 苦根 and 舍根; 舍根 represents a neutral state.  

---

### Root Classification and Core Self‑Statements  
- 乐根: arises from bodily and mental pleasures; recognized when one declares “I am pleasure.”  
- 喜根: originates from mental enjoyment; signalled by “I am pleasure” during intellectual or spiritual delight.  
- 忧根: root of anxiety or grief; indicated by the utterance “I am grief.”  
- 舍根: neutral root of non‑affection or abandonment; expressed as “I am neither pleasure nor pain.”  
- 苦根: root of suffering; produced by bodily and mental pain; associated with “I am suffering.”  

### Pleasure Roots and Their Alignment  
Both 乐根 and 喜根 generate pleasant experiences, aligning them as positive roots that reinforce enjoyment.  

### Contrasting Nature of Pleasure and Suffering Roots  
乐根, representing pleasure, is directly opposed to 苦根, which represents suffering. The two roots embody opposite emotional states derived from the same basic human experience of sensation.  

### Neutral Root and Its Distinct State  
舍根 differs from the pleasure roots by lacking feelings of pleasure or pain. Its identity is neither of the other four roots, providing a neutral emotional field.  

### Grief Root and Its Place in Practice  
忧根 serves as the root of anxiety or grief, differentiating it from pleasure roots and highlighting distress as an integral, treatable component of experience.  

### Practitioner's Mastery Sequence  
The text outlines that a practitioner studies, understands, and ultimately masters each root—pleasure, mental pleasure, grief, neutral, and suffering—to achieve complete liberation (完全 liberation).  

### Comparative Analysis of Root Pairings  
- 乐根 contrasts with 苦根 (pleasure vs suffering).  
- 乐根 contrasts with 舍根 (pleasure vs neutrality).  
- 乐根 and 喜根 are aligned (both positive).  

These comparisons clarify how each root occupies a specific position within the spectrum of mental states and how their mastery supports spiritual progress.
