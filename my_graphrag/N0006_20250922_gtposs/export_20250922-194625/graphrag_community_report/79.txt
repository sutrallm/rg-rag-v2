**Title**  
Monastic Pathways and Practices: 比牙, 比丘, and the Five Root Dynamics  

**Executive Summary**  
- The monastic community is divided into two primary roles: the **比牙**—a practitioner who may be ordained or a lay devotee—who follows the same core duties as a fully ordained **比丘**.  
- Both form part of a structured system that emphasizes *discipline (戒律)*, *meditative practices (如四念處, 七禅定)*, and *understanding the Five Roots (五根)*.  
- The **Four Right Efforts (四正勤)** serve as the central framework for eliminating unwholesome actions, cultivating wholesome actions, and sustaining progress.  
- A specialized practice called **extinction (滅想)** is highlighted for its powerful rewards, including dual fruit (二果), great merit (大功德), and eventual attainment of **阿罗汉** upon mastery of the Five Roots.  
- Stages such as **pre‑stream (预流)** and final liberation (涅槃) are the milestones for those who fully grasp and eliminate the Five Roots.  

---

### Core Components of Monastic Discipline  

- **戒律 Compliance** – Strict observance of ritual codes, including the Five Locks (戒).  
- **Meditative Focus** – Development through the Six Jhanas, Four Mindfulnesss, and Seven Spiritual Faculties.  
- **Study and Discussion** – Regular participation in Sarnath garden seminars and engagement with the Buddha’s sermons.  

### Five Root Framework  

- **Root Types** – 信 (faith), 轻/精进 (effort), 欲/急 (desire), 迷 (ignorance), 嗔/憎 (aversion) form the essential psychological conditioning for monks.  
- **Mastery Path** – Understanding each root, recognizing its manifestation as “I am pleasure,” “I am suffering,” or “I am grief,” leads to purification.  

### Four Right Efforts Structure  

1. **Prevention** – Avoiding new unwholesome actions (未生之惡不善法).  
2. **Breaking** – Destroying already existing unwholesome actions (已生之惡不善法).  
3. **Promotion** – Bringing forth wholesome actions that have not yet arisen (未生之善法).  
4. **Sustainment** – Maintaining and enhancing existing wholesome actions (已生之善法).  

- Related actions: **起志欲**, **精進**, **發勤**, **策心**.  

### Extinction Practice and Reward  

- **Technique** – Meditation on extinction (滅想) supported by **覺支** (e.g., 精進覺支, 定覺支).  
- **Outcomes** – Two levels of fruit (二果), great merit (大功德), and psychological benefits such as large detachment (大厭背), great stability (大安穩), and great enjoyment (大樂住).  

### Stages of Spiritual Progress  

- **Pre‑stream (预流)** – First stage after a monk conquers all Five Roots, guaranteeing eventual attainment of **阿罗汉**.  
- **Arhat (阿罗汉)** – Fully enlightened state achieved after full dissolution of the Five Roots.  

### Supporting Meditative Elements  

- **覺支** – Elements such as 精進覺支, 定覺支, and 念覺支 that hold practitioners steady during extinction.  
- **Four Establishments (四立)** – Breath observation and concentration practices reinforce the Seven Mind Concentrations.  

### Symbolic Journey  

- **River Motif** – The Gange (恆河) flowing eastward and into the sea symbolizes the monks’ progression toward enlightenment.  
- **Directional Guidance** – Practices emphasize renunciation and detachment (坤), reinforcing the path to cessation (滅).  

This condensed outline distills the essential practices, structures, and milestones that guide monastic practitioners from disciplined observance to ultimate liberation.
