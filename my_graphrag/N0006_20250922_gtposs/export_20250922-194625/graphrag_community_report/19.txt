**Sensation Aggregate (受) in Buddhist Teaching on Attachment, Craving, and Liberation**

---

### Executive Summary  
- **受** (sensation) is the second aggregate, generated when a sense organ contacts an object, producing physical and emotional experiences such as pleasure, pain, or neutrality.  
- The aggregate is **impermanent** and **non‑self**; if it is not recognized and relinquished, it breeds craving, attachment, and aversion, thereby sustaining the cycle of rebirth.  
- The sense organs—eye, ear, nose—act as gateways for *受*. Negative feelings manifest as *魔* (demon), while positive attachments emerge as *爱* (love or craving).  
- Texts like *煻煨* and teachings of 世尊 emphasize that understanding *受* and its transient nature is central to liberation.

---

### Key Finding: Sensation as the Root of Attachment  
*受* arises from contact between a sense organ and an object.  When the experience is viewed as permanent, it induces attachment. This attachment transforms into craving (愛) and can trigger aversion and suffering. Thus, the sensation aggregate is the primary source of binding the mind to endless cycles.

---

### Key Finding: Negative Aspects of Sensation Produce Demonic Influence  
Negative feelings tied to *受* manifest as *魔*.  When these sensations are sustained, they form aversion and precipitate pain symptoms (痛).  The demon’s influence demonstrates how unchecked negative sensations perpetuate suffering.

---

### Key Finding: Positive Attraction Arises from Sensation and Creates Craving  
*受* combines with *爱* as a collective aggregate.  The receiving aspect (受) fuels craving or love (愛), leading individuals toward grasping (取) and further entanglement in desire.  Recognizing this link is essential for cultivating non‑attachment.

---

### Key Finding: Sensory Organs Function as Gateways to Sensation  
The eye (眼), ear (耳), and nose (鼻) each generate *受* through their respective contacts—visual, auditory, and olfactory.  These organs are thus the primary channels through which experiences spawn sensations that can either enlighten or bind.

---

### Key Finding: Impermanence and Non‑Self Must Be Internalized  
Texts address *受* as impermanent and non‑self.  Without this understanding, the aggregate becomes a vehicle for craving.  The teachings of 世尊 and the discipline of *煻煨* explicitly focus on this impermanence to aid practitioners toward liberation.

---

### Key Finding: Teaching Contexts Emphasize Sensation Analysis  
*煻煨* and the discussion of 世界 highlight the importance of examining *受* alongside other aggregates.  The repeated pairing of *受* with other elements in these teachings signals its centrality to discussions on impermanence and the nature of suffering.

---

### Key Finding: Aversion toward Sensation as a Protective Recognition  
Groups such as 善男子 and 聖弟子 display aversion to *受*, indicating a conscious effort to acknowledge and resist the attachment potential of sensations.  This aversion is a protective stance that supports the path toward liberation.

---

These points collectively illuminate the profound role that the sensation aggregate plays in the Buddhist framework for understanding attachment, craving, and the possibility of liberation.
