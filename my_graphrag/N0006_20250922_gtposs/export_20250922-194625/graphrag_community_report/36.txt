**Title**  
Buddha’s Tower and the Four Divine Feet: Structure of Śamatha Training  

**Summary**  
The material outlines a Buddhist teaching center—referred to as the “tower”—where the Buddha instructs monks in the practice of śamatha (calm abiding). At this site the Buddha teaches the “four divine feet,” which are the definitive liberation. Each foot—diligence (勤), desire (欲), mind (心), and contemplation (觀)—represents mastery over a distinct stage of calm practice. The tower embodies the Buddha’s wisdom and serves as the focal point for both instruction and practice. Monks, as well as other practitioners such as 沙門 and 婆羅門, repeatedly engage with these four divine feet to attain progression.  

**Four Divine Feet as Meditative Stages**  
The “four divine feet” constitute a comprehensive framework for śamatha practice.  
- **勤 (Diligence) Tri‑SaṁṬhā** focuses on the diligent cultivation of concentration.  
- **欲 (Desire) Tri‑SaṁṬhā** addresses the mastery of the desire stage, guiding practitioners to let go of craving.  
- **心 (Mind) Tri‑SaṁṬhā** develops control over the mental aspect of calm abiding.  
- **觀 (Contemplation) Tri‑SaṁṬhā** enhances contemplative insight within the meditative sphere.  
Each pillar is described as “one of the four divine feet that constitute the concept of 四神足,” emphasizing their equal importance.  

**Tower as Site of Enlightenment Teaching**  
The tower is repeatedly identified as the physical and symbolic setting where the Buddha expounds on the four divine feet. The description notes that “the tower is the setting where the Buddha expounds on the concept of the four divine feet” and that Buddha’s wisdom (佛智) is “associated with the tower.” Thus the tower is not merely a location but an embodiment of the Buddha’s instructive presence.  

**Buddha’s Direct Instruction to Monks**  
The Buddha personally directs disciples to the tower, as seen in the instruction to 阿難 to “go to the tower and sit there as directed by 世尊.” Furthermore, the Buddha “travels to the tower, directing the monks to sit and deliver the discourse on the four divine feet.” This direct engagement underscores the importance of the tower as a place where doctrinal guidance is given face‑to‑face.  

**Practitioner Engagement with Divine Feet**  
Both 沙門 and 婆羅門 are noted as “one of the four divine feet” earners: “沙門成就神足之一分, 以此四神足修習、多修” and “婆羅門成就神足之一分, 以此四神足修習、多修.” This indicates that practitioners of different backgrounds repeatedly practice and refine the four divine feet, seeking deeper realization.  

These elements collectively reveal how the four divine feet function as a structured path within śamatha, how the tower serves as the central locus of teaching, and how the Buddha’s direct instruction fosters disciplined practice among monks and lay practitioners alike.
