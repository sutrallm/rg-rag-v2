**Title**  
Seven Mind Concentrations, Detachment, and Nirvana in the Ganges Commentary (Chapter 9)

**Summary**  
Chapter 9 of the Ganges Commentary outlines how monks practice the Seven Mind Concentrations and how detachment serves as a gateway to Nirvana. The river metaphor illustrates the gradual cleansing of attachment from the mind, while the chant *攝頌* reaffirms the chapter’s core teachings. The chapter’s central theme revolves around the active pursuit of liberation.

**Seven Mind Concentrations**  
Monks are instructed to follow the Seven Mind Concentrations, a structured method of meditation that shapes the mind toward greater focus and clarity. This practice is presented as the foundational technique for progressing along the Buddhist path.

**Detachment’s Role**  
Detachment is identified as a pivotal concept; it functions as the necessary condition that allows beings to disengage from worldly attachments and proceed toward the state of Nirvana. The text underscores detachment as a preparatory step before liberation can be attained.

**River Metaphor**  
The river metaphor is used to depict the dynamic flow of the mind: the current of attachment moves the mind downstream, whereas withdrawing into detachment cleanses it and guides it toward the ultimate word of stillness—Nirvana. This imagery serves to explain how continuous practice transforms the mind’s trajectory.

**Chant: 攝頌**  
*攝頌* is a chant that recites the main teachings of this chapter. Its inclusion signals the importance of vocal repetition in cementing the doctrinal points and supporting the meditation practices outlined.

**Theme of Seeking**  
The overarching focus of the chapter is the act of seeking. By engaging in deliberate meditation, adopting detachment, and reciting the chant, practitioners are encouraged to actively pursue the realization of Nirvana, demonstrating that liberation results from purposeful effort rather than passive expectation.
