**Title**  
Role and Characteristics of 岳父子 and 圣弟子 in Buddhist Literature  

**Executive Summary**  
- **岳父子** is a versatile title that denotes a holy or pious disciple.  
- The term is employed both to describe practitioners who cultivate specific faculties and to represent a Buddhist learner in an educational context.  
- These practitioners cultivate six root faculties, silence, sound, restraint, avoidance of excess, and departure from worldly suffering, and their conduct exemplifies the attainment of the pre‑stream state.  
- **圣弟子** refers to a distinct subset of the monastic community (比丘) and is characterised by strong aversion to five mental categories (受, 想, 色, 行, 識) and by possessing the attribute of 多聞 (skillful listening).  
- The dual usage of 岳父子 and the classification of 圣弟子 highlight how Buddhist texts differentiate between levels of commitment and practice within the monastic order.  

**Insights**

**Dual Function of 岳父子**  
The designation 岳父子 is used in two ways: first, it names a highly devout disciple who internalises and practices core Buddhist teachings; second, it serves an educational purpose by symbolising a Buddhist learner, sometimes linked to a divine father‑son pair. This dual application reveals the term’s flexibility in conveying both spiritual attainment and pedagogical roles.  

**Qualities Emphasized in 岳父子**  
Practitioners called 岳父子 cultivate six root faculties, access silence and sound, exercise restraint, avoid excess, and detach from worldly suffering. Their conduct demonstrates the attainment of the pre‑stream state, indicating a high level of doctrinal comprehension and experiential insight among the monastic community.  

**Classification of 圣弟子 within the Monastic Community**  
圣弟子 are identified as a subset of the wider group of monks (比丘). This classification sets them apart from general monastic members by distinguishing a more refined level of practice and commitment within the broader monastic order.  

**Core Attitudes of 圣弟子**  
The defining features of 圣弟子 include a pronounced aversion to five mental categories—受, 想, 色, 行, 識—and the possession of multi‑absorbing listening (多聞). These attributes illustrate a focused practice of renunciation and attentive listening, underscoring a particular mode of disciplined engagement in Buddhist training.  

**Polysemy and Educational Context**  
Because 岳父子 appears in both sacred and educational settings, it operates as a polysemous term in Buddhist literature. Recognising this multiplicity is essential for accurately interpreting passages where the term may shift from describing a high‑level disciple to representing a general learner.
