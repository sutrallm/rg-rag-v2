**Title**  
Three‑Way Support: The Dual‑Group Meditation Framework in Monastic Practice  

**Summary**  
The Three‑Way Support system is split into two distinct collections of practices. The first set, the Seven Spiritual Faculties—mindfulness, investigation, focus, energy, resolve, joy, and equanimity—forms the core training base and evolves through breath meditation alongside the Four Establishments. The second set, the Seven Mind Concentrations, dissolves into six specific sub‑practices (mindful attention, doctrine‑choosing, diligent effort, joyful enjoyment, light‑ease, and the concentration component) plus a foundational renunciation aspect that collectively guide monks toward Nirvana. Each of these sub‑practices is specially tuned to strengthen extinction practice, intensifying focus, stability, perseverance, and contemplative clarity. Monastic directives consistently emphasize that mastering these concentrations within extinction practice is essential for attaining enlightenment, a point underscored by the recitations of the Buddha Anāṇa and routine monastic practice statements.  

**Key Findings**  

*Foundations and Dual Structure*  
The Three‑Way Support framework is intentionally organized into two layers. The first layer, the Seven Spiritual Faculties, establishes the internal qualities required for disciplined meditation. The second layer, the Seven Mind Concentrations, builds a targeted set of meditative tools that each reinforce a specific cognitive or emotional focus. This two‑stage design ensures that foundational qualities are continuously reinforced while specialized concentration tools are applied directly to extinction practice.  

*Functional Mapping of Mind Concentrations*  
- **Mindful Attention (檵覺扶)** focuses awareness deliberately, providing the directed awareness essential for concentration.  
- **Doctrine‑Choosing (选法观支)** operates on a choice principle, allowing practitioners to select doctrines or intentions strategically within the extinction framework.  
- **Diligent Effort (精进禪支)** encourages perseverance and unwavering commitment during study and meditation.  
- **Joyful Enjoyment** contributes a positive affect that sustains engagement.  
- **Light‑Ease** adds a sense of relaxation that prevents tension building.  
- **Concentration Component** provides the core stability required for deep meditative states.  
- **Renunciation (捨覺支)** underlies the entire system, guiding practitioners toward the relinquishment of attachments necessary for Nirvana.  

*Role of Extinction Practice*  
Extinction practice is used as a unifying backdrop for all sub‑practices. By employing each concentration within extinction practice, monks deepen their ability to cut through mental hindrances. The accumulation of focused attention, wisdom, and effort under the extinction paradigm results in progressively deeper meditative absorption.  

*Path Toward Nirvana*  
Following the seven concentrations systematically leads monks to Nirvana. Repeated practice of the extinction meditation, coupled with the strengthening of the seven faculties and the execution of the concentrations, is portrayed as a clear pathway. The scriptural references and regnal chants that depict monks using “滅想俱行” with each concentration reinforce the belief that mastery of these practices is the guaranteed route to liberation.  

*Dynamic of Diligence and Doctrine Selection*  
Monastic instruction stresses diligent effort in conjunction with intentional doctrinal selection. This dynamic yields a disciplined practice space where mental focus is maintained and sustained through purposeful goal setting. The emphasis on effortless engagement with choice doctrine reflects a balanced approach that fosters both dedication and mental equanimity.  

These observations provide a concise framework for understanding how the Three‑Way Support system structures Buddhist monastic meditation, highlights the interdependencies among its components, and clarifies its intended outcome of achieving Nirvana.
