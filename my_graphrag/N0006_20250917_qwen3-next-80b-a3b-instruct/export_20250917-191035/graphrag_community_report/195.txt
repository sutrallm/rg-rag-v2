**Title: The Nature and State of the Saint**

**Summary**  
The saint is characterized by the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion, resulting in unwavering inner purity and emotional stability. This condition arises from perfect moral discipline and direct wisdom into the true nature of reality—impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, and absence of self. These qualities together lead to total liberation from suffering, craving, and fear.

**Moral Discipline as the Foundation**  
Ethical conduct and restraint from harmful actions form the essential basis for all other qualities of the saint. Without this discipline, the development of meditative focus and wisdom is not possible. It is the primary condition that enables the cultivation of inner purity and emotional resilience.

**Wisdom of Suchness as the Path to Freedom**  
The saint perceives all phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of self. This clear perception dispels delusion and removes attachment, generating dispassion. The realization is not theoretical but lived, directly dismantling the roots of suffering.

**Emotional Unperturbability Through Eradication of Ignorance**  
The saint remains unmoved by emotional fluctuations because ignorance—the root cause of misperception and reactivity—has been fully uprooted. This is not suppression or detachment through effort, but the absence of the conditions that give rise to disturbance.

**Liberation as the Consummation of the Path**  
The saint is entirely free from suffering, craving, and fear. This state is not aspirational but actualized, the inevitable outcome of perfected moral discipline and unshakable wisdom. There is no residual clinging, no psychological tension, and no need for escape.
