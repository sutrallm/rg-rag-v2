**Title: Xiuxing, Shan, and Jietui: The Path to Inner Freedom Through Mindful Practice**

**Summary**  
Inner freedom is achieved not through ritual or external forces, but through consistent mindful engagement in daily life. This practice dissolves greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering—and cultivates clear seeing of impermanence and non-self. The resulting inner stillness is both the sign and the experience of liberation.

**Mindful Daily Practice Is the Foundation of Liberation**  
The continuous application of mindful awareness during routine activities—walking, eating, speaking, sitting—forms the core of spiritual cultivation. This sustained presence transforms ordinary actions into tools for transforming the mind, making liberation a tangible outcome of everyday behavior rather than an abstract ideal.

**Inner Stillness Is the Symptom of Eradicated Afflictions**  
When the mind is no longer disturbed by greed, hatred, or delusion, a natural state of calm emerges. This stillness is not forced or temporary; it is a durable condition observed in those who have fully transcended the root poisons, marking the presence of deep transformation.

**Wisdom Arises from Direct Observation, Not Doctrine**  
Clear seeing of impermanence and non-self develops through persistent attention to the mind and phenomena in real time. This understanding is not acquired through belief or instruction but emerges organically from disciplined observation, serving as the only reliable pathway to freedom.

**Liberation Is Not a State Beyond Experience, But the Absence of Inner Turmoil**  
True emancipation is not an otherworldly condition or reward, but the complete dissolution of attachment to self and possessions. It is experienced as profound inner peace—an unshakable stillness that arises only when the three afflictions are fully dissolved.

**The Three Afflictions Are the Sole Obstacles to Freedom**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the exclusive barriers preventing liberation. No external practice, ritual, or divine aid can substitute for their eradication. Their removal is both necessary and sufficient for the emergence of lasting stillness and freedom.
