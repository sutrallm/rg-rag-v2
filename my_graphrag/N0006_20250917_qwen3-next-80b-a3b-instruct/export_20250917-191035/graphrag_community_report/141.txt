**True Stillness and Present-Moment Awakening**

**Summary**  
True stillness is attained through inner freedom from desire, fear, and regret, not through external isolation. It emerges only when full awareness is applied to ordinary, current activities, marking the beginning of release from cyclic patterns. This state is cultivated not by forceful effort but by allowing the mind to settle naturally, and it is inseparable from being fully present in each moment.

**Calming the Mind Naturally Leads to Stillness**  
True stillness does not arise from deliberate pursuit but from the mind’s natural cessation of agitation. Chan Ding facilitates this by enabling the mind to rest without striving, allowing it to settle into peace as internal disturbances subside on their own.

**Awareness in Ordinary Moments Is the Foundation of Liberation**  
Liberation is not an abstract goal but a lived experience rooted in complete attention to everyday actions—eating, walking, and breathing. The act of being fully aware in the present moment is not merely a practice but the very expression of the path to freedom.

**Stillness and Awakening Are Interdependent**  
True stillness cannot be separated from present-moment awakening. The absence of internal disturbances—desire, fear, regret—occurs simultaneously with full awareness of current experience. One cannot exist without the other; both are unified in the act of being present.
