**Title: Zhineneng and the Liberation of the Shengzhe Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
True liberation is achieved through transcendent wisdom (zhineneng), which arises from the direct observation of impermanence and non-self. This wisdom dissolves attachment (yuanchuang) to all experiences—whether pleasurable meditative states, emotional sensations, or spiritual goals—allowing the holy one (shengzhe) to remain unattached and free. Meditative concentration (chanding) alone is insufficient; it must be guided by zhineneng to prevent clinging and transform into liberating awareness.

---

**Elaboration Through Direct Observation of Impermanence and Non-Self**  
The foundation of liberation is the recognition that all phenomena—包括 meditative experiences, emotional states, and even notions of enlightenment—are transient and lack inherent selfhood. This understanding arises not through theory but through direct, sustained observation (wuwo guan zhao), which reveals that nothing possesses a fixed, independent essence. When this is seen clearly, attachment naturally dissolves, as there is no enduring entity to cling to and no permanent state to pursue or avoid.

**The Role of Transcendent Wisdom in Transforming Concentration**  
Deep meditative focus (chanding) provides the mental clarity necessary for this observation, yet it does not, by itself, lead to freedom. Without transcendent wisdom, concentration can become another object of attachment—such as clinging to bliss, light, or emptiness. Only when wisdom guides concentration does the practice transcend mere absorption and become a tool for liberation, dismantling the illusion of permanence and selfhood in every experience.

**The Definition of the Holy One Through Complete Non-Attachment**  
The holy one (shengzhe) is fully defined by the absence of attachment (yuanchuang), not by attainment. This individual neither grasps at pleasant states nor rejects unpleasant ones, including the most refined meditative experiences. Their freedom is marked by equanimity—observing all feelings and sensations (gan shou) without reaction—because they have realized that these are fleeting and selfless. Their state is not a destination but the end of the pursuit itself.

**The Mechanism of Dissolving Attachment Through Seeing Emptiness**  
Attachment is not overcome by willpower or suppression but through the direct realization that all objects of craving—whether sensory, emotional, or spiritual—are empty of intrinsic nature. When one sees that meditative bliss is impermanent and devoid of self, clinging to it ceases. This cessation is not forced; it occurs naturally as perception shifts. Thus, wisdom does not combat attachment—it renders it irrelevant by revealing its foundation as illusory.
