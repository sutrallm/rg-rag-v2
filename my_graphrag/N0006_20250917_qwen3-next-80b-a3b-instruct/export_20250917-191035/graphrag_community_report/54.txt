**Title: The Path of the Noble One: Transcending Anger, Ignorance, and Desire Through Chanding**

**Summary:**  
The text outlines a systematic path to inner tranquility through the eradication of three core afflictions—anger, ignorance, and greed—each rooted in a distorted perception of reality. These mental disturbances manifest as suffering, reactivity, and separation. The practice of chanding serves as the central method to calm the mind, dissolve aversion, and cultivate equanimity. Through sustained chanding, one develops wisdom that dispels ignorance, reveals impermanence and non-self, and ultimately uproots all defilements. Those who complete this path—Noble Ones and sages—achieve unshakable inner peace, free from anger, craving, and delusion, and remain undisturbed by adverse conditions or sensory pleasures.

---

**Chanding Reveals the True Nature of Reality and Dissolves Anger**  
Chanding is not merely a calming technique but a transformative practice that destabilizes the mental structures sustaining anger. By withdrawing attention from aversive thoughts and cultivating non-reactive awareness, it undermines the illusion of hostile, fixed entities and the perceived threat to a permanent self. As anger arises from misunderstanding reality—particularly impermanence and non-self—chanding replaces reactivity with equanimity, enabling the mind to perceive experiences without resistance. The sage, having perfected this practice, is utterly free from anger, having dismantled its foundation in delusion.

**Ignorance Is the Root of All Suffering and Must Be Uprooted by Wisdom**  
The fundamental source of anger, greed, grief, and craving is stupidity—ignorance of reality’s true nature: impermanence, non-self, and the interdependent nature of experience. This ignorance perpetuates misperceptions that fuel suffering: seeing permanence where there is change, self where there is none, and enduring satisfaction in fleeting sensory pleasures. Only through meditative insight cultivated via chanding can this root be penetrated. The Noble One and the sage are defined by their direct realization of reality, which eliminates ignorance and all arising afflictions.

**Inner Peace Arises Only Through the Extinction of the Three Defilements**  
True tranquility cannot coexist with craving, aversion, or delusion. Inner peace is the natural state of a mind cleared of these three poisons. Anger disrupts calm; greed destabilizes it; ignorance sustains both. The cessation of these is not passive but achieved through disciplined practice—particularly chanding—which simultaneously reduces reactivity and deepens insight. Indulgence in sense desires, likened to drinking poison, only reinforces attachment and inevitable grief. Liberation is marked by complete absence of these disturbances: the sage is not merely calm but entirely free from the mechanisms that generate suffering.

**The Noble One and the Sage Are Defined by Permanent Transformation, Not Temporary Calm**  
A Noble One is distinguished not by occasional restraint or temporary calm, but by irreversible eradication of greed, anger, and ignorance. This is maintained through continuous exertion in mindfulness and wisdom, ensuring no regression. The sage embodies the culmination of this process: fully stabilized mind, transcendent wisdom, and utter freedom from aversion. Their equanimity is unshaken by pleasure or pain, because they no longer identify with a self or perceive enduring objects in a world of flux. Their freedom is not an idealized state but the direct result of dismantling the cognitive distortions that bind ordinary consciousness.
