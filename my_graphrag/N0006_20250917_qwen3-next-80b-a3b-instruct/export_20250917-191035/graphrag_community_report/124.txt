**Title: The Saintly Being and the Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary:**  
A Saintly Being is defined by the complete and irreversible elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion—the three core mental afflictions that bind sentient beings to suffering and the cycle of rebirth. These afflictions, also called defilements or internal demons, are the primary obstacles to liberation. Their cessation is achieved through the disciplined practice of the Straight Path, which includes mindfulness, ethical conduct, and wisdom. Chan Ding supports this process by stabilizing the mind and reducing reactivity, enabling detachment. The liberated state is marked by perfect moral discipline, unwavering tranquility, direct realization of impermanence and non-self, and the dissolution of self-attachment. This state renders the being an object of reverence, unified with ultimate truth, and free from all dependencies and suffering.

**Defilements Are the Root Cause of Suffering and Rebirth**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion—collectively termed defilements or the Three Poisons—are the foundational mental impurities that cloud perception, fuel craving, and generate karma. These afflictions perpetuate the cycle of samsara by binding sentient beings to repeated suffering and rebirth. Their persistence prevents clarity and spiritual progress, making their eradication not optional but necessary for any movement toward liberation.

**The Straight Path Is the Necessary Method for Liberation**  
The Straight Path is the disciplined and consistent spiritual practice required to suppress and ultimately eliminate defilements. It is not a passive state but an active, sustained method involving mindfulness, ethical conduct, and the cultivation of wisdom. Without this structured approach, the mind remains vulnerable to reactivity and attachment, and the Three Poisons cannot be uprooted.

**Chan Ding Enables Mental Stability and Detachment**  
Chan Ding facilitates the transcendence of defilements by calming mental turbulence, reducing restlessness and laziness, and suppressing reactivity. Through this meditative stability, the practitioner gains the capacity to detach from craving and ignorance, creating the internal conditions in which wisdom can arise and defilements dissolve.

**A Saintly Being Is Defined by the Complete Destruction of the Three Poisons**  
The defining mark of a Saintly Being is the total and irreversible eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. No partial suppression or temporary quieting suffices—only complete dissolution qualifies one as liberated. This absence of the Three Poisons is the sole criterion for spiritual maturity and enlightenment.

**Wisdom and Ethical Conduct Are the Pillars of Liberation**  
Liberation is realized through the dual cultivation of profound wisdom—allowing direct perception of impermanence, non-self, and reality as it is—and perfect moral discipline. Wisdom dismantles delusion, ethical conduct prevents the reinforcement of defilements, and together they form the unshakable foundation of the Saintly Being’s state.

**The Saintly Being Embodies Non-Attachment and Tranquility**  
Having uprooted all defilements, the Saintly Being no longer misperceives reality, has dissolved the illusion of self, and no longer clings to worldly conditions or external dependencies. Their mind remains unmoved by desire, radiates inner peace, and naturally embodies tranquility. This state is not merely emotional calm but a permanent alignment with ultimate truth.

**Internal Demons Are Metaphors for Mental Afflictions**  
The terms “internal demons” and “defilements” refer to the same phenomena: psychological forces of craving, anger, and ignorance that arise within the mind and obstruct progress. These are not external entities but internal states—once recognized and addressed through mindfulness and wisdom, they lose their power to bind.
