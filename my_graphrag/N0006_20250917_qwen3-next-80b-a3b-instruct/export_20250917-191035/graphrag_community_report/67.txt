**Title: Wuzhizhuo, Shengye, and the Interplay of Chanding, Wuwu, and Zhihui yu Cibei**

**Summary**  
True liberation is achieved through non-attachment, which arises from the direct realization of non-self. This state is cultivated through meditative stability, which enables clear observation of phenomena without clinging. The fully awakened being — the saintly one — embodies enduring peace, moral integrity, and boundless compassion, having eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion. Wisdom and compassion are inseparable pillars: wisdom dissolves illusion, and compassion ensures ethical engagement. All these qualities are rooted in meditative concentration and culminate in a mind as open and undisturbed as empty space.

---

**Non-Attachment Arises from the Realization of Non-Self**  
The absence of clinging to experiences — including spiritual ones — emerges not through suppression but through recognizing the self as an illusion. When the notion of a fixed, independent self is seen through, attachment to transient phenomena naturally dissolves. This recognition of non-self is the method through which non-attachment is cultivated, not an abstract idea but a direct perception that transforms how one relates to all experience.

**Meditative Stability Enables the Development of Wisdom**  
Clear observation of phenomena, free from distortion or clinging, requires a mind that is stable and undisturbed. Meditative concentration establishes this foundation, allowing wisdom to arise by revealing the impermanent, interdependent, and selfless nature of all things. Without this mental steadiness, the insight necessary for non-attachment cannot take root or deepen.

**Saintliness is Defined by the Eradication of Disturbances and the Absence of Attachment**  
The saintly state is not merely moral conduct but the total elimination of greed, hatred, and delusion. This is evidenced by an inner peace that remains unshaken by internal or external conditions. Importantly, such a being does not cling even to spiritual experiences, demonstrating that non-attachment is not passive indifference but an active freedom from all forms of grasping.

**Wisdom and Compassion Are Mutually Dependent and Necessarily Balanced**  
Wisdom alone, without compassion, would lack ethical direction. Compassion alone, without wisdom, risks being misguided or emotionally reactive. True saintliness manifests only when wisdom, which sees through illusion, is coupled with compassion, which motivates action for the benefit of all beings. One cannot be fully realized without the other.

**The Embodied Sage Integrates All Qualities into a Unified State of Being**  
The sage does not separate practice from expression. Non-attachment, meditative stability, wisdom, compassion, and the realization of non-self are not isolated goals but interwoven dimensions of a single, lived reality. This unified state results in inner peace and moral integrity that are not achieved through effort alone but through the natural unfolding of a mind no longer bound by illusion.
