**Title: Jielv, Xinzi, and the Path to Zhihui in Spiritual Cultivation**

**Summary**  
Ethical conduct (jielv) is the essential foundation for mental stability, enabling the practice of mind cultivation (xinzi), which in turn leads to wisdom (zhihui). Without moral discipline, meditation and wisdom cannot arise reliably due to internal disturbances. Xinzi relies on diligent effort to sustain attention and dissolve habitual mental patterns, ultimately culminating in the transcendence of ego-driven conditioning. The saintly one is defined by the presence of jielv as the basis for purified mind and spiritual realization.

---

**Moral Discipline Enables Mental Stability**  
Ethical conduct (jielv) purifies behavior and intention, removing sources of regret and psychological disturbance. This purification creates the necessary conditions for deep meditation to take place without obstruction. Without this foundation, the mind remains agitated by misconduct, preventing the emergence of clarity and wisdom.

**Mind Cultivation Transforms Habitual Patterns**  
Mind cultivation (xinzi) is an active process that uses sustained mindfulness and diligent effort to overcome desire, fear, and attachment. It trains the mind to be steady and self-guided, dismantling ingrained reactions that cloud perception. This transformation is not passive but requires continuous, intentional practice.

**Diligent Effort Sustains the Practice of Mind Cultivation**  
The stability of the mind during cultivation depends on persistent effort (jingjin). Without this energy, inertia prevails and habitual patterns endure. Diligence is not optional—it is the driving force that allows attention to remain focused and change to occur.

**Wisdom Arises as the Culmination of Ethical and Mental Training**  
Wisdom (zhihui) is the natural outcome of cultivated mental clarity and moral integrity. It emerges when the mind is no longer clouded by misconduct or ego-driven conditioning. This wisdom is not theoretical; it is the direct result of aligned ethical conduct and sustained mental training.

**The Saintly One Is Defined by Moral Grounding**  
Spiritual attainment is inseparable from ethical discipline. The presence of jielv is the distinguishing mark of the saintly one, as it provides the necessary base for a purified mind and authentic realization. Without it, even advanced meditative experiences lack depth or permanence.

**Mind Cultivation Includes and Expands Upon Meditation**  
While meditation (chanding) is a core component of mind cultivation, it is not the entirety of the practice. Xinzi incorporates meditation as a means to an end—training the mind to be undisturbed and autonomous—while also requiring ethical grounding and diligent effort to be effective.
