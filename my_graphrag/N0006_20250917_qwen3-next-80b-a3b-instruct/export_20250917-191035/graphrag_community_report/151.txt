Title: Guanzhao and Non-Attachment in Meditation and Sage-Like Action

Summary:  
The practice of mindful observation, known as guanzhao, is consistently tied to the absence of attachment to outcomes. This principle extends from personal meditation to the conduct of the sage, who acts without seeking personal gain, peace, or reward. The core of the practice lies in observing phenomena as they arise and pass, without attempting to alter them or derive benefit from the experience.

Mindful Observation Through Non-Attachment  
Guanzhao, or mindful observation, is defined by the absence of attachment or aversion toward bodily and mental phenomena. This form of awareness does not aim to change, control, or extract value from what is observed. The act of observing is complete in itself, grounded solely in presence rather than pursuit of outcomes.

Alignment Between Practice and Sage-Like Conduct  
The behavior of the sage mirrors the meditative discipline of guanzhao. Both are characterized by pure motivation—oriented toward truth rather than personal benefit, peace, or reward. The sage’s actions, like the meditator’s observation, are free from attachment to results, demonstrating that the principle of non-attachment operates consistently across internal practice and external conduct.

Non-Attachment as Foundational to Observation  
Non-attachment to results is not a secondary aspect of mindful observation but its essential requirement. To observe without seeking to change or gain from the experience is what defines the practice. Without this framework, the observation ceases to be guanzhao and becomes an act of striving, undermining its foundational purpose.
