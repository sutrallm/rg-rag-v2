**Title: Chaoshizhixi, Zhenzhideziyou, and Chixuxiuli: The Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True freedom arises from the dissolution of all attachments—including those to spiritual experiences—through transcendent wisdom and unceasing practice. This freedom is not an external state but an inner condition of stillness and non-clinging, realized only by those who embody the absence of self and sustain daily mindfulness. Without persistent effort, liberation remains an abstract concept; with it, wisdom matures into lived silence.

**Continuous Practice Transforms Consciousness**  
The unbroken discipline of mindfulness and right view reshapes habitual patterns of thought and emotion over time. This daily effort is not optional but essential for the maturation of serenity and wisdom, ensuring that theoretical understanding becomes embodied reality.

**Transcendent Wisdom Dissolves All Clinging**  
The recognition of impermanence and non-self erodes attachment not only to worldly objects but also to meditative states, achievements, and spiritual identities. This wisdom prevents the misinterpretation of temporary experiences as final goals, freeing the mind from even the subtlest forms of grasping.

**True Freedom Is the Absence of Inner Bondage**  
Liberation is defined by a mind unshaken by conditions, free from fear and desire. It is not escape from the world but the complete relinquishment of internal dependencies, resulting in boundless stillness. This state is not attained through momentary insight but through the enduring embodiment of non-self.

**The Embodied Realization of the Shengzhe**  
Those who have fully realized the emptiness of all phenomena no longer cling to any identity, including spiritual ones. Their presence reflects the complete fusion of transcendent wisdom and true freedom—living proof that liberation is neither abstract nor distant, but a tangible condition of being.
