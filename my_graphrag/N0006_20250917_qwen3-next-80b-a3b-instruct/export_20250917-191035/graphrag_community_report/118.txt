**Title: The Nature of the Enlightened Being**

**Summary**  
The enlightened being is characterized by the complete absence of greed, hatred, and delusion, sustained by unwavering mindfulness and discipline. Through detailed observation of reality without distortion, such individuals cultivate pure ethical conduct and mental restraint, leading to liberation from all forms of clinging and the cyclical nature of existence. Their inner state is one of unshakable stillness, unmoved by external conditions, grounded in direct understanding of reality’s true nature.

**Ethical Conduct as a Foundational Practice**  
Enlightened beings consistently embody pure ethical behavior, which is not incidental but essential to their liberation. This conduct serves as the root from which meditative depth and wisdom arise, forming a stable base for the dismantling of conditioned patterns and the emergence of inner freedom.

**Discipline as the Scaffold for Awakening**  
Mental restraint and consistent ethical action are not temporary efforts but cultivated over lifetimes, forming the structural foundation for concentration and clear perception. Without this discipline, the_depth of awareness necessary for liberation cannot be sustained.

**Mindfulness as the Gateway to Direct Perception**  
The capacity to observe phenomena exactly as they are, free from projection or distortion, is made possible only through mindfulness. This nonjudgmental presence enables the unfolding of wisdom and is the continuous practice through which enlightenment is maintained.

**Liberation Defined by the Absence of Clinging**  
Enlightened beings are fully released from attachment—to material things, emotional states, and even spiritual achievements. This absence of clinging is not passive detachment but an active, realized freedom that transcends all conditioning and identifications.

**Unshakable Stillness as a Manifestation of Realized Wisdom**  
Their inner state remains undisturbed by any arising phenomenon, conditioned or otherwise, not through suppression but through profound understanding of reality’s impermanent and selfless nature. This stillness is the natural expression of having seen things as they truly are.
