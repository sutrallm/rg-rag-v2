**Title: Jinjin and Chaoshi: The Path to Liberation from Cyclic Existence**

**Summary**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth is achieved not through withdrawal from the world, but through unwavering diligent effort and wisdom of non-attachment in everyday life. Continuous mindfulness in ordinary activities, sustained by persistent effort, breaks the grip of craving and aversion—the roots of suffering. True freedom arises when the mind no longer chases pleasure or resists pain, and this state is maintained only through constant, active practice.

**Diligent Effort Sustains Mindfulness in Daily Life**  
Without persistent diligent effort, mindfulness cannot be maintained beyond formal meditation. The practice of staying aware during walking, standing, sitting, and lying down requires continuous exertion to prevent laziness and mental distraction. This effort is not occasional but constant, ensuring that awareness does not lapse into forgetfulness or complacency.

**Non-Attachment Is the Source of True Freedom**  
Freedom is not found in escaping the world, but in ceasing to be driven by pleasure, pain, favor, or adversity. When the mind no longer clings to or rejects any experience, liberation emerges naturally. This state is not passive; it is the result of cultivated wisdom that sees all sensations as impermanent and unworthy of attachment.

**Afflictions Fuel Cyclic Existence**  
Craving and aversion are the active forces that bind beings to birth and death. These afflictions arise from ignorance and perpetuate the cycle by conditioning reactions to experience. Breaking this cycle requires recognizing these patterns in daily life and uprooting them through mindful awareness.

**Everyday Actions Are the Arena of Liberation**  
Spiritual cultivation occurs in the midst of ordinary activities, not in isolation. The same mindfulness applied in meditation must extend to speech and action throughout the day. This practical application transforms routine moments into opportunities to dissolve attachment and realize impermanence.

**Liberation Demands Active, Ongoing Practice**  
Freedom from cyclic existence is not a static destination but a dynamic state upheld by relentless effort. It cannot be attained through intermittent practice or temporary insight. Only through sustained diligence in observing and releasing all attachments—moment by moment—can one remain free.
