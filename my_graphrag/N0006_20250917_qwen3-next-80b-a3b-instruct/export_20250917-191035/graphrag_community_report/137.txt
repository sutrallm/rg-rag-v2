**Title: Effortful and Continuous Practice as the Path to Liberation and Transcendence of Samsara**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved only through unwavering, daily spiritual discipline that prevents negligence and sustains meditative stability. This sustained effort directly cultivates saintly qualities and dismantles the causes of suffering—greed, hatred, delusion, craving, and ignorance. Without such persistent practice, one remains trapped in suffering; with it, transcendence becomes inevitable.

**Effortful Practice Sustains Meditative Stability**  
The cultivation of Chan Ding depends entirely on relentless, vigilant spiritual effort. Any lapse—no matter how brief—permits afflictions to resurface, undermining the stability of meditation. This practice acts as the foundational support for Chan Ding, preventing laziness and reinforcing mindfulness, thereby ensuring the practitioner remains anchored in the righteous path.

**Continuous Practice Leads to Profound Transformation**  
Liberation is not the product of sporadic or intense bursts of effort, but of unbroken, daily mindfulness. Small, consistent actions accumulate over time, gradually eroding ignorance and attachment, much like water wearing away stone. This steady, round-the-clock self-correction is essential to transform superficial awareness into lasting spiritual realization.

**Saintly Qualities Arise Only Through Sustained Discipline**  
Those who have transcended the cycle of birth and death possess qualities that cannot be attained without continuous, effortful practice. The presence of such qualities is not accidental or inherited—it is the direct outcome of persistent diligence that eliminates craving and ignorance, establishing a state beyond samsaric bondage.

**Effortful and Continuous Practice Are Inseparable on the Path**  
The terms “effortful practice” and “continuous practice” refer to the same disciplined approach, differing only in emphasis—one highlights the quality of vigilance, the other the duration of persistence. Together, they define the singular, necessary condition for transcending suffering and achieving true liberation.
