**Title: The Eradication of Fannao Through Jiaocha and Wuwo: Path to Nirvana for Shengzhe**

**Summary**  
Suffering originates in fannao—greed, hatred, and delusion—which stem from misidentifying the five aggregates (wuyun) as a permanent self. These mental disturbances are sustained by attachment, discrimination, and emotional reactions, binding beings to cyclic existence. Liberation is achieved not through supernatural experiences or temporary calm, but through the complete extinction of fannao. This is accomplished by jiaocha—mindful awareness of the present moment—combined with wuwo, the realized understanding that no self exists within the aggregates. When wisdom (zhihui) and non-attachment (chaoshi) arise through direct observation, afflictions dissolve naturally. Those who have fully eliminated these defilements, known as shengzhe, dwell in jiepo: a state of pure freedom where suffering ceases and nirvana is realized.

**Fannao Arises from Misapprehension of the Five Aggregates**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion emerge when an individual falsely perceives the five aggregates—form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—as constituting a self or as belonging to a self. This mistaken identification generates clinging and resistance, fueling mental distress and perpetuating the cycle of birth and death. The aggregates themselves are impermanent and devoid of inherent identity; their misperception is the foundational error that gives rise to suffering.

**Mindful Awareness Dissolves Afflictions Through Non-Attachment**  
Direct observation of the present moment without clinging to past or future—known as jiaocha—serves as the primary method to neutralize afflictions. By witnessing thoughts, sensations, and emotions as they arise without reaction or identification, the mind ceases to generate craving or aversion. This practice allows fannao to dissolve naturally, not through suppression, but through the absence of nourishment from conceptual thought and emotional clinging.

**Wisdom Reveals Non-Self, Extinguishing Afflictions at the Root**  
The realization of non-self (wuwo) removes the source of fannao by exposing the illusion of a permanent identity within the aggregates. Observing the transient nature of form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness leads to the natural unraveling of attachment. When one sees clearly that “I” and “mine” have no basis in reality, the mental poisons lose their power. This understanding, cultivated through mindfulness, is not theoretical but experiential, leading directly to inner purity.

**Complete Extinction of Defilements Defines Liberation**  
Liberation is not marked by altered states, psychic powers, or temporary peace, but by the total eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. This state—jaebo—is distinct from mere suppression; it is the permanent burning out of afflictions like extinguished wood. Those who have attained this, called shengzhe, no longer harbor any trace of mental defilements. Their existence is characterized by compassion and clarity, and they abide in nirvana: the final cessation of suffering and freedom from cyclic existence. The entire path of discipline, concentration, and wisdom is structured toward this single outcome.

**Nirvana Is the Natural Consequence of Defilement Removal**  
Nirvana is not an external goal or state to be attained, but the inherent condition that emerges when all inner defilements are purged. It is identical with the absence of fannao. Every meditative practice—calming the mind, observing the present, recognizing non-self—serves only to clear away the obscuring layers of craving and ignorance. Once these are gone, peace and freedom remain as the natural condition of the mind.
