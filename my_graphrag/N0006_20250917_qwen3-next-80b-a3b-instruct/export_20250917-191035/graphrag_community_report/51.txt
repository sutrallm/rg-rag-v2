**Title: Transcending Qingjing: The Nature of Non-Attachment in Spiritual Practice**

**Summary:**  
True spiritual maturity lies not in attaining states like purity or serenity, but in releasing all attachment—even to those very states. The highest form of wisdom is the absence of clinging, whether to pleasure, suffering, or spiritual achievements. Seeking qingjing is valid, but holding onto it defeats its purpose.

**Seeking Purity Without Clinging**  
The practice of pursuing qingjing—mental clarity and calmness—is acknowledged as valuable. However, the moment one clings to this state as an endpoint or possession, it transforms into a form of attachment. This contradicts the very aim of the practice, revealing that the value of qingjing lies not in its attainment, but in the freedom from needing it.

**Non-Attachment to Spiritual States**  
The most advanced condition is not defined by the presence of elevated mental states, but by the complete absence of attachment to them. Even spiritual achievements such as purity or serenity become obstacles when treated as goals to be held. True mastery is marked by non-abiding—remaining unanchored, even in the presence of what is conventionally considered noble or desirable.

**Wisdom as the Absence of Clinging**  
Real wisdom is not measured by the depth of one’s experiences or the purity of one’s mind, but by the absence of fixation. Clinging to the idea of purity, even as a spiritual ideal, is identified as a subtle trap. The liberation described arises not from acquiring qualities, but from letting go of the need to possess them—regardless of how virtuous or refined they may seem.
