**The Practice of Contemplation of Non-Self and Its Foundations in Dependent Origination**

**Summary**  
The practice of Contemplation of Non-Self is a meditative discipline rooted in the understanding that all phenomena—physical and mental—are impermanent and arise only through interdependent causes and conditions. This practice directly realizes the philosophical truth of Non-Self by exposing the illusion of a permanent, independent self. It is sustained by the principle of Dependent Origination and relies on the recognition of impermanence to dissolve attachments to selfhood. Wisdom emerges as a natural outcome of this sustained observation.

**Observation of Impermanence Undermines the Illusion of a Permanent Self**  
To engage in Contemplation of Non-Self, one must perceive all experiences as transient. The fleeting nature of thoughts, sensations, and conditions reveals that no stable, enduring identity persists. This constant flux refutes the notion of a fixed self, making it impossible to cling to any phenomenon as “me” or “mine.” Without impermanence as a lens, the illusion of permanence in identity cannot be challenged.

**Dependent Origination Sustains the Practice of Contemplation of Non-Self**  
The meditation is not arbitrary; it is anchored in the understanding that nothing arises on its own. Every experience is shaped by preceding causes and supporting conditions. Recognizing this interdependence dismantles the belief in autonomous existence. Since nothing stands alone, no entity can be identified as a separate, intrinsic self. This framework provides the logical and experiential basis for the entire practice.

**Wisdom Is Expressed Through the Continuous Practice of Contemplation of Non-Self**  
True understanding does not reside in intellectual agreement with the doctrine of Non-Self, but in the sustained act of observing phenomena as they arise and pass. This ongoing attention cultivates a direct, non-conceptual clarity. It is through this daily, moment-to-moment observation—rooted in impermanence and interdependence—that wisdom becomes tangible and transformative, rather than abstract.
