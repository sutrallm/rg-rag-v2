Title: Zheng Jian and the Path to Non-Self Through Meditation

Summary:  
True spiritual freedom arises from recognizing the illusion of self and attachment through accurate perception of reality. Meditation’s purpose is not to seek special experiences or moral elevation, but to dissolve suffering by letting go of “I” and “mine,” grounded in the understanding of impermanence and the rejection of ritualism and superstition. Without correct perception, meditation loses its direction and fails to lead to liberation.

—

Non-attachment is the natural outcome of seeing through the illusion of self  
The practice of meditation ultimately aims not at achieving altered states or moral superiority, but at dismantling the false sense of ownership over “I” and “mine.” When this illusion is seen clearly, attachment dissolves on its own, removing the root cause of suffering.

Impermanence is the critical lens for realizing non-self  
Understanding that all phenomena are transient — Wu Chang — is essential to perceiving the constructed nature of self. This recognition is not abstract but serves as the direct mechanism by which identification with “I” and “mine” is undone.

Right view is the indispensable foundation for liberation  
Liberation from bondage cannot occur without Zheng Jian — a clear and accurate perception of reality. This includes rejecting ritualism, superstition, and the pursuit of merit. Without it, meditation becomes directionless and ineffective, deprived of its transformative power.

Liberation requires dismantling false spiritual constructs  
True freedom emerges not through external practices or moral posturing, but through the steadfast unwavering rejection of illusions — including the belief in a permanent self or the efficacy of ritual acts. The path to freedom lies in seeing things as they are, without distortion.
