**Title: TRUE_PRACTICE and the Dissolution of the Ego as the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True liberation is achieved solely through continuous, non-judgmental awareness of the present moment, which reveals the impermanent and selfless nature of all experience. The ego — the constructed belief in a permanent, separate self — is the root cause of suffering and the sole obstacle to freedom. External rituals such as chanting, mantra recitation, or asceticism do not lead to liberation unless accompanied by inner awareness; without it, they reinforce the very illusion they are meant to transcend. True practice is not defined by form, but by the persistent dissolution of self-attachment through mindful observation of arising and passing phenomena.

---

**The Ego as the Core Demonstration of Ignorance**  
The sense of “I” and “mine” is not a natural condition but a constructed identity born from misunderstanding reality. It arises from the delusion that there exists a fixed, independent self, when all phenomena are in constant flux. This belief in separateness and permanence fuels clinging and aversion, generating sustained suffering. Liberation cannot occur until this foundational misperception is fully unraveled.

**External Rituals Without Inner Awareness Reinforce Illusion**  
Chanting, mantra recitation, and ascetic practices, when performed mechanically or as means to achieve personal gain, become extensions of ego rather than tools for its dissolution. Their value lies not in their form or frequency, but in the quality of awareness they are paired with. Without direct attention to the impermanent nature of thoughts and feelings, such activities deepen identification with the self.

**Liberation Is Only Realized Through the Complete Dissolution of Self-Attachment**  
No state, power, or experience — however profound — constitutes true freedom unless the sense of ownership over experience is absent. Liberation is not an attainment or a special condition; it is the absence of the framework that insists “I” am the experiencer. Only when all striving, self-referential clinging, and desire for personal gain cease can the mind rest in the unfiltered flow of present-moment experience.

**True Practice Is Defined by Continuous Observation of Impermanence**  
The essence of authentic cultivation is not adherence to doctrine or ritual, but the unwavering attention to the arising and passing of thoughts, emotions, and sensations — without labeling, resisting, or identifying with them. This ongoing observation dismantles the illusion of solidity in experience, naturally eroding the ego’s grip. The practice is not about achieving stillness or bliss, but about witnessing change without interpolation of self.
