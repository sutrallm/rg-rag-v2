**Title: Non-Attachment, No-Self, and the Three Poisons in the Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
True freedom arises from the complete dissolution of clinging to self and experience, achieved through the direct realization that all phenomena are impermanent and devoid of inherent identity. The root causes of cyclic suffering—greed, hatred, and delusion—are uprooted not through suppression but through wisdom that exposes their illusory nature. Meditation is effective only when it leads to non-attachment even to its own states, and the awakened individual, free from all forms of possession—including the idea of enlightenment—embodies moral purity and unshakable liberation.

---

**Impermanence Enables Non-Attachment**  
The recognition that all experiences—meditative, emotional, or perceptual—are transient dismantles the illusion of ownership. When one sees that nothing endures, the urge to grasp or reject fades naturally, as there is no stable foundation upon which to build attachment. This understanding is not abstract but experiential, forming the practical ground from which freedom emerges.

**No-Self Eliminates the Basis for Clinging**  
The conviction that the sense of “I” or “mine” is a constructed falsehood, not a fixed reality, renders clinging meaningless. When there is no enduring self to possess anything—including spiritual attainments—attachment loses its target. This realization is inseparable from the perception of impermanence, as the absence of permanence confirms the absence of an independent self.

**The Three Poisons Sustain Cyclic Existence**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the foundational forces that bind beings to recurring cycles of suffering and rebirth. They are not mere distractions but the active engines of illusion, perpetuating mistaken views of reality. Their eradication is not optional; it is the necessary condition for breaking free from samsara.

**Wisdom Dissolves the Three Poisons**  
Direct understanding of the illusory nature of experience undermines the power of desire, aversion, and ignorance. When phenomena are seen as conditional, fleeting, and selfless, the emotional and cognitive fuel feeding the three poisons vanishes. This is not a moral victory but a cognitive transformation that dissolves their grip at the root.

**Meditative Practice Requires Non-Attachment to Outcomes**  
True meditative stillness is not measured by the depth of concentration or the presence of bliss, light, or emptiness, but by the absence of ownership over these states. When even the highest meditative experiences are seen as passing conditions without identity, the practice becomes a vehicle for liberation rather than another form of spiritual grasping.

**Liberation Is Defined by Freedom from Clinging, Not Attainment**  
The state of liberation is not marked by achievement or status but by the cessation of grasping and rejection. The awakened individual does not cling to the idea of being liberated, nor to any identity as an “enlightened being.” This absence of self-referential attachment confirms the depth of realization.

**Ignorance Fuels the Cycle of Suffering**  
The fundamental error of perceiving impermanent, selfless phenomena as permanent and self-owned is the root of all bondage. This misperception gives rise to craving and aversion, which in turn sustain the endless rounds of birth and death. Only the reversal of this misapprehension can bring true release.

**Awakened Beings Embodies Purity and Non-Attachment**  
Those who have fully uprooted the three poisons live in moral clarity and mental quietude, free from the distortions of desire and self-centeredness. They are held in reverence not because of supernatural qualities, but because their conduct and presence reflect the absence of all forms of clinging, including attachment to spiritual identity.
