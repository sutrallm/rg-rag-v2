Title: The Buddha and the Self-Realization of Liberation

Summary:  
Liberation is achieved only through personal inner realization, not through external intervention. The Buddha serves as a guide who illustrates the path but cannot deliver awakening to others. True freedom arises when an individual illuminates their own mind, making any reliance on outside forces—including the Buddha—meaningless in the attainment of this state.

—  

The Buddha is a guide, not a source of salvation  
The Buddha’s role is limited to pointing the way toward awakening; he does not possess the power to grant enlightenment. His function is symbolic, like a finger directing attention to the moon, not the moon itself. Any expectation that liberation can be transferred or bestowed by him misunderstands the nature of the process.

Liberation is self-ignited  
No external entity, including the Buddha, can initiate or complete the process of liberation. It requires an inner transformation—each individual must light their own lamp. Dependence on guidance ends where personal realization begins.

Awakening and liberation are one and the same  
The terms are interchangeable, both denoting the internal realization of truth. This state ends reliance on any external aids, rituals, or figures. The moment of awakening is not an external event but a spontaneous and self-driven illumination of the mind.
