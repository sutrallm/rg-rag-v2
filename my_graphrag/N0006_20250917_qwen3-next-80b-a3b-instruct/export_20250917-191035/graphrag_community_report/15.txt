**Title: Xianxia and Zhizhi: The Path of Present-Moment Wisdom and Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**  
Spiritual practice is not confined to formal meditation but unfolds in every moment of daily life. True wisdom emerges from sustained attention to the present, where the impermanent and selfless nature of experience becomes clear. This understanding naturally dissolves attachment to meditative states and the illusion of a separate self. Diligence in everyday presence, not isolation, is the foundation for liberation.

---

**Present-Moment Awareness as the Sole Ground of Practice**  
Every breath, thought, and action is inherently part of spiritual cultivation. There is no need for special settings, rituals, or external conditions — the present moment itself contains everything required for awakening. Formal meditation is not separate from daily life but is an extension of it.

**Wisdom Arises from Observing Impermanence and Non-Self**  
Deep awareness of the present reveals that all phenomena are transient and without fixed identity. This perception is not intellectual but experiential, and it frees the practitioner from clinging to any state of mind, including those regarded as enlightened or peaceful.

**Diligence Is Continuous and Integrated into Daily Activity**  
Persistent effort is not reserved for retreats or scheduled practice periods. It is applied steadily in all activities — walking, speaking, thinking — making every moment an expression of commitment to clarity and release from delusion.

**The Dissolution of the Separate Self Through Present Engagement**  
When attention remains fully in the present, the artificial boundary between “practicing” and “being” vanishes. The thought “I am practicing” no longer arises because there is no separate “I” apart from the unfolding moment.

**The Sage Embodies Presence and Wisdom Naturally**  
The sage does not strive to attain a higher state but abides in the present as their natural condition. Their response to life is unclouded by attachment or aversion because they see reality as it is — impermanent, selfless, and unrestrained by concepts.

**Wisdom Depends on Persistent Effort in the Now**  
Without sustained attention and effort in the present moment, the understanding of reality’s true nature remains inaccessible. Delusion and attachment are overcome only through the consistent, unwavering observation of each arising experience.
