**Title: Sheng Zhe and the Wisdom of Chu Yue Zhu Zhai: Liberation Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
The enlightened being, Sheng Zhe, is defined by the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion—the Three Poisons—and embodies a state of liberation characterized by unshakable inner peace, perfect moral discipline, and profound wisdom. This state is not attained through external rituals or blessings but through the internal practice of truthful observation, rational reflection, and meditative stillness. The foundational wisdom of Chu Yue Zhu Zhai—non-attachment to all phenomena as impermanent and insubstantial—directly dismantles the roots of suffering by dissolving the illusion of self and eliminating clinging. Sheng Zhe transcends the cycle of rebirth, is untouched by desires or emotions, and exemplifies liberation as an inherent realization, not an external gift.

**Sheng Zhe Is Defined by the Eradication of the Three Poisons**  
Sheng Zhe is unmistakably marked by the total uprooting of greed, hatred, and delusion. These three afflictions, repeatedly named as San Du and Ji e, are not merely subdued but extinguished entirely. Their removal is not supplementary but central—it is the explicit criterion for being called a sage. Without this eradication, no other quality, including moral discipline or meditative concentration, constitutes true liberation.

**Liberation Arises from Inner Wisdom, Not External Practices**  
The path to freedom requires no rituals, superstitions, or external blessings. The resources for liberation are innate and activated only through truthful observation, rational reflection, and disciplined inner practice. Sheng Zhe does not seek grace or intervention from outside; their realization emerges from within, revealing that enlightenment is a process of uncovering what is already present—not acquiring something foreign.

**Chu Yue Zhu Zhai Is the Core Wisdom That Dissolves Attachment and the Illusion of Self**  
The wisdom of transcending attachment reveals that all phenomena are impermanent and insubstantial. This understanding directly dismantles the sense of self (“Wo”), which is identified as the root of clinging. By seeing reality as it is—without false identification with the five aggregates—clinging to pleasure, pain, or even spiritual experiences ceases. This insight is not abstract; it is the active mechanism that uproots greed, hatred, and delusion.

**True Liberation Is Marked by Non-Attachment to All Experiences, Including Meditative States**  
A true Sheng Zhe does not cling to calmness, peace, or any meditative attainment. Clinging—even to spiritual states—binds one to suffering. True stillness is stable only when grounded in non-attachment, ensuring that even the fruits of practice are not grasped. This complete freedom from clinging distinguishes the sage from those who mistook temporary tranquility for final liberation.

**Sheng Zhe Has Transcended Karmic Cycles Through Purification**  
By eliminating the Three Poisons and the illusion of self, Sheng Zhe has severed the karmic flow that binds beings to repeated birth, aging, sickness, and death. Their actions and mental states no longer generate the conditions for rebirth, as the root causes—ignorance, greed, hatred, and delusion—are entirely absent. Liberation from samsara is not a metaphysical claim but the direct result of inner purification.

**Moral Discipline, Wisdom, and Stillness Are Interwoven Traits of the Enlightened State**  
The perfected being is not defined by isolated virtues but by their inseparable union: perfect moral discipline, profound wisdom, and unshakable meditative concentration. These are not sequential stages but concurrent expressions of the same realized state. Wisdom sustains discipline; discipline deepens stillness; stillness reveals wisdom. Together, they form the entirety of the liberated mind.

**The Sage Embodies the Absence of the Five Desires and Emotional Equanimity**  
Sheng Zhe is completely free from the five desires, demonstrating full mastery over the senses and emotional reactivity. This freedom is not suppression but the absence of craving—arising from the direct perception of impermanence and non-self. Their equanimity is not apathy but clarity unmoved by external stimuli or internal fluctuations.
