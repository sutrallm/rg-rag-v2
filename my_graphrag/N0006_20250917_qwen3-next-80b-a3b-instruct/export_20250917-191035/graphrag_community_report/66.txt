**Title: True Stillness and Ultimate Freedom in Jingjie Practice**

**Summary**  
True stillness is cultivated not through environmental withdrawal but through inner awareness and equanimity, independent of external conditions. This state emerges from chanding and reflects complete liberation from emotional reactivity, conceptual attachments, and the fear of death. Freedom is attained only when the illusion of a separate self is dissolved, revealing an innate state of clarity, fearlessness, and desirelessness. True stillness is both the practice and the proof of this liberation.

**True Stillness Is Cultivated Through Inner Awareness, Not External Quiet**  
Stillness is not the absence of noise or turbulence in the environment, but the persistent presence of inner calm regardless of what surrounds a person. External conditions hold no inherent power to disturb or define this state—only the maintainance of awareness and equanimity does. This distinguishes true stillness from passive isolation or escape.

**Ultimate Freedom Is Achieved Through the Dissolution of the Separate Self**  
Liberation is not partial or conditional; it is complete only when the sense of being a distinct, separate entity is fully seen through. All forms of bondage—emotional reactivity, attachment to ideas, and fear of mortality—originate from clinging to the illusion of self. Removing this illusion restores a natural, unstained condition.

**True Stillness Is the Visible Expression of Ultimate Freedom**  
The ability to remain untouched by external circumstances is not merely a meditative skill but the tangible hallmark of having transcended conditioning. When a person embodies stillness amid chaos, it signifies that emotional and conceptual dependencies have been relinquished, and the innate, fearless, desireless state has been reclaimed.
