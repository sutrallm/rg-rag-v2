**Title: Jie Tuo, Zhi Hui Yu Ci Bei, and Jing Jin Xiu Xing: The Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
Liberation is attained not through isolated qualities but through the unified practice of wisdom, compassion, and unwavering diligence. These three elements are interdependent: wisdom reveals the emptiness of desire and the root of suffering, compassion executes kindness without attachment, and diligent practice sustains this alignment in daily life. Only those who consistently embody this triad transcend rebirth and mental afflictions, becoming liberated beings who remain unshaken by external conditions.

---

**Wisdom Reveals the Nature of Suffering**  
True understanding arises from recognizing that desire and attachment are the sources of suffering. This clarity does not dismiss reality but penetrates its illusory nature, dissolving the grip of craving. Such perception is not theoretical but experiential, forming the foundation for inner stillness that persists regardless of circumstance.

**Compassion Operates Free of Attachment**  
Kindness without discrimination or clinging transforms ordinary environments into spaces of sacred practice. This quality neither seeks reward nor recoils from difficulty—it responds naturally, unperturbed by personal gain or loss, and flows from the same depth of understanding that sees reality as it is.

**Diligent Practice Sustains Liberation**  
Continual, vigilant cultivation of Dharma teachings is non-negotiable. Laziness and sensual indulgence are recognized as obstacles that reinforce the cycle of birth and death. Only through relentless, daily effort can the harmonious states of wisdom and compassion be stabilized and made enduring.

**Liberation Is Defined by Integrated Qualities**  
No single attribute—wisdom or compassion alone—produces liberation. It emerges exclusively from their seamless union: one sees deeply, the other acts purely. Those who embody this balance are liberated beings, no longer subject to suffering, rebirth, or mental disturbances.

**Mindfulness Is the Foundational Path**  
The most essential practice for achieving liberation is the regular application of mindfulness in everyday experience. It is through sustained attention to the nature of presence that insight into impermanence and detachment naturally unfolds, making liberation accessible in the ordinary moment.
