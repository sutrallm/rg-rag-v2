**Title: Wu Ming and the Path to Liberation from Samsara**

**Summary:**  
Wu Ming, or ignorance, is the foundational cause of suffering and endless rebirth. It manifests as a mistaken belief in a permanent self and inherent existence, giving rise to craving, aversion, and attachment. These afflictions sustain the cycle of samsara. Liberation is achieved only through the eradication of ignorance via mindfulness, wisdom, and sustained effort, which together dissolve false perceptions and end all afflictions. Those who have completely uprooted ignorance are free from suffering and no longer bound to rebirth.

**Ignorance Fuels All Afflictions**  
Wu Ming is the originating source of Tan Ai (greed and attachment) and Gan Cheng (anger and aversion). The mistaken view that phenomena and the self are permanent leads to clinging to pleasurable experiences and resistance to unpleasant ones. These emotional reactions are not isolated but are direct consequences of misperceiving reality.

**Mindfulness and Wisdom Dissolve False Perception**  
Zhengnian (mindfulness) and wisdom work as direct counterforces to ignorance. Mindfulness illuminates the true nature of phenomena, while wisdom dismantles the delusion of inherent existence and self. Together, they dissolve the false beliefs that bind the mind to samsara.

**Liberation Comes Only Through Eradicating Ignorance**  
The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth is sustained solely by ignorance. Those who have fully uprooted it are free from desire, hatred, and all afflictions. Liberation is not achieved through partial effort or temporary suppression, but through the complete removal of the root delusion about self and reality.

**Sustained Effort Enables Progress Toward Freedom**  
Jingjin, or continuous effort, is the necessary means to overcome ignorance. Without persistent practice, the mind remains entangled in patterns of craving and aversion. Only through steady, unwavering application can one distance oneself from the mechanisms of samsara and move toward cessation of suffering.
