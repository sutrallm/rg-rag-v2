**Title: Chanding and the Imperative of Continuous Mindful Awareness in Spiritual Practice**

**Summary:**  
Sustained, unwavering mindful awareness in every moment of daily life is presented as the foundational method for cultivating Chanding. Without relentless effort, mental distractions and attachment prevail, preventing the attainment of meditative concentration, peace, or liberation. The practice is likened to the peril of walking on thin ice—failure to maintain vigilance results in spiritual collapse. Chanding cannot be achieved through sporadic effort; it demands constant, disciplined presence.

**Maintained Awareness Is the Sole Path to Chanding**  
Chanding is not developed through intermittent meditation or passive reflection but exclusively through the continuous observation of thoughts and feelings during routine activities—walking, standing, sitting, speaking, responding. This daily, moment-to-moment attention, free from attachment or aversion, is the only practical means by which Chanding is nurtured.

**Diligent Effort Prevents Spiritual Regression**  
Any lapse in consistent striving causes attention to unravel, leading to the abandonment of the path. Without persistent, non-negotiable effort, the mind defaults to distraction and attachment, undoing progress. The comparison to walking on thin ice underscores the fatal consequences of complacency—meditation yields no deep peace or liberation without this relentless discipline.

**Meditation Alone Is Insufficient Without Mindful Presence**  
Even dedicated periods of meditation fail to produce deep states of peace or liberation if not anchored in constant mindful awareness throughout the day. The practice of formal meditation is rendered ineffective without the foundation of non-stop presence in ordinary actions, making mindfulness not an accessory but the core requirement.
