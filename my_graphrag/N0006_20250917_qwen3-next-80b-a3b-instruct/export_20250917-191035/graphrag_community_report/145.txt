**Title: Transcending Attachment and the Nature of the Saintly Realm**

**Summary:**  
True freedom arises not from attaining states or experiences but from realizing the absence of a permanent self within the five constituents of existence. Saintly beings embody this understanding through unwavering ethical conduct and profound clarity, remaining undisturbed by meditative bliss, suffering, or supernatural abilities. Their liberation is complete because they do not cling—even to the idea of being liberated. This freedom stems from seeing all phenomena as impermanent and without inherent identity, dismantling the illusion of “I” that fuels suffering and cyclic existence.

---

**The Foundation of Liberation Lies in Seeing the Five Aggregates as Non-Self**  
The five constituents—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are not “I” nor “mine.” Recognizing their transient, selfless nature is the essential step toward liberation. Clinging to them as personal or enduring perpetuates bondage, while seeing them clearly ends identification with suffering and desire.

**Saintly Beings Are Defined by Non-Attachment to All Experiences, Including Spiritual Achievements**  
Those who have reached completion do not seek or hold onto meditative joy, mystical powers, or even the notion of enlightenment. Their inner stability comes from not grasping at outcomes of practice, whether pleasurable or painful. This unwavering non-clinging is what makes them objects of reverence, even among celestial beings.

**Self-Attachment Is the Root of Suffering and the Obstacle to True Freedom**  
The belief in a fixed, independent self—expressed in thoughts like “I practice,” “I attain,” or “I am enlightened”—reinforces ignorance and keeps beings trapped in cycles of craving and aversion. This illusion is not merely a mental error but the core mechanism that sustains unrest. Liberation occurs only when this illusion is fully dissolved.

**Transcending Attachment Requires Seeing All Phenomena as Illusory**  
Freedom is attained not through accumulation but through deep perception of impermanence and lack of inherent existence. When all experiences—whether mundane or spiritual—are seen as transient and empty of substance, attachment naturally falls away. This perspective is not intellectual but direct, forming the basis for effortless non-grasping.

**Ethical Conduct and Meditative Stillness Are the Expression, Not the Cause, of Liberation**  
Saintly beings maintain pure conduct and dwell in quietude not as practices to achieve freedom, but as natural results of seeing reality clearly. Their stillness reflects the absence of inner conflict; their discipline arises from no longer being driven by desire or aversion. These qualities are manifestations of prior realization, not steps toward it.

**The Path to Freedom Is Complete Only When Even Liberation Itself Is Not Clung To**  
The deepest barrier to ultimate liberation is the subtle attachment to the idea of having attained liberation. True freedom means not holding on to any state, concept, or result—even the most exalted. Only when the mind is utterly unbound, even from its own spiritual achievements, is complete openness and inherent wholeness realized.
