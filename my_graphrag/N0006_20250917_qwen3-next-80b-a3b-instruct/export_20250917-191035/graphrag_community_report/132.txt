**Title: Greed, Saints, and Transcendent Wisdom in the Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
The text presents a systematic account of how greed—rooted in attachment and fueled by delusion—binds beings to cyclic existence, and how its complete eradication through wisdom, ethical conduct, and meditative discipline defines the state of sainthood. Saints are those who have fully extinguished greed, hatred, and delusion, achieving unshakable peace and freedom from all forms of clinging, including spiritual achievements. Transcendent wisdom, which perceives the impermanent, selfless, and unsatisfactory nature of all phenomena, is the direct force that dissolves greed and delusion, enabling renunciation and liberation. This liberation is not merely an absence of desire but a stable, radiant stillness that even celestial beings revere.

---

**Greed as the Engine of Cyclic Existence**  
Greed arises from ignorance about the true nature of reality and manifests as clinging to sensory pleasures, possessions, spiritual attainments, and the notion of self. It is a primary force that sustains the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth by generating karma through attachment and craving. Without its cessation, spiritual progress remains obstructed, and the mind remains bound by dissatisfaction and unrest. Greed is not merely a moral failing but a fundamental distortion of perception, sustained by the illusion that external or internal experiences can offer lasting fulfillment.

**Saints as Embodiments of Eradicated Defilements**  
Saints are defined by the total absence of greed, hatred, and delusion. Their state is not transient calm but a stable, unshakable abiding in clarity and equanimity, untouched by internal fluctuations or external conditions. They no longer seek validation, pleasure, or even spiritual rewards, having severed attachment to every form of outcome. Their inner peace surpasses the tranquility of divine beings, not through power or status, but through the complete removal of craving and delusion. This state is the natural result of sustained practice in ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom.

**Transcendent Wisdom as the Destroyer of Clinging**  
Transcendent wisdom is the direct perception of impermanence, non-self, and unsatisfactoriness in all phenomena. This understanding dismantles the foundation of greed by exposing the futility of attachment—whether to worldly objects or meditative bliss. It does not suppress desire through force, but dissolves its basis by revealing its emptiness. This wisdom also removes delusion by correcting the mistaken view of permanence and selfhood, and it neutralizes hatred by dissolving the illusion of fixed, threatening identities. It is the final and essential factor that completes the path, rendering greed powerless and liberating the mind.

**Chanding as the Stabilizing Practice of Non-Attachment**  
Chanding is the disciplined cultivation of stillness and mental clarity, serving as the practical means through which attachment to pleasurable states is gently released. It is not merely concentration but a deep, unwavering abiding that protects the mind from being pulled by craving or distraction. Saints are distinguished by their perfected chanding—not as a fleeting state, but as a continuous, imperturbable presence. This stability arises from wisdom’s insight into the futility of clinging and is the environment in which renunciation becomes natural and effortless.

**Renunciation as the Fruit of Seeing Clearly**  
True renunciation is not forced abstinence but the spontaneous letting go that follows seeing reality as it is. It arises when one recognizes that all experiences—pleasant or painful, mundane or mystical—are impermanent and devoid of inherent self. This understanding removes the craving that fuels attachment, making renunciation not a sacrifice but a liberation. It extends beyond material possessions to include spiritual attainments, ensuring that even meditative joys do not become obstacles. Without this wisdom-based renunciation, spiritual practice remains incomplete and easily entangled in subtle forms of greed.

**Indolgence as the Silent Obstacle to Liberation**  
Spiritual laziness or laxity arises from delusion, which obscures the urgency of practice and fosters complacency. When the mind fails to see the dangerous nature of attachments, effort diminishes, and progress stalls. Indolgence prevents the cultivation of chanding, the development of wisdom, and the practice of renunciation. Overcoming it requires sustained mindfulness and the recognition that only disciplined, vigilant effort can break the cycle perpetuated by greed and ignorance.

**The Interdependence of Delusion and Greed**  
Delusion—the fundamental ignorance of reality’s true nature—is the root from which greed grows. The belief that phenomena are permanent, satisfying, or inherently “mine” creates the basis for craving. In turn, greed reinforces delusion by driving repeated attachment, which clouds further insight. This mutual reinforcement sustains the cycle of rebirth. To sever greed is to uproot delusion, and to dispel delusion is to dissolve greed. They are not separate enemies but two aspects of the same obstacle to freedom.

**Liberation Defined by the Absence of Greed**  
Liberation is not a state to be achieved through accumulation, but the complete removal of craving and clinging. It is defined by the mind’s utter freedom from seeking, grasping, or aversion—not through suppression, but through the quieting of attachment at its source. Saints embody this liberation: they are no longer moved by internal or external stimuli, because nothing remains to bind them. Their freedom is not supernatural, but natural—the consequence of seeing through illusion, fully and finally.
