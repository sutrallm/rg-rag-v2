**Title: The Path of Ji Jie, An Zhi, and Sheng Hui to Cheng Jie**

**Summary**  
Ethical conduct in body, speech, and mind forms the necessary base for achieving mental stillness, which in turn enables the direct realization of reality’s true nature—impermanence, non-self, and suffering. This transcendent understanding eradicates attachment and delusion, leading to full liberation from the cycle of rebirth. Without moral discipline, wisdom lacks depth and becomes distorted. The enlightened one embodies this complete transformation, abiding in unshakable peace.

**Ethical Conduct Anchors Mental Clarity**  
Moral discipline, structured through tri-fold practice in body, speech, and mind, is not optional but foundational. It prevents meditative states from becoming unstable or unwholesome. Without this grounding, even deep concentration cannot lead to authentic transformation, as wisdom without ethical grounding remains superficial or distorted.

**Stillness Emerges from Mindful Awareness**  
A tranquil, undisturbed mind is not achieved through force, but through sustained mindfulness. This inner stillness creates the conditions necessary for clear perception, allowing the mind to settle beyond distraction and turbulence, making way for deeper realizations.

**Wisdom Unveils Reality’s True Nature**  
Transcendent wisdom arises not as intellectual understanding but through direct perception: all phenomena are transient, devoid of a fixed self, and inherently linked to dissatisfaction. Recognizing these truths dismantles clinging, as one sees that suffering stems from attaching to what is impermanent and selfless.

**Liberation Is the Culmination of Wisdom**  
Complete freedom from the cycle of birth and death does not come from ritual or belief but from the full maturation of transcendent wisdom. Only when greed, hatred, and delusion are utterly eradicated does one attain emancipation.

**The Enlightened One Embodies the Full Path**  
The sage is defined not by titles or appearances but by the absence of afflictions and the presence of constant stillness. This state is the natural result of having seen reality clearly and released all attachments, embodying the ultimate goal of spiritual practice.
