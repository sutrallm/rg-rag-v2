**Title: The Illusion of Self and the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Suffering arises from the mistaken belief in a fixed, independent identity and the attachment to personal possessions as “mine.” This false sense of self is not real but composed of fleeting physical and mental processes. Liberation comes only when this illusion is fully recognized and abandoned, leading to the dissolution of craving, aversion, and attachment. The psychological bond between “myself” and “mine” is the root of bondage, and releasing it is essential to achieving freedom.

**The Illusion of Self as the Origin of Suffering**  
All suffering stems from misidentifying with a fabricated sense of identity that is believed to be permanent and independent. This belief is an illusion, constructed from transient phenomena, and it is the core expression of delusion. When one mistakenly takes these fleeting experiences as a stable self, it triggers emotional reactions such as craving and aversion, which perpetuate distress.

**The Interconnection of “Myself” and “Mine”**  
The psychological clinging to personal identity (“myself”) and ownership (“mine”) are inseparable aspects of egoic identification. They arise together from the same false assumption—that there is a enduring entity deserving of possession and protection. This dual fixation reinforces attachment and prevents release from emotional entanglement.

**Liberation Through the Abandonment of Egoic Identification**  
Freedom is not attained through external change but through the clear recognition and letting go of the fabricated self and its associated possessiveness. When the delusion of a permanent self and its ownership is seen for what it is—empty and illusory—attachment dissolves. This relinquishment is the sole condition for true liberation, as it eliminates the roots of suffering at their source.
