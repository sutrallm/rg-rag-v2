**Title: ZHENIAN, SHENGE, AND SHENGJIE: The Path of Mindfulness, Wisdom, and Liberation**

**Summary**  
True liberation is achieved through sustained mindfulness, which cultivates wisdom that sees all phenomena as impermanent and selfless. This wisdom dissolves attachment—including to spiritual goals—and extinguishes greed, anger, and delusion. The enlightened being lives in unshakable peace, free from all dualities, not through external rituals or isolation, but through constant inner awareness and non-attachment. Liberation is not an endpoint to be clung to, but the natural result of seeing reality clearly.

---

**Mindfulness Sustains All Spiritual Progress**  
Continuous moment-to-moment awareness of bodily and mental experience in daily activities acts as the foundational practice. It is not a ritual but an unceasing attitude of clear observation. This mindfulness anchors the mind in the present, preventing fixation on past or future, and provides the stable base for deeper wisdom. Without it, the illusion of self cannot be deconstructed, and no other practice can take root.

**Wisdom Arises Only Through Direct Observation**  
Transcendent wisdom emerges from seeing the impermanent, selfless, and empty nature of all phenomena. This understanding is not abstract but arises directly from mindful observation. It dissolves clinging not only to worldly objects but also to meditative attainments and even the concept of liberation itself. True wisdom is experiential, not doctrinal, and is the sole cause of release from suffering.

**Liberation Is Freedom from All Clinging, Including Spiritual Goals**  
Final emancipation is attained when even the attachment to Nirvana and the idea of being liberated is abandoned. This state is not passive resignation but active freedom from all forms of grasping—whether toward gain or loss, pleasure or pain. Liberation is complete only when the mind no longer identifies with any state, achievement, or identity, including enlightenment.

**The Enlightened Being Embodies Non-Attachment and Inner Stillness**  
The awakened individual is marked by pure conduct, extinguished afflictions, and unshakable peace. They are unaffected by emotions or external conditions, residing beyond duality. Their presence is defined by the absence of greed, anger, and delusion, and their actions reflect non-attachment without indifference—responding clearly and freely, like a mirror that reflects without holding.

**True Practice Requires No External Form**  
Spiritual maturity is not dependent on location, ritual, or appearance. Inner stillness and continuous awareness are sufficient regardless of environment. Meditation is not confined to seated posture or isolated retreats; it is the unbroken presence of mindful awareness in all actions and situations. This redefines spiritual discipline as an internal orientation, not an external performance.

**Letting Go Is the Natural Expression of Seeing Non-Self**  
Non-attachment is not emotional detachment but the spontaneous release of clinging that follows from recognizing the absence of a fixed self. When one sees clearly that “I” and “mine” are illusions, attachment loses its foundation. This letting go is cultivated through mindfulness and flows naturally from wisdom—action unbound by desire or aversion.

**Constant Effort Is the Engine of Progress**  
Progress toward liberation demands unceasing diligence. This sustained effort manifests as mindfulness in every moment, maintaining ethical conduct and meditative stability without lapse. It is not occasional practice but the daily, hourly, and continuous reinforcement of awareness and non-grasping. Without this persistent cultivation, stagnation and regression are inevitable.

**The Three Poisons Must Be Fully Extinguished**  
Greed, anger, and delusion are the roots of all suffering and bondage. Their complete eradication is the defining mark of awakening. No degree of meditative absorption or intellectual understanding substitutes for their total removal. Only when the mind is free from these three can true peace and liberation arise.

**Mindfulness Directly Enables Wisdom and Liberation**  
The practice of continuous mindfulness is not merely preparatory—it is the living mechanism through which wisdom develops and liberation unfolds. By observing the arising and passing of all phenomena, the mind naturally sees impermanence and non-self, which dissolves illusion and breaks the cycle of rebirth. Without mindfulness, wisdom cannot arise; with it, liberation is inevitable.
