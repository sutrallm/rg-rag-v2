**Title: Transcending Attachment Through Internal Observation and Chan Ding**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is achieved not through external practices such as rituals or mantras, but through internal observation—mindfully witnessing the arising and passing of thoughts. This practice cultivates Chan Ding, a state of meditative clarity that reveals the impermanent, suffering, and non-self nature of all experiences. **Meditative Clarity Reveals the Illusory Nature of Experience**  
Chan Ding, developed through internal observation, allows the practitioner to perceive thoughts and sensations as transient and insubstantial. This clarity dismantles the illusion that experiences are stable, inherently satisfying, or self-owned. As a result, attachment naturally loosens without force or resistance.

**Internal Observation Is the Sole Path to Liberation**  
No external practice—whether rituals, mantras, or offerings—leads to true liberation. Only the continuous, mindful awareness of each thought as it emerges and fades enables the direct realization of the mind’s true dynamics. This is the only method described as authentic and effective.

**Transcending Attachment Erases Delusion**  
Letting go of clinging to meditative bliss, powers, or achievements is not a passive act but the natural outcome of seeing phenomena as impermanent and non-self. This perception directly opposes ignorance by replacing false beliefs about reality with unmediated awareness, thereby dissolving the roots of suffering.

**Mindfulness of Thoughts Cultivates Meditative Concentration**  
The practice of observing thoughts—not suppressing or analyzing them, but simply noting their flow—is the mechanism that builds and sustains Chan Ding. This ongoing attention is the only activity required to develop the depth of concentration that leads to transcendence.
