**Title: Neiguan as the Path to Liberation from Lunhui**

**Summary**  
Liberation from cyclic suffering (lunhui) is achieved solely through neiguan—the direct, non-judgmental observation of bodily and mental phenomena. This practice causes the dissolution of the illusion of self (wuwo) and reveals the impermanent nature of all experience (wuchang). Supporting practices—chanding, jingjin, and zaizhi—enable neiguan by quelling mental agitation and overcoming desire and laziness, but only inner observation delivers true realization. Rituals, mantras, and external worship are explicitly rejected as ineffective.

---

**The Sole Authentic Path to Liberation**  
Neiguan is identified as the only authentic method for escaping lunhui. All other approaches—rituals, mantras, and external worship—are dismissed as inadequate. Liberation arises not through external acts but through the sustained, clear observation of internal processes as they arise and pass.

**Inner Observation Reveals Non-Self**  
Through persistent neiguan, individuals directly perceive that thoughts and sensations are transient and impersonal. This perception dismantles the illusion of a permanent self (wuwo), revealing that no enduring identity exists within any experience. This realization is not conceptual but experiential, emerging solely from direct observation.

**Meditative Concentration Emerges from Observation**  
Chanding, the practice of quieting the mind, does not stand alone as a goal but functions to enable neiguan. The stillness it produces allows for sustained, clear attention to bodily and mental phenomena—making observation the mechanism through which concentration deepens, not a separate state to be attained.

**Impermanence Is Directly Perceived Through Observation**  
The nature of all sensory and mental phenomena (wuchang) is not understood through doctrine but through the continuous practice of neiguan. The fleeting, unstable quality of every sensation and thought becomes evident only when observed without interference, making observation the唯—means of recognizing impermanence.

**Overcoming Desire and Laziness Breaks the Cycle**  
Jingjin is necessary to sever the bonds of samsara, specifically by confronting the emotional and motivational forces—desire and laziness—that sustain cyclic existence. Without addressing these, even skilled observation cannot take root, as they actively obstruct the clarity required for liberation.

**Liberation Requires Seeing Reality Without Delusion**  
True freedom comes from experiencing reality as it is, unfiltered by belief, craving, or aversion. Neiguan alone delivers this clarity, because it removes the distortions of mind and allows phenomena to be witnessed in their raw, unaltered state—making it the only method capable of ending suffering.
