**Title: Sheng Hui, Ku, Wu Chang, and Wu Wo: The Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is attainable through the full cultivation of transcendent wisdom, which is grounded in direct recognition of impermanence, non-self, and suffering. These three characteristics of existence are interrelated: suffering emerges from clinging to what is transient and without a fixed self. Without ethical conduct, wisdom loses its depth and becomes ineffective. True understanding arises not through intellectual abstraction, but through direct perception of reality as it is.

**Transcendent wisdom arises from direct perception of impermanence**  
The nature of all phenomena is continually changing, and this is not a theoretical concept but a perceptual reality. Recognizing this constant flux is central to transcendent wisdom, as it undermines the illusion of stability that fuels attachment.

**Non-self is recognized in the flow of mental and physical phenomena**  
No enduring, independent self exists within the stream of experience. This realization dismantles the foundation of ego-based clinging, as all components of identity are seen as transient and conditioned.

**Suffering originates from attachment to impermanent and selfless phenomena**  
Dissatisfaction is not inherent in external conditions but in the mind’s resistance to their transient and selfless nature. Clinging to what cannot be held—whether objects, identities, or experiences—generates persistent unease.

**Ethical conduct is a necessary foundation for authentic wisdom**  
Without moral grounding, the understanding of impermanence and non-self becomes detached, superficial, or misapplied. Wisdom cannot flourish in isolation from upright behavior.

**Liberation is the outcome of fully developed transcendent wisdom**  
When the seen, felt, and known are fully understood through the lens of impermanence and non-self, attachment dissolves, and the cycle of suffering ends. This liberation is not an aspiration but the natural result of clear seeing.
