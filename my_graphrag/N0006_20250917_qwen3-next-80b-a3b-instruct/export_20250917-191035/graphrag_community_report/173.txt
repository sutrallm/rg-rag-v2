**Title: Wuwo Zhihui and the Path of Non-Attachment through Interdependent Origination and Meditative Concentration**

**Summary**  
The realization that the self is illusory forms the core of Wuwo zhihui, which is cultivated through meditative concentration and rooted in the understanding that all phenomena arise dependently and lack inherent existence. Transcending attachment—whether to material objects or spiritual experiences—is the natural expression of this understanding, as it arises from seeing all things as impermanent and selfless, not as possessions or permanent entities.

**The Wisdom of Non-Self Arises from Interdependent Origination**  
The notion that “I” is illusory is not an abstract idea but a direct consequence of recognizing that every phenomenon emerges only through a web of interrelated causes and conditions. Nothing exists independently or possesses an unchanging self-nature. This understanding undermines the belief in a fixed, separate self and forms the foundation for Wuwo zhihui.

**Meditative Concentration Enables the Realization of Non-Self**  
Chanding is not merely a technique but the essential practice through which the intellectual understanding of non-self becomes direct experience. It is the means by which one moves beyond conceptual thinking to embody the view that all phenomena are interdependently arisen and devoid of inherent existence.

**Transcending Attachment Is the Manifestation of Non-Self Understanding**  
When one sees all phenomena—including spiritual states such as bliss or supernatural powers—as transient and selfless, clinging naturally drops away. This release is not forced suppression but the spontaneous outcome of seeing things as they are: conditioned, fleeting, and not owned. Thus, detachment is not a goal apart from wisdom but its living expression.
