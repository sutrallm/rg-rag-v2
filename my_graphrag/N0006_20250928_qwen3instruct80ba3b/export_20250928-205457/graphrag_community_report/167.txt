**Title: Transcending Attachment Through Zhi Guan: The Role of Impermanence and Non-Self in Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation arises from the direct realization that all phenomena—包括 spiritual experiences—are impermanent and lack an enduring self. This understanding dismantles attachment and the afflictions rooted in greed, hatred, and delusion. The dual practice of calm abiding and penetrating insight (Zhi Guan) enables this realization, with Chan Ding providing the mental stability necessary for observing the transient and selfless nature of all experiences. Right Mindfulness interrupts the cycle of afflictions, while indulgence accelerates their growth. Neither spiritual powers nor meditative bliss qualify as liberation if clung to, as they too are conditioned and fleeting.

**Observation of Impermanence Undermines All Forms of Clinging**  
All phenomena, including spiritual attainments like bliss, tranquility, and supernatural powers, arise and cease without enduring substance. Recognizing this constant flux reveals the illusory basis of attachment—whether to material objects, emotional states, or spiritual experiences. Since attachment to anything impermanent generates suffering, seeing impermanence directly weakens the impulse to grasp, even toward the most refined meditative states.

**Non-Self Realization Dissolves the Root of Suffering**  
There is no permanent, independent, or controlling self within the aggregates of body and mind. All experiences arise interdependently and are devoid of self-nature. This understanding is not abstract but must be directly observed through the lens of impermanence. When the illusion of a fixed self is seen through, the conviction that “I” am the experiencer of pleasure or pain collapses—removing the foundation of craving and aversion.

**Calm Abiding Enables Penetrative Observation of Reality**  
Mental stability (Chan Ding) is not an end in itself but the essential ground from which penetrating insight emerges. Without this calm, the mind cannot sustain clear observation of arising and ceasing phenomena. Calm abiding supports the practice of Zhi Guan by preventing distraction, allowing the mind to perceive the true nature of experience without interference from agitation or dullness.

**Afflictions Arise from and Reinforce Delusion and Indulgence**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are not isolated emotions but manifestations of the foundational error of believing in a self. This delusion is perpetuated by indulgence—the neglect of diligent practice—which allows afflictions to grow unchecked. Indulgence and afflictions feed each other: the more one indulges, the stronger the afflictions become; the stronger the afflictions, the harder it is to maintain practice.

**Right Mindfulness Prevents the Proliferation of Afflictions**  
By observing thoughts, feelings, and sensations as they arise without reaction, mindfulness interrupts their habitual escalation. This practice does not suppress afflictions but allows them to be seen as transient events, preventing their consolidation into grasping or aversion. It is through this non-reactive awareness that the cycle of clinging is gradually undone.

**Spiritual Powers Are Not Liberation, But Further Objects to Be Untangled From**  
Supernatural abilities that arise in deep meditation are conditioned phenomena—subject to impermanence and devoid of self. Their occurrence does not indicate spiritual attainment, and attachment to them compounds delusion. Transcending attachment means seeing even these extraordinary states as empty of lasting value, reinforcing that liberation lies not in experience, but in the cessation of clinging to all experience.
