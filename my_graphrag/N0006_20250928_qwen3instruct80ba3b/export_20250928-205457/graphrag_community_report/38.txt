**Title: Niepan and the Path of the Shengzhe: Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
The ultimate state of liberation, Niepan, is the complete cessation of suffering, the cycle of rebirth, and the three root afflictions—greed, hatred, and delusion. It is not a place, a goal, or a conditioned state to be acquired, but the immediate, unconstructed realization of a mind free from clinging, seeking, and conceptual fabrication. This state is realized exclusively through wisdom that perceives the impermanent, non-self nature of all phenomena, supported by the structured path of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and liberating insight. The perfected being, known as Shengzhe or Jue Xing Zhe, embodies this state: equanimous, unattached, and free from all striving, including the desire for liberation itself. All practices—morality, concentration, mindfulness, and letting go—are means to dissolve attachment and ignorance, culminating in the realization that no self exists to be liberated and nothing needs to be attained.

---

**The Threefold Training is the Only Path to Niepan**  
Moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom form an indivisible, sequential structure that leads to liberation. None of the three can succeed alone; discipline purifies behavior, concentration steadies the mind, and wisdom uproots ignorance. This structured progression, known as Jieming Sanxue, is the exclusive means through which the defilements of greed, hatred, and delusion are extinguished. Without the full integration of these stages, liberation cannot arise.

**Niepan Is Not Attained—It Is Realized Through Non-Attachment**  
Niepan is not a new state to be gained, a reward for effort, or a condition reached through rituals, powers, or doctrinal adherence. It is the unveiling of what has always been present: a mind free from clinging. The realization that nothing has been lost, nothing needs to be acquired, and no self exists to attain anything—known as Wusuode—defines this state. Attempting to "achieve" Nirvana reinforces the very attachment that obscures it.

**Wisdom Is the Direct Cause of Liberation**  
Only through direct, non-conceptual insight—Chizhou or Zhengjian—into impermanence, suffering, and non-self can ignorance be uprooted. This wisdom does not come from study, ceremony, or external practices, but from unmediated observation of reality. Without this insight, even deep meditative states remain incomplete. Wisdom dissolves the illusion of permanence and self, which is the foundation of all suffering.

**Non-Attachment Is the Defining Characteristic of Niepan**  
Niepan is synonymous with Wuzuo: the absence of grasping and rejecting. It is not passive indifference but a dynamic freedom from all forms of clinging—including attachment to peace, enlightenment, or even the idea of non-attachment. The mind in this state neither seeks nor avoids; it responds without identification or aversion. This is also called Wuzhu xin or the non-abiding mind.

**The Shengzhe Embodies the Final Realization**  
The Shengzhe and Jue Xing Zhe are one: the fully awakened being whose mind is like empty space—receptive, undisturbed, and unconditioned by fortune or misfortune. Having eradicated delusion and all craving, they dwell in complete freedom from rebirth and suffering. Their presence reflects the end of striving; they neither cling to life nor fear death, having abandoned the illusion of ownership, achievement, or identity.

**Letting Go (Puta) Is Liberation Itself**  
To let go is not a step toward Nirvana—it is Nirvana. The cessation of clinging to self, outcomes, spiritual states, or even the path itself is the immediate realization of freedom. PuTa is not a technique but the natural result of seeing through illusion. When the mind no longer identifies with "I," "mine," or "achiever," liberation is present.

**The Cycle of Rebirth Ceases with the Extinction of Delusion**  
Birth and death (Shengsi Lunliu) are sustained by ignorance (Chi) and clinging (Zezhuo). When the mistaken belief in a permanent self and eternal phenomena is completely dissolved, the chain of dependent origination breaks. The cessation of delusion directly ends the cycle, not through force, but through understanding.

**Rituals and External Forms Cannot Lead to Liberation**  
True awakening does not depend on mantras, offerings, ceremonies, or outward displays of piety. Even the strongest discipline or most profound meditation, if rooted in seeking, remains conditioned. Liberation arises only when internal transformation—the abolition of ignorance and the end of craving—takes place. This is the meaning of Zhihui Chaoguo Yishi.

**The Mind Is Already Free—Liberation Is the Removal of Obstruction**  
Niepan is not something created, earned, or inherited. It is the unconditioned nature of mind, obscured only by clinging and conceptual fabrication. When all forms of attachment—including the desire to attain Nirvana—are dissolved, the mind naturally abides as it always has: complete, unaltered, and at peace. This is Wusuode: nothing gained, nothing lost.

**Non-Seeking (Wusuoqiu) Is the Final Expression of Nirvana**  
True peace is found when the mind ceases all craving—including the craving for awakening. The Shengzhe does not desire freedom, rebirth, recognition, or even continued existence. The absence of seeking is not apathy; it is the fullness of freedom, where no inner tension or yearning remains. This is the state described as Wusuoqiu—the ultimate tranquility.
