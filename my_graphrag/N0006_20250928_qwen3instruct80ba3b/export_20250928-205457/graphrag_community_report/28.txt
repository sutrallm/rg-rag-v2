**Title: Jiexiang, Ziying, and Siling: The Path to Liberation in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (jiexiang) is achieved through the dissolution of craving, aversion, and delusion, enabled by wisdom (zhihui) rooted in personal experience (ziying) and sustained by the contemplation of death (siling). It is not granted by external authority or textual study but requires direct, self-verified understanding of non-self (wuwo) and impermanence. Ethical conduct (jie) and mental focus (jingjin) are essential supports, with death serving not as an endpoint but as a motivator for unwavering practice. Those who have fully realized this state—shengzhe—are free from all suffering.

**Ethical Conduct Is Indispensable but Not Sufficient for Liberation**  
Jie forms a foundational element on the path to jiexiang. While it cannot alone bring about liberation, its presence is mandatory for progress. Without it, the necessary conditions for deeper practice do not arise, making it a non-negotiable prerequisite.

**Wisdom Must Be Personally Verified, Not Borrowed**  
Zhihui arises only through direct, internal realization and cannot be transmitted by texts or teachers alone. Ziying emphasizes that true understanding is self-verified, requiring the practitioner to confront experience firsthand rather than rely on faith in external sources.

**Death Is a Catalyst for Practice, Not an End**  
Siling is not viewed as final cessation but as a natural transition within dependent origination. Contemplating death deepens mental focus (jingjin) and prevents complacency, propelling practitioners to engage with their path more urgently and authentically.

**Non-Self Is the Direct Key to Dissolving Attachment**  
Recognizing wuwo—the absence of a permanent, independent self—directly undermines clinging. This understanding breaks the cycle of attachment that sustains suffering and is the very mechanism through which jiexiang manifests.

**Wisdom Extinguishes Clinging Through Insight into Impermanence and Non-Self**  
Zhihui does not function abstractly; it operates by revealing the transient and selfless nature of all phenomena. This revelation leads to detachment, extinguishing the roots of craving and resulting in liberation.

**Liberation Is Achieved Only Through One’s Own Practice**  
Ziying establishes that jiexiang cannot be granted by authority, ritual, or doctrine. It is the personal, experiential unfolding of understanding that alone allows for transcendence, making internal effort the sole pathway to freedom.

**Those Who Have Attained Liberation Are Beyond All Defilements**  
Shengzhe represent the realized state: free from greed, hatred, and delusion, and no longer bound by the cycle of birth and death. Their existence confirms the possibility of jiexiang and serves as the definitive outcome of the path outlined.
