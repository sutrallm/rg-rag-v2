Title: The Nature of Liberation and the Obstruction of Legal Attachment

Summary: True liberation requires transcending not only worldly attachments but also the conceptual attachment to enlightenment itself. The very pursuit and fixation on spiritual results constitute the obstruction it seeks to overcome.

—

The pursuit of spiritual attainment is itself a form of attachment  
Fixating on the outcome of practice—such as the notion of liberation—mirrors the very bondage one aims to escape. This attachment to results, even those framed as noble or spiritual, does not dissolve the underlying conditioning but reinforces it.

  
The concept of liberation cannot be clung to without undermining its purpose  
To hold onto the idea of liberation as a goal or achievement is to remain bound by mental constructs. True transcendence involves letting go not only of material desires but also of the doctrinal framework that defines release, rendering the notion of “liberation” itself irrelevant in the final state.
