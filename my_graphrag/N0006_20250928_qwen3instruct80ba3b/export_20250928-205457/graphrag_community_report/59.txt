**Title: Sheli, Wuzhu, and Jingji: The Path to Liberation Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary**  
True freedom arises from the systematic letting go of all forms of clinging—whether to material gains, spiritual achievements, or the very idea of liberation. The progression from renunciation (sheli) to non-abiding mind (wuzhu) to true stillness (jingji) constitutes a unified path wherein each stage deepens the absence of attachment. Wisdom (zhihui) underlies this process, revealing the impermanence and egoless nature of all phenomena, rendering clinging unnecessary. Liberation (jiede) is not a destination to be grasped but the natural state of a mind unbound by any condition or concept.

---

**Renunciation of Worldly and Spiritual Attachments Creates the Foundation for Inner Stability**  
True renunciation (sheli) is not physical withdrawal but the conscious release of attachment to fame, wealth, power, spiritual states, and even the notion of non-attachment itself. This letting go is driven by the recognition that all phenomena are impermanent and devoid of self. Without this release, pursuits tied to external validation or internal attainments obstruct meditative stability and clarity, making sheli the necessary preconditions for deeper transformation.

**Non-Abiding Mind Embodies the Final Expression of Wisdom and Freedom**  
The non-abiding mind (wuzhu) arises when wisdom fully permeates perception, revealing that even liberation must not be clung to. This state transcends all dualities—pleasure and pain, gain and loss, existence and non-existence—remaining open and ungrasping like empty space. Wuzhu is not an abstract ideal but the lived reality of a mind free from aversion, craving, and conceptual fixation. It is the direct manifestation of perfected wisdom and the defining quality of true liberation (jiede).

**True Stillness Is the Natural Outcome of a Non-Clinging Mind**  
Inner calm (jingji) is not achieved through silence or isolation but through the unshakable presence of a mind that does not react to external noise, adversity, or changing circumstances. This stillness emerges only when the mind has fully embodied non-abiding (wuzhu), as it no longer grasps at favorable conditions or recoils from unfavorable ones. Jingji is the experiential confirmation that liberation is not a distant goal but a present, unmoved awareness untouched by the flux of the world.
