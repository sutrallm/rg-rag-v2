**Title: The Buddha’s Path to Self-Reliant Liberation in Chanding Practice**

**Summary**  
The Buddha is the sole originator of the path to nirvana, which is achieved exclusively through personal effort. His teachings, forming the authoritative basis for chanding, stress mindfulness, non-attachment, and direct insight into impermanence, non-self, and dependent origination. Liberation is not granted by divine intervention, rituals, or teacherly authority but is earned through the practitioner’s own mental purification. Trust in the path arises only after firsthand experience of its outcomes.

**The Buddha as the Foundational Guide**  
The Buddha is recognized as the discoverer and teacher of the path to nirvana, and his doctrine serves as the sole authoritative source for chanding. His role is not to confer enlightenment but to outline the method by which it may be realized. His teachings on ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom structure the practice and are treated as indispensable.

**Liberation Requires Personal Effort**  
Nirvana cannot be bestowed by gods, teachers, or rituals. It is attained only when the individual eliminates delusions and purifies the mind through their own consistent practice. This self-reliant nature of liberation means that no external agent can substitute for the practitioner’s inner work.

**Chanding as the Embodiment of Buddha’s Method**  
Chanding is taught by the Buddha as a core practice for mental purification and liberation. It is directly grounded in his teachings, particularly the cultivation of insight into anatta (non-self), impermanence, and dependent origination. The method derives its validity and structure entirely from his guidance.

**Faith Arises from Direct Experience**  
Trust in the path is not based on doctrine or authority alone but on the practitioner’s firsthand encounters with its results. Only after personally witnessing the transformation of the mind through practice does genuine conviction emerge, reinforcing the necessity of individual engagement over passive belief.
