**The Seven Steps of Buddhist Cultivation: Jiude, Ding, and Hui as the Path to Liberation**

Summary:  
The sequence of moral purity, meditative concentration, and liberating wisdom forms an unbroken developmental chain leading to nirvana. Ethical conduct is the indispensable starting point, enabling mental stability, which in turn gives rise to wisdom. Each stage is causally dependent on the prior one, with no progression possible without fulfillment of the preceding condition. This structured path defines the complete process of spiritual transformation in traditional Buddhist practice.

Moral purity is the foundation for mental stability  
Ethical discipline in action and speech must precede any attainment of meditative concentration. Without purified conduct, the mind cannot settle into the stability required for deeper practice. This order is not optional but structural: moral purity creates the necessary conditions for concentration to emerge.

Meditative concentration enables the emergence of wisdom  
The clarity and steadiness cultivated through concentration serve as the direct cause for wisdom to arise. Wisdom does not appear independently or through intellectual effort alone; it emerges naturally as a consequence of sustained mental focus established in the previous stage.

The sequence culminates in liberation  
Each stage—moral conduct, concentration, wisdom—builds upon the one before, forming a defined progression that leads to the final goal. Liberation is not an isolated achievement but the inevitable result of completing the full sequence, making the process a coherent and irreversible pathway.
