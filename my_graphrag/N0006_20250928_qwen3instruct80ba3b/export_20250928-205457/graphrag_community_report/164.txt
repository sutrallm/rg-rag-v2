**Title: Energetic Practice and Chan Ding as Pathways to Liberation Through No-Self Realization**

**Summary**  
Sustained, effortful spiritual practice is indispensable for cultivating Chan Ding—a stable, focused mental state—that enables wise observation of reality. This observation reveals the impermanent, suffering, and non-self nature of all phenomena, dissolving the illusion of a permanent identity and thereby liberating the practitioner. Teachings serve only as guiding tools; attachment to them impedes awakening. Without rigorous energy and vigilance, the mind succumbs to dullness and distraction, blocking the arising of transformative realization.

**Effortful Practice Prevents Mental Laxity and Enables Chan Ding**  
Without consistent, vigilant effort, the mind becomes lazy, distracted, or sunk in ignorance. This energetic discipline is not optional—it is the foundation required to establish and maintain Chan Ding. Laxity directly undermines the mental clarity needed for deeper realization, making sustained effort a non-negotiable condition for progress.

**Chan Ding Serves as the Stable Ground for Wise Observation**  
Chan Ding provides the undisturbed mental platform from which clear, direct observation arises. It is not the goal itself but the necessary condition that allows the practitioner to perceive phenomena as impermanent, conditioned, and devoid of self. Without this stability, the mind cannot sustain the focus required to see beyond surface appearances.

**Wise Observation Leads to Liberation Through Non-Self Realization**  
The direct seeing of all phenomena as impermanent, suffering, and without inherent self dissolves the false sense of “I” and “mine.” This realization is not intellectual but experiential, and it is the decisive factor in ending suffering. It does not depend on meditative states or concepts of liberation—it transcends them by seeing their conditioned nature.

**Teachings Are Means, Not Ends**  
Doctrinal instructions and practical methods function as tools—like a finger pointing to the moon—to guide the practitioner toward direct realization. Clinging to these teachings as ultimate truths obstructs awakening. Their value lies solely in their capacity to lead beyond themselves, not in their form or content.

**Continuous Mindfulness Reveals Conditioned Nature and Dissolves Self-Attachment**  
Through unwavering attention, the practitioner observes how all mental and physical experiences arise from interdependent causes. This ongoing observation gradually weakens attachment to a fixed identity, culminating in the dissolution of the illusion of self. Liberation is not attained by force but by unmasking the false through sustained clarity.
