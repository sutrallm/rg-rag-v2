**Title: Wu Li, Chanding, and Zezhu in the Threefold Path to Nie Pan**

**Summary**  
The path to Nie Pan requires the balanced cultivation of five spiritual faculties—Faith, Diligence, Mindfulness, Concentration, and Wisdom—across the three stages of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Deep concentration enables the recognition of impermanence, suffering, and non-self, which culminates in non-attachment to even spiritual experiences. This non-attachment, rooted in insight into the emptiness of self, is the direct means to liberation. All stages and faculties are interdependent; imbalance leads to stagnation, while harmony ensures progress toward the ultimate goal.

---

**Balance Among the Five Spiritual Faculties is Essential for Unimpeded Progress**  
Faith, Diligence, Mindfulness, Concentration, and Wisdom must be equally developed like the two wheels of a cart or the two wings of a bird. Without balance, particularly between Concentration and Wisdom, progress stalls. Mindfulness sustains continuous awareness, while Wisdom prevents spiritual experiences from becoming objects of clinging. Each faculty supports the others across all stages of practice.

**Deep Concentration Enables the Realization of Non-Attachment**  
Chanding, as the second stage of the threefold path, provides the stable mental foundation from which Zezhu emerges. Only through sustained concentration can one clearly perceive the impermanent, suffering, and non-self nature of all phenomena, including bliss and psychic powers. This clarity dissolves attachment even to spiritual attainments.

**Non-Attachment to Spiritual Experiences is the Direct Route to Liberation**  
Zezhu—the letting go of all clinging, even to meditative bliss or powers—arises not from rejection but from seeing the emptiness of self. This cognitive shift, grounded in Wu Wo Guan, transforms spiritual practice from acquisition to release. Liberation is achieved not by accumulating experiences, but by releasing all forms of attachment.

**The Threefold Path Structures the Cultivation of Balance and Realization**  
Moral discipline (戒) establishes the foundation. Concentration (定) deepens mental stability and clarity. Wisdom (慧) dissolves delusion by revealing the true nature of reality. These stages are sequential yet interwoven, with the five spiritual faculties actively nurtured throughout. The culmination of this path is Nie Pan, realized only when wisdom transforms concentration into non-attachment.
