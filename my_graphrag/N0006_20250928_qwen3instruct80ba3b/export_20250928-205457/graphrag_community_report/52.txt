**Title: Chizhu and Buzhizhixing in the Path to Niépán**

**Summary**  
True spiritual realization is marked by the absence of clinging—to meditative states, to the notion of practice, and to the idea of attainment. This state is forged through the direct recognition of impermanence, non-self, and suffering, leading to a natural dissolution of all self-centered frameworks. The highest expression of this progression is the complete absence of any conceptual anchor, including the concept of non-attachment itself, which defines the condition of Niépán.

**Chizhu Emerges from the Realization of Wuwo**  
The release from attachment to meditative experiences, outcomes, or escape from samsara arises not through effortful discipline but from the direct perception that no inherent self exists to hold onto anything. When the illusion of a static, independent identity dissolves, there is no longer a subject to cling to states of peace, enlightenment, or avoidance—even the desire to be free becomes irrelevant.

**Chanding Enables Chizhu by Dissolving Meditative Clinging**  
Deep stillness of mind creates the clarity necessary to recognize that even the most refined meditative states are impermanent and lack intrinsic value. This awareness prevents the practitioner from mistaking temporary mental conditions for final liberation, ensuring that spiritual experiences do not become subtle forms of attachment disguised as progress.

**Buzhizhixing Is the Final Expression of Non-Attachment**  
The complete absence of the thought “I am practicing” or “I have attained” is not a stage to be reached, but the natural outcome of true non-attachment. Even the conceptual framework of non-attachment must be let go; the moment one clings to the idea of having achieved freedom, the self reasserts itself. This is the defining mark of Niépán—no trace remains of striving, achieving, or identifying with any spiritual position.

**Buzhizhixing Manifests as the Absence of All Notions of Attainment**  
When no conceptual structure—whether of practice, progress, or liberation—remains in the mind, the interpretive mechanism of the self ceases to operate. This is not a passive state of emptiness but the active absence of all self-referential framing, leaving only undisturbed awareness without object, goal, or identity.
