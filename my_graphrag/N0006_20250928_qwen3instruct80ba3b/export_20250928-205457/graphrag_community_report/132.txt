**Title: The Path of Non-Attachment: Wisdom, Sensory Desire, and Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True liberation arises not from suppressing desire or pursuing meditative bliss, but from recognizing the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all experiences—whether sensory or spiritual. Attachment, even to spiritual achievements, perpetuates bondage. Wisdom, cultivated through stable mental focus, reveals the true character of phenomena, enabling natural disengagement. The sage transcends all forms of craving, including attachment to enlightenment itself, by observing without clinging.

**Sensory Pleasures Are Illusory Traps**  
Pleasurable sensory experiences, though momentarily appealing, are inherently harmful because they induce craving. This craving binds individuals to suffering and cyclic existence. The comparison to honey on a razor blade illustrates how the allure of such experiences conceals their destructive nature. Recognizing their impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless character allows for release without forceful resistance or passive indulgence.

**Meditative States Are Not the Goal**  
Mental stability provides the foundation for deeper understanding, but it does not guarantee liberation. Without wisdom, meditative bliss can become another object of attachment, trapping the practitioner in a refined form of bondage. True freedom emerges only when wisdom prevents clinging even to the most refined spiritual states.

**Impermanence Is the Key to Release**  
The fundamental observation of all phenomena as transient dissolves the root of clinging. This awareness applies equally to material pleasures and spiritual accomplishments. When seen as fleeting, nothing remains worth holding onto, making attachment unnecessary and unnatural.

**Wisdom Neutralizes Greed at Its Source**  
The craving for pleasurable experiences—identified as greed—is the mechanism that binds being to suffering. Wisdom functions not by opposing desire, but by revealing its true nature as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and without self. This understanding naturally ends the urge to grasp, rendering the antidote inherent in perception itself.

**The Sage Transcends All Attachments**  
The fully developed individual observes all phenomena—sensory, emotional, or spiritual—without attachment. Even the experience of spiritual attainment is seen as transient and not-self. This non-clinging is the mark of complete freedom, where no form of experience, however elevated, can ensnare the mind.
