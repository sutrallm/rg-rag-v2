Title: The Fragility of Spiritual Progress and the Necessity of Daily Practice

Summary:  
Sustained spiritual advancement demands unwavering daily effort. Any interruption—no matter how brief—erases progress accumulated over time. Progress is not cumulative in the conventional sense; it is maintained only through consistent, uninterrupted practice. Without this discipline, regression is inevitable, and liberation remains unattainable.

Daily Practice Is the Sole Criterion for Sustained Advancement  
Spiritual growth does not accumulate through sporadic or intermittent effort. It is entirely dependent on continuous daily action. The metaphor of dripping water wearing through stone illustrates that slow, persistent exertion, not intensity or irregular bursts, is what leads to transformation. Without this rhythm, no meaningful movement toward liberation occurs.

Even a Single Day of Neglect Causes Regression  
The attainment of spiritual progress is exceptionally fragile. A lapse in practice—even for just one day—undoes gains built over many days. This underscores that progress is not stored or preserved; it is actively sustained. Its absence immediately triggers decline, revealing that spiritual development operates on a precarious balance where consistency is non-negotiable.

Liberation Is Only Accessible Through Unbroken Discipline  
Freedom from the cycle of samsara is not achieved through occasional devotion or sporadic dedication. It is reserved exclusively for those who maintain daily, unceasing practice. Any deviation introduces the risk of backsliding, making habitual discipline the only path forward. Without it, regression is not a possibility—it is a certainty.
