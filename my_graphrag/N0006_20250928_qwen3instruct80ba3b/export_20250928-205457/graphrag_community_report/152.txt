**Title: Baoyu Zhineng, Wuwo Zhengjian, and Riyang Jiaozhao as the Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
Liberation is achieved through the integrated practice of transcendent wisdom, the correct view of no-self, and continuous mindfulness in daily life. These elements form a seamless progression: moral discipline stabilizes the mind, meditative concentration deepens focus, and wisdom—manifested as seeing all phenomena as impermanent and selfless—dissolves attachment. This wisdom is not abstract but embodied in daily awareness, where every thought and sensation is observed without identification. Meditative experiences, though inviting, are neither goals nor realities to be clung to. True freedom arises when the illusion of an enduring self is seen through in all moments, and even spiritual attainments are held lightly.

---

**The Path of Moral Discipline, Concentration, and Wisdom is Sequential and Interdependent**  
Moral discipline establishes the foundation for mental stability; meditative concentration builds upon this stability, creating the clarity needed to develop wisdom. Wisdom, the culmination of this threefold training, is not isolated but depends on the prior two. Without discipline, concentration lacks steadiness; without concentration, wisdom cannot take root. Together, they form a structured trajectory from conditioned behavior to unconditioned freedom.

**Transcendent Wisdom Sees All Phenomena as Impermanent and Without Self**  
This wisdom perceives form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—not as fixed entities but as transient, interdependent events. It recognizes that even meditative experiences such as light, sound, or joy are fleeting and should be met with equanimity. No phenomenon, including spiritual attainments, possesses an independent essence. The release from clinging arises not from suppression but from clear perception of their true nature.

**The View of No-Self is the Core Mechanism for Ending Attachment**  
Understanding that “I” and “mine” are illusions created by the aggregation of constantly changing processes dismantles the root of suffering. This understanding is not theoretical but realized through direct observation of thoughts and emotions as they arise and pass. In daily life, this view transforms into non-identification: a saintly being does not claim ownership of experiences, even those labeled spiritual.

**Daily Mindfulness Extends Meditative Clarity into Ordinary Activity**  
Continuous awareness while walking, eating, speaking, or sitting is not a separate practice but the natural expansion of meditative concentration. It ensures that the stability gained in stillness is not lost in motion. This sustained presence allows the view of no-self to be tested and confirmed in real-time, making liberation a lived reality rather than a transient state.

**Contemplative Insight into the Five Aggregates Dissolves the Illusion of Identity**  
By methodically observing each component of experience—body, sensation, perception, mental activity, and consciousness—as impermanent and self-less, the mind ceases to construct a center of ownership. This practice dismantles the belief in a controller or experiencer behind thoughts and feelings. It is through this direct, repeated seeing that the sense of “I” loosens and eventually dissolves.

**Meditative Experiences Are Not Ends but Signposts to Be Non-Attached To**  
Lights, sounds, joy, and lightness arising in deep practice are natural occurrences but do not indicate final realization. Grasping them as signs of progress or rejecting them as distractions both reinforce subtle attachment. The correct response is to observe them with the same equanimity as any other sensation—recognizing them, letting them be, and moving beyond.

**True Liberation Requires Wisdom to Prevent Attachment to Meditative States**  
Even profound stillness and clarity, cultivated through concentration, can become objects of clinging if not seen through the lens of transcendent wisdom. Without wisdom, the mind may mistake temporary peace for permanent freedom. Liberation occurs only when even the most refined meditative experiences are understood as conditioned and impermanent.

**Saintly Beings Embodied the Complete Path Through Non-Identification**  
They have mastered moral discipline, sustained meditative stability, and fully internalized the view of no-self. Their presence reflects constant non-attachment—not because they suppress desire, but because they see no self to be satisfied. Every moment, including emotional upheaval or tranquil stillness, is met with the same clear seeing: all is arising and passing, without owner, without controller.
