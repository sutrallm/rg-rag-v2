**Title: The Path to Liberation: Jie Ming Hui, Non-Attachment, and the Cessation of Suffering**

**Summary**  
Liberation from suffering and the cycle of rebirth is achieved solely through the integrated cultivation of ethical conduct, mental concentration, and liberating wisdom. This threefold training dismantles the root causes of suffering—craving, aversion, and ignorance—by revealing the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all phenomena. True freedom is not external or divine but internal and self-realized, manifesting as non-attachment to all states, including spiritual attainments. Mindfulness in daily life and at the moment of death are critical practices that sustain this path. The liberated being naturally embodies moral purity, inner stillness, and compassionate wisdom, having relinquished all clinging, even to the notion of liberation itself.

---

**Ethical Conduct is the Foundation for Mental Stability**  
Moral discipline prevents harmful actions and creates the necessary conditions for concentration to arise. Without ethical behavior, the mind remains agitated by guilt, regret, and external distractions, making deep meditation impossible. Discipline stabilizes outward conduct, which in turn supports the internal unification of mind required for wisdom to develop.

**Concentration Unifies the Mind to Cultivate Insight**  
Concentration—the steady focus of the mind on a single object—calms mental chatter and establishes one-pointedness. It is not an end in itself but the essential medium through which insight arises. Without this mental stability, the depth of awareness required to perceive the true nature of reality cannot be sustained.

**Wisdom Reveals Non-Self and Dissolves Attachment**  
Wisdom arises from direct observation of impermanence, suffering, and non-self, particularly through the understanding that the five aggregates—form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are not a permanent “I” or “mine.” This insight dismantles the illusion of self, which is the root of craving, aversion, and delusion. As this understanding deepens, clinging naturally ceases.

**Non-Attachment Is the Natural Expression of Wisdom**  
When one sees all phenomena—including spiritual experiences, meditative states, and even the idea of liberation—as transient and selfless, attachment dissolves without force. This is not passive rejection but active relinquishment born of clear seeing. True non-attachment includes letting go of attachment to non-attachment itself.

**Liberation Is Self-Realized Through Internal Transformation**  
Emancipation is neither granted by external forces nor attained after death. It is the direct, personal result of systematically eradicating mental afflictions through the threefold training. It involves no acquisition of new states but the complete cessation of clinging to self, desire, and aversion, ending mental bondage in this lifetime.

**The Moment of Death Determines the Final Outcome**  
The quality of consciousness at the time of death—marked by clarity, awareness, and non-clinging—directly shapes the post-mortem trajectory. This moment is not isolated but the culmination of daily mindfulness practice. Without consistent presence in ordinary life, the mind will be scattered and bound by craving at death, perpetuating further rebirth.

**The Liberated Being Embodies Saintly Qualities Naturally**  
One who has realized liberation manifests moral purity, mental stability, and penetrating wisdom effortlessly. These qualities are not performed or sought but arise spontaneously from the absence of defilements. Such individuals are free from greed, hatred, pride, and delusion, and no longer depend on external conditions for peace.

**Non-Abiding Is the Final Expression of Freedom**  
The highest state is one in which the mind does not cling to any experience—not even to peace, enlightenment, or liberation itself. This non-abiding mind mirrors the openness of empty space: free from grasping, aversion, and dualities. It is the living embodiment of ultimate peace, beyond all concepts and attainments.

**Cessation of Desire Is the Key to Ending Suffering**  
Suffering ends not through suppression but through seeing the true nature of desire: its impermanence and the futility of finding satisfaction in phenomena that are inherently unstable. Craving fuels rebirth; its complete cessation, rooted in insight, is the decisive condition for liberation.

**Compassion and Wisdom Must Be Balanced**  
True freedom is not cold detachment. Wisdom prevents emotional entanglement, while compassion ensures engagement with others without self-reference. The enlightened character is defined by this harmony—active care grounded in the understanding that all beings are bound by the same delusions.

**All Practices Converge to Eliminate the Three Root Afflictions**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion—manifestations of craving, aversion, and ignorance—are uprooted only through the unified practice of ethical conduct, concentration, and wisdom. They arise from misperceiving the five aggregates as self; their dissolution is the direct path to final release.

**The True Practitioner Walks the Path Without Seeking Results**  
One who walks the path correctly does not seek supernatural experiences, external validation, or even the outcome of liberation. They cultivate right view, mindfulness, and effort in everyday activities, allowing realization to unfold naturally through continuous awareness and non-clinging.

**Liberation and Nirvana Are Synonymous States of Inner Freedom**  
The cessation of suffering and the end of rebirth—whether called liberation (jiede) or nirvana (nihua)—refer to the same inner condition: the total stilling of craving and ignorance, realized through direct insight, not divine intervention or ritual. This state is deathless because it is free from the conditions that produce birth and death.

**Daily Mindfulness Prepares the Mind for Final Release**  
Continuous mindful awareness—whether eating, walking, or feeling anger—trains the mind to observe craving as it arises, preventing its proliferation. This daily practice is the gradual foundation for mastery at death, where even the smallest trace of clinging can lead to further entanglement.

**The Concept of Attainment Must Be Let Go of in the Final Stage**  
Even the idea of “I am liberated” must be relinquished. True freedom is found in non-attainment—where practices, goals, and even the self-concept of the practitioner dissolve. Liberation is not a state to be possessed but the complete absence of all grasping, including the grasping toward liberation.
