**Title: Chao-yue Zhizhuo and Wuwo Guan as the Path to Liberation through Zhengfa**

**Summary**  
Liberation from suffering and the cycle of rebirth is achieved through transcending all forms of attachment, particularly to meditative experiences such as bliss or spiritual powers. This transcendence arises solely from the direct contemplation of non-self—observing the five aggregates as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of inherent identity. The practice is rooted in the Four Foundations of Mindfulness, which cultivate the deep concentration necessary to see reality clearly. Wisdom, sustained by right effort and mindfulness, dismantles the illusion of “I” and “mine,” leading to non-clinging, inner freedom, and the realization of Nirvana. Those who complete this path become enlightened beings, embodying pure morality and penetrating wisdom.

---

**Transcending Attachment Through Non-Self Contemplation**  
The cessation of suffering and the attainment of liberation occur only when attachment is fully relinquished. This is not achieved through pursuit of meditative states, but through the realized understanding that all phenomena—including spiritual experiences—are impermanent and lack an enduring self. Observing the five aggregates as transient and impersonal dismantles the sense of ownership (“mine”) and identity (“I”), removing the root cause of clinging and aversion. Without this recognition, even spiritual achievements become new forms of bondage.

**The Four Foundations as the Direct Path to Wisdom**  
The practice of observing the body, feelings, mind, and mental objects with sustained awareness is the core method prescribed for liberation. Through this continuous, clear observation, one naturally develops deep mental stability. This stability is not a goal in itself, but the necessary condition for wisdom to arise—revealing the impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of every experience. Without this framework, insight into non-self cannot be cultivated systematically.

**Deep Concentration as the Enabling Condition**  
Mental stillness, gained through sustained mindfulness of the Four Foundations, provides the clarity required to perceive phenomena as they truly are. This concentration is not ephemeral calm, but a steady foundation that permits observations to penetrate beneath surface appearances. It is the soil in which wisdom grows, allowing one to see the selfless nature of experience without being swept away by attraction or repulsion.

**Wu Wo Guan as the Cognitive Anchor of Liberation**  
Contemplating the non-self nature of the five aggregates is the specific practice that dismantles egoic clinging. It is not theoretical speculation but direct, experiential observation of form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness as arising from interdependent conditions, devoid of permanence or core identity. This single practice is sufficient to dissolve the illusion of self and sever the chains of rebirth.

**Nirvana as the State of Non-Attachment**  
The ultimate realization—Nirvana—is defined not as a transcendent realm, but as the complete absence of clinging. It is the natural outcome when all phenomena, including the most subtle spiritual experiences, are seen as empty of self. Attachment, even to enlightenment or insight, prevents liberation; thus, Nirvana is reached only when even the desire to attain it is released.

**Zhengfa as the Integrated System of Practice and Wisdom**  
The true Dharma is not a collection of doctrines, but a living path: guided by right view, sustained by right effort, protected by right mindfulness, and grounded in right concentration. Each element reinforces the others, ensuring that practice does not devolve into mechanical repetition or spiritual grasping. The entire system is oriented toward one outcome—the cessation of suffering through non-attachment.

**Enlightened Beings as the Embodiment of Completed Practice**  
Those who fully realize non-self and relinquish all attachment are described as enlightened beings. They live not by dogma, but by the unshakable understanding that nothing is worth holding onto. Their morality is pure because it arises from insight, not fear or reward. Their freedom is visible in their equanimity—as they no longer identify with any state, experience, or outcome.

**Wisdom as the Active Agent of Release**  
True wisdom does not remain passive observation; it actively reveals the illusory nature of ownership and identity. When one sees that form, feeling, and thought are empty of self, attachment naturally falls away. This is not a moral decision or a suppression of desire, but a deep recognition that what was once clung to was never truly there to be possessed.
