**Title: Chen Hui, Ta Yu, Yu Chi, and the Sage’s Path to Liberation**

**Summary**  
The text outlines three primary mental afflictions—aversion (Chen Hui), greed (Ta Yu), and delusion (Yu Chi)—that sustain suffering by distorting perception and attachment to experience. It further describes specific meditative practices designed to counter each affliction directly, with the sage serving as the ideal state where all three afflictions have been fully abandoned. The practices are not abstract but are precisely mapped to transform habitual mental patterns, leading to a clear, undistorted view of reality.

---

**Aversion Is Countered by Loving-Kindness Meditation**  
Loving-kindness meditation is invoked specifically to neutralize anger and resistance toward unpleasant experiences. This practice directly opposes the mindset of rejection, replacing it with a stable, non-resistant attitude, enabling mental peace even in the face of discomfort.

**Greed Is Countered by Not-Retention Meditation**  
The clinging to pleasurable sensations and objects is addressed through not-retention meditation. This method undermines the habitual tendency to hold onto transient experiences, weakening the grip of attachment that binds beings to cyclic suffering.

**Delusion Is Countered by Dependent Origination Meditation**  
Delusion arising from misunderstanding reality’s true nature—such as perceiving the impermanent as permanent—is dissolved through meditative contemplation of dependent origination. This practice reveals the interdependent and conditional nature of all phenomena, dismantling false perceptions of self, permanence, and inherent happiness.

**The Sage Embodies Freedom from All Three Afflictions**  
The sage is defined not by ritual or doctrine but by the complete absence of aversion, greed, and delusion. This state is characterized by equanimity, non-attachment, and unobstructed perception, demonstrating that liberation is the direct result of applying corrective meditative methods to habitual mental patterns.

**Methods Are Targeted to Specific Mental Habits**  
Each meditation technique corresponds precisely to one affliction: breath-counting for distraction, element-analysis for pride, not-retention for greed, loving-kindness for aversion, and dependent origination for ignorance. This specificity indicates that transformation requires tailored approaches, not generic spiritual exercises.
