**Title: The Enlightened Being: Liberation Through Moral Discipline, Meditative Concentration, and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
An enlightened being has fully transcended suffering by eradicating greed, anger, and delusion, and by realizing the impermanent, empty, and non-self nature of all phenomena. This state is achieved not through rituals or external aids, but through the integrated practice of moral conduct, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Their mind is permanently still, unperturbed by worldly conditions, and free from all attachment to desire, gain, or loss. This freedom is recognized as boundless purity and is revered even by celestial beings.

**Moral Discipline as the Foundation for Inner Purity**  
The path to liberation begins with pure moral conduct, which establishes the ethical grounding necessary for mental clarity. Without this foundation, deeper states of stillness and wisdom cannot take root. The elimination of the Three Poisons—greed, anger, and delusion—is the definitive mark of this purity, and it arises not from suppression but from consistent, self-driven ethical practice.

**Meditative Concentration as the Ground for Clear Perception**  
 Perfect meditative concentration is a state of undistracted presence, sustained by continuous mindfulness. It is not mere relaxation but a cultivated stillness that allows the mind to settle free from agitation. This stability is essential, as it creates the conditions in which wisdom can arise—without it, insight remains fragmented and unreliable.

**Wisdom as the Transforming Force of Liberation**  
 Wisdom is the direct, unshakable recognition that all phenomena are empty, impermanent, and not-self. It is not theoretical knowledge but experiential understanding that dissolves clinging. This understanding naturally leads to detachment, not as an effort to renounce, but as a spontaneous release born of seeing reality as it is. Wisdom completes the path, extinguishing the roots of suffering and actualizing the state of Nirvana.

**Non-Attachment as the Natural Expression of Liberation**  
 True detachment is not avoidance or denial, but the effortless relinquishment of clinging that follows deep insight. An enlightened being no longer identifies with desires, possessions, or outcomes because they see them as inherently unstable. This absence of attachment is manifested as inner peace undisturbed by praise, blame, gain, or loss—revered not for supernatural power, but for the profound stillness and integrity of a mind unconditioned by the world.
