Title: Spiritual Practice and the Elimination of the Three Poisons

Summary:  
Spiritual practice—rooted in meditation, ethical conduct, and mindfulness—is the essential and only means to achieve liberation from suffering and the cycle of birth and death. This liberation is defined solely by the complete removal of greed, hatred, and delusion, which are identified as the exclusive origins of all bondage and distress. Without sustained practice, liberation is unattainable.

Similarly, the state of being enlightened is characterized not by external achievements or phenomena, but by the total absence of these three afflictions, which alone determine purity and freedom.

—

The Path to Liberation Requires Consistent Practice  
Liberation from suffering and the cycle of birth and death cannot be achieved without disciplined, ongoing engagement in meditation, ethical conduct, and mindfulness. These activities are not optional enhancements but necessary components of the journey. Any absence of consistent effort renders progress impossible, establishing practice as the sole viable route forward.

The Three Poisons Are the Sole Root of Suffering  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are identified as the singular sources of all suffering and bondage. No other causes are suggested or implied. Their presence sustains distress; their absence defines freedom. This singular focus elevates them above all other potential explanations for human turmoil.

Enlightenment Is Defined by the Absence of Afflictions  
An enlightened being is not characterized by powers, visions, or supernatural attributes, but exclusively by the total eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. This absence is the measure of purity and the criterion of freedom. The state is not advanced by accumulation but by complete removal.

The Purpose of Practice Is Exclusively to Eradicate the Three Poisons  
Every aspect of spiritual practice—whether meditation, ethics, or mindfulness—serves one unchanging aim: the annihilation of greed, hatred, and delusion. No other objective is presented. The practice’s value lies entirely in its capacity to dismantle these three afflictions, making their removal the ultimate and only goal.
