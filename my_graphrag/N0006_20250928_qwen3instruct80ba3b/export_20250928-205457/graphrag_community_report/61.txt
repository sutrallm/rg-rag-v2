**Title: Shengzhi, Jingjin, and Xinxiu: The Path of the Shengren to Wufenbian**

**Summary**  
The path to liberation is articulated through three interdependent practices — Shengzhi (transcendent wisdom), Jingjin (diligent effort), and Xinxiu (mind cultivation) — which together enable the emergence of Wufenbian (non-discrimination). These practices lead to the embodied state of the Shengren, who have abandoned attachment, defilements, and dualistic perception. Concentration (Chanding) serves as the foundational support for wisdom, sustained by effort and expressed through continuous mindfulness. Realization of impermanence, suffering, and non-self directly terminates clinging, resulting in equanimous awareness where all phenomena are seen without distinction.

---

**Shengzhi Arises from Direct Observation of Impermanence, Suffering, and Non-Self**  
Transcendent wisdom emerges not through doctrine or abstraction but through firsthand recognition that all phenomena are transient, inherently unstable, and devoid of a fixed self. This direct perception dissolves attachment to outcomes, including the pursuit of liberation itself. The realization of non-self is the core expression of this wisdom; suffering is understood as a consequence of clinging to what is impermanent.

**Jingjin Sustains Concentration and Prevents Mental Slackness**  
Diligent effort is the driving force that stabilizes meditative focus. Without consistent, vigilant practice, concentration cannot be maintained, and the conditions for deeper understanding collapse. This effort is not forced or strained but steady and unwavering, serving as the active support that keeps mindfulness alive and prevents regression into indulgence or sloth.

**Xinxiu Cultivates Non-Discriminatory Awareness Through Continuous Mindfulness**  
Mind cultivation is the ongoing practice of observing mental events as they arise and pass, in every moment of daily life. It is not directed toward achieving powers or accumulating merit, but toward dissolving attachment to mental states. Through this unbroken awareness, the mind learns to witness experience without labeling it as good or bad, holy or profane, thereby naturally nurturing non-discrimination.

**Chanding is the Structural Bridge Between Effort, Cultivation, and Wisdom**  
Concentration is neither a passive state nor an end in itself. It is the practical outcome of diligent effort and the active expression of mind cultivation. It provides the stable mental platform from which transcendent wisdom can arise, enabling the clear perception of impermanence, suffering, and non-self. Without concentration, wisdom cannot manifest.

**The Shengren Embodies Perfected Practice and Wisdom**  
Those who have fully realized the nature of reality are marked by inner stillness, freedom from greed, hatred, and delusion, and seamless integration of diligent effort and mindfulness. Their presence demonstrates the culmination of wisdom: non-discrimination is not a concept they adopt, but a natural condition of their awareness. They serve as living examples of liberation.

**Wufenbian Is the Natural Consequence of Fully Developed Wisdom**  
When transcendent wisdom is complete, the mind no longer divides experience into opposing categories. The distinguishing of pleasure from pain, good from bad, or self from other ceases. This state of undivided awareness is not a suppression of perception but a seeing through illusion — phenomena are recognized as empty of inherent difference, and judgment dissolves into equanimity.
