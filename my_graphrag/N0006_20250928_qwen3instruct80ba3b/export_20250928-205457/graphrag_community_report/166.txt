**Title: Sheng Zhe and the Path to Liberation Through Chan Ding and Mindfulness**

**Summary**  
Sheng Zhe represents the perfected state of human awakening, free from all afflictions and the cycle of rebirth. This state arises from the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion, supported by flawless moral conduct and unwavering meditative stability. Through the disciplined practice of Chan Ding, the five hindrances are dissolved, enabling direct observation of the body, feelings, mind, and phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of self. This non-attached awareness, cultivated through the Four Foundations of Mindfulness, leads to the irreversible realization of no-self and liberation from the Three Realms of existence.

**Chan Ding as the Foundation of Clarity**  
The disciplined mental state known as Chan Ding is the essential condition for overcoming desire, aversion, sloth, restlessness, and doubt. It provides the stable, concentrated focus necessary to engage deeply with the Four Foundations of Mindfulness. Without this inner stillness, observation remains reactive and clouded. Chan Ding is not merely a meditative technique but the sustained embodiment of mental purity that renders the hindrances powerless, allowing the mind to perceive reality without distortion.

**The Four Foundations of Mindfulness as the Path of Direct Realization**  
Contemplating the body as impure, feelings as suffering, mind as impermanent, and mental phenomena as non-self forms a systematic, non-judgmental method for stripping away illusion. This practice does not rely on belief or dogma but on personal, direct observation. Each foundation targets specific conditioned patterns of clinging, and through sustained attention, they dissolve the conviction of a fixed, independent identity. The daily application of this mindfulness in ordinary life—Zheng Nian—ensures that awakening is not confined to meditation but becomes the natural way of being.

**Eradication of the Three Poisons as the Mark of Freedom**  
The complete absence of greed, hatred, and delusion defines the Saintly One. These three roots of suffering are not merely subdued but uprooted, eliminating the compulsive drives that sustain cyclic existence. Their removal is not an emotional state but the result of profound insight into the nature of phenomena. This eradication renders the mind unmoved by external conditions and frees it from the illusion of separateness that fuels craving and aversion.

**Transcendence of the Three Realms and the Illusion of Self**  
Having dismantled the false sense of self through direct observation, Sheng Zhe is no longer subject to the laws of the desire, form, or formless realms. Liberation is not escape but recognition: all experiences are conditioned, fleeting, and without intrinsic identity. With the dissolution of the notion of “I” as practitioner, achiever, or observer, there is no one left to be bound. This realization, synonymous with Nirvana, ends the cycle of birth and death because there is no center to be reborn.

**Moral Purity as the Ground for Awakening**  
Perfect moral conduct is not an ethical obligation separate from wisdom but its necessary foundation. The Saintly One’s flawless chijie creates the inner harmony that allows Chan Ding to deepen and mindfulness to function without interference from remorse or conflict. Ethical living is not external discipline but the natural expression of a mind no longer driven by greed or aversion, making it inseparable from the process of awakening.
