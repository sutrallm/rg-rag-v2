**Title: The Path from Delusion to Liberation: Jieming, Wangzhu, and Zhihui**

**Summary**  
Delusion (jieming) is the foundational cause of suffering, leading individuals to misperceive transient phenomena as permanent, pleasurable, and self-existing. This misperception gives rise to attachment (wangzhu), which binds beings to cyclical suffering. The only means to dismantle this cycle is through liberating wisdom (zhihui), which directly perceives the true nature of reality—impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of self. When wisdom is fully developed, attachment ceases and delusion is eradicated, resulting in the state of a saint who has transcended all bondage.

**Delusion fuels attachment through mistaken perception**  
The core mistake underlying human suffering is the misperception that impermanent phenomena are stable, pleasurable, and owned by a self. This distortion generates attachment, as beings cling to things they falsely believe will bring lasting satisfaction or define their identity. Without this misapprehension, attachment would have no foothold.

**Wisdom is the direct remedy for delusion and attachment**  
Liberating wisdom (zhihui) functions not as intellectual knowledge but as an acute, transformative recognition of reality’s true nature. By seeing all phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and non-self, wisdom dismantles the illusions that sustain both delusion and the craving that follows from it. This process is not gradual suppression but immediate dissolution of false views.

**The cessation of attachment defines the saint**  
A saint is identified not by external signs but by the complete absence of delusion and attachment. This state arises only when wisdom has fully penetrated the illusion of permanence, pleasure, and selfhood. The saint’s freedom is not derived from external conditions but from the internal eradication of the roots of bondage.

**Moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom constitute a sequential path**  
The threefold training—moral discipline, meditative concentration, and liberating wisdom—is presented as an ordered framework for transformation. Each stage prepares the ground for the next: moral discipline steadies conduct, concentration focuses the mind, and wisdom ultimately uproots the ignorance that sustains the entire cycle of suffering. The path is linear and interdependent, with wisdom as the culminating and decisive factor.
