**Title: The Saintly Being and the Path to Liberation: Ethics, Concentration, and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
The enlightened being, known as Shengzhe, has fully eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion—the root causes of suffering—and thus transcended the cycle of birth and death. This liberation is achieved through the unified, sequential cultivation of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and liberating wisdom. Shengzhe dwell in unshakable stillness, free from all attachment, including to spiritual achievements, and remain perpetually mindful of the impermanent, selfless nature of all phenomena. Their purity of conduct, depth of concentration, and penetrating insight arise not from external aid but from relentless self-reliant practice. This state is not an abstract ideal but the direct result of completing each stage of the path: ethical restraint enables mental calm, which in turn gives rise to wisdom that uproots ignorance. Compassion and wisdom are unified in their actions, and their freedom is marked by non-abiding awareness—neither grasping nor rejecting any experience.

**Moral Discipline as the Unshakable Foundation**  
The foundational step toward liberation is the perfect observance of ethical precepts, which guard against harmful actions of body, speech, and mind. This discipline prevents remorse, creates inner peace, and establishes the conditions necessary for meditative concentration to arise. For the saintly being, moral conduct is not a restrictive code but a spontaneous expression of inner purity, free from compulsion or pride. It is the indispensable starting point upon which all higher attainments rest.

**Meditative Concentration as the Bridge to Insight**  
Through disciplined cultivation, the mind achieves deep stability—Chanding—free from distraction, agitation, and attachment even to meditative bliss. This unwavering concentration is not an end in itself but the essential middle stage that arises from ethical discipline and enables the direct perception of reality. In this settled state, phenomena are seen clearly as arising and passing, revealing their impermanent, selfless nature. Without this level of mental focus, wisdom cannot take root.

**Liberating Wisdom as the Final Liberation**  
Wisdom is the transformative power that dismantles delusion by revealing the true nature of all phenomena: impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of self. This insight erases the mistaken beliefs in permanence, pleasure, and identity that fuel craving. It leads to non-attachment not only to worldly pleasures but also to spiritual attainments, including the very idea of liberation. The saintly being’s wisdom is direct, unobstructed, and boundless—like a mirror reflecting all things without clinging to any.

**The Three Poisons as the Source of Bondage**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the interconnected roots of all suffering. Greed binds through attachment to sensory pleasures and desired states; hatred manifests as aversion and resistance; delusion distorts perception, making transient experiences appear solid, satisfying, and self-existing. The saintly being has fully extinguished all three, not through suppression but through seeing them as empty illusions. Their absence defines the state of true freedom.

**Non-Attachment as the Nature of Freedom**  
True liberation is the state of mind that does not abide in anything—neither in pleasure nor pain, neither in spiritual experiences nor in the notion of being liberated. This non-abiding awareness, free from conceptual grasping, is the natural outcome of wisdom. The saintly being does not strive for enlightenment, nor cling to its fruits; they are completely at ease, beyond all dualities and desires.

**Self-Reliant Practice as the Only Path**  
Enlightenment cannot be granted by teachers, deities, or rituals. It arises solely from one’s own continuous, vigilant, and non-slothful awareness. The role of the Buddha or any guide is to point the way—not to save, but to illuminate the path. The saintly being embodies this truth: their freedom is self-earned, arising only through unwavering inner effort and the disciplined cultivation of ethics, concentration, and wisdom.

**The Sequential Path to Ultimate Liberation**  
The progression is precise: moral discipline leads to mental calm; calm leads to penetrating wisdom; wisdom leads to complete release. Each stage is necessary, interdependent, and irreversible. To abandon any one is to invite instability or delusion. The saintly being is one who has completed all three in perfect harmony, and thus dwells beyond the three realms of existence, unshaken by even the highest celestial states.

**Compassion and Wisdom as Unified Expression**  
Insight into emptiness does not lead to withdrawal but to active, boundless compassion. The saintly being sees the emptiness of self, other, and action, yet continues to engage with sentient beings without attachment or expectation. Compassion flows naturally from wisdom; they are not two separate qualities but one unified mode of being—clear seeing combined with boundless goodwill.

**Constant Vigilance as the Mark of Realization**  
The saintly being never becomes complacent, even after profound attainments. They remain alert, recognizing that backsliding is possible through subtle attachment or pride. This unceasing diligence is not a burden but the natural expression of their deep understanding: freedom is not a destination to be maintained but a state of non-clinging that must be continuously embodied.

**The Realization of Nirvana**  
Nirvana is not a place or a state to be attained, but the cessation of all afflictions, the end of mental fabrications, and the transcendence of rebirth. The saintly being does not “enter” nirvana—they dwell in it, because all conditions for suffering have been uprooted. Their very existence manifests the unconditioned, free from greed, hatred, delusion, birth, aging, sickness, and death.
