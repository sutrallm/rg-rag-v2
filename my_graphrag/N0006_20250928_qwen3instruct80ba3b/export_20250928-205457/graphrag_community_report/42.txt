Title: The Three Poisons and Their Removal in Buddhist Liberation

Summary:  
Hatred (Chen) and craving (Taan) are afflictive mental states rooted in ignorance (Wu Ming), driving suffering and the cycle of rebirth. Both are extinguished in the state of Niepan, achievable only when all three poisons—Chen, Taan, and ignorance—are fully eradicated. An Shengzhe embodies this complete removal, having overcome hatred and its companions.

Hatred and craving both stem from ignorance and sustain the cycle of suffering.  
They are not isolated reactions but interconnected afflictions, both arising from the same foundational delusion. Their persistence keeps beings bound to rebirth, and their cessation defines liberation.

Complete release from suffering requires the annihilation of all three poisons.  
Only through the total extinction of hatred, craving, and ignorance—collectively termed the three poisons—can Niepan be realized. No partial removal suffices; the eradication of one poison is inseparable from the eradication of the others.

An Shengzhe has transcended hatred and its roots.  
This state signifies full liberation, marked by the absence of hatred alongside the other two poisons. The term identifies an individual who has ended the mental conditions that perpetuate rebirth, not merely reduced them.

Ignorance is the source from which craving emerges.  
Craving (Taan) does not arise independently; it is conditioned by ignorance (Wu Ming). This causal relationship is central to understanding how suffering is generated and sustained, forming the structural basis of the Four Noble Truths.
