**Title: The Buddha’s Path to Liberation: Non-Attachment, Non-Self, and Present-Moment Realization**

**Summary:**  
The historical teacher known as FO provided a method for liberation rooted in direct experience, not worship or dogma. Liberation is not granted by the teacher or achieved through rituals, but realized through personal practice centered on breaking the illusion of self, cultivating a mind of no-seeking, and experiencing freedom in the present moment. The teachings emphasize turning away from reliance on words, personalities, or external rewards to instead realize the nature of reality through meditative awareness.

---

**Breaking the Illusion of Self Enables Immediate Liberation**  
The five aggregates—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are understood as transient and not constituting a permanent self. Recognizing this truth dismantles the root of attachment and suffering, allowing liberation to occur without delay. This understanding is not theoretical but experiential, and it is the foundational shift required for genuine freedom.

**Liberation Is Achieved Only Through Self-Realization, Not External Authority**  
FO serves as a guide who demonstrated the path, but does not bestow liberation. No ritual, invocation, or devotion to his person can substitute for the practitioner’s own awareness and effort. The role of the teacher is to point the way, not to remove the burden of practice. True freedom arises when one stops depending on others for salvation.

**The Mind of No-Seeking Is the Highest Practice**  
Actions performed without desire for reward, praise, or fear of punishment align with the state of liberation. Seeking outcomes—even spiritual ones—reinforces clinging and contradicts the very nature of freedom. The absence of motive is not passivity, but a state of perfect alignment with reality as it is, unmediated by ambition or aversion.

**Nirvana Is Not a Future Goal but the Present Cessation of Clinging**  
The cessation of suffering is not a distant state to be reached after countless lifetimes, but is realized the moment the mind stops grasping, rejecting, or projecting. This state is synonymous with nirvana and is accessible now, through the direct application of meditative awareness. It is not an abstract ideal, but the immediate outcome of non-clinging.

**Doctrinal Understanding Must Transcend Text and Teacher**  
Relying on words or personalities leads to bondage; true understanding comes from direct experience. The Buddha used the finger-and-moon analogy to show that teachings are tools to see clearly, not objects to worship. Intellectual belief in doctrine without inner realization remains superficial. **Non-Self and Breaking the Illusion of Self Are Interchangeable Realizations**  
The concept of wuwo (non-self) and the practice of pochu wo zhi (breaking the illusion of self) describe the same truth from different angles—one conceptual, the other experiential. They are not separate doctrines but two sides of the same liberation: seeing through the fiction of a fixed, enduring identity.

**Liberation Is the Process; Nirvana Is the Culmination**  
Jiechu—the active unfolding of freedom—is the path taken through the dissolution of attachment, while niepan—the stillness beyond suffering—is its natural result. One cannot be attained without the other. Liberation is not an event received, but a process completed through sustained, self-directed practice.

**Neither Following Personalities Nor Seeking Rewards Aligns with Authentic Practice**  
Veneration of the teacher or desire for spiritual gain distracts from the inner work required. Both actions stem from the same root: attachment to external validation. Authentic practice requires turning inward, letting go of all external references, and accepting responsibility for one’s own awakening.
