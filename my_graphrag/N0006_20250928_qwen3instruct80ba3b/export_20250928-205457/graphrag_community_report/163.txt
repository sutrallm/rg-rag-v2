**Title: Jielv, Chanding, and Zhishi: The Threefold Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Moral discipline (Jielv) is the essential foundation for mental calm (Chanding), which in turn enables transcendent wisdom (Zhishi). Without ethical conduct, meditation lacks stability; without meditative focus, wisdom cannot take root. Wisdom—recognizing impermanence, non-self, and emptiness—is the direct cause of liberation (Jietuo) and the cessation of suffering. The Middle Way (Zhongdao) guides all three stages, rejecting extremes and fostering balanced practice. Enlightened beings (Shengzhe) fully embody this path, demonstrating freedom through unwavering ethical conduct, deep concentration, and unshakable insight. Liberation is not an attainment but the natural outcome of seeing reality without distortion.

---

**Moral Discipline Enables Mental Clarity**  
Ethical conduct (Jielv) purifies the heart by preventing harmful bodily, verbal, and mental actions. This restraint eliminates regret and guilt, creating the inner peace necessary for stable meditation. Without it, the mind remains agitated and incapable of deep concentration.

**Meditative Absorption Births Unshakable Wisdom**  
Concentration (Chanding) arises only from a mind purified by ethical discipline. This focused stillness provides the clarity required for transcendent wisdom to emerge. Without concentration, wisdom is scattered and fleeting, unable to endure or transform perception.

**Wisdom Extinguishes Ignorance and Ends Suffering**  
Transcendent wisdom (Zhishi) sees all phenomena as impermanent, non-self, and empty. This direct insight dissolves clinging, aversion, and delusion, extinguishing ignorance. It is the final and highest factor in the threefold training, and the sole cause of liberation and nirvana.

**The Middle Way Anchors All Practice**  
The balanced path of the Middle Way (Zhongdao) avoids both indulgence and self-mortification. It is embodied in ethical conduct, applied in meditation through non-forced stillness, and realized in wisdom as non-attachment to experience. This approach prevents distortion and allows true perception to arise.

**Liberation Is the Natural Result of Right Seeing**  
Freedom (Jietuo) is not a state to be possessed but the inevitable outcome of seeing reality as it is—without grasping or rejecting. It is fully expressed in those who have perfected moral conduct, concentration, and wisdom, leaving no trace of greed, hatred, or delusion.

**Enlightened Beings Embodiment of the Path**  
Enlightened beings (Shengzhe) manifest perfected moral discipline, deep meditative stability, and penetrating wisdom. Their lives reflect freedom from suffering and cyclic existence, demonstrating that true liberation arises through the integrated cultivation of these three elements.

**Right View Realizes Dependent Origination**  
Correct understanding (Zhenlian) sees all phenomena as dependently arisen—conditioned, impermanent, and lacking independent existence. This vision directly dissolves attachment, aligns with the nature of reality, and serves as the doorway to lasting freedom.

**Effort Sustains Insight**  
Sustained mindfulness and diligent effort (Jingjin) keep the mind attuned to the truth of impermanence and non-self. Without persistent attention, the clarity of wisdom fades, and ignorance returns.
