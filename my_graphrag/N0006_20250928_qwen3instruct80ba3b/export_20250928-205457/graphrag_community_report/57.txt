**Title: The Path to Liberation: Jie Ming Hui, Mindful Awareness, and the Realization of Non-Self**

**Summary**  
Liberation is achieved solely through the integrated practice of ethical conduct, mental stability, and insight wisdom—known as Jie Ming Hui—cultivated in daily life through continuous mindful awareness. This path leads to the direct realization of non-self, the cessation of desire, and the unraveling of all mental afflictions. True liberation is not granted externally, not attained after death, and not marked by supernatural phenomena; it is an internal, self-realized freedom characterized by non-attachment, the collapse of the illusion of a permanent self, and the relinquishment of all concepts of attainment. The quality of mind at death, shaped entirely by daily practice, determines the final outcome: liberation or continued rebirth. Those who fully embody this path naturally exhibit moral purity, mental clarity, and liberating wisdom—manifestations of a mind no longer bound by craving, aversion, or delusion.

**Ethical Conduct, Mental Stability, and Insight Wisdom Form the Sole Path to Liberation**  
Liberation arises only when ethical conduct prevents harmful actions, mental stability steadies the mind, and insight wisdom dissolves ignorance. These three elements must be fully developed in alignment; none alone is sufficient. Their combined cultivation systematically dismantles the root causes of suffering—attachment, aversion, and delusion—leading to the permanent cessation of mental bondage. This is the necessary and sufficient means to transcend the cycle of rebirth.

**Mindful Awareness in Daily Life Sustains and Extends Meditative Stability**  
The practice of maintaining clear, non-attached observation during all activities—eating, walking, feeling anger—is not supplementary but foundational. It transforms formal concentration into a continuous, natural state of presence, preventing the mind from scattering. This seamless extension of mental stability into everyday life builds the terrain upon which insight into impermanence and non-self arises.

**Liberation Is the Cessation of Self-Clinging Through Insight Into the Five Aggregates**  
The mistaken perception of a permanent self arises from identifying with the five aggregates—form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness. Liberation is the realization that these are transient, interdependent, and devoid of inherent self. Seeing their true nature dismantles clinging to “I” and “mine,” which in turn ends desire and suffering. This insight is not abstract but experiential, cultivated through sustained observation.

**The Mind at Death Is the Ultimate Test of Practice**  
The mental state at the moment of death is not a random outcome but the direct result of lifelong training. A clear, unattached, fearless mind—free from craving, aversion, and delusion—leads to liberation. A scattered or clinging mind ensures continued rebirth. This critical juncture proves that daily mindfulness is not optional; it is the very preparation for the final moment of transition.

**True Freedom Is Found in the Abandonment of All Concepts of Attainment**  
Even the idea of being “liberated,” “practicing,” or “attaining enlightenment” must be relinquished. The final stage is not the acquisition of a higher state, but the complete non-residing mind—unattached even to the notion of liberation itself. This is the expression of utter freedom: no goal to reach, no self to attain it, no state to cling to.

**Liberated Beings Naturally Manifest Moral Purity, Concentration, and Wisdom**  
Those who have realized liberation do not perform saintly acts to prove their status. Instead, impeccable discipline, unwavering mental stability, and penetrating wisdom arise spontaneously as expressions of a mind freed from afflictions. These traits are not cultivated separately but emerge as inseparable fruits of inner release.

**Cessation of Desire Is Rooted in Seeing Impermanence and Non-Self, Not Suppression**  
Ending suffering does not come from forceful denial of cravings. It arises through direct observation of how all phenomena are unstable, unsatisfactory, and empty of self. In this seeing, desire naturally subsides—not because it is fought, but because its foundation is seen through. This is the true meaning of cessation.

**Liberation and Nirvana Are Synonymous States of Inner Freedom**  
Jiede and nihua refer to the same condition: complete release from the cycle of birth and death, achieved solely through insight, not divine favor, ritual, or posthumous reward. It is an internal transformation—free from dependence, untouched by conditions, and untouched by the illusion of self. There is no journey beyond it; it is the end of all seeking.

**Renunciation Is Not External Abandonment But Internal Non-Attachment**  
True renunciation means letting go of seeking, validation, and the pursuit of results—whether worldly or spiritual. It is not about leaving society, but about releasing inner clinging. This internal non-attachment is the condition that allows the path to unfold naturally, without force or striving.
