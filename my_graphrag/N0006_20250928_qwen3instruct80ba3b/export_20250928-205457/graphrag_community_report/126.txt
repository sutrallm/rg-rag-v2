**Title: Wisdom, Right Intention, and the Path to Liberation in Meditative Practice**

**Summary:**  
The text outlines a pathway to liberation rooted in direct experiential understanding of reality’s fundamental qualities—impermanence, suffering, and non-self. This understanding, termed wisdom, is cultivated through mental clarity and sustained by right intention, which orients the practitioner against greed, hatred, and delusion. Without right intention, wisdom lacks ethical and cognitive foundation; without wisdom, meditative clarity becomes another object of attachment. The fully realized being is one who has uprooted afflictive mental states through this integrated process.

---

**Right Intention Establishes the Ethical Ground for Liberation**  
Recognizing greed as the origin of suffering, hatred as corrosive, and delusion as obscuring darkness shapes the internal landscape necessary for transformation. This intentional alignment does not suppress or indulge these states but meets them with awareness and release. It acts as the moral and mental preparation that makes wisdom possible, ensuring that effort is not misdirected or fueled by the very afflictions it seeks to dissolve.

**Wisdom Arises Through Direct Perception of Impermanence**  
The first and essential gateway to liberation is the unfiltered observation that all phenomena are transient. This clear seeing is not intellectual but experiential—it dismantles the illusion of permanence upon which attachment is built. Without this perception, deeper realizations cannot take root, as the foundation of conditioned existence remains unexamined.

**Non-Self is the Culmination of True Understanding**  
Wisdom is incomplete without the full recognition that no enduring, independent self exists within any experience. This realization is not an abstract concept but a direct, embodied knowing. It is both the fruit and the measure of wisdom: the absence of clinging to any notion of “I” or “mine” signals the depth of one’s understanding.

**Meditative Clarity Must Be Transcended to Prevent New Forms of Clinging**  
While mental stillness and focus provide the conditions for clear seeing, they are not ends in themselves. When wisdom arises, it reveals that even the most refined meditative states are impermanent and devoid of inherent self. This prevents the practitioner from mistaking stillness for liberation and ensures progress continues beyond temporary tranquility.

**Right Intention Sustains Diligent Practice**  
The persistent, skillful effort known as diligent practice is energized and guided by right intention. When one clearly perceives greed, hatred, and delusion as sources of suffering, action naturally becomes aligned with release rather than reinforcement. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle: intention directs effort, effort deepens seeing, and seeing strengthens intention.

**Liberation is Embodied in the Uprooting of Afflictions**  
A being who has attained the highest realization is defined not by titles or experiences but by the complete absence of greed, hatred, and delusion. These mental poisons are not merely subdued or managed—they are uprooted through profound, direct realization of impermanence, suffering, and non-self. This absence is the ultimate expression of transformed being.
