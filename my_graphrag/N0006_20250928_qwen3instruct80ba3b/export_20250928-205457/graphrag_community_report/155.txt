**Title: Understanding Non-Self, Dependent Origination, and Liberation in the Teachings of Yiqiefawuwo and Yuanqixingkong**

**Summary:**  
The teachings center on the absence of inherent self in all phenomena, the conditional nature of existence, and the realization of liberation as the direct perception of this emptiness. The five aggregates are revealed as conditioned and transient, debunking the illusion of a permanent “I.” Liberation arises not through escape from experience but through seeing the interdependent, empty nature of all conditions. Non-attachment wisdom emerges naturally from this understanding, and those who realize it attain freedom without reliance on external states or doctrines.

**The five aggregates as the primary example of non-self**  
The text identifies the five aggregates as the central illustration of Yiqiefawuwo — demonstrating that what is mistakenly perceived as a stable, personal self is in fact a transient accumulation of conditioned elements. This falseness of selfhood is not abstract but rooted in observable, momentary phenomena.

**Dependent origination as the foundational truth**  
Yuanqixingkong expresses the universal principle that all aspects of existence — from suffering to liberation — are not self-originated but arise only through interdependent conditions. This conditionality negates permanence and independence, establishing the basis for understanding emptiness across all domains of experience.

**Nirvana as the realization of conditionality, not an alternative state**  
Liberation is not portrayed as a transcendent realm separate from everyday life. Instead, nirvana is defined as the direct perception of dependent origination and emptiness — recognizing that the very nature of conditioned phenomena, when seen clearly, is itself freedom.

**Conditioning factors as the universal mechanism of existence**  
All things, without exception, derive from yanyin — the interlocking causes and conditions that make up reality. Nothing exists in isolation; every appearance, including notions of self or liberation, is sustained by this web of interdependence.

**Non-attachment wisdom as the natural outcome of recognizing non-self**  
When the false attribution of self to transient phenomena is seen through, a form of wisdom emerges that no longer clings. This wisdom is not cultivated through effort alone but arises as a direct consequence of seeing the absence of inherent nature in all that appears.

**Liberated beings attain freedom through realization of non-self**  
Those described as shengnZhe reach their state not by external action or ritual, but by fully comprehending that all phenomena — including their own sense of identity — are devoid of self. This understanding dissolves attachment at its root.

**The philosophical unity of Yiqiefawuwo and Yuanqixingkong**  
The teaching of non-self cannot be separated from the doctrine of dependent origination. The former is the experiential application; the latter is its theoretical ground. Both affirm the same core reality: phenomena are conditioned and lack intrinsic existence.

**Yuanqixingkong as the unfolding of conditioning factors**  
The truth of dependent origination and emptiness is not an isolated concept but the direct expression of how yanyin operate. All appearances, however subtle or profound, are nothing more than the dynamic play of conditions — no entity stands outside this process.
