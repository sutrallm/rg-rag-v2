**Title: Transcending Attachment and Self-Reliant Liberation in Meditative Practice**

**Summary**  
True inner freedom emerges not from external validations or meditative experiences, but from the sustained observation of the five aggregates as impermanent and without self. This practice dismantles the illusion of a lasting ego, dissolving greed, hatred, and delusion, and terminates the cycle of rebirth. Non-attachment to even the most refined meditative states—such as joy, emptiness, or perceived liberation—is essential. This state of unshakable self-reliance arises only through direct, moment-by-moment awareness of non-self, cultivated by mindfulness.

**Non-Attachment to Meditative States Is Essential for Liberation**  
Even profound meditative experiences—such as joy, lightness, emptiness, or perceived supernatural powers—must not be clung to. True liberation does not depend on these states; clinging to them, even as signs of progress, reinforces attachment. The wisdom of non-abiding requires recognizing all phenomena, including meditative achievements, as conditioned and transient. This prevents the mind from fixating on transient states as endpoints or validations of attainment.

**Inner Freedom Arises Solely from Internal Realization**  
Freedom independent of external conditions—such as praise, offerings, or mystical signs—is not cultivated through external reinforcement but through matured inner awareness. This self-reliant state is exemplified by the shengzhe, whose liberation is untouched by worldly validations. It emerges only when the mind no longer seeks confirmation or satisfaction from anything outside its own direct perception of reality.

**Observation of the Five Aggregates Breaks the Illusion of Self**  
The deliberate, moment-to-moment contemplation of form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness reveals their impermanent, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature. This direct observation dismantles the false belief in an enduring ego. By seeing that none of these aggregates constitute a "me" or "mine," the structural foundation of ego-clinging is removed, leading to the dissolution of the cycles of suffering.

**Mindfulness Enables Continuous Non-Self Awareness**  
Mindfulness functions as the practical vehicle through which the observation of non-self is sustained in daily life. It allows the recognition of impermanence and the absence of self to unfold in real time, preventing the mind from slipping into habitual identification with passing thoughts, sensations, or emotions. Without this ongoing awareness, the understanding of non-self remains theoretical rather than transformative.

**Non-Self Observation Eliminates the Root of Ignorance**  
The core迷惑 of ignorance—the belief in a permanent, independent self—is directly countered by the sustained practice of observing the five aggregates as ever-changing and selfless. This practice is not intellectual but experiential; it does not merely acknowledge impermanence but sees it in every moment, thereby disabling the mental pattern that sustains delusion and fuels clinging.

**Non-Attachment Is the Natural Result of Non-Self Insight**  
When phenomena are seen as conditioned, fleeting, and lacking an inherent self, clinging to them naturally ceases. Transcending attachment is not a forced discipline but the spontaneous outcome of this clear perception. As the illusion of self dissolves, so too does the impulse to grasp or reject experiences, leading to a state of unconditioned ease.

**Self-Reliant Liberation Requires No-Self Contemplation Alone**  
Ziyu jiefu cannot be achieved through external practices, rituals, or elevated meditative states. It arises exclusively from the consistent and deep practice of wuwo guanzhao—the non-self observation of the five aggregates. Without this practice, dependence on meditative experiences or external validations persists, and true inner freedom remains unattainable.
