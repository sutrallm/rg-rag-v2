**Title: Jie Tui, Jing Zhou, Zheng Jian, and Wu Xiu Er Xiu: The Path to Present-Moment Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (Jie Tui) is not a future goal but a present realization arising from non-attachment and direct understanding of reality. It is achieved through continuous mindfulness (Jing Zhou), grounded in correct understanding (Zheng Jian), and culminates in the recognition that no practice is needed — enlightenment is inherent. True liberation is marked by the absence of clinging to any state or attainment.

---

**Liberation Arises in the Present Through Non-Attachment**  
Liberation (Jie Tui) is not distant or conditional; it emerges immediatly when clinging ceases. It is equated with nirvana and requires no progression over time. Its occurrence is tied to the relinquishing of attachment to experiences, states, or goals, not to external conditions or future achievement.

**Continuous Mindfulness Is the Direct Path to Liberation**  
Sustained awareness of bodily actions, feelings, thoughts, and mental phenomena as they arise and pass away constitutes the practical method leading to liberation. This moment-to-moment observation is not preparatory but is itself the expression of liberation, as explicitly linked to the attainment of Jie Tui.

**Correct Understanding Enables Mindful Awareness**  
The foundation for continuous mindfulness lies in right view — the accurate perception of impermanence, non-self, suffering, dependent origination, and emptiness. Without this understanding, awareness lacks direction and depth, failing to align with ultimate reality.

**True Liberation Is Marked by Non-Clinging to Attainments**  
Even meditative states and insights must be released. Liberation is incomplete if one clings to them as achievements. The hallmark of realized freedom is Wu Zhu — the absence of attachment, even to the very experiences of awakening.

**Enlightenment Is Already Complete — No Practice Is Needed**  
When right view is fully established, the illusion of a separate self practicing toward a goal dissolves. The realization that all is inherently perfect renders effort unnecessary. This surrender — Wu Xiu Er Xiu — is not passive resignation but the recognition that nothing needs to be added or changed.
