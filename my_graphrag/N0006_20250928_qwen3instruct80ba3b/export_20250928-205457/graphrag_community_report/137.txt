**Title: Complete Liberation and the Saintly Being in the Tradition of Non-Clinging**

**Summary:**  
Complete liberation is achieved not through pursuit or effort toward states or goals, but through the total end of clinging, striving, and conceptual distinction. A saintly being embodies this state through pure moral conduct and genuine wisdom, having eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion. This state is characterized by stillness, freedom from emotional reactivity, and transcendence of rebirth. Liberation and nirvana are identical in meaning — both describe a mind no longer bound by attachment to self or phenomena.

**Complete liberation is the end of all grasping and conceptual striving**  
It is not reached by seeking powers, states, or objectives, but solely through the complete cessation of clinging to “self” and “what belongs to self.” This requires ending all striving and the mental habit of making conceptual distinctions, leading to a state untouched by pursuit or agenda.

**A saintly being is defined by the eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion**  
This person is not identified by external achievements or supernatural abilities, but by the inner condition of having uprooted the three roots of suffering. Their moral conduct is pure, their wisdom is genuine, and their mind rests without disturbance.

**The saintly being resides in stillness and is free from rebirth**  
Having transcended all forms of clinging — to self, to phenomena, to attainment — the saintly being abides in perfect stillness, unmoved by emotional reactivity. This condition brings an end to the cycle of birth and death, marking a fundamental rupture in the continuity of conditioned existence.

**Liberation and nirvana are one and the same state of mind**  
These terms are not separate goals or stages, but synonyms for the mind that no longer clings, seeks, or constructs distinctions. It is a condition of perfect clarity and tranquility, arising only when all grasping has ceased.
