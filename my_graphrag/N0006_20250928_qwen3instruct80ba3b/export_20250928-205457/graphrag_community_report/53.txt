**Title: Zheng Nian, Wuchang, and Yuanqi: The Path to Jietai Through Mindfulness, Impermanence, and Dependent Origination**

**Summary**  
The path to ultimate liberation (Jietai) is grounded in the sustained practice of continuous mindfulness (Zheng Nian), which enables direct observation of impermanence (Wuchang) and dependent origination (Yuanqi) in daily life. Through this observation, the illusion of a permanent self (Wuwo) dissolves, leading to non-attachment (Wuzhai), equanimity, and freedom from the rebirth cycle. Wisdom (Zhihui) arises not through belief but through experiential insight into the transient, conditionally arisen nature of all phenomena. The saintly practitioner (Shengzhe) attains Jietai by eliminating greed, hatred, and delusion, culminating in the cessation of suffering (Niepan). Mindfulness is both the method and the vessel through which this transformation occurs in every moment of ordinary activity.

**Observation of Impermanence Dissolves Attachment**  
By continuously observing the arising and ceasing of breath, emotions, thoughts, and physical sensations during daily activities, one directly witnesses that all conditioned phenomena—body, mind, relationships, and experiences—are fleeting, like dew or flickering lights. This recognition undermines the illusion of permanence, which is the root of clinging. When phenomena are seen as transient, attachment becomes irrational and unsustainable, leading naturally to non-attachment and inner peace.

**Mindfulness Sustains Concentration and Enables Deep Observation**  
Continuous mindfulness (Zheng Nian) is not a passive state but an active, moment-to-moment attention that stabilizes the mind into deep concentration (Chanding). This unwavering focus creates the mental stillness required to observe subtle patterns of experience—particularly the fleeting nature of the five aggregates. Without mindfulness, concentration cannot be maintained, and deep observation remains inaccessible.

**Dependent Origination Reveals the Conditioned Nature of Suffering and Liberation**  
All phenomena, including suffering and freedom from it, arise due to specific causes and conditions. Ignorance fuels craving, which perpetuates the cycle of birth and death. Liberation does not result from external forces or fate but from transforming mental habits through insight. Understanding this causal chain—where each link arises only when its predecessor is present—shows that ending ignorance ends suffering entirely.

**Non-Self Arises from Seeing Impermanence and Conditionality**  
When the five aggregates—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—are observed directly, no stable, independent “I” can be found. The sense of self is revealed as a transient collection of changing elements, conditioned by causes and continuously shifting. This experiential realization (Wuwo)—that nothing within the aggregates constitutes a permanent self—is not theoretical but directly seen through mindfulness of impermanence.

**Wisdom Emerges From Direct Experience, Not Doctrine**  
Wisdom (Zhihui) is cultivated not by intellectual understanding but by seeing impermanence, conditionality, and non-self in real time. It is through mindfulness that one verifies these truths firsthand: observing thoughts arise and vanish without an owner, noticing feelings change before attachment can take hold, and realizing the absence of a fixed self behind experience. This direct perception dismantles conceptual clinging and is the foundation of liberation.

**Liberation Is the Cessation of the Rebirth Cycle**  
Freedom (Jietai) and the rebirth cycle are mutually exclusive states. One who has eliminated greed, hatred, and delusion has transcended the causes that drive rebirth. This state is described as a mind like a clear sky—unmoved by gain or loss, praise or blame, birth or death. It is not an abstract ideal but the natural outcome of practicing mindfulness, recognizing impermanence, and understanding the conditional nature of all experience.

**The Five Aggregates Are the Field for Non-Self Insight**  
The practice of observing the five aggregates is not abstract meditation but an ongoing inquiry into the nature of present experience. By seeing that form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness are constantly changing, impermanent, and interdependent, one realizes none can be called “me” or “mine.” This specific observation (Wuwu) is the practical method that dissolves the root of suffering.

**Mindfulness Is the Daily Embodiment of Liberation Practices**  
Zheng Nian is not confined to sitting meditation. It is the continuous presence in walking, eating, speaking, and silence—the application of awareness to all actions. In this way, mindfulness becomes the living expression of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness, preventing afflictions from growing by observing their arising and letting them pass without reaction. It is through this daily discipline that delusion is gradually dismantled, and liberation is realized.
