**Title: Wuzhi, Wuguan, and Zhongji Jingmi: The Path to Liberation in Traditional Buddhist Realization**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is achieved not through external actions or attainment of blissful states, but through inner transformation rooted in direct observation of impermanence, non-self, and the cessation of clinging. The disciplined practices of mindfulness and contemplation dismantle mental distraction and egoic identification, leading naturally to a state of unconditioned stillness. This process is self-sustaining: recognizing the transient nature of all phenomena undermines attachment, which in turn dissolves the illusion of a fixed self, culminating in the cessation of suffering and rebirth.

---

**The Transcendence of Attachment as the Core Mechanism**  
Non-attachment to all phenomena — including spiritual experiences like bliss or supernatural abilities — is the essential condition for liberation. This state of transcending attachment arises from deep meditative observation and is not an emotional suppression but a clear seeing of the nature of all things. It is the direct response to understanding impermanence and non-self, and without it, liberation cannot occur. Even the pursuit of spiritual states becomes a form of clinging if not viewed through this lens.

**Contemplation of Impermanence as the Foundational Practice**  
Recognizing that all conditioned experiences — wealth, relationships, the body, and even mental states — are fleeting, like dew or lightning, breaks the grip of desire. This practice does not merely reduce craving; it uproots the belief that anything external or internal can offer lasting satisfaction. It is the practical tool that generates the wisdom necessary to disengage from the illusion of permanence, making it the gateway to deeper insight.

**Non-Self Insight as the Deeper Layer of Liberation**  
Seeing the five aggregates — form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness — as not “me” or “mine” dissolves the foundation of ego. This realization does not come from intellectual acceptance but from sustained, direct observation. When the notion of a separate, enduring self is seen through, craving loses its target, rebirth loses its momentum, and suffering ceases. This insight deepens and completes the understanding initiated by impermanence.

**Mindfulness as the Continuous Path to Stability**  
Sustained awareness in daily life is not an auxiliary practice but the very medium through which liberation is realized and maintained. Without this daily vigilance, mental distraction returns, reanimating attachment and delusion. The liberated mind is not one that has escaped the world but one that no longer reacts to it with craving or aversion, because it has seen through the mechanisms that stir those reactions.

**Ultimate Stillness as the Natural Outcome**  
When clinging and seeking cease, the mind rests in its unconditioned nature — free from striving, constructs, and mental fabrication. This state is not created, achieved, or attained; it is revealed when the layers of distraction and self-view are stripped away. It is the silent, boundless presence that follows the complete cessation of suffering, not a goal to be reached, but the default condition of a mind no longer bound by illusion.

**Liberation as the Embodiment of Transcendence**  
The holy one embodies the full result of this path: no afflictions remain, no roots of suffering persist. This being has ended the cycle of rebirth not by escaping life, but by transforming perception within it. Their existence is no longer governed by craving or aversion; they dwell in a state where Nirvana is not a distant goal but the immediate, uncontrived reality of their presence.
