**Title: The Sage, the Three Poisons, and the Path to Nirvana**

**Summary:**  
The text describes a system in which liberation from cyclic existence is achieved through the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion—recognized as the root sources of suffering. A sage, or saintly being, is defined by the total extinction of these three afflictions, resulting in unshakable tranquility, pure moral conduct, and direct realization of reality’s true nature: impermanence, non-self, and dependent origination. This state, known as nirvana, is not attained through external displays or supernatural feats but through sustained meditative practice, ethical discipline, and wisdom that dismantles the illusion of permanence and ownership. Delusion, as the foundational ignorance, gives rise to both greed and hatred, binding beings to rebirth. Its dissolution through insight into the selfless and conditioned nature of all phenomena is the sole pathway to final freedom.

**Delusion is the Root of All Afflictions**  
Delusion—defined as the false perception of a permanent, independent self and the misapprehension of phenomena as inherently satisfying or stable—is the underlying cause of both greed and hatred. It arises from ignorance of impermanence, non-self, and dependent origination. This fundamental misunderstanding fuels attachment to fleeting experiences and aversion to unpleasant ones, creating the emotional and psychological mechanisms that sustain cyclic existence. Without the eradication of this misperception, liberation is impossible, as it is the ground upon which craving and resistance take root.

**Greed and Hatred Are Dual Expressions of Ignorance**  
Greed manifests as clinging to pleasurable experiences, possessions, identities, or spiritual attainments, driven by the illusion that these can provide lasting satisfaction. Hatred emerges as anger, rejection, or aversion toward what is perceived as threatening or undesirable, rooted in the same delusion of permanence and selfhood. Both are not independent forces but reciprocal reactions—one seeking to hold on, the other to push away—stemming from the core distortion of reality. Their presence perpetuates suffering and rebirth; their absence marks the end of afflictive mental states.

**The Sage Embodies the Extinction of the Three Poisons**  
A sage is one who has completely uprooted greed, hatred, and delusion through direct insight and meditative stabilization. Their mind is characterized by unwavering calm, boundless equanimity, and complete non-attachment—not as passive withdrawal, but as the direct result of seeing all phenomena as impermanent, selfless, and conditioned. This state is mirrored in pure moral discipline, sense-gate restraint, and continuous mindfulness. Their inner radiance, described as void-like and unblemished, reflects the absence of all defilements and the absence of any need to project or prove spiritual attainment.

**Nirvana is the Cessation of Delusion and Its Manifestations**  
Nirvana is not a place or transcendent realm, but the total extinguishment of the three poisons—especially delusion, the root of all suffering. It is synonymous with liberation from rebirth and the end of future suffering. This final freedom is achieved only when ignorance is replaced by clear seeing: recognizing the five aggregates as not-self, dissolving the illusion of control, and ceasing all craving and aversion. The extinction of greed ends clinging; the extinction of hatred ends reactivity; the extinction of delusion ends the very basis of identification—and together, they constitute complete release.

**Saintly Qualities Emerge from Wisdom and Moral Purity**  
The perfected state of saintly beings is expressed through disciplined ethical conduct, mental clarity, and unshakable inner stillness. These qualities are not external achievements but internal transformations arising from the direct insight into reality’s true nature. Moral discipline is not ritualistic observance but the natural expression of a mind freed from self-centered impulses. Meditation stabilizes the mind to reveal delusions; wisdom dismantles them. The cultivation of loving-kindness and equanimity further dissolves partiality, allowing the heart to expand beyond attachment and aversion.

**Liberation Requires Sustained Effort and Insight**  
Freedom from samsara is not passive; it demands continuous diligence in meditation, ethical living, and mindful observation. The mind naturally reverts toward ignorance without vigilance. Chan Ding (concentration) enables the mind to become still enough to perceive the false constructs of self and permanence, allowing wisdom to arise and dissolve delusion. Through this process, attachment is severed, aversion fades, and the cycles of karma are broken. Liberation is the outcome of aligning action, awareness, and understanding—where insight becomes the compass and moral conduct the path.
