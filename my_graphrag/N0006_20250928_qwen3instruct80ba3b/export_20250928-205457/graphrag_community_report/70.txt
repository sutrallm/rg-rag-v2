**Title: Stillness and Inner Peace in Chan Ding Practice**

**Summary**  
Stillness and inner peace are interconnected, stable mental states achieved through the practice of Chan Ding and mindfulness. They arise not through suppression but through the natural cessation of craving, aversion, and delusion. These states are characterized by freedom from agitation, non-reactivity to stimuli, and independence from external conditions. Chan Ding is the primary method for cultivating stillness, which in turn reflects the unshakable peace of those who have transcended attachment. Enlightened beings exemplify this condition, remaining undisturbed by praise, blame, gain, or loss.

**Stillness is the natural outcome of Chan Ding practice**  
The practice of Chan Ding is defined by its result: a calm, undisturbed mind. By quieting mental disturbances, Chan Ding leads directly to stillness, which is not forced but emerges as craving and aversion subside. This state is not an absence of activity but an active, aware non-reactivity that manifests when the mind no longer clings or resists.

**Stillness and inner peace are experientially equivalent**  
The terms stillness of mind and inner peace refer to the same condition—the absence of mental agitation, suffering, and craving. Both are marked by freedom from external and internal disturbances, and neither depends on favorable circumstances. They are expressions of a mind no longer driven by greed, hatred, or delusion.

**Stillness reflects the unshakable peace of liberation**  
When attachment dissolves through detachment, the mind settles into profound stillness, which is not merely a temporary calm but the abiding condition of a liberated mind. This state is unshaken by praise, blame, gain, or loss, and it serves as both the result and the visible expression of enlightenment.

**Enlightened beings manifest stillness regardless of circumstance**  
Those who have attained liberation dwell in a permanent state of unshakable inner peace. Their stillness is not contingent on environment or experience; it remains steady even amid fluctuating external conditions, demonstrating that true tranquility arises from within, not from external control.
