**Title: Delusion, Greed, and Hatred as the Root Causes of Samsara and the Path to Liberation by Saintly Beings**

**Summary:**  
The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth (samsara) is sustained by three interlinked afflictions: delusion, greed, and hatred. Delusion—the fundamental misperception of reality as containing permanent, independent self and inherently satisfying phenomena—is the root cause of both greed and hatred. Greed manifests as clinging to pleasures and identities, while hatred arises as aversion to unpleasant experiences, both stemming from delusion. Liberation is achieved only through the complete eradication of these three afflictions, which is the defining trait of saintly beings. This is accomplished through wisdom, meditative concentration, and non-clinging, which together dismantle false perceptions of permanence and selfhood, leading to the cessation of suffering and ultimate freedom.

**Delusion is the Foundational Misunderstanding of Reality**  
Delusion arises from failing to perceive impermanence, non-self, and dependent origination. It is not passive ignorance but an active distortion—mistaking fleeting phenomena as stable, owned, or inherently satisfying. This misperception creates the illusion of a permanent self and fuels all other afflictions. Without its eradication, liberation is impossible, as delusion underlies the entire cycle of rebirth. It is the root from which craving and aversion grow, and its destruction through direct insight into the true nature of phenomena marks the attainment of saintly realization.

**Greed is the Clinging Born of Deluded Perception**  
Greed emerges when delusion leads beings to believe that phenomena can provide lasting satisfaction. It manifests as attachment to sensory pleasures, possessions, spiritual attainments, and even identities. This clinging perpetuates the cycle of rebirth by generating karmic continuity and sustaining emotional dependence on external conditions. Greed is inseparable from delusion: it cannot exist without the false belief in permanence and selfhood. Its complete extinction is a hallmark of saintly beings, who have severed all attachment and thus broken the link between perception and craving.

**Hatred is the Aversion Rooted in Misguided Expectations**  
Hatred arises as a reaction to experiences perceived as threatening, unpleasant, or undesirable—an aversion fueled by the delusion that reality should conform to one’s preferences. It is not merely anger but a deep-seated resistance rooted in the false belief that pain can and should be eliminated to protect a permanent self. This rejection generates inner turmoil, obstructs mental clarity, and produces negative karmic outcomes. Like greed, hatred is a dual expression of the same root delusion: grasping at what is liked and pushing away what is disliked. Its cessation defines the saintly state, characterized by unwavering equanimity.

**Liberation is Defined by the Total Extinguishment of the Three Afflictions**  
Final freedom is attained only when delusion, greed, and hatred are fully eradicated. This state, known as nirvana, is equivalent to the cessation of samsara and is exclusive to those who have realized the true, impermanent, and selfless nature of all phenomena. Saintly beings are identified by their complete absence of these three afflictions—their minds no longer respond with craving or aversion, nor are they bound by false perceptions. Their freedom is not theoretical but experiential, arising from sustained meditative discipline and direct insight that dismantles the illusion of self.

**Meditative Concentration and Wisdom Are the Means to Break the Cycle**  
Through sustained concentration, the mind becomes stable enough to see through delusions of permanence and selfhood. This clarity enables wisdom to arise, which directly opposes ignorance by revealing the conditioned and empty nature of all things. Non-clinging is cultivated alongside this insight, preventing attachment from reasserting itself. Wisdom does not merely correct thought but transforms perception, dissolving the roots of greed and hatred. Without this combination of concentration and wisdom, the mind reverts to ignorance, and the cycle continues. Liberation is not passive but requires continuous, diligent practice to uproot deep-seated patterns of misperception.
