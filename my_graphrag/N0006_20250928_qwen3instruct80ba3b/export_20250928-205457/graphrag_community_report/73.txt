**Title: Wu Zhu and Nirvana: The Non-Attached Mind as the Culmination of Buddhist Liberation**

**Summary**  
True liberation is realized not through pursuit of spiritual achievements, but through the complete relinquishment of all attachment—including to enlightenment itself. The state of non-abiding mind, characterized by the absence of clinging to experiences, goals, or identity, is both the path and the destination. This state aligns perfectly with nirvana, which arises only when greed, hatred, delusion, and the illusion of self are extinguished. The highest practice is to cease seeking, allowing natural freedom to emerge.

**Non-Abiding Mind Defines Liberation**  
Freedom is not found in attaining higher states of meditation or insight, but in releasing all attachment to them—including bliss, realizations, or spiritual powers. A mind that abides nowhere does not fixate on any experience as ultimate, rendering it free from the constraints of desire and identification.

**Nirvana Is the Cessation of Clinging, Not an Achievement**  
Nirvana is not a destination to be reached through effort or method, but the natural result of extinguishing ignorance and attachment. It is realized only when the seeking of enlightenment stops, revealing that the end is not something gained, but something revealed when all striving ceases.

**No-Self Naturally Leads to Non-Attachment**  
When the notion of a permanent “I” is seen through, there is no longer a center from which to cling to experiences, states, or attainments. The dissolution of self-identity removes the basis for all grasping, allowing the mind to remain untouched by any phenomenon.

**Non-Attachment to Attainment Is the Practical Expression of Freedom**  
The act of letting go of goals—including the goal of liberation—is not passive resignation, but active disengagement from all mental fixation. This refusal to hold any state as superior or final is the living embodiment of liberation in daily practice.
