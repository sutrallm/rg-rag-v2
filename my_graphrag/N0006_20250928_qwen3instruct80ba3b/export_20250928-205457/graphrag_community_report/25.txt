**Title: Jiefu, Wuwo, and the Threefold Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (Jiefu) is not an external achievement but the uncovering of the mind’s innate purity, attained only through the complete dissolution of the self-illusion (Wuwo) and the cessation of all clinging. This state arises exclusively from the integrated practice of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom—the Threefold Training. True freedom is marked by the absence of greed, hatred, and delusion, and is realized not through rituals or psychic powers, but through persistent mindfulness and non-attachment, even to spiritual experiences. Awakening occurs in daily life through direct observation of impermanence and non-self, leading to the natural letting go of all forms of attachment.

---

**The Dissolution of Self is the Final Release**  
The complete end of suffering occurs when the illusion of "I" and "belonging" is fully uprooted. This non-self (Wuwo) is not a concept to be believed but a reality observed: all phenomena, including thoughts and experiences, are empty of inherent existence. When the sense of a separate self collapses, the root of craving and fear vanishes, revealing freedom. Liberation is the direct result of this realization—nothing remains to cling to, not even the idea of being liberated.

**Liberation Requires No External Aid**  
Jiefu cannot be attained through rituals, divine grace, supernatural abilities, or changes in environment. Even meditative bliss or psychic powers, if clung to, become obstacles. The path is internal and active: it demands the sustained disciplines of ethical conduct, focused attention, and penetrating understanding. Without the eradication of ignorance and desire through these internal practices, liberation remains inaccessible regardless of external circumstances.

**Mindfulness is the Mechanism of Freedom**  
Moment-to-moment awareness (Zhengnian) prevents defilements from taking hold. It is not passive observation but active non-attachment to whatever arises—thoughts, emotions, sensations, or spiritual experiences. This steady presence reveals the transient, impersonal nature of all things, making clinging impossible. In this way, mindfulness is the living expression of liberation, making it real in every instant, whether in solitude or amidst society.

**Wisdom Reveals What Is, and Thus Frees**  
True wisdom (Zhihui) emerges from direct, unfiltered observation of conditioned phenomena. Seeing the five aggregates as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and not-self dismantles the false construction of a lasting identity. This seeing is not intellectual but experiential—it naturally leads to letting go. When one comprehends the empty nature of all experience, attachment dissolves without effort, and the mind becomes as open as space and clear as a mirror.

**The Threefold Training is the Only Path**  
Moral discipline (Jie), meditative concentration (Chan), and wisdom (Hui) are inseparable components of the complete journey to freedom. None alone suffices; discipline steadies the mind, concentration deepens observation, and wisdom penetrates illusion. Together, they form a unified whole that enables the practitioner to transcend attachment to all phenomena—including spiritual attainments—and arrive at true liberation. No other combination or method leads to this result.

**Freedom is Expressed in Non-Attachment**  
Shengzhe (the sage) embodies liberation not by possessing extraordinary powers but by living without grasping. Wu Zhang (non-attachment) is not indifference but the absence of inner resistance or craving toward any experience—pleasure or pain, gain or loss, presence or absence. It is the natural outcome of seeing things as they are: fleeting, impersonal, and empty. To be free is to meet each moment without adding the narrative of “me” or “mine.”
