Title: Zhongdao Xingxiu: The Middle Way Practice of Balanced Mindfulness

Summary:  
The Middle Way practice emphasizes a balanced approach to meditation and spiritual development, rejecting both sensual indulgence and extreme asceticism. It explicitly discourages attachment to meditative signs and supernatural powers, treating them as impermanent byproducts rather than objectives. Liberation is framed as arising from present-moment mindfulness and balanced effort, guided by correct understanding that avoids false views about meditative experiences.

—

Presence of Meditative Signs Does Not Signal Progress  
Experiences such as lights, visions, or feelings of bliss—known as meditative signs—may arise during deep concentration, but they are conditioned and impermanent. Engaging with or clinging to these phenomena distracts from the path to true liberation. The practice treats them as neutral occurrences, not indicators of advancement.

Supernatural Powers Are Not Goals of Practice  
Extraordinary abilities said to emerge from advanced meditation are acknowledged as possible side-effects, but they are not pursued or valued as endpoints. Seeking such powers diverts attention from the essential work of balanced mindfulness and correct understanding.

Balance in Meditation Prevents Extremes  
The practice of concentration is neither intensified to excess nor withheld entirely. It is cultivated with steady, moderate effort, avoiding both indulgence in pleasurable states and harsh self-denial. This equilibrium ensures stability without attachment.

True Liberation Relies on Correct Understanding, Not Experience  
Progress on the path depends on insight that avoids denying meditative experiences outright or overstating their importance. Liberation is grounded in present-moment awareness and balanced effort, not in the pursuit or identification with any mental or supernatural phenomenon.
