Title: The Illusion of Self and Its Role in Suffering

Summary:  
The notion of a fixed self is identified as the foundational delusion that gives rise to all division and suffering. This delusion extends naturally into attachment to what is perceived as belonging to the self, forming an inseparable system of subject and object. Together, they establish the root of continuous cycles of existence.

1. The illusion of self is the core expression of ignorance  
The belief in a distinct, enduring “I” is not merely a misconception but the central manifestation of fundamental unawareness. This belief is the origin point from which all dualistic thinking and emotional distress emerge, as it creates the illusion of separation between the self and the world.

2. Attachment to possessions and experiences arises from the self-illusion  
The craving for “mine”—whether objects, relationships, or states of being—stems directly from the initial fixation on “I.” This attachment is not separate but intrinsically linked to the self-concept, forming two poles of the same mental structure.

3. Self and possession together form the root of cyclic existence  
The interconnected dynamic of subject (“I”) and object (“mine”) functions as a unified system that perpetuates ongoing cycles. Neither can exist independently in this context; their mutual reinforcement sustains the very structure that binds beings to continuous becoming and suffering.
