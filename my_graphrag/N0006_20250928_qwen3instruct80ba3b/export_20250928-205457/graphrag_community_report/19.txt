**Title: Xinjiede, Shenger, and Niepan: The Nature of Liberated Awareness**

**Summary:**  
Liberation of the mind (xinjiede) is the culmination of spiritual practice, achieved through the extinction of craving, aversion, and attachment. It is an internal state, independent of ritual, and is equivalent to nirvana (niepan). The saintly one (shenger) is the individual who has fully realized this state, characterized by unbroken awareness (zhengnian) and wisdom that perceives impermanence and non-self without attachment. Such a person embodies self-sufficient inner luminosity, free from suffering and the cycle of rebirth.

**The liberated mind is nirvana**  
The terms xinjiede and niepan are not merely related—they are identical in meaning. Liberation of the mind is not a metaphor or path toward nirvana; it is nirvana itself, realized when defilements are extinguished. This equivalence underscores that spiritual freedom is not an external reward but the direct outcome of inner transformation.

**The saintly one abides in unbroken awareness**  
Shenger are defined by their continuous presence in zhengnian—a state of undisturbed, unwavering awareness. This is not a temporary meditation state but a permanent condition of being, marking the dissolution of mental disturbances and the stabilization of clarity. It serves as the observable hallmark of their liberation.

**Defining wisdom is seeing without attachment**  
Shenger possess wuzhi, a wisdom that sees the true nature of reality—impermanence and non-self—without clinging or aversion. This is not intellectual understanding but a perceptual shift that eliminates suffering at its root. Their freedom arises not from knowledge alone, but from the absence of reaction to what is perceived.

**Liberation is self-sufficient and internal**  
The state of shenger and xinjiede does not depend on external rites, objects, or social recognition. Inner luminosity emerges naturally from the extinction of craving, making the liberated being self-contained. This wholeness renders them free from the cycle of rebirth and untouched by suffering, as their experience of reality is no longer conditioned by desire or discrimination.
