**Title: Nihun and Jieduo: The Present-Moment Cessation of Suffering Through Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**  
Nihun and Jieduo are not distant goals or metaphysical states but immediate experiences arising when attachment and resistance dissolve in the present moment. They are synonymous, both resulting from the direct recognition of non-self and sustained by concentrated mindfulness. Liberation occurs not through rituals or external forces, but through letting go and clear seeing here and now.

**Concentrated Mindfulness Enables the Extinguishing of Suffering**  
When mindfulness is focused and unwavering, it allows one to remain fully in the present. This presence disrupts the continuity of attachment, the root cause of suffering. Without habitual clinging, the conditions that sustain distress naturally cease.

**Non-Self Recognition Directly Produces Liberation**  
The dissolution of the illusion of a fixed, separate self removes the basis for clinging and resistance. This recognition is not theoretical but experiential, and it is the primary mechanism through which suffering ends—clinging cannot persist where there is no sense of self to protect or fulfill.

**Nihun and Jieduo Are Equivalent Realizations**  
Both terms describe the exact same phenomenon: the immediate stoppage of suffering when attachment and resistance dissolve. Neither refers to a future state, a destination, or a divine reward. They are identical in nature, differing only in terminology, and both are accessible in the present through clear perception and non-attachment.

**Liberation Requires No External Ritual or Intervention**  
Freedom from suffering arises solely from internal transformation—letting go and seeing clearly. No ceremonial act, prayer, or divine aid is necessary. The path is found in the direct, personal act of releasing holds on experience as it is.
