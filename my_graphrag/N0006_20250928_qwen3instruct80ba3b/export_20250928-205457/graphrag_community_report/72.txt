**Title: Nirvana as the Cessation of Suffering Through the Threefold Training**

**Summary:**  
Nirvana is the complete cessation of suffering, achieved through the total eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. It is not a state of annihilation or eternal existence, but a transcendence beyond all conceptual extremes, marked by peace, non-attachment, and the end of the cycle of rebirth. This state is realized only through the sequential cultivation of moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom, with the final step requiring the letting go of even the desire for enlightenment. Attachment to spiritual experiences, including meditative bliss or supernatural powers, must be transcended. The attainment of Nirvana is the definitive mark of the sage or liberated one, who resides in a non-abiding mind—free from clinging, goals, and conceptual frameworks.

---

**The Threefold Training is the Sole Path to Liberation**  
Liberation from suffering is accessible only through the ordered progression of moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom. These three elements form an indivisible sequence: discipline steadies conduct, concentration calms the mind, and wisdom penetrates the true nature of reality. Without the full development of this threefold path, the defilements cannot be extinguished, and Nirvana remains unattained.

**Nirvana is the End of Rebirth Driven by Attachment**  
The cycle of existence—referred to as becoming or rebirth—is sustained by karma and clinging. Nirvana is not merely a spiritual state but the definitive cessation of this cycle. Its realization is marked by the complete absence of attachment, particularly the root causes of craving and ignorance. When these are fully eradicated, birth and death no longer apply.

**True Liberation Requires Non-Attachment Even to Enlightenment**  
The final barrier to Nirvana is the subtle clinging to spiritual achievements—meditative bliss, supernatural powers, or the aspiration for enlightenment itself. This clinging, termed Zaizhigrasping, must be dissolved through transcendent wisdom. Only when the mind ceases to pursue any goal, including liberation, does the non-abiding state arise, which is the essence of Nirvana.

**Wisdom Arises from Concentration and the Four Foundations of Mindfulness**  
Concentration purifies the mind, allowing direct perception of impermanence, non-self, and non-attachment. This meditative clarity is the gateway to wisdom—the realization of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness—which is the direct path to Nirvana. Such wisdom does not rely on doctrine but on experiential insight into the nature of all phenomena, including the transient quality of meditative attainments.

**Nirvana is the Natural Outcome of Realizing the Four Noble Truths**  
The cessation of suffering, the Third Noble Truth, is not metaphorical but the literal extinguishing of all conditioned existence. This cessation is not achieved through force or will alone, but through the full integration of understanding and practice. When one sees clearly that all phenomena are impermanent and unsatisfactory, craving dissolves, and with it, the arising of suffering and rebirth.

**The Attained One Resides Beyond Concepts and Goals**  
One who has realized Nirvana is no longer bound by conceptual frameworks—neither existence nor non-existence, neither gain nor loss. This state is described as tranquility, non-birth, and ultimate peace. The liberated person does not seek, rest, or cling; they simply are, free from all mental constructs, including the idea of having “attained.” This non-abiding mind is the only true expression of freedom.
