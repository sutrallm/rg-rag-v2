**Title: Jietuo, the Four Noble Truths, and the Role of the Buddha in Buddhist Liberation**

**Summary**  
Liberation (Jietuo) is the ultimate aim of Buddhist practice, achieved only through personal effort and direct realization of the Four Noble Truths. It is not granted by external forces, rituals, or teachers, but requires the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion. The Buddha revealed the path but does not awaken others; each individual must realize truth for themselves. The Eightfold Path is the method prescribed to end suffering, and Nirvana is the final state attained when clinging — including to liberation itself — is fully relinquished. Ignorance is the foundational cause of suffering, and the dissolution of the illusion of self through understanding the five aggregates leads directly to liberation.

---

**The Path to Liberation Is Self-Activated**  
No external agent — not the Buddha, not a wise teacher, nor divine intervention — can bestow liberation. The Buddha points to the path, and the wise teacher guides, but the realization must arise from within the practitioner through personal practice, mindfulness, and direct insight. Liberation is an internal transformation, not an external bestowal.

**Freedom Is Found in the Eradication of Root Afflictions**  
The three roots of suffering — greed, hatred, and delusion — must be completely uprooted to achieve liberation. Their elimination is not a gradual softening but a total dissolution, which ends the cycle of rebirth and binds the practitioner to Nirvana. This eradication is the core action required to break free from suffering.

**The Four Noble Truths Define the Structure of Liberation**  
The Four Noble Truths provide the sole doctrinal framework for understanding suffering and its cessation. They affirm that suffering is real, its origin lies in craving and ignorance, its end is possible through Nirvana, and the Eightfold Path is the method to realize that end. Without comprehending and enacting this structure, liberation cannot be attained.

**Nirvana Is the Culmination, Not the Process**  
Jietuo refers to the journey of dismantling clinging and illusion, while Nirvana is the final, unconditioned state reached upon completion of that journey. They are distinct: one is the path, the other the destination. Nirvana emerges only when the self is fully seen as empty and all forms of attachment, even to the idea of freedom, are relinquished.

**Ignorance Is the Foundational Cause of Suffering**  
The root of craving — and therefore of the entire cycle of suffering — is ignorance (Wu Ming). Without understanding the true nature of reality, particularly the absence of a permanent self, craving persists. This makes the correction of ignorance the indispensable first step toward liberation.

**The Illusion of Self Must Be Dissolved**  
Direct realization that the five aggregates (Wuyun) are not-self breaks the illusion of a permanent, independent identity. This understanding is not theoretical but experiential, and it is the key that unties the knot of attachment binding beings to samsara. Without this realization, liberation remains inaccessible.

**The Eightfold Path Is the Practical Method, Not a Belief System**  
The Eightfold Path is not a set of dogmas to be accepted but a way of life to be lived. It is the fourth Noble Truth and the practical expression of how to end suffering. Its components — from right view to right mindfulness — guide the practitioner in daily conduct, mental discipline, and wisdom, making it the operational framework for achieving Jietuo.

**The Buddha’s Role Is Revealing, Not Saving**  
The Buddha illuminated the path to liberation but did not, and cannot, awaken others. His function was to reveal the truths that lead to freedom, not to perform the work of awakening. Every practitioner must walk the path themselves, as no one else can realize the truth for them.
