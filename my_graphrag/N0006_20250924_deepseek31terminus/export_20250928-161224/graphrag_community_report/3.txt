**Report on the State of Mental Liberation and Its Prerequisites**

**Summary**

This report details the state of mental liberation (心解脱), its defining characteristics, and the path to its attainment. Mental liberation is a state of profound freedom and release achieved through dedicated spiritual practice, primarily meditation. It is characterized by an unwavering wisdom that perceives the impermanent and illusory nature of all phenomena. A practitioner who attains this state is entirely free from attachment (执著) to any experience, state, or achievement, including the fruits of their own practice. This liberation is a significant milestone on the path toward the ultimate goal of Nirvana.

**The Nature of Mental Liberation**

Mental liberation is a state of complete mental freedom and emancipation. It signifies a release from afflictions, attachments, and all forms of bondage. A practitioner in this state can perceive the true nature of the world—specifically, the arising and passing away of all things—exactly as it is. The mind becomes autonomous, unbound by any phenomenon, comparable to a lotus leaf that remains unstained by water. This state is a mark of complete accomplishment in spiritual practice.

**Freedom from Attachment as a Defining Condition**

A fundamental characteristic of a liberated practitioner is the absence of attachment (执著) to any state, object, or conceptual boundary. This includes non-clinging to spiritual achievements or realizations. Attachment is identified as a primary source of suffering and a major obstacle that must be overcome. The liberated mind does not generate clinging, as it clearly sees the illusory nature of all phenomena.

**The Central Role of Wisdom**

Wisdom is both the means to achieve mental liberation and its resulting quality. This wisdom is cultivated through meditation and enables the practitioner to observe the impermanent nature of the world without generating attachment. It provides a deep, penetrating insight into the truth that all conditioned phenomena are transient (诸法无常). The function of this wisdom is to see through the虚幻本质 of worldly phenomena, leading to a state of non-attachment.

**Meditation as the Primary Pathway**

Meditation (禅定) is the principal method for attaining mental liberation. Through the practice of meditation, a practitioner can realize the liberated state of mind. This practice enables the observation of impermanence and the development of the non-attaching wisdom that characterizes liberation. Mental liberation is, therefore, a state directly achieved through meditative practice.

**The Impermanent Nature of All Phenomena**

The essential characteristic of all existence (世间万物) is its impermanent and transient nature. The truth of impermanence (无常) is the fundamental reality that a liberated practitioner is able to perceive clearly and directly. This perception involves an accurate awareness of the continuous process of the birth and death of all things.

**Mental Liberation as a Precursor to Nirvana**

Mental liberation represents a crucial achievement on the spiritual path, leading toward the ultimate state of Nirvana. It is a foundational liberation that allows the practitioner to progress toward this final goal. Nirvana is understood as a more ultimate state of emancipation, representing the complete fulfillment of the liberation begun with心解脱.

**Metaphors for the Liberated State**

The state of mental liberation is illustrated through specific metaphors. The practitioner's mind is described as being as firm and unmoved by worldly praise or blame as a solid rock is unmoved by the wind. Furthermore, the liberated state is compared to a lotus, which emerges pure and unstained from muddy water, symbolizing a mind that remains unaffected by the world.
