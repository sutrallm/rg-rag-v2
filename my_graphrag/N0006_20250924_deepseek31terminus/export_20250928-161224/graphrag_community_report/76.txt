**Report on the Buddhist Doctrine of Karma**

**Summary**
The text describes Karma, or the law of cause and effect, as the fundamental principle governing existence within the cycle of rebirth (Samsara). All experiences of suffering and happiness are direct results of an individual's past actions of body, speech, and mind. The doctrine posits that undisciplined thoughts lead to affliction, while diligent spiritual practice is the method to eliminate suffering. The ultimate goal is to achieve enlightenment and liberation, a state where one is no longer bound by the effects of Karma.

**The Mechanism of Karma's Origin and Cessation**
Karma is generated by actions and is the cause of continued existence in Samsara. Its generation is conditional: when the mind is free from attachments and obstructions, Karma cannot arise. Conversely, indulging in deluded thoughts inevitably produces afflictions. Upon achieving enlightenment, the accumulated Karma can be dissolved, indicating that its power is not permanent once its root cause is eradicated.

**Karma as an Inevitable Law of Moral Causality**
The doctrine defines Karma as a natural and inescapable law where virtuous actions lead to happiness and non-virtuous actions lead to suffering. This cause-and-effect relationship is likened to a seed that must bear its corresponding fruit, emphasizing the certainty of the outcome. This principle applies across past, present, and future lifetimes within the cycle of rebirth.

**The Path to Liberation Through Understanding Cause and Effect**
A deep understanding of the law of Karma is presented as the essential foundation for transcending Samsara and attaining Nirvana. The text specifies that the method for cutting off afflictions is diligent spiritual practice. The state of liberation is defined as being free from the bondage of Karma, which is the final consequence of correctly applying this understanding.

**The Application of Karma to Specific Practices**
The principle of Karma provides the rationale for specific practices such as generosity (布施). The positive results or merits from acts of giving are explained as a direct function of the law of cause and effect, ensuring that such actions will bring about good retribution.
