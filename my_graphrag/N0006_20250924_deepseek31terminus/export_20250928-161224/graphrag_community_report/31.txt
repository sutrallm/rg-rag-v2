**Report on the Concept of the 'Other Shore' (彼岸) in Spiritual Liberation**

**Summary**
The text describes the "Other Shore" (彼岸) as the ultimate state of liberation, achieved through rigorous spiritual practice. This state is characterized by freedom from the cycle of birth and death, the transcendence of dualistic concepts like good and evil, and the complete cessation of suffering. It is a condition of profound peace and purity, attained by sages who have perfected their cultivation.

**The 'Other Shore' as the Final Goal of Spiritual Practice**
The "Other Shore" represents the ultimate state of liberation, which is the final destination for a practitioner. It is a state of absolute safety and the absence of suffering, directly contrasted with the "near shore" (此岸), which is full of pain. Reaching this state signifies that an individual has transcended the cycle of birth and death (生死轮回) and will no longer fall back into it.

**The Path to Liberation Requires Purification and Perfection**
Attaining the "Other Shore" is not spontaneous; it requires diligent and continuous practice. One must purify the mind, cease deluded thoughts, and complete all aspects of spiritual cultivation. The text specifies that this achievement is the result of perfecting the threefold training of precepts, meditation, and wisdom (戒定慧三学), leading to a state where all defilements are eternally extinguished.

**Characteristics of an Attained Sage**
An individual who has reached the "Other Shore" possesses specific qualities. They are free from attachments, their speech is pure, and they have laid down the burden of the five aggregates (五蕴). Their state is marked by an absence of desire, freedom from doubt, and the severing of all clinging. This condition is a portrayal of Nirvana, symbolizing a state far removed from all bonds and afflictions.

**Transcendence of Duality and Worldly Recognition**
Upon reaching this ultimate liberation, a sage transcends all relative or dualistic concepts, specifically including the dichotomy of good and evil (善恶相对概念). This elevated state commands respect, as even celestial beings (诸天) pay homage to such a purified and accomplished practitioner.

**The Lotus as a Metaphor for Purity Amidst Imperfection**
The purity of a sage is illustrated through the metaphor of a lotus flower blooming unstained from muddy water. This symbolizes the saint's ability to achieve a state of清净 (purity and clarity) despite originating from or existing within an impure environment.
