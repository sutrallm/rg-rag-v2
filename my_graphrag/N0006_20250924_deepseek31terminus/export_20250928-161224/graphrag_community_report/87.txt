**Report on the Path to Spiritual Accomplishment**

**Summary**

This report details the attributes of an accomplished spiritual being, referred to as a Sage or Saint (聖者), and the systematic path required to attain this state. The Sage is characterized by perfect moral discipline (戒), deep meditative concentration (定), and profound wisdom (慧), which together enable the complete eradication of mental afflictions and liberation from all suffering. The path to this achievement is the Threefold Training (三学), a progressive practice where moral discipline serves as the foundation for developing concentration, which in turn gives rise to wisdom. Practitioners are advised to respect the immutable law of cause and effect (karma), diligently perform good deeds, guard against minor misdeeds, and maintain constant mindfulness to progress toward the ultimate goal of Nirvana.

**The Attributes of an Accomplished Sage**

A Sage is a noble person who has attained a high level of spiritual realization and liberation. Such an individual possesses pure moral discipline and perfect wisdom. Their mind is tranquil, stable like the earth, and unshaken by worldly circumstances such as praise or slander. Having severed the root of all afflictions like greed, hatred, and delusion, they have transcended the suffering inherent in the cycle of birth and death. Sages abide in the joy of the Dharma, and their actions and speech naturally accord with truth. Their attainment is so great that they are revered and praised by celestial beings (諸天). A specific type of Sage, an Arhat (阿羅漢), has reached the birthless state of Nirvana, free from all defilements and clinging.

**The Progressive Path of the Threefold Training**

The definitive path to becoming a Sage is the Threefold Training, a sequential practice of moral discipline (戒), concentration (定), and wisdom (慧). These three components are interdependent and mutually supportive, likened to the three legs of a tripod cauldron, all necessary for stability. Moral discipline is the foundational practice, providing the purity and stability required for the mind to enter meditative states. From this foundation of discipline arises deep concentration. This concentrated, tranquil mind then becomes the basis from which true wisdom emerges. The complete and perfect cultivation of these three studies leads to the final goal of liberation.

**The Role of Moral Discipline as the Foundation**

Moral discipline is the essential starting point for all spiritual practice. It involves upholding ethical precepts, which is compared to maintaining clear water that attracts wise people and washes away impurities. This practice protects the body and mind, leading to inner peace and joy. A calm and pure mind, resulting from disciplined conduct, is the necessary condition for successfully entering deep states of meditative concentration. A Sage is fundamentally characterized by the possession of pure moral discipline.

**Meditative Concentration as the Catalyst for Wisdom**

Meditative concentration, or 畠定, is a state of deep, focused attention and mindfulness achieved through diligent practice. It allows the practitioner to achieve inner stillness and freedom from internal and external distractions. This state of mental stability and tranquility is not an end in itself but is the crucial catalyst for the development of wisdom. By calming the mind and focusing it, concentration allows one to clearly perceive the illusory and impermanent nature of worldly phenomena. This direct perception gives rise to discerning insight into the true nature of reality.

**The Ultimate Goal of Discerning Wisdom**

Wisdom is the ultimate objective of the Threefold Training. It is the profound understanding that dispels the fundamental darkness of ignorance, allowing for the complete severance of afflictions. This wisdom is not intellectual but arises directly from the experience of meditative concentration. It enables the practitioner to fully comprehend the law of cause and effect and the nature of arising and passing away. With this wisdom, the roots of mental defilements are彻底断除, leading to the attainment of Nirvana, a state where all afflictions are extinguished.

**The Necessity of Diligent Practice and Cause and Effect**

Achievement on this path requires sustained and diligent effort. Practitioners must constantly maintain mindfulness and awareness to guard their minds against negative thoughts. They are encouraged to actively perform good deeds, as even minor good actions accumulate positive karma over time, just as dust particles form a hill. Conversely, minor evil deeds must not be underestimated, as their karmic consequences accumulate like water droplets eventually filling a container. This reflects the immutable law of cause and effect, which governs spiritual outcomes. Negligence allows afflictions to arise, while diligent practice leads to their destruction.
