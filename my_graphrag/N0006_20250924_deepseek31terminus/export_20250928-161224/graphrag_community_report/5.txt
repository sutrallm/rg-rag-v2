**Report on Buddhist Practice and Attainment**

**Summary**  
This report synthesizes the core principles, practices, and objectives of a Buddhist practitioner as defined in the provided text. The analysis reveals a structured path where an individual, through disciplined practice of moral precepts, meditation, and wisdom, aims to purify the mind, overcome afflictions, and ultimately achieve liberation from the cycle of birth and death. The roles of key figures like the Buddha and foundational concepts such as the Dharma are established as essential guides on this path.

**The Path of the Practitioner**  
An individual engages in spiritual cultivation, with the ultimate goal of becoming a sage or an enlightened being. This path requires diligent practice of moral discipline, meditation, and the development of wisdom. The practitioner's primary focus is the training and regulation of their own mind, aiming to achieve a state of inner peace, stability, and ultimately, liberation.

**Foundational Practices: Moral Discipline, Meditation, and Wisdom**  
The practitioner's cultivation is built upon a triad of training: upholding pure moral precepts as a foundation, practicing meditation to achieve mental focus and tranquility, and developing wisdom to perceive the true nature of phenomena. This systematic approach is the complete path leading to Nirvana.

**The Goal of Mental Purification and Liberation**  
A central objective is the purification of the mind from defilements such as greed, hatred, and delusion. Through sustained practice, the practitioner seeks to sever all afflictions and bonds, resulting in a state of mental liberation where the mind is free from attachment and disturbance. This liberated state is characterized by inner stillness and peace.

**The Principle of Diligent Vigilance**  
The practitioner must maintain a constant attitude of diligence and vigilance, avoiding negligence. This unwavering effort is necessary to progress on the path, achieve meditative stability, avoid the control of the lord of death, and successfully transcend the cycle of birth and death.

**The Guiding Role of the Buddha and the Dharma**  
The practitioner follows the teachings and doctrines proclaimed by the Buddha. These teachings provide the essential methods, principles, and wisdom for practice. The Dharma is to be personally realized through practice, not merely heard, and it guides the practitioner toward liberation.

**Cultivating Wisdom and Insight**  
The practitioner develops wisdom through meditation and mental liberation. This wisdom enables a direct insight into the impermanent, suffering, and selfless nature of all conditioned phenomena, particularly the five aggregates. Such insight leads to non-attachment and freedom from suffering.

**The State of the Accomplished Sage**  
A practitioner who successfully completes the path becomes a sage or an enlightened being. This state represents the highest spiritual achievement, characterized by the complete destruction of mental afflictions, freedom from all fears, and a mind that no longer clings to any attainment, including supreme spiritual fruits.
