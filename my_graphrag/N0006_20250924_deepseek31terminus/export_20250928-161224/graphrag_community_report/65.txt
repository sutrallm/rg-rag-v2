**Report on Buddhist Practice Regarding the Senses and Liberation**

**Summary**
This report outlines the core principles of a specific Buddhist practice. The path involves cultivating non-attachment to the six senses and their corresponding external objects. This disciplined approach directly results in the cessation of suffering, which is synonymous with achieving a state of pure liberation.

**The Relationship Between Senses and Their Objects**
The six senses—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind—are directly linked to six specific types of external objects: form, sound, odor, taste, touch, and mental phenomena. Each sense perceives its designated object.

**The Core Disciplines for Practitioners**
A practitioner must refrain from generating greed or craving for the six senses themselves. Simultaneously, a practitioner must avoid any attachment or grasping towards the six sense objects, ensuring the mind remains unstained by them.

**The Consequence of Non-Attachment**
The deliberate practice of not generating greed for the senses and their objects causes suffering to cease naturally. This cessation is not a separate event but the direct outcome of this specific discipline.

**The Ultimate Objective**
The final goal of this practice is a state described as pure liberation, where the mind is free from defilement. This state of being unbound is achieved through the cessation of suffering, which is itself achieved through non-attachment to the senses and their objects.
