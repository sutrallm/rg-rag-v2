**Report on the Metaphorical Relationship Between a Water Droplet and a Lotus Leaf**

**Summary**
The text establishes a consistent metaphorical framework where a water droplet's inability to adhere to a lotus leaf symbolizes a state of non-attachment. This non-attachment is presented as a desirable mental quality, achieved by letting go of craving, which results in an unstained and pure mind.

**The Symbolism of Non-Adherence**
The water droplet's physical property of not sticking to the lotus leaf is the central metaphor. This characteristic directly represents the philosophical concept of non-attachment, where one interacts with the world without becoming bound or stained by it.

**The Lotus Leaf as the World**
The lotus leaf is explicitly defined as a metaphor for the world. The relationship between the droplet and the leaf illustrates the ideal way to exist within the world: present but not clinging, interacting but remaining separate.

**The Resulting State of Mind**
Letting go of craving is identified as the method to achieve the state symbolized by the water droplet. This state is described as having a pure mind, which remains unstained by its interactions with the world, just as the water droplet remains separate from the lotus leaf.

**The Equivalence of Terms**
The concepts are expressed using both English and Chinese terms, which are used interchangeably. "Water droplet" is equivalent to "水珠", "lotus leaf" to "荷葉", "non-attachment" to the state achieved by letting go of "贪爱", and the resulting "pure mind" to "清净心".
