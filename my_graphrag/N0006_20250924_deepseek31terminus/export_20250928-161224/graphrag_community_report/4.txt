**Report on the Practice and Outcomes of 禅修 (Meditative Practice)**

**Summary**

The text defines 禅修 as a core meditative practice aimed at achieving mental liberation. This practice, which involves concentration and mindful awareness, enables a practitioner to overcome fundamental attachments, observe the true nature of existence, and ultimately attain a state of freedom from the cycle of birth and death. The primary goal for a practitioner is 心解脱 (mental liberation), which is reached by cultivating wisdom and overcoming craving through sustained practice.

**Key Findings**

**The Purpose and Method of 禅修**
禅修 is a disciplined method of mental and spiritual cultivation. Its purpose is to stabilize the mind, allowing for clear observation of phenomena and leading to liberation from disturbances and desires. The practice is founded on two key components: focused concentration and mindful awareness. It is the designated path for training and focusing the 心念 (mind-consciousness) and is closely associated with achieving a state of 禅定 (meditative absorption).

**The Practitioner's Objective: Liberation from Cyclic Existence**
The central objective for a practitioner engaging in 禅修 is to achieve 解脱 (liberation), specifically 心解脱 (mental liberation). This state is characterized by freedom from 生死束缚, the bondage to the cycle of birth and death (samsara). The practice enables the practitioner to transcend danger, fear, and attachment to life and death, thereby breaking this cycle.

**The Role of Wisdom in Achieving Liberation**
The attainment of liberating wisdom is a direct outcome of 禅修. This wisdom is not merely intellectual but is cultivated through the practice itself. It allows the practitioner to perceive the true, impermanent, and ultimately illusory nature of all phenomena. This profound understanding is essential for achieving liberation.

**Overcoming Attachment through Observation**
A key function of 禅修 is to provide the means to overcome 执著 (attachment/clinging), which originates from 贪念 (craving/desire). The practice allows a practitioner to clearly observe the arising and passing away of all phenomena. By observing this impermanence directly, the practitioner can cease to generate craving for transient states, leading to a liberation where one no longer clings to any experience or condition.
