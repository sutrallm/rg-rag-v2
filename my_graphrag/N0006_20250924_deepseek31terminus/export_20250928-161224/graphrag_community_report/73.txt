**Report on the Path to Nirvana and the State of the Liberated Being**

**Summary**
This report outlines the fundamental principles and practices leading to the ultimate goal of Nirvana, a state of final liberation characterized by perfect peace and the cessation of suffering. The path involves the diligent eradication of afflictions such as craving, attachment, and conceit through specific practices including mindfulness, wisdom, and meditation. The report details the nature of Nirvana, the qualities of a practitioner who has attained it, such as an Arhat or Sage, and the necessary conditions for its achievement.

**The Nature of Nirvana**
Nirvana represents the ultimate goal of Buddhist practice, signifying final liberation, perfect peace, and the complete cessation of suffering. It is an unconditioned state beyond all dualities and conceptual thinking, described as unshakable, cool, tranquil, secure, and stainless. This state is liberation from the cycle of birth and death (samsara), where an enlightened being no longer creates new karma and is no longer subject to rebirth. It is the ultimate bliss, the final, stable abode of peace, attained by severing all craving and attachment.

**Prerequisites for Attaining Nirvana**
Achieving Nirvana requires the extinguishing of fundamental afflictions. The primary obstacles are greed, hatred, and delusion, often summarized as the three root poisons. A specific and powerful form of desire, craving, is identified as a root cause of suffering and must be eradicated. The conceit or arrogance related to the self must also be removed. The complete cessation of these defilements is a necessary condition for entering Nirvana.

**The Path of Practice Leading to Liberation**
The practitioner follows a defined path to achieve liberation. This path involves diligent effort and includes specific practices. Mindfulness is a core practice that constitutes the path. Meditation is a method that leads to the attainment of Nirvana. Diligence, including a form known as non-negligence, is crucial. The cultivation of transcendental wisdom is the key to liberation, illuminating ignorance and providing insight into the true nature of phenomena. This wisdom allows one to see the five aggregates as illusory and understand dependent origination and emptiness.

**Characteristics of the Liberated Being**
An individual who has attained Nirvana, such as an Arhat or a Sage, embodies specific qualities. Such a being has completely severed all afflictions and is free from craving and attachment. They have purified the mind, reached the "other shore" of liberation, and abide in a state of non-clinging towards any object or state. They are unstained by worldly disturbances, like a lotus flower unsullied by the mud from which it grows. They have attained the state of non-birth and dwell securely in Nirvana, having exhausted all defilements.

**The Relationship Between Key States and Nirvana**
Several states are integral to the process of achieving the final goal. The state of desirelessness, meaning freedom from greed and attachment, is both a practice and a stage that leads to the tranquility of Nirvana. Mental liberation is an important state or achievement on the path, which ultimately culminates in the more complete state of Nirvana. Liberation itself is synonymous with or leads directly to Nirvana, which is its ultimate expression and final achievement.
