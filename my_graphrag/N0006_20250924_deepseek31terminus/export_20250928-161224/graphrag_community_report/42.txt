**Report on the Path to Mental Liberation through Impermanence and Non-Attachment**

**Summary**

This report details the interrelationship between mental liberation, the principle of impermanence, and the state of non-attachment. The path involves a practitioner using meditative practice to gain wisdom. This wisdom enables a clear and thorough insight into the impermanent nature of all phenomena. Directly perceiving this truth results in non-attachment, where one no longer clings to any state, achievement, or sensory pleasure. This non-attachment is the defining characteristic of mental liberation, a final state of freedom from fear, desire, and all bondage.

**The Centrality of Impermanence as an Observable Truth**

The principle of "世间无常" (shì jiān wú cháng) is a fundamental truth stating that all worldly phenomena are subject to change, decay, and are without a permanent essence. This characteristic of constant arising and passing away is the true nature of reality. This principle is not merely a concept but an observable fact that can be clearly seen and directly perceived through developed wisdom.

**Meditative Practice as the Pathway to Liberation**

The primary method for achieving mental liberation is meditative practice, specifically "禅修" (chán xiū) or "禅定" (chán dìng). A practitioner engages in this discipline to subdue the mind and cultivate the necessary conditions for insight. It is the established pathway through which one progresses toward the goal of a liberated mind.

**Wisdom as the Mechanism for Insight and Detachment**

Wisdom is the crucial faculty developed through practice. Its primary function is the contemplation and understanding of impermanence. This wisdom allows a practitioner to perceive the illusory nature of phenomena and to observe the mind itself. It is the direct cause for abandoning attachment and achieving detachment.

**Non-Attachment as the Defining Quality of Liberation**

Non-attachment is the state of not clinging to any phenomenon, state, or attainment. It is a manifestation of wisdom and the direct outcome of insight into impermanence. This includes non-attachment to any "境界" (jìng jiè), meaning any spiritual state or level of achievement reached through practice, as well as to sensory pleasures and future rewards.

**The Liberated State of a Practitioner**

A practitioner who attains mental liberation through this process is characterized by a mind free from all bonds. Such an individual has a deep, personal realization of impermanence and possesses non-attached wisdom. They do not grasp at any internal state or external condition, resulting in a state of fearlessness, purity, and ultimate freedom.
