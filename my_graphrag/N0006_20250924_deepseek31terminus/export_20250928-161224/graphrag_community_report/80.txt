**Report on Spiritual Cultivation: Mind, Afflictions, and Meditation**

**Summary**

This report details the core principles of spiritual practice derived from the provided text. The central theme is the relationship between the mind (心念), its negative states (afflictions, negligence, distraction), and the practices (meditation, mindfulness, diligence) used to overcome them. The ultimate goal is achieving liberation through mental stability, tranquility, and the eradication of afflictions. Key entities include the practitioner (修行者), the sage (圣者), and the specific practices and states they engage in and attain.

**The Centrality of Mind Regulation**

The mind is the primary object of spiritual cultivation. It is subtle, rapidly changing, and prone to distraction by desires and external conditions. An uncontrolled mind, likened to a flickering lamp or a wild horse, leads to afflictions, suffering, and bondage within the cycle of birth and death. Conversely, a mind that is diligently focused, guarded, and subdued through practices like mindfulness and correct awareness achieves stability, clarity, and peace. This controlled state is the foundation for meditative concentration, spiritual progress, and ultimate liberation.

**Afflictions as the Root of Suffering**

Afflictions are negative mental states—such as greed, hatred, delusion, and distraction—that cause internal turmoil, suffering, and bondage. They arise from a scattered and uncontrolled mind. The fundamental cause of being bound to the cycle of suffering is these afflictions. The practitioner's objective is to overcome and sever them through moral discipline, meditation, and wisdom. A sage is defined as one who has completely eradicated all afflictions.

**Negligence as a Foundational Obstacle**

Negligence is a state of spiritual carelessness,懈怠, and lack of vigilance. It is the opposite of diligence and mindfulness. This state is identified as the root cause for the arising of afflictions and is a primary obstacle to meditation. Negligence leads to danger, control by negative forces, and perpetuates the cycle of suffering. Overcoming negligence is essential, achieved through the diligent practice of meditation and maintaining mindfulness.

**Meditation as the Path to Liberation**

Meditation is the core method for transforming the mind and achieving liberation. Its practice requires focusing the mind, maintaining correct mindfulness, and diligence. Meditation directly counteracts distraction, negligence, and afflictions, leading to inner stability, peace, and tranquility. It is the pathway to attaining states of inner stillness, liberating wisdom, and ultimately, Nirvana. Meditation is the second component of the threefold training of moral discipline, meditation, and wisdom, which together aim to sever the roots of afflictions.

**The Interdependence of Mindfulness, Diligence, and Wisdom**

Mindfulness is the essential method for practicing meditation and guarding the mind against distraction and affliction. Diligence, characterized by unwavering effort and the absence of negligence, is a prerequisite for entering, abiding in, and mastering meditative states. Wisdom, which arises from a stabilized mind through meditation, has the power to cut off afflictions by illuminating the true nature of reality, such as impermanence. These three elements—mindfulness, diligence, and wisdom—are mutually supportive and crucial for progress.

**The Sage as the Achieved State**

A sage represents the successful outcome of spiritual practice. Sages diligently cultivate their minds, abide in a state of tranquility, and have completely severed all afflictions. They possess liberating wisdom, are free from attachment, and have transcended the cycle of birth and death. Their state is characterized by enduring peace and stability, the ultimate goal of the path.
