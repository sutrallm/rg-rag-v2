**Report on the Characteristics and Practices of the Wise Person (Zhi Zhe)**

**Summary**

This report details the defining attributes, practices, and outcomes associated with a wise person. The analysis reveals that such an individual is characterized by profound wisdom, unwavering mindfulness, and disciplined self-cultivation. Key practices include diligent guarding and taming of the mind, the consistent application of mindfulness, and the cultivation of meditative calm and insight. The ultimate objectives of these endeavors are liberation from all fetters, the attainment of Nirvana, and the establishment of a pure life in accordance with the Dharma.

**Defining Attributes of a Wise Person**

A wise person possesses deep understanding and is a paragon for practitioners. Their mind is firm, pure, and unshakable by external praise or blame, as internal greed and hatred have been eradicated. They are not overcome by afflictions, are free from attachment to the five aggregates, and uphold precepts purely. Their speech is careful, their demeanor serene, and they are widely respected.

**The Centrality of Mind Guarding and Taming**

A primary responsibility is the diligent guarding and skillful taming of mental inclinations. This involves constant vigilance to prevent the mind from falling into the net of craving, similar to a dike holding back floodwaters or a herdsman watching cattle. Through this unrelenting alertness, they avoid spiritual dangers and all faults.

**The Instrumental Role of Mindfulness**

Mindfulness is a treasured tool. It is guarded like a treasure and used to protect awareness, prevent negligence, and sever afflictions. Maintaining mindfulness allows for secure abiding in the Dharma and is a means to achieve liberation from suffering.

**The Practice of Calm and Insight Meditation**

The wise diligently cultivate meditative practices aimed at fostering calm and insight. This practice stabilizes the mind, dispels external obstacles, and leads to a state of conflict-free absorption where the senses are stilled. It is a primary method for achieving liberation.

**Pursuit of Liberation and Nirvana**

The ultimate aim of these practices is to transcend all suffering, sever sorrow, and immediately enter Nirvana, thereby ending the cycle of birth and death. This is achieved by understanding the empty nature of all phenomena and following the path taught by the Buddha.

**Contrast with the Foolish**

The characteristics and outcomes of a wise person are often presented in direct contrast to those of a foolish person, highlighting the fundamental differences in their approach to life and spiritual goals.

**Role as an Exemplar**

A wise person serves as a spiritual friend and guide, an object worthy of association and emulation. Lay disciples are encouraged to become wise persons through proper practice.
