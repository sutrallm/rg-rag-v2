**Report on the Distinction Between the Wise and the Foolish in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**
This report outlines the fundamental principles of Buddhist practice as derived from the provided text. It establishes that adherence to the Buddha's teachings, comprising the threefold training of morality, concentration, and wisdom, leads to safety and fearlessness. The text emphasizes the critical importance of associating with wise, virtuous friends for guidance, while explicitly warning against the detrimental influence of foolish companions, who are characterized by doubt and harmful behavior.

**The Path to Safety and Fearlessness**
The ultimate objective of following the Buddha's teachings is to achieve a state of safety, security, and fearlessness. This state is described as the final result of dedicated practice. A practitioner reaches this goal through persistent and diligent effort in adhering to the teachings.

**The Core Framework: The Threefold Training**
The Buddha's teachings are encapsulated in the threefold training of morality (戒律), concentration (禅定), and wisdom (智慧). This framework provides a practical path for spiritual practice, emphasizing ethical conduct, mental discipline, and profound understanding. These three components are identified as the central and most important elements of the teachings.

**The Essential Role of the Virtuous Friend**
A wise person, termed a virtuous friend, possesses a stable mind and deep wisdom. This individual is the ideal teacher and companion for a practitioner. The text explicitly instructs practitioners to associate with, learn from, and follow such individuals, as they provide essential guidance for learning and practicing the true teachings.

**The Detrimental Influence of the Foolish Companion**
A foolish person is characterized by stubborn adherence to wrong views, an inability to comprehend the true meaning of the teachings, and behavior that is harmful to themselves and leads to negative consequences. Such a person is considered a negative companion. The text explicitly states that foolish companions should be avoided, as association with them brings about worry and distress.

**A Clear Dichotomy of Guidance**
The text presents a direct contrast between two types of individuals: the wise, virtuous friend and the foolish, negative companion. This contrast serves to clarify the fundamental difference between them in terms of their characteristics, behavior, and the outcomes of associating with them. The guidance is unequivocal: one should be sought for closeness and the other for avoidance.

**The Practitioner's Responsibility of Diligence**
A practitioner is defined as one who follows the Buddha's teachings. Success in this endeavor requires continuous, unremitting, and diligent practice. The practitioner's primary responsibility is to persevere diligently along the path outlined by the teachings.

**The Foolish Rejection of Core Teachings**
A key characteristic of a foolish person is their specific doubt and distrust toward the threefold training of morality, concentration, and wisdom. This skepticism toward the core of the teachings prevents them from gaining any benefit and solidifies their status as a negative influence to be avoided.
