**Report on the Path to Mental Liberation through Meditative Practice**

**Summary**

This report details the process and outcomes of achieving mental liberation (心解脱) through dedicated meditative practice (禅修). The core finding is that mental liberation is a state of profound freedom from attachment (执著) and mental defilements, characterized by the wisdom to perceive the impermanent and illusory nature of all phenomena. This state is attained through the practice of 禅修, which stabilizes the mind and cultivates the insight necessary for liberation. The ultimate goal of this path is Nirvana, with mental liberation representing a key milestone.

**The Nature of Mental Liberation**

Mental liberation is a state of profound freedom and release. It signifies the mind's liberation from afflictions, attachments, and bondage. A practitioner who achieves this state is no longer bound by any phenomena and experiences complete mental autonomy. A key characteristic is the ability to observe the world without being stained or attached, akin to a lotus leaf repelling water.

**The Central Role of Meditative Practice**

The primary method for achieving mental liberation is the consistent practice of 禅修. This practice involves focused concentration and mindful awareness to calm the mind, leading to clarity and stability. Through 禅修, practitioners train their minds to observe the arising and passing of phenomena clearly, which is foundational for developing liberating insight.

**Wisdom as the Fruit of Liberation**

Mental liberation is intrinsically linked to the attainment of wisdom. This wisdom is a direct, penetrating insight into the true nature of existence, specifically the impermanence and illusory quality of all things. It is through this wisdom that attachments are abandoned. This wisdom is not an abstract concept but a functional quality that enables non-attachment to any state, including the fruits of spiritual practice itself.

**The Abandonment of Attachment**

A defining feature of a liberated mind is the complete absence of attachment (执著). This attachment, rooted in craving, is identified as the fundamental cause of suffering and bondage. The liberated practitioner does not cling to any experience, object, state, or achievement. This non-clinging extends to all phenomena and is essential for ultimate freedom.

**The Object of Liberated Observation**

Practitioners who have attained mental liberation possess the ability to perceive reality as it is. They can observe the fundamental characteristic of the world: the constant arising and passing away of all conditioned things. They clearly see the truth of impermanence in all phenomena and deeply comprehend the illusory nature of worldly appearances.

**The Goal Beyond Liberation**

Mental liberation is a significant achievement on the spiritual path but is not the final endpoint. It is a precursor state that leads toward the ultimate goal of Nirvana. The practitioner, having achieved mental liberation, continues to progress toward this ultimate fulfillment of liberation.
