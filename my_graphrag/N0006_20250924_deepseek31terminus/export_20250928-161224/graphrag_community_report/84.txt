**Report on the State of Mental Liberation and its Prerequisites**

**Summary**

This report details the state of mental liberation (心解脱), a condition of profound freedom and release achieved through meditative practice (禅修). An individual who attains this state gains the wisdom to perceive the impermanent and illusory nature of all phenomena (世间无常). This insight results in a complete absence of attachment (执著) to any state, experience, or achievement, including the fruits of spiritual practice itself. The path to this liberation is defined by the cultivation of wisdom, primarily through the method of meditative concentration, which enables the practitioner to transcend the cycle of birth and death.

**Key Findings**

**The Nature of Mental Liberation**
Mental liberation is a state of complete mental freedom and emancipation, characterized by the release from afflictions, attachments, and bondage. An individual in this condition possesses a mind that is unshakable and autonomous, no longer bound by any phenomenon. The state is likened to a lotus leaf, which remains unstained by water, symbolizing a mind that does not cling to or is tainted by any experience.

**The Central Role of Impermanence**
A fundamental truth observed by the liberated mind is that all worldly phenomena are impermanent (世间无常). Everything is subject to constant change, arising, and passing away; nothing possesses a permanent, unchanging essence. The direct and thorough insight into this principle is a defining characteristic of one who has achieved mental liberation.

**The Abandonment of Attachment**
The state of mental liberation is defined by the absence of attachment (执著). This refers to the cessation of clinging to experiences, states, objects, or conceptual boundaries. The liberated practitioner does not generate such clinging towards any condition, including spiritual attainments or realizations. This non-attachment is a manifestation of wisdom.

**The Path of Meditative Practice**
Mental liberation is attained through the dedicated practice of meditation (禅修). This practice involves focused concentration and mindful awareness, stabilizing the mind to allow for clear observation of the true nature of phenomena. It is the primary method for developing the wisdom that leads to liberation and for transcending the bondage of the cycle of birth and death.

**The Resulting Wisdom**
The achievement of mental liberation is accompanied by the arising of a specific wisdom. This wisdom involves a deep insight into the impermanent and illusory nature of all things. It enables the practitioner to perceive the world as it truly is, without distortion, and is intrinsically non-attaching. This wisdom is both the means to achieve liberation and its resultant quality.

**The Relationship Between Non-Attachment and Liberation**
Non-attachment (离执) is the direct outcome of the wisdom gained through mental liberation. By clearly seeing the impermanent nature of phenomena, the basis for attachment is removed. The liberated mind naturally does not cling to any state or achievement. This freedom from attachment is synonymous with the liberation itself, leading to a purified mind and ultimately指向 the attainment of Nirvana.
