**Report on the Characteristics and Practices of the Wise Person (智者)**

**Summary**

This report details the defining attributes, practices, and outcomes associated with a Wise Person (智者). The individual attains wisdom through diligent spiritual practice, characterized by vigilant guarding and mastery of the mind. Key practices include maintaining mindfulness, observing phenomena correctly, and diligently cultivating meditative calm and insight (止觀). The ultimate goals of this path are liberation from all fetters, the attainment of Nirvana, and a state of stable peace free from suffering, greed, and fear. The Wise Person serves as a model practitioner, embodying purity, discipline, and profound understanding of the Dharma.

**Characteristics and Practices of the Wise Person**

**The Defining Quality of Wisdom**
A Wise Person possesses and embodies wisdom, understanding the principles of practice. Their speech, actions, and insights are manifestations of this wisdom, which they use to subdue restless thoughts and achieve mental stability and liberation.

**Vigilant Guarding and Mastery of the Mind**
A primary duty is the diligent guarding and skillful training of the mind. They protect their mental state to prevent it from falling into negligence and to guide it toward the correct path. This involves constant vigilance to avoid the dangers of heedlessness and the net of craving, akin to an artisan removing flaws or a herdsman guarding cattle.

**The Central Role of Mindfulness**
Mindfulness is a treasured tool for the Wise Person. They guard it like a precious jewel, using it to maintain awareness of mental changes, prevent negligence, and sever afflictions. A firm mindfulness allows them to abide securely in the Dharma.

**Cultivation of Meditative Calm and Insight (止觀)**
The Wise Person diligently practices 止觀, a meditative discipline aimed at cultivating tranquility and insight to stabilize the mind. This practice, pursued with vigor, pacifies mental chatter and leads to a conflict-free state of absorption, ultimately resulting in liberation from all fetters.

**Liberation from Greed and Fear**
Through wise observation of phenomena and feelings, the mind is liberated from greed and fear. This liberation leads directly to a state of suffering-free peace and stability. The Wise Person, having abandoned craving and attachment, is not overcome by afflictions and remains unattached to the five aggregates.

**Adherence to Purity and Discipline**
The Wise Person upholds pure precepts, speaks carefully, and maintains serene conduct. Their pure ethical discipline attracts respect. They follow the wholesome path as taught by the Buddha, establishing a pure life in accordance with the Dharma, and avoid all evil or unwholesome practices.

**The Ultimate Goal: Attainment of Nirvana**
The purpose of practice is to attain Nirvana, thereby ending the cycle of birth and death. Through their diligent efforts, the Wise Person can sever afflictions in the present moment and enter Nirvana, reaching this ultimate, secure destination.

**Contrast with the Foolish Person**
The characteristics and behaviors of the Wise Person are often presented in direct contrast to those of the foolish person, highlighting the fundamental differences in their approach to life and spiritual goals.

**The Wise Person as an Exemplar**
The Wise Person, also referred to as a sage or true Brahmin, represents a practitioner who has reached a mature state. They serve as a spiritual friend and exemplar, an object worthy of reverence and a guide for lay disciples to emulate through proper practice.
