**Report on the Path to Nirvana in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**
This report outlines the essential components for attaining Nirvana, the ultimate goal of Buddhist practice characterized by liberation from suffering and the cycle of birth and death. The path requires the eradication of fundamental afflictions—craving, conceit, and attachment—through specific practices including wisdom, mindfulness, diligence, and meditation. The culmination is a state of perfect peace, achieved by those who have severed all defilements.

**The Nature of Nirvana**
Nirvana represents final liberation, perfect peace, and the complete cessation of suffering. It is an unconditioned state beyond dualities, described as unshakable, cool, tranquil, secure, and stainless. This state is liberation from the cycle of birth and death, where an enlightened being no longer creates new karma and is free from rebirth. It is the ultimate bliss and the final, stable abode of peace.

**Prerequisites for Attainment**
Nirvana is attained by extinguishing the flames of affliction, specifically greed, hatred, and delusion. A necessary condition is the eradication of craving, conceit, and attachment. The roots of defilement must be severed, and all fetters and delusive thoughts must be eliminated.

**Essential Practices**
Diligent practice is crucial, involving mindfulness, clear comprehension, and a thorough understanding of the emptiness of the five aggregates and dependent origination. Key methods include the cultivation of Prajna wisdom to illuminate ignorance, meditation to achieve tranquility, and the practice of non-attachment. Guarding the mind, moral discipline, and sustained effort are fundamental to this path.

**The Role of Wisdom**
Prajna wisdom is the supreme wisdom that provides profound insight into the true nature of phenomena. It is the key to liberation, enabling practitioners to see the five aggregates as illusory, naturally let go of craving and attachment, and ultimately certify Nirvana.
