**Report on the Nature and Overcoming of Afflictions (Kleshas)**

**Summary**

This report details the fundamental role of afflictions (Kleshas) as the primary cause of suffering and cyclic existence (Samsara). It establishes that these mental defilements, such as greed, hatred, and delusion, originate in the mind and can be overcome through specific practices. The definitive method for their elimination involves the cultivation of wisdom and meditative concentration (Dhyana), leading to a state of liberation. The ultimate proof of complete enlightenment is the severance of all afflictions, a state exemplified by an Arhat who has attained Nirvana.

**Key Findings**

**Afflictions as the Root of Suffering**
Afflictions are identified as the factors leading to internal suffering, bondage, and psychological distress. They are the root cause of pain and what binds sentient beings to the cycle of rebirth. These mental impurities are considered adversaries that must be confronted and overcome.

**The Origin and Nature of Afflictions**
These afflictions arise from the mind itself. They are characterized as defilements or impurities that need to be eliminated. Negligence is cited as a key condition that allows afflictions to emerge and proliferate, whereas abandoning negligence leads to their destruction.

**The Path to Overcoming Afflictions**
The primary means to pacify and eliminate afflictions is through the application of wisdom, which severs their roots. A specific method highlighted is meditative concentration (Dhyana), a practice through which afflictions are calmed and ultimately extinguished. The goal for a practitioner is to free themselves from the disturbance and bondage of these afflictions.

**The Result of Eliminating Afflictions**
The complete eradication of all afflictions results in full enlightenment and liberation. This state is marked by the severance of all bonds created by afflictions. In Nirvana, every affliction is utterly burned away. An Arhat is described as a perfected being who has achieved this birthless state of Nirvana by severing all afflictions and abandoning all clinging, thereby becoming fearless and free from defilements. A sage or noble one is defined by this total eradication of afflictions and their roots.
