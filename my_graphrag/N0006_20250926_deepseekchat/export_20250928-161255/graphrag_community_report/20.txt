**Title: The Path to Liberation Through Zen Practice**

**Summary:** This report explores the principles of Zen practice and their role in achieving mental liberation and spiritual fruition. It highlights the significance of non-attachment (*无执*), the understanding of impermanence (*无常*), and the ultimate state of liberation (*涅槃*) in Zen Buddhism. The practice of meditation (*禅定*) is emphasized as a crucial method for overcoming suffering and attaining enlightenment.

---

**Non-Attachment as Wisdom**  
*无执* represents a state of non-attachment, where individuals are free from clinging to conditions or outcomes. This wisdom, cultivated through Zen practice, enables mental liberation and the ability to observe the rise and fall of phenomena without attachment. It allows practitioners to achieve spiritual fruition without generating desire or grasping.

---

**Impermanence as a Path to Non-Attachment**  
Understanding impermanence (*无常*) is essential for achieving *无执*. Recognizing the transient nature of all conditions helps practitioners release attachments, fostering a deeper sense of freedom and clarity in their spiritual journey.

---

**Meditation as a Tool for Liberation**  
Meditation (*禅定*) is a fundamental practice for those seeking ultimate liberation (*究竟解脱者*). It serves as a means to eradicate afflictions and transcend the cycle of suffering, leading practitioners toward the ultimate state of enlightenment.

---

**Nirvana: The Ultimate Liberation**  
*涅槃* represents the ultimate state attained by those who achieve complete liberation. It is the culmination of Zen practice, where all afflictions are severed, and the cycle of suffering is permanently transcended.

---

**Liberation Through Non-Attachment**  
Ultimate liberators (*究竟解脱者*) embody the wisdom of *无执*, completely eradicating the roots of afflictions. Their mastery of non-attachment is a testament to the profound transformation achievable through dedicated Zen practice.
