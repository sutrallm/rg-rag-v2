**The Four Foundations and Mindful Awareness as Pathways to Liberation**

**Summary**  
Sustained observation of the body, feelings, mind, and mental objects uncovers craving and attachment as the source of suffering. This observation, practiced continuously during meditation and daily activities as mindful awareness, systematically develops chanding—stable, non-reactive attention that leads to liberation. Chanding is not a separate practice but the cultivated outcome of applying mindful awareness to the four foundations.

**Mindful Awareness Is the Consistent Method for Observing the Four Foundations**  
Continuous attention to bodily movements, breath, and mental states in all activities is the specific technique used to engage with the four foundations. This practice is not limited to formal meditation but extends to daily life, ensuring that observation remains unbroken and effective in counteracting dullness and distraction.

**Chanding Emerges Through Systematic Practice of the Four Foundations**  
Chanding is developed not by isolated techniques but through the repeated, disciplined application of mindful awareness to the body, feelings, mind, and mental objects. It is the natural result of this structured observation, manifesting as sustained focus and mental balance.

**Craving and Attachment Are Revealed Through Direct Observation of Sensations and Mental Phenomena**  
By observing the four foundations with non-reactive attention, the patterns of attachment to physical sensations and mental contents become visible. This exposure identifies craving as the central mechanism that sustains suffering, making liberation possible through clear perception rather than forceful suppression.
