**Title: WuZhui and Jingji: The Path of Non-Attachment to True Stillness**

**Summary**  
True stillness arises not through external conditions or spiritual attainment, but through the complete cessation of clinging. This is achieved through sustained mindfulness that reveals the impermanent, selfless nature of all experience, leading to non-attachment—even to meditation, virtue, and liberation itself. The structured practice of observing breath, sensation, feeling, and mental phenomena progressively dismantles attachment, culminating in inner peace that is unaffected by external chaos. Enlightenment is marked not by extraordinary states, but by the absence of grasping and the presence of equanimity in every moment.

---

**Non-Attachment is the Core of Liberation**  
The cessation of clinging—whether to pleasure, pain, meditative states, spiritual accomplishments, or even the idea of liberation—is the defining feature of awakening. When the mind no longer grasps or rejects experience, it naturally settles into stillness. This is not passive indifference, but active freedom from the compulsion to control or possess any phenomenon, including enlightenment itself.

**True Stillness Is Inner, Not External**  
Stillness is not found in silence, isolation, or sacred places. It is the natural condition of the mind when freed from greed, hatred, and delusion. Regardless of noise, circumstance, or social approval, inner tranquility emerges when the mind no longer reacts to or identifies with what arises. This state is accessible in the midst of chaos, not by escaping it.

**Mindfulness Is the Direct Path to Non-Attachment**  
Observing the breath, bodily sensations, feelings, and mental phenomena without judgment progressively reveals their transient, insubstantial nature. This awareness dissolves the illusion of permanence and selfhood, making clinging unsustainable. Each stage of observation dismantles attachment by showing that nothing endured long enough to be held onto.

**Liberation Is Not Attained—It Is Revealed**  
Nirvana is not a destination reached through striving, rituals, or divine intervention. It is the natural outcome of letting go. When all grasping ends—including the desire to be liberated—the mind’s inherent peace is no longer obscured. Liberation is recognized, not achieved, because it was always present beneath the layers of attachment.

**Non-Attachment Extends Even to Virtue and Enlightenment**  
The deepest letting-go includes releasing attachment to virtuous states, spiritual progress, and the concept of nirvana itself. Clinging to “being enlightened” or “being good” is still clinging. True freedom is complete—unconditional and unmoved even by the most noble spiritual achievements.

**Non-Self Realization Removes the Basis for Clinging**  
When observation reveals that no fixed, independent self exists behind experience, the foundation for ownership vanishes. Without the sense of “mine” or “me,” there is no one to grasp at pleasure or resist pain. Equanimity arises not by effort, but naturally when the illusion of self dissolves.

**Presence Is the Sole Arena of Liberation**  
Freedom is found exclusively in the immediate moment—breath, sensation, thought—as they arise, without delay, resistance, or fabrication. To seek liberation in the future, in another place, or through another method is to misunderstand its nature. It is only in full, non-reactive awareness of the now that stillness reveals itself.

**External Rituals and Signifiers Are Irrelevant**  
Sacred locations, ceremonies, deities, or approval from others hold no power to bring liberation. True freedom depends solely on internal awareness and equanimity. Whether in a monastery or a marketplace, peace arises only when the mind is unburdened by dependence on outward forms.

**Gradual Practice Leads to Perfect Letting-Go**  
The structured progression—from breath to mental phenomena—is not merely a technique, but a methodical unmasking of the impermanent and selfless nature of all experience. Each step weakens the habit of clinging until, at the highest stage, even the desire for liberation is released, completing the process of total non-attachment.

**Non-Attachment Is the Mark of the Awakened**  
One becomes a holy being not through supernatural ability or moral perfection, but through the absence of clinging. The hallmark is not visible achievements or profound experiences, but the quiet, unshakable disengagement from all conditions and concepts, even the most sublime.
