Title: Shenghe and Qingjing: The Nature of the Awakened Being

Summary:  
The awakened being, known as Shenghe, is defined by complete moral purification and direct understanding of reality, resulting in the absence of greed, hatred, and delusion. This state is marked by Qingjing—a mind of unshakable stillness, free from defilement and agitation, akin to empty space or an extinguished lamp. Qingjing is not an isolated condition but the direct outcome of sustained ethical practice and mindful effort, and it serves as the essential, defining characteristic of Shenghe.

—

Shenghe Is Achieved Through Sustained Moral and Mental Discipline  
Achieving the state of Shenghe requires constant, mindful effort over time. It is not attained through fleeting actions or temporary states, but through the unwavering cultivation of ethical conduct and meditation. Only through this sustained process does the individual fully extinguish the roots of suffering and become an awakened being.

Qingjing Defines the Inner State of the Awakened Being  
The mind of Shenghe is characterized by Qingjing—complete freedom from defilement and agitation. This is not a passive condition but an active manifestation of inner purity. The analogies of empty space and an extinguished lamp emphasize the absence of disturbance, attachment, and residual impulse, revealing a mind that is fundamentally unshakable and unattached.

Qingjing Is the Natural Expression of Shenghe, Not a Separate Goal  
Qingjing is not merely a tool or a step toward awakening, but the inherent quality of Shenghe. It is the visible manifestation of moral purification and direct insight. The likeness to empty space and an extinguished lamp underscores that Qingjing is the final, stable condition of the awakened state—nothing remains to disturb it, and nothing is added to it.
