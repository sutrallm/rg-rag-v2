**Title: Wuzhizhe, Shengin, and Cibei: The Architecture of Non-Attachment and Compassion in Spiritual Perfection**

**Summary**  
The framework centers on the interdependent transformation from delusion to liberation, where the eradication of false notions of self and permanence gives rise to a wisdom that dissolves attachment. This wisdom, known as Wuzhizhe, is the foundation for Shengin — the saintly being who embodies pure moral conduct and freedom from afflictions. From this state, Cibei emerges not as emotional reaction but as effortless, non-clinging compassion, inseparable from wisdom. The perfected being, Shengre, manifests this unity fully, exhibiting boundless care without ego, transaction, or disturbance. All spiritual progression hinges on Chanding and Chashi — mental clarity and cognitive discernment — which support the dissolution of Huai, the root of suffering.

---

**The Eradication of Delusion Enables Liberation**  
Huai — the fundamental misunderstanding of reality as permanent, self-existent, and independent — is the origin of all afflictions, including craving and aversion. Its removal is not a mere suppression but a structural unraveling of false views about self and permanence. Only when this root is fully uprooted can one attain Jiede, the state of complete liberation, exemplified by the Shengin. This individual does not merely avoid suffering but transcends its conditions entirely, no longer bound by the illusion of ownership or control.

**Wisdom Without Attachment Defines the Enlightened State**  
Wuzhizhe — the wisdom that sees through all clinging — is not abstract knowledge but a direct, experiential recognition of impermanence and selflessness. It dismantles attachment to meditative bliss, supernatural powers, and even spiritual accomplishments. This wisdom is the defining trait of the Shengzhe, who no longer identifies with any attainment or sense of self. Without Wuzhizhe, even advanced spiritual practices remain entangled in ego, rendering them ineffective as paths beyond cyclic existence.

**Compassion Arises Only From Non-Attachment**  
Cibei is not emotional sympathy or moral duty but the natural, cool, and clear expression of care that flows from Wuzhizhe. It requires the complete absence of “giver,” “receiver,” and “act of giving.” True compassion is non-reactive, uninvolved in gain or loss, and devoid of affection or aversion. It is cultivated not through effortful goodwill but through the realization of non-self and an undisturbed perception of emptiness. This form of compassion cannot exist without the prior dismantling of ego-based motivation.

**Mental Clarity and Discernment Are the Supporting Foundations**  
Chanding generates the mental stability necessary to perceive reality clearly, creating the ground for Wuzhizhe to arise. Chashi, the cognitive dimension of wisdom, enables the recognition of emptiness without falling into conceptual traps. Together, they support Cibei by ensuring compassion is not rooted in illusion, craving, or mistaken notions of separateness. Wuguanzhao further reinforces this by reinforcing the non-attachment that keeps compassion pure and unconditioned.

**The Perfected Being Embodies Unity of Wisdom and Compassion**  
Shengre represents the full manifestation of both Cibei and Wuzhizhe — a being who has extinguished all defilements and dwells in serene non-contention. This state is characterized by selfless action, revered not by force or dogma but by the natural radiance of clarity and care. The Shengin and Shengre are not distinct; the former is the path, the latter the fruition. Both are inseparable from the complete cessation of Huai and the unwavering presence of non-clinging awareness.
