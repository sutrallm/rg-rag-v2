**Title: The Three Trainings and the Liberation of Saintly Beings in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**  
Liberation is achieved only through the integrated practice of ethical discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom. Ethical conduct forms the essential foundation, enabling stable concentration, which in turn allows wisdom to arise. This wisdom—rooted in direct observation of impermanence, suffering, and non-self—leads to transcendence of worldly suffering. Saintly beings, marked by perfected conduct and mental purity, are revered even by celestial beings due to their complete release from cycles of rebirth and attachment.

**Ethical Conduct as the Foundation**  
Moral discipline of body, speech, and mind is the first and indispensable component of the path. Without purity in ethical behavior, spiritual progress lacks stability and becomes fleeting. It provides the necessary ground for meditative concentration to develop without obstruction.

**Meditative Concentration as the Catalyst**  
Meditative concentration stabilizes the mind, creating the clarity required for wisdom to penetrate the true nature of phenomena. It is the second stage in the threefold framework and cannot arise or endure without the sanctity established by ethical discipline.

**Wisdom as the Culmination**  
Wisdom emerges through mindful observation of arising phenomena, revealing impermanence, suffering, and non-self. It is the final and highest training, without which even the most disciplined and concentrated practice remains incomplete. This wisdom is the direct cause of liberation.

**Transcendence and Veneration**  
Those who have perfected ethical conduct and wisdom become saintly beings—free from suffering and rebirth. Their mental serenity, detachment, and profound understanding compel reverence from celestial beings, who recognize their attainment as a rare and supreme realization.

**Non-Self as the Highest Realization**  
The deepest understanding that leads to liberation is the recognition of no-self. It is not merely an intellectual concept but a direct perceptual truth that dissolves attachment and ends the cycle of suffering.
