**Title: Chiyu, Wuwo Wuzhi, and the Path to Liberation Through Contemplation of Impermanence**

**Summary:**  
The path to liberation centers on the direct realization that all conditioned experience—manifested through the five aggregates—is transient, devoid of a permanent self, and inherently unsatisfactory. This understanding, cultivated through the practice of observing impermanence, dismantles the three root afflictions of greed, hatred, and delusion. As clinging to self and phenomena fades, non-attachment arises, culminating in the cessation of suffering and the attainment of nirvana. Wisdom emerges not through doctrine alone but through mindful, equanimous observation of bodily and mental phenomena in their fleeting nature. The enlightened being embodies this wisdom, having extinguished all afflictions and transcended even spiritual attachments.

---

**The Three Poisons Are the Root Cause of All Suffering**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are identified as the fundamental afflictions that bind beings to cyclic existence. These mental states give rise to clinging and misperception, which in turn generate suffering. Their complete eradication defines the state of the enlightened being and is necessary for the cessation of dissatisfaction in conditioned existence.

**Impermanence Is the Core Object of Meditative Observation**  
All physical and mental phenomena—whether sensations, thoughts, or bodily decay—are to be observed as transient, like flowing water or lightning. The systematic contemplation of this impermanence is not abstract but experiential, targeting the five aggregates as the direct field of awareness. This observation dismantles the mistaken belief in a stable, enduring self and dissolves the attachment that sustains suffering.

**The Five Aggregates Represent the Illusory Basis of Self**  
Form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness collectively constitute all conventional human experience. These aggregates are inherently impermanent and conditionally arisen. Misidentifying them as a permanent, independent self is the central delusion. Their observation under contemplation reveals their insubstantial nature, leading directly to the realization of no-self.

**Wisdom Arising from Concentration Enables Non-Attachment**  
A calm and stable mind, developed through meditative concentration, creates the conditions for profound understanding to emerge. This wisdom does not remain theoretical; it leads to non-attachment even to desirable meditative states, recognizing them as transient and not to be clung to. True insight reveals that nothing—spiritual or mundane—is exempt from impermanence.

**Suffering Motivates the Pursuit of Liberation**  
The pervasive unsatisfactoriness inherent in conditioned existence is not merely an observation but a catalyst. Recognizing suffering as an inescapable feature of life prompts the seeker to cultivate wisdom. Without this recognition, the motivation to transcend the cycle of attachment and rebirth remains incomplete.

**Enlightenment Is the Final Dissolution of Self and Clinging**  
The ultimate aim is the complete dissolution of the illusion of a separate, enduring self, resulting in no-self and non-attachment. This condition is synonymous with nirvana—the end of suffering. The enlightened being, having extinguished the three poisons and realized the true nature of reality, no longer identifies with any aggregate or experience as “me” or “mine.”
