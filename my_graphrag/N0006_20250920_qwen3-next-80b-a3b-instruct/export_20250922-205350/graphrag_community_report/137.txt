**Title: Chanding, Wisdom, and Compassion as Foundations of Inner Freedom**

**Summary:**  
True inner freedom is achieved through the disciplined practice of Chanding, which trains the mind to remain undisturbed by external conditions. This state is sustained by the combined practice of wisdom and compassion—wisdom to perceive reality clearly, and compassion to respond to suffering without attachment or aversion. Non-attachment emerges naturally from this balance, avoiding both emotional disengagement and entanglement.

**Chanding Cultivates Mental Resilience**  
Chanding directly enables a mind that remains undisturbed by environ-mental conditions like noise, criticism, or sensory pleasures. This practice is not merely passive endurance but an active training that reshapes how the mind interacts with the external world, laying the foundation for sustained inner stability.

**Wisdom and Compassion Operate as Interdependent Forces**  
Wisdom provides the clarity to see things as they are, while compassion ensures a balanced, empathetic response to suffering. Neither alone suffices: wisdom without compassion risks cold detachment; compassion without wisdom risks emotional overwhelm. Together, they form a unified approach that sustains equilibrium.

**Non-Attachment Arises from Balanced Engagement**  
Non-attachment is not withdrawal from experience but the natural outcome of wisdom and compassion in action. It avoids the extremes of emotional entanglement and detachment by allowing responses that are clear, grounded, and free from reaction driven by liking or aversion.

**Inner Freedom Is Sustained by Integrated Practice**  
Independence from externals is not accidental or temporary; it is the result of consistently applying wisdom to perceive reality and compassion to engage with it. This dual cultivation creates a mind that is neither pulled by pleasure nor repelled by discomfort, maintaining calm regardless of conditions.
