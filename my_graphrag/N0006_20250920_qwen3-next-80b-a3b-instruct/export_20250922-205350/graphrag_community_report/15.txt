**Title: Liberation Through Non-Doing: The Path of Chanding and the Non-Abiding Mind**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is present and accessible now, not as a distant goal but through immediate mindful awareness and the absence of attachment or resistance. True freedom is realized not by striving to practice, but by ceasing all effort to become a practitioner. The mind, when unattached and unfixed—like space—naturally aligns with its true nature. This state, called non-abiding, is cultivated through chanding, which reveals that effortlessness is the highest form of practice. Liberation is synonymous with the cessation of clinging, achieved by understanding the Five Aggregates and embodied by those who have transcended all defilements.

**Chanding is the Practical Path to Liberation**  
Sustained mindfulness and non-attachment in meditation are not merely techniques but the actual means through which freedom is realized. This practice is not about achieving a state, but about allowing the mind to rest in its natural condition without manipulation or pursuit.

**Non-Doing Is the Highest Form of Practice**  
The most profound cultivation occurs when one abandons the desire to progress, avoid failure, or even identify as a practitioner. Liberation is not reached through force or strategy but through the complete cessation of striving, allowing the mind to align effortlessly with its innate nature.

**The Non-Abiding Mind Is Liberation Itself**  
Liberation is defined by the absence of fixation—neither clinging to nor rejecting any experience, including meditative states or the idea of attainment. This mental condition, likened to space, is characterized by utter non-grasping and is the defining trait of true freedom.

**Saints Embodiment of the Non-Abiding State**  
Those who have fully realized liberation are free from all fundamental defilements and the cycle of samsara. Their hearts remain unshaken, unattached, and undisturbed, reflecting the same non-abiding quality that defines the state of liberation itself.

**Liberation and Nirvana Are Equivalent**  
The cessation of suffering and the end of clinging are fully captured in the terms liberation and nirvana. Both point to the same ultimate condition: freedom arising not from external change, but from inner non-attachment.

**Understanding the Five Aggregates Ends Clinging**  
Recognizing the true nature of the Five Aggregates dismantles the basis for attachment. This realization directly leads to the cessation of clinging and is the essential step toward realizing liberation.

**Effortless Alignment With Mind’s Natural State Is True Liberation**  
The deepest freedom is not produced by effort, discipline, or willpower. It emerges when effort dissolves and the mind no longer resists or grasps—allowing it to rest in its natural, uncontrived state.
