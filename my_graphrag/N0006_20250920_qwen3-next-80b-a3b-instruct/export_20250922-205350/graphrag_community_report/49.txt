**Title: ChaoYue ZhuZhe and the Path of Mindfulness, Wisdom, and Compassion**

**Summary:**  
True liberation arises from the union of continuous mindfulness and deep insight into impermanence and non-self, leading to entire release from clinging—even to spiritual experiences and nirvana itself. This state, known as ChaoYue ZhuZhe, is sustained by persistent effort and cannot be achieved without mindfulness as its operational foundation. Compassion and wisdom must be practiced together; either alone leads to imbalance. Liberation is not attained through rituals or forms, but through the transformation of mind, marked by non-attachment, mirror-like awareness, and the cessation of afflictions through present-moment observation.

---

**Mindfulness Sustains Meditative Stability and Prevents Afflictions**  
Continuous, non-judgmental awareness of body, feelings, mind, and mental objects—known as Zhennian—acts as the essential support for meditative concentration (Chanding). Without this unwavering attention, concentration dissolves and afflictions such as greed, hatred, and delusion arise. Mindfulness operates not only in formal meditation but throughout daily activities, enabling real-time recognition of arising desires and attachments, which allows for their natural dissolution through observation rather than suppression.

**Transcending Attachment Requires Insight into Impermanence and Non-Self**  
The realization that all phenomena—including meditative bliss, spiritual attainments, and even nirvana—are impermanent, conditioned, and devoid of a fixed self leads to the release of all clinging. This understanding, cultivated through contemplation of the five aggregates, directly undermines the belief in solidity or permanence, making true letting go possible. This insight is not abstract—it is the cognitive mechanism through which freedom from suffering is actualized.

**Compassion and Wisdom Must Be United to Attain Perfect Enlightenment**  
Boundless goodwill toward all beings (compassion) and clear seeing of reality’s true nature (wisdom) function as inseparable wings. Compassion alone risks attachment to outcomes; wisdom alone results in cold detachment. Only when both are balanced does one achieve true transcendence—free from clinging to self, experience, or attainment—leading to complete awakening (Bodhi) and the end of rebirth.

**True Practice Lies in Mind Transformation, Not Ritual Forms**  
External practices such as sitting meditation, chanting, or mantra hold value only insofar as they support internal change. Clinging to the identity of “practitioner,” progress, or spiritual experiences—however refined—constitutes a subtle form of attachment. Authentic practice is the embodiment of mirror-like awareness: no self-reference, no attachment to outcomes, no identification with states, even those labeled as “enlightened.”

**Persistent Effort Maintains the Continuity of Liberation**  
The realization of ChaoYue ZhuZhe is not a one-time event but a sustained unfolding. Without persistent effort, the mind reverts to ignorance and habitual clinging. This ongoing diligence enables uninterrupted observation of the non-self nature of experience, ensuring that wisdom does not fade and letting go becomes irreversible.

**Letting Go Is the Expression of Liberation Itself**  
True letting go (Fangxia) is not passive resignation or denial—it is the active, experiential release from clinging born of direct insight. It manifests when one no longer believes in the permanence or inherent value of any state, including peace or stillness. This release is not a means to an end; it is liberation itself—the absence of bondage, the natural state of the awakened mind.

**The Sage Embodies Non-Attachment to All Phenomena, Including Nirvana**  
The highest expression of this path is found in the sage, who has fully dissolved all forms of clinging—even the attachment to nirvana. This final release confirms that true freedom is not a state to be possessed but a condition of complete non-ownership, where even the concept of attainment is transcended.
