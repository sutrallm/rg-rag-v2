**Title: The Nature of Enlightenment and the Dissolution of Self**

**Summary**  
An enlightened being has fully dismantled the illusion of a permanent, independent self through direct realization of impermanence and emptiness. This liberation is achieved by severing greed, hatred, and delusion, resulting in unwavering stillness, freedom from all conditioned existence, and transcendence beyond worldly and supernatural desires. Such a state is marked by non-attachment, the absence of self-oriented practice, and is revered by all realms due to its authentic realization.

**The Eradication of the Three Poisons Defines Liberation**  
Liberation is characterized by the complete removal of greed, hatred, and delusion. These three afflictions are the foundational sources of suffering and bondage; their total elimination marks the singular hallmark of an enlightened state, distinguishing it from all other conditions of being.

**The Illusion of Self Is Dissolved Through Direct Realization**  
The conviction of a permanent, independent identity is revealed as a misconception. This illusion is undone not through intellectual understanding but through direct insight into the absence of self, leading to a state where the conceptual framework of self and practice no longer holds sway.

**Transcendence of the Three Realms Signifies Ultimate Freedom**  
Enlightenment encompasses liberation from all manifestations of conditioned existence—the realms of desire, form, and formless. This transcendence is not partial or provisional but total, representing the final outcome of the path and the cessation of all samsaric bonds.

**Stillness Is the Natural Condition of the Liberated**  
An enlightened being resides in unshakable inner stillness, untouched by craving, aversion, or disturbance. This stillness is not an achieved state of calm but the natural expression of a mind free from the forces that previously disturbed it.

**Non-Attachment Defines the Mode of Action**  
The liberated no longer engage in practice as a means to attain or affirm identity. Their actions arise without attachment to self or outcome, reflecting the dissolution of the conceptual boundary between practitioner and practice, self and world.

**Reverence Arises from Authentic Realization, Not Supernatural Power**
