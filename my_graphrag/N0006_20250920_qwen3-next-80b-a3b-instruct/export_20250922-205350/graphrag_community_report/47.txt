**Title: The Path of Jie, Ding, and Hui: Liberation Through Ethical Conduct, Concentration, and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
Liberation from the cycle of birth and death is achieved through the integrated development of ethical discipline (Jie), meditative concentration (Ding), and penetrating wisdom (Hui). Jie establishes mental purity and stability by restraining harmful actions, creating the necessary foundation for Ding. Ding uniﬁes the mind, suppressing distraction and enabling Hui to arise—direct, experiential realization of impermanence, non-self, and conditioned arising. Hui uproots ignorance and the three poisons (greed, hatred, delusion), leading to non-attachment (Wuzhang) and mental liberation (Jiexie). The enlightened sage (Shenggu者) embodies all three, dwelling in quietude, free from the three realms and spontaneous in action. Practice must be whole: hearing, reflection, and direct realization (Wensisixiu) must converge; without integration, progress is incomplete. Even spiritual attainments, including Nirvana, must not be clung to—true freedom is non-abiding.

**Ethical Conduct Anchors Mental Clarity**  
Moral discipline (Jie) purifies body and speech, eliminating guilt and mental agitation that obstruct meditative stability. By restraining harmful actions and cultivating right conduct, Jie creates the inner quietude essential for concentration to arise. Without this foundation, the mind remains scattered and unstable, incapable of sustaining focus. Jie is not merely rule-following but an active safeguarding of mental clarity, enabling the mind to be receptive to deeper insight. The enlightened sage exemplifies perfect Jie—moral purity is inseparable from their awakened state, standing as the indispensable root of all spiritual progress.

**Concentration Enables Penetrative Realization**  
Meditative concentration (Ding) is the unifying of the mind on a single object, cultivating unwavering stillness and mental cohesion. It arises only when ethical discipline has purified the mind of distraction and turmoil. Ding serves as the central medium through which wisdom emerges, providing the sustained focus required to observe reality without interference. Without Ding, insight is shallow, speculative, or fleeting. The matured state of Ding reveals mental stillness not actively sought or resisted, aligning with the state of non-abiding. The enlightened sage maintains perfect Ding—mind undisturbed, steady, and fully present—allowing wisdom to unfold with depth and clarity.

**Wisdom Uproots Ignorance and Ends Suffering**  
Penetrating wisdom (Hui) is the direct, experiential realization that all phenomena are impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of inherent self. It is not intellectual understanding but the matured fruit of disciplined practice grounded in Jie and sustained by Ding. Hui dismantles the illusion of permanence and identity by illuminating the true nature of experience—through contemplation of impermanence (Wuchangguan) and non-self (Wuwo), craving and clinging are uprooted. This wisdom is the direct antidote to ignorance (Wuming), the root of the cycle of birth and death (Shengsi). It extinguishes greed, hatred, and delusion (Tan Chen Chi), leading to liberation of the mind (Jiexie) and the transcendence of the three realms (Sanjie). Hui is the culminating light of the path—its realization is Nirvana, expressed as effortless non-attachment (Wuzhang).

**Non-Attachment Is the Natural Expression of Realization**  
True liberation manifests not as possession of states but as complete non-abiding (Wuzhang)—a mind that neither clings to worldly experiences nor spiritual attainments, including Nirvana itself. This state arises spontaneously when wisdom sees through the illusion of self and permanence. Clinging to enlightenment as “my achievement” reproduces the very egoic structure sought to be dissolved. Wuzhang is thus not a goal to reach, but the natural outcome of wisdom’s clarity. It is likened to empty space—holding all phenomena without being stained by them. This is the mind of the enlightened sage: free from pursuit, fear, or seeking, beyond the pull of desire or aversion.

**The Integrated Path Requires All Three Phases**  
The complete spiritual journey (Wensisixiu) demands the integration of hearing the teaching, reflective understanding, and direct practice. Each phase supports the next: ethical discipline (Jie) is the foundation cultivated through Dharma learning; concentration (Ding) is the practicing medium that deepens insight; wisdom (Hui) is the realized outcome. Failure in any stage undermines the whole. The path begins with Jie, advances through Ding, and culminates in Hui—each mutually reinforcing. Jie ensures clarity; Ding provides stability; Hui brings illumination. Together, they sever the causal chain of rebirth (Siwang lunhui) and lead to the cessation of suffering. Superior meditative states (Chanding) are not endpoints but expressions of this maturation—when concentration is perfected, wisdom naturally blooms, and liberation follows.
