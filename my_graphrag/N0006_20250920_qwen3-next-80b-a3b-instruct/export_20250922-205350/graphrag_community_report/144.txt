**Title: The Path of the Saintly Being: Eradication of the Three Poisons and Liberation from Samsara**

**Summary**  
A saintly being achieves complete freedom from cyclic existence by fully uprooting greed, hatred, and delusion—known as the three poisons. These afflictions are the sole roots of suffering, rebirth, and bondage. Liberation is not attained through external means but through the disciplined cultivation of ethical conduct, mental focus, and wisdom. The cessation of these three poisons defines enlightenment and seals the end of samsara. The illusion of self, maintained by craving and ignorance, dissolves through meditative insight, dismantling the foundation of rebirth. Only those who sever these poisons completely attain serene extinction and are revered even by celestial beings.

**The Three Poisons Are the Sole Cause of Samsara**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion—manifesting as craving, aversion, and ignorance—are explicitly identified as the fundamental roots of cyclic existence. Every instance of suffering, rebirth, and attachment stems from these three. Their presence keeps beings bound to the cycle, while their total extinction is the singular condition required to break free. No other cause is cited; all other factors, such as karma or clinging, are shown to arise from these roots.

**Liberation Is Defined by the Complete Cessation of the Three Poisons**  
True freedom from samsara is not described as an event or state of bliss, but as the total eradication of the three poisons. This cessation is the hallmark of saintly attainment and enlightenment. Nirvana is equated directly with this annihilation of greed, hatred, and delusion. No level of spiritual practice, however advanced, is deemed sufficient unless it results in their complete uprooting.

**The Three Pillars of Practice Lead to Eradication**  
The path to liberation is structured around three interdependent practices: ethical conduct, mental focus, and wisdom. Chanding—understood as a form of meditative cultivation—is one of these pillars, alongside precepts and wisdom. Together, they enable the practitioner to dismantle the three poisons. This framework is presented as the complete and necessary method; no alternative path is suggested.

**The Illusion of Self Sustains Samsara**  
The root of craving and ignorance lies in the false identification with the aggregates—the belief in a permanent, independent self. Through wuwo guan, this illusion is dismantled, thereby severing the cycle at its origin. The dissolution of self-view is not an auxiliary practice but a direct means of ending the poisons, as they depend on the illusion for their persistence.

**Recklessness and Lack of Effort Perpetuate Bondage**  
The cycle of suffering continues unchecked when one remains reckless or fails to engage in disciplined practice. Effortful application of the three pillars is required to transcend samsara; without it, beings remain ensnared. The text implies that liberation is neither accidental nor passive—it demands sustained, intentional cultivation.
