**Title: Jiedinghui and the Path to Nierhua: The Interdependent Components of Liberation**

**Summary**  
The Threefold Training (Jiedinghui)—moral discipline (Jie), meditative concentration (Chanding), and wisdom (Hui)—forms the only systematic path to Nierhua, the state of complete liberation from suffering, craving, and rebirth. Each component is indispensable and sequentially dependent: moral discipline purifies action and eliminates inner turmoil, enabling stable concentration; concentration steadies the mind to allow wisdom to arise; wisdom directly perceives impermanence, selflessness, and suffering, dissolving ignorance and attachment. Liberation is not attained by isolating any single element but through their unified cultivation. The realized being (Shengzhe) embodies this integration, having fully relinquished all attachments—including to spiritual achievements—through transcendent wisdom (Chengzhi) and complete letting go (She li yiqie). True freedom (Nierhua) is defined by the absence of clinging, even to the concept of attainment, and is only realized when all defilements are extinguished and the mind rests in non-dual awareness.

---

**Moral Discipline as the Unshakeable Foundation**  
Moral discipline (Jie) is the first and necessary stage of the Threefold Training. It purifies behavior, removes regret, and creates the mental clarity required for sustained concentration. Without ethical conduct, the mind remains agitated and unstable, making deeper meditative states unattainable. This foundational element is not optional but structurally essential: no concentration or wisdom can emerge reliably without it.

**Meditative Concentration as the Central Bridge**  
Chanding, the meditative pillar, lies between ethical grounding and liberating wisdom. It stabilizes mental activity, allowing the practitioner to observe phenomena without distraction or reaction. This steady focus is not an end in itself but the essential condition through which insight into reality becomes possible. Concentration alone does not lead to liberation; its role is to prepare the mind for wisdom.

**Wisdom as the Culminating Force**  
Wisdom (Hui), embodied as Chengzhi, is the final and highest component of the path. It is the direct perception of impermanence (Wuchang), selflessness, and the inherent suffering of conditioned existence. This insight dismantles the illusions that fuel craving and aversion. It arises naturally from sustained concentration and is grounded in moral discipline. Wisdom does not merely inform—it transforms, eradicating defilements and dissolving attachments at their root.

**Complete Letting Go as the Defining Act of Liberation**  
She li yiqie—the total relinquishment of all attachments, including those to meditative experiences, powers, or even the notion of spiritual progress—is not a step along the way but the very condition of ultimate freedom. Without this total non-clinging, even profound meditative states remain forms of bondage. Liberation is not marked by attainment but by the absence of grasping.

**Non-Attachment to Attainment as the Final Test**  
Buzhi guocheng—the practice of not clinging to any realization or state of progress—is the practical expression of wisdom in action. Any sense of “having achieved” enlightenment, including subtle identification with meditative joys, becomes a new form of ignorance. True freedom is only present when even the idea of “spiritual attainment” is released, revealing the mind’s unconditioned nature.

**Nierhua as the State of Non-Dual Peace**  
Nierhua is not a place, goal, or mystical experience but the complete cessation of craving, clinging, aversion, and delusion. It is realized only when the mind is wholly free from fixation and rests in non-dual awareness. It cannot be approached through partial efforts or isolated practices. Every component of Jiedinghui must be fully actualized—discipline, concentration, and wisdom—as one unified process.

**The Realized Being Embodies the Full Path**  
Shengzhe is not defined by extraordinary powers or mystical knowledge but by the seamless embodiment of Jiedinghui. Such a being demonstrates moral purity, unwavering mental stability, and unshakable wisdom. Their defining quality is She li yiqie: they have let go of every attachment, including attachment to being liberated. Their existence is the living proof of the path’s totality.

**The Necessity of Seeing Impermanence**  
Wuchang (impermanence) is the primary object of contemplation through which Chengzhi arises. Without firsthand realization of the transient, unsatisfactory, and selfless nature of all phenomena, wisdom cannot emerge. This understanding is the gateway that transforms meditation from tranquility into liberation.

**Freedom from External Appearances as a Prerequisite**  
Jiefu—the non-clinging to any experience, even subtle or refined meditative states—is indispensable for reaching Nierhua. The mind must release all attachments, whether coarse or refined, to attain unconditioned peace. Clinging—even to bliss, clarity, or emptiness—sustains the cycle of rebirth.

**The Unity of Jiedinghui as the Sole Path**  
Jiedinghui is not a collection of separate practices but a single, integrated system. Each element departs from the other: discipline enables concentration, concentration awakens wisdom, and wisdom completes liberation. No component can function independently. To isolate any part renders the entire path ineffective. Only their simultaneous and interdependent realization leads to Jietao—the termination of suffering and the cycle of rebirth.
