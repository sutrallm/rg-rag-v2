**Title: Transcending Attachment and True Stillness in the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True liberation arises not from attaining meditative experiences such as bliss, light, or emptiness, but from relinquishing all attachment to them—including the sense of being enlightened. True stillness is not external quiet but an inner state of non-reactivity, where the mind reflects conditions without clinging or rejecting. This state, equivalent to nirvanic peace, emerges only when one recognizes all phenomena as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and without self—merely the Five Aggregates in constant flux. Even saints remain free by not identifying with any state, attainment, or identity.

**Meditative Experiences Must Be Relinquished to Achieve Liberation**  
Experiences like ease, light, psychic powers, and emptiness, though pleasant or impressive, are ultimately illusory. Clinging to them—even as markers of progress—fuels a subtle form of craving that entrenches the self in cycles of attachment. True progress requires recognizing these states as temporary phenomena and letting them pass without fixation.

**True Stillness Is Internal Non-Reactivity, Not External Silence**  
Stillness is not defined by the absence of sound or physical solitude. It is the mind’s capacity to remain undisturbed—like a mirror—whether surrounded by noise or solitude. This quality arises from absence of attachment and aversion, reflecting conditions without translating them into identity or desire. It is the living embodiment of nirvanic peace.

**Liberation Requires Seeing All Phenomena as Impermanent and Selfless**  
The foundation for release lies in directly perceiving that all experiences—meditative or otherwise—are composed of the Five Aggregates, continuously changing and devoid of a fixed core. This understanding dismantles the illusion of a permanent self and prevents even the most refined states from becoming objects of identification.

**Enlightenment Is Not an Identity to Be Clung To**  
Even the notion of being “enlightened” or a “saint” is a form of attachment if held as an identity. Those who have realized full liberation do not maintain any conceptual grip—even on spiritual status. Freedom is complete only when no state, achievement, or label is clung to as a destination or proof of worth.
