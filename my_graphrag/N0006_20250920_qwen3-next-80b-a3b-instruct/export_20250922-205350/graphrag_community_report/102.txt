Title: The Foundation of Compassionate Action in Bodhisattva Practice

Summary:  
Compassion arises from the direct perception of suffering among all beings. This compassion forms the essential basis for the awakening mind, which extends equally to every suffering being without distinction. The motivation to relieve suffering is the driving force behind the Bodhisattva path.

Compassion is rooted in the observation of suffering  
The act of compassion emerges not from abstract principle but from witnessing the reality of pain endured by all living beings. This perception directly triggers the aspiration to liberate them from suffering, anchoring the entire practice in concrete experience.

The awakening mind is universal and undifferentiated  
The mindset that propels Bodhisattva action extends equally to every being in distress. There is no selection or hierarchy in its scope—any being experiencing suffering is the valid target of this resolve, making universality its defining characteristic.

Relief of suffering is the purpose of the path  
All actions aligned with the Bodhisattva way are oriented toward the removal of suffering. This objective is not symbolic or indirect; it is the sole reason the awakening mind arises and sustains itself.
