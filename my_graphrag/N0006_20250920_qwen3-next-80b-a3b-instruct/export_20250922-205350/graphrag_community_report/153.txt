**Title: Present-Moment Awareness, Senging, and Chanding in the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
Sustained mindfulness of the present moment, supported by unwavering effort, is the foundation for developing meditative concentration and cultivating the non-self view. Without consistent effort, concentration cannot arise or endure. This process gradually dissolves ignorance by revealing the transient and impersonal nature of experience, leading to clarity and liberation.

**Persistent Effort Enables Meditative Concentration**  
Meditative concentration cannot be achieved or maintained without continuous, determined effort. The stability of concentration is entirely dependent on this sustained engagement—without it, concentration fails to emerge and cannot be preserved.

**Present-Moment Awareness Breaks Ignorance**  
Focusing fully on immediate experiences—breath, steps, thoughts—without attachment to past or future removes distortion. This practice dismantles ignorance by exposing experience as impermanent and impersonal, resulting in clearer perception.

**Effort Sustains Mindfulness; Mindfulness Deepens Concentration**  
Mindfulness of the present is an active, demanding practice requiring unrelenting effort. This same effort not only maintains awareness but also serves as the mechanism through which concentration is built and preserved, creating a reciprocal relationship between the two.

**Non-Self View Arises From Continuous Presence**  
The understanding that experience lacks a fixed self is not abstract but emerges directly from sustained attention to each unfolding moment. As awareness remains undistracted, the impersonal and transient characteristics of all phenomena become unmistakable, reinforcing this view.
