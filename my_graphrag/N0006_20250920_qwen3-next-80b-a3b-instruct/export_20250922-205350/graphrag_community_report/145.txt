**Title: Saintly Beings and the Path to True Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True liberation is achieved through the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion, realized solely through inner awakening, non-attachment, and direct experiential insight. Saintly beings manifest this state by embodying perfected moral discipline, unshakable meditative stability, and wisdom that sees all phenomena as transient and selfless. Their freedom is absolute—unconditioned by rituals, deities, external environments, or spiritual identities—and arises from the total abandonment of clinging to self, desire, and outcomes.

---

**Perfected Moral Discipline as the Foundation**  
The foundation of a saintly being’s emergence lies in perfected moral discipline, which is not optional but indispensable. This discipline serves as the essential first stage upon which all further spiritual development rests, creating the stability necessary for deeper transformation.

**Unshakable Meditative Stability as the Pathway**  
Chanding—a state of deep, unwavering meditative focus and mental clarity—enables the mind to settle beyond distraction, allowing for the direct perception of reality. It is not an end in itself but a vital practice that clears the way for profound understanding.

**Wisdom That Sees Beyond Self and Entity**  
Chaoshi—the unshakable wisdom inherent to saintly beings—reveals all phenomena as empty, transient, and devoid of inherent self. This understanding dismantles the illusion of permanence and identity, dissolving the basis for attachment and aversion.

**Complete Eradication of the Three Poisons**  
The defining mark of true liberation is the total removal of greed, hatred, and delusion. These are not merely subdued but uprooted entirely, leaving no residue to sustain suffering or the cycle of rebirth. Their absence is what makes liberation complete.

**Freedom Through Non-Attachment to Identity and Outcome**  
Sheli—perfect abandonment of self, desire, and outcome—eliminates the internal structures that bind one to samsara. When even the notion of “I am pure” or “I have attained” is relinquished, the mind no longer clings to spiritual achievements as forms of possession.

**True Liberation Is Self-Sufficient and Unconditioned**  
The state of Nirvana is not reached through rituals, talismans, deities, or environmental conditions. It arises exclusively from internal awakening and equanimity. External supports, even seemingly spiritual ones, are not only unnecessary—they become obstacles when clung to.

**Clinging to Spiritual Experiences Binds One to Samsara**  
Attachment to any form of identity, experience, or result—even those perceived as spiritually elevated—reinforces the sense of a separate self. This clinging, whether to worldly possessions or meditative states, perpetuates dependence and prevents ultimate freedom.
