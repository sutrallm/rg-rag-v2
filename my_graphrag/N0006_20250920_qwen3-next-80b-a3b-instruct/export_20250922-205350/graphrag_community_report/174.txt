**Title: Wuzhiguo, Jiefang, and Netan: The Path of Non-Attachment and Ultimate Freedom in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**  
The text outlines a coherent spiritual framework centered on the dissolution of self-identification and attachment as the means to liberation. Through deep meditative practice, one comes to perceive all phenomena as transient and devoid of inherent self, leading naturally to non-clinging. This non-attachment is not passive but the active expression of freedom, culminating in a state where the mind is untethered from all emotional disturbances and conditioned responses. The path is structured around the cultivation of concentration and wisdom, with realization of impermanence and selflessness as the foundational turning point. Liberation and nirvana are presented as identical outcomes—the complete cessation of greed, hatred, and delusion.

**Wuzhiguo Arises from the Realization of Zhufayuwu**  
The understanding that all phenomena—body, thought, emotion, and environment—arise dependently and lack fixed essence leads directly to non-clinging. Recognizing their fleeting nature, like dew or lightning, dismantles the illusion of ownership and identity, making the mindset of Wuzhiguo inevitable.

**Chanding Enables the Realization of Impermanence**  
Deep meditative practice is the necessary foundation for perceiving the insubstantiality of all experiences. Without Chanding, the perception of phenomena as transient and self-less remains theoretical; it is through sustained stillness that this recognition becomes experiential and transformative.

**Wuzhiguo Is the Expression of True Liberation**  
Freedom is not an abstract ideal but is embodied in the absence of clinging to “I” and “mine.” The act of letting go—Wuzhiguo—is not merely a step toward liberation but its living form, demonstrating that freedom is defined by the absence of attachment rather than the presence of any state.

**Jiefang and Netan Are Equivalent States**  
The complete eradication of afflictions—greed, hatred, delusion—and the freedom from all emotional disturbances caused by gain, loss, praise, or blame are described identically in both terms. Jiefang, as the condition of being unbound by worldly conditions, is not distinct from Netan, the cessation of suffering.

**Dinghui Is the Direct Path to Netan**  
The balanced development of concentration and wisdom is presented as the essential method leading to the final cessation of all suffering. Neither concentration alone nor wisdom alone suffices; their harmonious cultivation is required to reach the state where all defilements are extinguished.

**A Shengzhe Has Fully Embodied Liberation**  
One who has extinguished all defilements and is no longer driven by conditions exists in the realized state of Jiefang. This individual is not defined by attainment or experience, but by the absence of bondage—no longer reacting to life’s fluctuations as if they concerned a self.
