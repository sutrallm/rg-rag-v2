**Title: The State of the Saintly Being: Mastery of Conduct, Concentration, and Wisdom**

**Summary:**  
The saintly being is defined by the complete eradication of greed, hatred, and delusion, resulting in liberation from samsara and enduring inner peace. This state is not achieved through external rituals or scriptural adherence but through direct awakening. It rests on three pillars: perfect moral conduct, unwavering meditative stability, and profound wisdom derived from direct realization of impermanence, not-self, and suffering. Such a being transcends all worldly stimuli, neither clinging to nor rejecting experience, and is sustained solely by inner equanimity. Reverence arises naturally from this state, not from external authority or achievement.

**Perfect Moral Conduct as the Foundation**  
Moral conduct is not merely a practice but an inherent trait of the saintly being, reflecting the complete abandonment of harmful intentions. This perfection is not imposed externally but emerges as a natural expression of inner purity, free from the three poisons of greed, hatred, and delusion. It serves as the necessary base upon which deeper states are built, demonstrating that ethical living is inseparable from liberation.

**Meditative Stability as the Anchor of Peace**  
The state of chanding—enduring meditative stability and inner stillness—enables the saintly being to remain untouched by mental disturbances. This is not passive stillness but an active, sustained silence that allows no disturbance from external stimuli, emotional reactivity, or attachment. It is through this stability that the mind becomes a clear mirror, capable of reflecting reality without distortion.

**Wisdom Through Direct Realization of Reality’s Nature**  
True wisdom arises not from study or tradition but from direct, unmediated perception of the three characteristics: impermanence, not-self, and suffering. This realization dissolves all clinging and aversion, revealing all phenomena as empty of inherent existence. The saintly being sees the world as it is—transient, impersonal, and inherently unsatisfying—and thus finds no ground for attachment, desire, or resentment.

**Liberation as the Fruit of Integrated Practice**  
The saintly being’s transcendence of samsara is not the result of isolated efforts but the unified outcome of perfected conduct, concentration, and wisdom. Each supports the others: conduct purifies action, concentration steadies the mind, and wisdom uproots ignorance. Together, they culminate in nirvana—a state beyond evaluation, beyond external influence, and beyond the cycle of becoming.

**Stability Found Only in Inner Equanimity**  
The saintly being does not seek security in wealth, status, praise, or comfort, understanding these as transient and unreliable. True stability comes from inner stillness, where the mind no longer reacts to gain or loss, honor or disgrace. This freedom is not a withdrawal from the world but a state of non-interference—remaining undisturbed by all that arises, because nothing is taken as real or lasting.
