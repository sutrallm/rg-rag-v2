**Title: Liberation in Buddhist Practice: The Path of Chanding, Wisdom, and the Eradication of the Three Poisons**

**Summary:**  
Liberation is the complete cessation of suffering and the cycle of rebirth, achieved not through external means or future attainment, but through immediate, non-attached awareness in the present moment. It arises from the integrated practice of ethical discipline, meditative stillness (chanding), and direct wisdom that perceives impermanence, non-self, and conditional origination. The three poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—are the root causes of bondage; their total uprooting defines the liberated state. This state is not manufactured but uncovered through letting go, with subtle defilements like spiritual pride requiring relentless wisdom to dissolve. Only through sustained self-effort, free from craving for results, can one realize the naturally pure, unconditioned mind.

---

**Meditative Stillness (Chanding) Unveils the Naturally Pure Mind**  
Chanding is not a state to be forced or achieved, but a process of removing mental obstructions to reveal the mind’s inherent stillness and clarity. It is cultivated through daily practice by sramanas and embodied fully by the staged sage, serving as the foundation for wisdom. When maintained without attachment to meditative experiences—such as bliss or luminosity—it prevents spiritual pride and supports the realization that the mind’s purity is always present, not created. Chanding alone is insufficient; its true power emerges only when paired with wisdom to avoid clinging even to tranquil states.

**Wisdom as the Decisive Cause of Liberation**  
Wisdom emerges from direct perception of impermanence, non-self, and conditional origination. It uproots ignorance by recognizing that all phenomena—including thoughts, emotions, and meditative states—are transient and devoid of inherent identity. This understanding dissolves clinging, especially to the notion of a self, and turns conceptual knowledge into lived experience. The highest wisdom transcends attachment even to the Dharma itself, ensuring liberation is not mistaken for attainment. It is this direct insight, not ritual or belief, that severs the cycle of rebirth and makes freedom irreversible.

**Eradication of the Three Poisons Defines the Liberated State**  
Greed, hatred, and delusion are the fundamental afflictions sustaining cyclic existence. Their complete cessation is the sole criterion for liberation, replacing them with pure awareness and equanimity. An enlightened being, whether called a saintly being, sramana, or staged sage, is identified solely by the absence of these three roots. Delusion—the misperception of permanence and selfhood—is particularly critical; its removal dismantles the egoic structure that generates suffering. Even after progress in discipline and concentration, lingering traces of these poisons, including subtle forms like spiritual pride, must be fully dissolved through unwavering insight.

**Ethical Discipline and Self-Effort Are the Unavoidable Foundations**  
Moral purity in body, speech, and mind is the first pillar of the path, creating the conditions for concentration and wisdom to arise. No external force, deity, or ritual can substitute for personal responsibility—liberation must be earned through consistent, sustained exertion. The threefold structure of ethical discipline, meditative stillness, and wisdom forms an inseparable progression: discipline calms the mind, stillness deepens focus, and wisdom dismantles ignorance. Without this sequence, effort remains fragmented and incapable of uprooting the deep-seated patterns of craving and self-view.

**Non-Attachment and Present-Moment Awareness Are the Expressions of Freedom**  
Liberation is not found in achieving special states but in stabilizing pure awareness—observing the six sense bases and mental phenomena without attachment or aversion. Realizing that experiences arise and pass due to interdependent causes removes the illusion of solidity and permanence. This awareness, applied continuously in the present, reinforces the non-self view and prevents new karmic formations. The liberated mind does not seek enlightenment, nor does it cling to the idea of having attained it; its freedom is unconditioned, spontaneous, and already complete.
