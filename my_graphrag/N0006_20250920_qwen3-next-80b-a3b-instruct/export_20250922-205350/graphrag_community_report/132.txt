**Title: Wisdom, Conditional Origination, and the Path to Liberation**

**Summary:**  
True liberation emerges from a mind unattached to all experiences—whether pleasurable or painful—through the direct understanding that all phenomena arise dependently, lack inherent existence, and are impermanent. This understanding, rooted in conditional origination, dismantles the illusion of a fixed self and world, allowing the mind to release grasping without seeking outcomes. Meditation experiences, including bliss or luminosity, are not ends but opportunities to deepen this timeless recognition.

**Wisdom that transcends attachment dissolves grasping**  
The mind frees itself from clinging to meditative states—such as bliss or luminosity—not by rejecting them, but by recognizing their transient, selfless nature. This non-attachment is not passive indifference but an active release born of clear seeing, and it is the immediate cause of liberation.

**Conditional origination reveals the emptiness of all phenomena**  
All experiences, pleasant or unpleasant, arise only through interdependent causes and conditions. They have no fixed essence or independent identity. Recognizing this truth dissolves the belief in a solid, enduring self and world, removing the root of suffering.

**Meditation without wisdom leads to stagnation**  
Chanding, or meditative absorption, is not liberation in itself. Without the discernment that all meditative experiences are impermanent and selfless, practitioners risk attaching to them, reinforcing the illusion of permanence and hindering progress.

**Liberation is the direct result of seeing emptiness**  
Freedom arises not from attaining new states, but from realizing that all phenomena—including the sense of “I” and “mine”—are empty of inherent existence. This realization ends the delusion of a fixed identity and the craving that sustains it.

**Understanding conditional origination is the foundation of freeing wisdom**  
All liberating wisdom stems from this single recognition: nothing exists independently. Every experience, thought, or state is conditioned, fleeting, and selfless. This framework transforms how one relates to all mental and sensory phenomena, making non-attachment natural and effortless.
