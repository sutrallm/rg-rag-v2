**Title: JIEFU and the Path of the Sage: Liberation Through Non-Attachment and Effortless Presence**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (JIEFU) is the complete cessation of clinging to self and phenomena, achieved not through external rituals or mystical experiences, but through direct awareness and the abandonment of desire. It is realized through a sequential, threefold path: moral discipline stabilizes body and speech, meditative concentration quiets the mind, and wisdom dismantles ignorance by revealing impermanence, suffering, and non-self. The sage, having perfected this path, embodies effortless stillness, transcends all longing for attainment, and manifests liberation as their natural state. True teaching guides others to this direct realization, not intellectual understanding.

---

**Foundational Path: Discipline, Meditation, and Wisdom**  
Liberation proceeds through an inseparable sequence: moral precepts establish the ground for ethical conduct, meditative concentration calms mental turbulence to create clarity, and wisdom directly perceives the emptiness of all phenomena. Each stage depends on the prior one; discipline supports meditation, meditation enables wisdom, and wisdom culminates in liberation. This ordered progression is the only reliable way to uproot suffering.

**Liberation as Non-Attachment to All Experiences**  
JIEFU is not the acquisition of blissful states or spiritual signs, but the total abandonment of clinging—even to meditative joy or enlightenment itself. Even the most refined experiences are recognized as impermanent and devoid of inherent substance. True freedom arises when one no longer grasps at any state, internal or external, including the notion of “I am liberated.”

**Wuzhiso: Wisdom That Sees Through Illusion**  
The wisdom of transcending attachment reveals all phenomena as dependently arisen, unsatisfactory, and without self. This insight emerges from deep meditative awareness, where the five aggregates are observed as empty. It is the defining trait of the sage, who no longer identifies with any mental or sensory occurrence, thereby dissolving the root of suffering.

**Wuzaozuo: Liberation Without Striving**  
The highest expression of freedom is the cessation of all striving—when the mind no longer pursues concentration, enlightenment, or even non-attachment. The very intention to attain liberation is a form of clinging. True JIEFU arises spontaneously when effort dissolves, and the mind rests in its original, unstirred state—without practice, without goal, without form.

**The Sage as Embodied Liberation**  
The sage has perfected moral discipline, sustained meditative stability, and fully realized wisdom. They are not defined by titles or external recognition, but by the complete absence of craving, fear, and identity. Their presence is not a result of achievement, but the natural expression of already-established freedom—revered not for power, but for the quiet cessation of all disturbance.

**Teaching as Guidance to Direct Realization**  
The purpose of teaching is not to convey doctrines or rhetorical beauty, but to point beings toward personal introspection and experiential insight. It guides one along the sequential path—from ethical restraint to meditative calm to the unraveling of self—not as an intellectual exercise, but as a lived transformation leading to spontaneous liberation.

**Diligent Practice Sustains the Path**  
Liberation is not accidental but the fruit of unwavering, continuous effort. Moral conduct, meditative discipline, and the cultivation of wisdom must be maintained without lapse or laziness. Only through sustained practice does the mind become capable of enduring the depth of insight that dissolves attachment at its core.

**The Final Condition: Letting Go Is Liberation Itself**  
JIEFU is not a future destination to reach, but the immediate effect of ceasing to hold on. Liberation is synonymous with non-clinging. There is no other means to Nirvana than the cessation of craving—for pleasure, for existence, for awakening itself. In that letting go, the mind is already free.
