**Title: Jiede and the Path to Liberation in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary:**  
Liberation (Jiede) is the immediate realization of freedom from suffering, craving, and cyclic existence (Shengshi), achieved by eradicating ignorance, attachment, and mental afflictions through the integrated practice of ethical discipline, meditative stability, and wisdom. This state—known as Nirvana (Nihua)—is not a distant or supernatural condition but the present-moment experience of non-attachment, non-abiding, and the direct perception of impermanence, non-self, and emptiness. Liberation cannot be attained through belief or supplication; it arises only through personal cultivation via hearing, reflecting, and practicing. The noble one (Shengzhe) embodies this realization, having transcended all conditioned states, including the notion of liberation itself.

---

**The Threefold Practice of Hearing, Thinking, and Realizing is the Only Path to Liberation**  
True liberation requires the seamless integration of listening to authentic teachings, deeply reflecting on their meaning, and personally realizing them through sustained meditation. None of these elements alone suffices; abstract knowledge without practice leads to no awakening, and blind practice without understanding remains ineffective. Only when all three are fully cultivated does the mind become capable of uprooting delusion and attaining freedom.

**Meditative Stability (Chanding) is the Essential Foundation for Wisdom (Zhigui)**  
A calm and stable mind, cultivated through meditative practice, is not optional but necessary for wisdom to arise. Without this mental grounding, true insight into impermanence, non-self, and emptiness cannot penetrate. Chanding provides the clarity and focus required to perceive reality as it is, making it the indispensable prerequisite for liberation.

**Wisdom (Zhigui) Directly Destroys Ignorance (Wuming) and Craving (Wuyu)**  
The profound understanding of the empty, impermanent, and selfless nature of all phenomena dismantles the root delusion that sustains suffering. This wisdom sees through the illusion of a fixed, enduring self—thereby undermining attachment and aversion. When craving and ignorance are extinguished, the cycle of rebirth ceases, and liberation emerges as a natural consequence.

**Liberation (Jiede) is the Ceaseless State of Non-Abiding (Wuzhu)**  
True freedom is not the attainment of a new state but the absence of clinging to any state—even spiritual ones. The liberated mind does not grasp at peace, enlightenment, or supernatural experiences. It remains vast and unbound, free from fixation on past, present, or future, and fully at ease in the direct experience of reality as it unfolds.

**Liberation Manifests as the Extinguishing of Afflictions (Fannao)**  
Mental disturbances such as greed, hatred, and delusion are not merely obstacles but the very fuel of cyclic existence. Liberation is not partial relief but complete eradication of these afflictions. When they are fully uprooted, there is no longer any force to bind the individual to birth, aging, sickness, and death.

**The Noble One (Shengzhe) Embodies Liberation as a Living Reality**  
One who has realized Jiede no longer identifies with any conditioned state but effortlessly abides in Nirvana. This person is not defined by rituals, titles, or beliefs, but by the irreversible cessation of craving and ignorance. Their presence testifies that liberation is not mythological but a tangible, experiential transformation accessible through diligent practice.

**Impermanence (Wuchang) and Non-Self (Wuxing) Are the Keys to Ending Suffering**  
Attachment arises from the mistaken belief in permanence and selfhood. Deep contemplation of all phenomena as transient and devoid of inherent identity dissolves the desire to possess, control, or avoid. This understanding is not intellectual but direct—its cultivation in the present moment is what unshackles the mind from the cycle of rebirth.

**Liberation is Not a Future Goal but an Immediate Realization**  
Jiede is neither an afterlife promise nor a distant spiritual milestone. It is the immediate recognition of reality as it is—when greed, hatred, and delusion are abandoned here and now. Nirvana, therefore, is not a place or state beyond this life, but the awakened condition experienced when the mind is free from clinging.
