Title: Chanding and Daily Mindfulness in Everyday Life

Summary:  
Chanding is established as a mental state rooted in total presence—free from distraction and mental wandering—achieved through sustained non-judgmental awareness of breath, movement, and thought. This state is not confined to formal meditation but is extended into routine activities such as walking, sitting, eating, and speaking. The consistent practice of bringing this awareness into daily actions transforms ordinary moments into expressions of stillness, creating a seamless continuity between meditation and lived experience.

Chanding is Defined by Present-Moment Anchoring  
The core of chanding lies in the complete orientation of awareness toward the immediate present. It is characterized by the absence of attachment to memories or anticipations, and by the absence of conceptual thought patterns. This mental condition is not passive; it is an active, focused state of observation without evaluation, grounded in the direct perception of bodily sensation, breath, and mental activity as they unfold.

Daily Mindfulness Extends Chanding into Action  
The practice of daily mindfulness ensures that the state of chanding is not limited to seated meditation. By cultivating non-judgmental awareness during routine behaviors—whether walking, eating, or speaking—the distinction between formal practice and ordinary life dissolves. This integration allows stillness to become a constant background condition, present in motion and interaction alike, reinforcing the continuity of presence throughout the day.
