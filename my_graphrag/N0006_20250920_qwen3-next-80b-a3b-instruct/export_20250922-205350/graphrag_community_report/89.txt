**Title: The Nature of Attachment and the Path to Liberation Through Non-Self**

**Summary**  
Attachment arises from a mistaken belief in a permanent self and possessions, manifesting as craving for sensory pleasures, status, and spiritual achievements. This clinging binds the mind to conditioned experiences, sustaining suffering and the cycle of rebirth. Even spiritual attainments and the identity of being a "修行者" (practitioner) become subtle forms of attachment. The recognition of non-self directly dismantles this root misunderstanding, revealing that all forms of clinging—including conceit based on comparison—are obstacles to liberation.

**Attachment to Sensory and Material Desires Fuels Suffering**  
The pursuit of sensory enjoyment and material gain stems from the erroneous belief in a permanent "I" and "mine." This craving is not incidental but foundational to ongoing dissatisfaction, as it anchors the mind in impermanent conditions, perpetuating the cycle of birth and death.

**Spiritual Attainments Can Become New Forms of Binding**  
Attachment does not end with material desires; it extends to experiences such as meditative states, notions of liberation, or the identity of being a spiritual practitioner. These are not escapes from clinging but refined expressions of it, masking the continuous reinforcement of self-identification.

**Conceit Arises from Comparison and Reinforces Self-View**  
A subtle yet persistent form of attachment is conceit, rooted in comparing oneself to others—whether as superior, equal, or inferior. It is not merely pride but an underlying clinging to a sense of self, and thus remains an affliction even alongside spiritual progress.

**Non-Self Is the Direct Antidote to All Forms of Clinging**  
The understanding that "I" and "mine" are illusions dismantles the very basis of attachment. This recognition is not theoretical but practical—it removes the foundation upon which all craving, including spiritual grasping and conceit, depends. Without this wisdom, even the most advanced practices remain entangled in conditioned existence.
