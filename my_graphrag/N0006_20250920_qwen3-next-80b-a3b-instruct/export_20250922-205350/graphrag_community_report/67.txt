Title: Stillness, Clarity, and Present-Moment Awareness as the Path to Liberation

Summary:  
Liberation is achieved not through future attainment but through continuous, undisturbed awareness in the present moment. This awareness, cultivated through meditation, ethical conduct, and mindful observation of breath, sensation, and movement, leads to mental stillness. Stillness, free from craving and aversion, enables clarity — a direct, non-judgmental perception of reality — which transforms understanding and culminates in unwavering inner peace.

—

Stillness arises from the purification of mind and ethical discipline  
Mental calm is not passive but the result of releasing attachment and aversion. Ethical behavior reduces inner turmoil and regret, creating the Conditions necessary for the mind to settle into quietude. This state is sustained through meditation and is the foundation upon which deeper awareness is built.

Clarity emerges only in the absence of mental distortion  
When the mind is still, it perceives experience without judgment, suppression, or indulgence. This direct, non-conceptual awareness of reality as it is — in breath, sensation, and movement — is not abstract but operational. It is this unfiltered perception that facilitates transformation.

Presence is the sole arena for awakening  
Awakening occurs exclusively in the now. Practices such as Chan Ding and mindful observation are effective only when fully engaged with the immediate experience of breathing, bodily sensation, or action. No future state, distant goal, or conceptual understanding replaces the necessity of presence.

Liberation is realized through unwavering stillness, not future attainment  
Freedom is not an endpoint to be reached but a condition lived continuously. Those who are free reside in a mind untouched by agitation, craving, or aversion. This condition is not mystical or external; it is the natural outcome of sustained, clear awareness in every moment of daily life.
