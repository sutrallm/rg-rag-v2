**Title: Chan Ding and the Path to Liberation from the Three Poisons**

**Summary:**  
Chan Ding is a disciplined meditative practice rooted in sustained mindfulness and diligent effort, designed to still the mind and reveal the true nature of reality. It directly counters the three root causes of suffering—craving, aversion, and delusion—by cultivating clarity, stability, and non-attachment. Through consistent daily practice, it enables the perception of impermanence and emptiness, dissolving the illusion of a fixed self and freeing the practitioner from the cycle of birth and rebirth. True mastery transcends attachment to the practice itself, embodying a non-dual awareness beyond effort and identity.

---

**Sustained Mindfulness and Diligent Effort Are Essential for Mental Stability**  
Chan Ding depends on continuous, focused attention and unwavering effort to prevent distraction and mental laxity. Without persistent daily discipline, the mind becomes unsettled, and meditative attainment fades. This steady cultivation is not occasional or superficial but requires vigilant observation and steadfast commitment to maintain inner clarity and suppress mental turbulence.

**The Three Poisons Fuel Samsara, and Chan Ding Uproots Them**  
Craving, hatred, and delusion are the foundational causes of suffering and the binding force of repeated birth and death. Chan Ding is not merely a calming technique but a direct method to suppress and ultimately eliminate these poisons. By stabilizing awareness, the practice undermines their influence, interrupting their role in perpetuating the cycle of samsara.

**Realization of Impermanence and Emptiness Leads to Non-Attachment**  
Through clear and stable observation in Chan Ding, one perceives the transient nature of all experiences. This directly weakens clinging. Deeper still, the practice reveals that phenomena lack inherent, independent existence—understanding that all is empty. This realization dissolves the basis for attachment, as nothing is found to be permanent or truly graspable, leading to genuine freedom.

**True Meditation Transcends the Concept of Practitioner and Practice**  
The highest form of Chan Ding moves beyond identifying as a “practitioner” or seeking specific meditative states. It is characterized by the absence of clinging to results, experiences, or the notion of self in action. This non-dual, non-efforting awareness emerges only when the illusion of a separate self has been seen through, aligning with the ultimate nature of reality.

**Inconsistent Practice Reinforces Samsara; Daily Discipline Is the Antidote**  
Failure to maintain regular, diligent practice allows negligence and ignorance to deepen, reinforcing the patterns of craving and delusion that drive rebirth. Only through unbroken, daily discipline can the mind remain grounded in clarity, prevent regression, and steadily uproot the forces that entangle beings in the cycle of suffering.
