**Buddhist Principles: Non-Self, Impermanence, and the Four Establishments of Mindfulness**  

**Executive Summary**  
This report outlines core Buddhist concepts emphasizing the absence of inherent self in all phenomena, the transient nature of existence, and the role of the Four Establishments of Mindfulness as a structured practice. It explains how observing these characteristics directly addresses suffering and attachment, leading to liberation through systematic mindfulness cultivation.  

**Universal Absence of Inherent Self**  
All phenomena lack a permanent, self-sufficient identity. This foundational teaching, cultivated through the Four Establishments of Mindfulness, dissolves clinging to fixed notions of self and is essential for liberation from suffering.  

**Transient Nature Driving Non-Attachment**  
Recognizing the impermanent and ever-changing quality of all observable events and experiences results in non-attachment. This realization, central to the Impermanence View, eliminates reliance on transient conditions.  

**Four Establishments as Systematic Observation Framework**  
This practice structures awareness around the body, feelings, mind, and phenomena to analyze their interdependent and unstable nature. It builds concentration while systematically revealing the true characteristics of existence.  

**Inherent Suffering from Impermanence**  
All phenomena inherently involve suffering due to their impermanent and unstable nature. Observing feelings as suffering is a key aspect of the Four Establishments, directly linking transience to dissatisfaction.  

**Interdependent Origin of Phenomena**  
Phenomena arise solely through mutually dependent conditions, confirming their emptiness of independent existence. This interdependence is critical to understanding reality within mindfulness practice.  

**Mindfulness Practice Leads to Liberation**  
Consistent dedication to the Four Establishments of Mindfulness culminates in nirvana. Diligent observation of impermanence and non-self erodes attachment, ending the cycle of suffering entirely.
