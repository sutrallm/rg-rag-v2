**Four Foundations of Mindfulness: Observing Body, Feelings, Mind, and Phenomena to Achieve Liberation**  

This Buddhist meditation technique systematically observes four key areas—body, feelings, mind, and phenomena—to correct mistaken perceptions and reduce attachment. It employs specific contemplations such as body impurity, inherent suffering in feelings, mind impermanence, and phenomena non-self. The practice cultivates awareness and understanding to overcome self-clinging and attain liberation.  

**Body Impurity Contemplation Reduces Physical Attachment**  
Observing the body’s unclean nature addresses craving for physical form and delusion.  

**Feeling Observation Highlights Suffering Nature**  
Sensations and emotions are examined to comprehend their impermanent and suffering characteristics, leading to release from pleasure attachment.  

**Mind Observation Focuses on Impermanence**  
Mind states are observed for their transient nature within mindfulness practice.  

**Phenomena Analysis Reveals Non-Self**  
Examining all phenomena demonstrates the absence of independent self.  

**Integrated Observation Develops Clear Comprehension**  
Continuous awareness of body, feelings, mind, and phenomena cultivates understanding and mindfulness.  

**Ego Attachment Release Through Non-Clinging Practice**  
Practitioners overcome self-identification by relinquishing clinging through disciplined observation of the four foundational areas.
