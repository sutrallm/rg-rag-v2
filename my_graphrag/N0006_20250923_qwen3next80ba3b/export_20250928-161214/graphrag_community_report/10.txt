**Suffering Origins and Liberation in Buddhist Teachings**  

Suffering arises from attachment, greed, impermanence, and mental afflictions. Its cessation is achieved through moral conduct, meditation, mindfulness, and wisdom. Nirvana represents the ultimate freedom from suffering, attained by transcending rebirth cycles and eliminating all delusions.  

**Primary Causes of Suffering**  
Attachment to impermanent phenomena is the root cause. Greed directly generates distress among sentient beings. Mental afflictions serve as the primary source of suffering within cyclic existence. Impermanence of all phenomena contributes fundamentally to suffering. Desire perpetuates craving, and craving is identified as the primary driver of dissatisfaction.  

**All Feelings Contribute to Suffering**  
Pleasant feelings are inherently part of suffering due to impermanence. Unpleasant feelings directly contribute through aversion and distress. Neutral feelings remain impermanent and conditioned, thus part of cyclic suffering. All sensory experiences, whether agreeable or disagreeable, are inseparable from dissatisfaction when perceived through clinging.  

**Conditioned Existence and Suffering**  
Conditioned phenomena and the five aggregates are inherently linked to suffering when clung to as self. Samsara is characterized as a sea of suffering. The cycle of birth and death is marked by pervasive suffering in all stages, with rebirth itself serving as the root cause.  

**Dependent Origination as Origin of Suffering**  
Suffering originates from the causal chain of dependent origination.  

**Path to End Suffering**  
Moral precepts lead to cessation of suffering. Meditation (samatha-vipassana) cultivates concentration and wisdom, fostering detachment from cravings. Abiding in stillness and non-attachment enable transcendence. Observing feelings as suffering is a core aspect of mindfulness practice. Tranquility supports the cessation of suffering.  

**Nirvana as Freedom from Suffering**  
Nirvana represents the complete transcendence of suffering through eliminating all afflictions and delusions. It is the ultimate goal of Buddhist practice.  

**Liberated Practitioners Transcend Suffering**  
Sages and saints fully transcend suffering through realizing reality’s true nature. They attain freedom by upholding precepts, wisdom, mindfulness, and spiritual practice. Reference to "超越痛苦" (transcending suffering) and "断尽烦恼，痛苦得以超越" (eliminating afflictions, suffering is overcome) affirms this state.
