**Buddhist Teachings on Consciousness, Aggregates, and Nirvana**  

Consciousness is recognized as a conditioned and illusory aspect within the five aggregates, arising dependently on sensory experiences. The text emphasizes the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth driven by karma, which is transcended through nirvana. Symbolic representations such as candle flame and wind illustrate external influences on consciousness instability, while deep ponds signify cultivated tranquility. Mindfulness practice enables observation of phenomena’s constant arising and ceasing, and clinging to consciousness is explicitly advised against.  

**Consciousness Component of the Five Aggregates**  
Consciousness functions as the awareness component within the five aggregates, processing sensory experience through dependence on senses and objects.  

**Conditioned and Illusory Nature**  
Consciousness arises dependent on conditions and is described as illusory like a magic trick.  

**Nirvana Transcends Cyclic Existence**  
Nirvana transcends the continuous cycle of birth, death, and rebirth driven by karma, freeing one from cyclic existence.  

**Avoiding Clinging to Consciousness**  
The Buddha instructs to avoid clinging to consciousness as part of practice.  

**Symbolic Representations of Consciousness States**  
Candle flame symbolizes unstable consciousness influenced by external forces; wind represents these forces, while strong wind causes the flame to sway. Deep ponds symbolize tranquility and clarity achieved through mental cultivation.  

**Observing Phenomenal Change Through Mindfulness**  
Consciousness observes each moment of arising and ceasing of phenomena during mindfulness practice.  

**External Environment Impact on Stability**  
External environment directly influences the stability of consciousness, as demonstrated by wind causing candle flame to sway.
