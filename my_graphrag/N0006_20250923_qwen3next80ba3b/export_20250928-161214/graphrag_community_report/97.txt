**Five Hindrances: Core Obstacles in Meditation Practice**  

**Summary**  
The Five Hindrances—greed, hatred, sloth, restlessness, and doubt—consistently obstruct meditation and spiritual progress. Restlessness disrupts concentration through agitation and wandering thoughts. Dullness and sleepiness impair mental alertness. Remorse, anger, and doubt further hinder practice by causing anguish, mental clouding, and uncertainty. Mindfulness and diligent effort are essential for addressing these barriers, ultimately enabling stable concentration.  

**Five Hindrances Composition**  
The Five Hindrances are defined as greed, hatred, sloth, restlessness, and doubt, forming the primary obstacles to meditation and spiritual progress.  

**Restlessness Characteristics**  
Restlessness manifests as agitation, anxiety, and excessive wandering thoughts, directly disrupting concentration and calm during meditation practice.  

**Dullness and Sleepiness**  
Dullness requires removal for effective meditation, while sleepiness is explicitly identified as one of the five hindrances obstructing practice. Both states impede mental alertness.  

**Remorse Component**  
Regret or guilt concerning past actions, termed remorse, constitutes part of the fourth hindrance (掉悔) and impedes meditation through anguish and distraction.  

**Anger's Role**  
Anger obstructs mental clarity and is cited as one of the Five Hindrances affecting focus and stability during meditation.  

**Sloth Impact**  
Sloth directly affects focus and energy levels during meditation practice, as established within the Five Hindrances framework.  

**Doubt Disruption**  
Doubt undermines the meditation process through uncertainty and skepticism regarding teachings or practice, creating persistent obstacles.  

**Overcoming Techniques**  
Mindfulness serves as a key method for addressing the Five Hindrances, while diligent effort is necessary for their complete eradication.  

**Concentration Result**  
Successful management of the Five Hindrances leads to stable concentration during meditation practice.
