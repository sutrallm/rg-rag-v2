**Conditioned Phenomena: Impermanence, Non-Self, and No Inherent Existence in Buddhism**  

All phenomena dependent on causes and conditions are characterized by impermanence, non-self, and no inherent existence. These traits define the true nature of reality in Buddhist teachings. Conditioned phenomena inherently produce suffering due to their transience, arise through dependent origination, and manifest as illusory and transient. Direct perception of this reality, free from distortion, is essential for understanding.  

**Impermanence as a Constant Process**  
All conditioned phenomena constantly arise and cease moment by moment, described as rapid and continuous change in Buddhist teachings. This attribute is fundamental to their existence.  

**Non-Self as an Inherent Quality**  
Non-self is an inherent aspect of the true nature of phenomena, indicating the absence of an independent self or essence in all conditioned experiences.  

**No Inherent Existence**  
Conditioned phenomena lack inherent or independent existence, a fundamental aspect of their true nature requiring direct realization for understanding reality.  

**Suffering as Inherent in Conditioned Existence**  
Suffering arises from the impermanent nature of all conditioned phenomena, manifesting as an inseparable element of their existence.  

**Dependent Origination Governing Manifestation**  
Conditioned phenomena arise through dependent origination, where each phenomenon depends on specific causes and conditions for its existence.  

**Illusory Nature of Phenomena**  
Conditioned phenomena are transient and unreal, metaphorically compared to dreams or bubbles to illustrate their illusory and impermanent character.  

**Direct Perception of Reality**  
True understanding emerges from directly perceiving conditioned phenomena without distortion, cultivated through mindfulness and wisdom.  

**Pleasure as a Conditioned Phenomenon**  
Pleasure is one instance of conditioned phenomena, subject to impermanence and suffering like all other conditioned experiences.
