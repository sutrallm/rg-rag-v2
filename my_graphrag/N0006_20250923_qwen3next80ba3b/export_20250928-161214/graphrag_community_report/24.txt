**Five Aggregates: Illusion, Magic, and Banana Plant Metaphors**  

Five aggregates—form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—constitute sentient beings trapped in samsara. Described through metaphors of illusion and magic, they represent deceptive perceptions leading to attachment and suffering. The banana plant metaphor illustrates emptiness, as peeling reveals no core. Impermanence is a defining trait, and wisdom dispels illusions by revealing the true nature of phenomena.  

**Illusion as Metaphor for Aggregates**  
The five aggregates are depicted as illusory, reflecting false conceptions of reality that fuel attachment and suffering.  

**Magical Nature of the Aggregates**  
The aggregates are likened to magic, highlighting their deceptive and non-substantial character.  

**Banana Plant Metaphor Reveals Emptiness**  
Peeling a banana plant shows no core, symbolizing the emptiness of the five aggregates and their lack of inherent existence.  

**Impermanence Defines Aggregates**  
The aggregates are transient, with mental formations specifically observed for impermanence, which is critical to understanding suffering’s origin.  

**Formations Sustain Samsaric Cycle**  
Mental formations, as conditioned phenomena, perpetuate samsara, and clinging to them is advised against to break this cycle.  

**Wisdom Dispels Illusion**  
Realizing the true nature of phenomena through wisdom eradicates contradictions created by the aggregates, reducing suffering.
