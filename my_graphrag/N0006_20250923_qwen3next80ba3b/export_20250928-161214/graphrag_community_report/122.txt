**Buddhist Pathway to Liberation: Wisdom, Impermanence, and Non-Self**  

**Summary**  
This report outlines the foundational principles of Buddhist practice aimed at achieving liberation from suffering. Central to this path is wisdom, which dispels ignorance by revealing reality’s true nature. Impermanence—the transient quality of all phenomena—serves as the critical basis for cultivating non-attachment and dismantling clinging to transient phenomena. Non-self, the absence of an inherent, permanent identity in all existence, directly undermines ego-based attachment. Attachment to impermanent objects, experiences, or states is identified as the root cause of suffering, perpetuating the cycle of rebirth. The threefold training in moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom provides the structured framework for spiritual progress, while mindfulness practices systematically observed through the Four Establishments of Mindfulness deepen understanding of conditioned existence. Together, these elements enable the cessation of suffering and transcendence of cyclic existence.  

**Role of Wisdom in Dispelling Ignorance**  
Wisdom dispels fundamental delusion by revealing ultimate truth, acting as a lamp that pierces darkness. It is cultivated through study, meditation, and reflection, enabling the eradication of afflictions such as greed and hatred. This profound insight dismantles misconceptions about permanence and self, directly facilitating liberation from samsara and suffering.  

**Impermanence as the Foundation for Liberation**  
All conditioned phenomena are transient and subject to constant change, forming the basis for developing non-attachment. Observing impermanence prevents clinging to fleeting experiences, reduces suffering, and dismantles the illusion of permanence. This understanding serves as the key to transcending cyclic existence and achieving spiritual freedom.  

**Attachment as the Root Cause of Suffering**  
Clinging to impermanent phenomena—mistakenly perceived as stable—directly generates suffering. This attachment perpetuates rebirth and binds beings to samsara, serving as the primary obstacle to liberation. Abandoning attachment through wisdom is essential for ending distress and attaining freedom.  

**Threefold Training for Progressive Development**  
The integration of moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom forms the essential path to enlightenment. Ethical precepts create purity of conduct, concentration stabilizes the mind, and wisdom realizes ultimate truth. Together, these components systematically eliminate afflictions and support the cessation of suffering.  

**Non-Self Understanding as the Path to Non-Attachment**  
Recognizing the absence of an inherent, unchanging self in all phenomena dismantles ego-based clinging. This insight, cultivated through mindfulness practices, reveals the interdependent nature of existence and directly leads to freedom from attachment and mental bondage.  

**Dependent Origination as the Causal Basis of Phenomena**  
All phenomena arise from interdependent causes and conditions, illustrating their emptiness of inherent existence. This doctrine explains the chain of afflictions and suffering, demonstrating that phenomena lack self-sufficient nature. Understanding this principle is pivotal for dissolving false perceptions and achieving liberation.  

**Mental Liberation Through Non-Attachment**  
Freedom from attachments, afflictions, and delusions is achieved by refraining from clinging to transient states or outcomes of practice. This mental state—characterized by inner peace and clarity—transcends even spiritual attainments, ensuring unobstructed progress toward complete freedom.  

**Four Establishments of Mindfulness for Cultivating Insight**  
Focused observation of the body, feelings, mind, and phenomena develops clear awareness of impermanence and non-self. This systematic practice reveals the unsatisfactory nature of conditioned existence, directly supporting the cultivation of wisdom and the cessation of suffering.
