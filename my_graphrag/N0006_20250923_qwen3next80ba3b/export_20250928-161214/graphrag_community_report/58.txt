**Meditation and Afflictions: Pathways to Inner Peace in Buddhist Practice**  

**Summary**  
Meditation is a core Buddhist practice that overcomes mental defilements known as afflictions—such as greed, hatred, delusion, anger, and sloth—to achieve inner peace, tranquility, and liberation from suffering. Afflictions root suffering and cyclic existence (samsara), with ignorance as their primary cause. Meditation addresses these through ethical precepts, focused attention, mindfulness, and diligence, as symbolized by a lotus in mud or a tamed elephant. The Threefold Training and Four Foundations of Mindfulness form structured frameworks for dissolving afflictions, directly linking their eradication to Nirvana and freedom from rebirth.  

**Afflictions as the Root Source of Suffering**  
Afflictions are mental defilements, including the three root poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—and disturbances like anger, sloth, restlessness, doubt, and desire. They obscure the true nature of reality, increase due to clinging to phenomena, and perpetuate suffering within cyclic existence. Clinging to the Five Aggregates directly generates afflictions, described as "执著于五蕴假合的身心，如同水中捞月，徒增烦恼." Ignorance serves as the fundamental root cause of all mental afflictions, with their cessation required to transcend suffering and rebirth.  

**Meditation as the Principal Pathway to Inner Peace**  
Meditation cultivates inner peace through calming the mind, eliminating distractions, and stabilizing mental states. It achieves this via mindfulness, focused attention, and spiritual disciplines, as reflected in the Chinese phrase "获得内心安宁." This state is defined as profound calm, tranquility, and stability, free from emotional turmoil, afflictions, and agitation. Monks and practitioners engage in meditation as a core discipline to develop mindfulness, mental clarity, and resilience against external stimuli, directly linking the practice to inner freedom.  

**Integrated Methods for Affliction Reduction**  
Meditation’s efficacy relies on ethical precepts as an essential foundation for effective practice, concentration to remove mental dispersion, and mindfulness to dissolve afflictions through present-moment awareness. Diligent practice prevents laxity and heedlessness, while the Threefold Training integrates morality, concentration, and wisdom to systematically address afflictions. Specific techniques like the Four Foundations of Mindfulness completely eradicate afflictions when practiced consistently. Concentration eliminates greed and delusion through focused mental stability, and mindfulness sustains clarity to avoid afflictions taking root.  

**Liberation Through Affliction Eradication**  
The complete cessation of afflictions enables liberation from suffering and rebirth, as stated in "断尽烦恼，超越生死痛苦" and "断尽烦恼，证得究竟清净." Nirvana extinguishes all mental obstructions, symbolizing freedom from the cycle of existence. Practices like wisdom cultivation directly eradicate greed and hatred by revealing reality’s impermanent and non-self nature. Saints achieve complete freedom through profound insight, ethical conduct, and meditation, embodying inner peace and tranquility as a result of eradicated afflictions.  

**Symbolic Representations of Meditation States**  
Metaphors illustrate meditation’s transformative role: the lotus blooming unstained from mud represents maintaining purity amid challenges; elephant taming symbolizes controlling the distracted mind; pool water depicts a calm, undisturbed state; boiling water signifies scattered thoughts requiring taming; and the rare udambura flower underscores the difficulty of proper meditation practice. These symbols emphasize the necessity of overcoming negligence and external distractions to achieve mental clarity and tranquility.
