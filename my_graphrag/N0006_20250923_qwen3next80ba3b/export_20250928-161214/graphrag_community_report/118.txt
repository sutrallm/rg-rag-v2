**Four Foundations of Mindfulness: Realizing Non-Self through the Five Aggregates for Liberation**  

The Four Foundations of Mindfulness practice systematically observes body, feelings, mind, and phenomena to expose the impermanent and illusory nature of the five aggregates. Clinging to these aggregates—not form, feeling, perception, mental formations, or consciousness—is identified as the root cause of suffering and cyclic existence. Specific contemplation methods, such as body impurity and feeling analysis, directly counter craving and delusion. Realizing non-self, severing mental fetters, and cultivating dispassion enable liberation from suffering. Metaphors like pool water, banana plant, and candle flame illustrate mental clarity, aggregate impermanence, and consciousness instability.  

**Five Aggregates as Source of Suffering**  
The five aggregates—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—comprise temporary, impermanent components of existence. Clinging to them as a self generates inherent suffering, as they lack independent existence and are illusory.  

**Clinging Generates Afflictions**  
Attachment to phenomena, particularly the five aggregates and sensory objects, creates greed and afflictions. This craving perpetuates samsara, necessitating abandonment for liberation.  

**Body Impurity Contemplation Overcomes Craving**  
Observing the body’s unclean and decaying nature counters physical attachment and lust, addressing delusion through mindfulness of physical impermanence.  

**Feeling Observation Reveals Suffering’s Nature**  
Mindful attention to sensory experiences and emotions highlights their transient and distressing characteristics, facilitating release from pleasure-seeking.  

**Non-Self Realization Ends Delusion**  
Understanding that all phenomena lack inherent existence dismantles ego-centered views, breaking delusion and enabling liberation.  

**Mental Fetters Must Be Severed**  
The bonds binding beings to cyclic existence and suffering require cutting through mindful practice, leading to ultimate freedom.  

**Pool Water Symbolizes Mental Clarity**  
A calm, undisturbed pool reflects the state of mind achieved through meditation, accurately perceiving reality without distortion.  

**Banana Plant Illustrates Aggregate Impermanence**  
The banana plant’s coreless structure serves as a metaphor for the five aggregates’ transient and empty nature.  

**Candle Flame Depicts Consciousness Instability**  
A candle flame swaying in wind represents consciousness’s reliance on conditions and its unstable, ephemeral character.  

**Middle Way Avoids Extremes**  
Steering clear of sensual indulgence and self-mortification, the Middle Way balances practices to prevent destructive clinging.  

**Dispassion Enables Liberation**  
Non-attachment to sensory experiences and phenomena is essential for achieving liberation from suffering.  

**Impermanence and Non-Self Are Interdependent**  
Impermanence and selflessness are interconnected realities describing the insubstantial nature of all phenomena, foundational to mindful observation.
