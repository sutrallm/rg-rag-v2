**Threefold Emptiness in Buddhist Charity: Merit, Ethics, and Non-Attachment Practices**  

Charitable giving in Buddhist practice is guided by the Threefold Emptiness framework, where giver, receiver, and donation object lack inherent existence. This practice, when performed without attachment, accumulates merit alongside ethical precept observance. Wisdom underpins this understanding, enabling transcendence of dualistic thinking and alignment with ultimate reality beyond conceptual constructs.  

**Elements of Charity in Threefold Emptiness**  
The giver, receiver, and donated object each lack inherent existence and form the interdependent components of the Threefold framework in charitable acts.  

**Wisdom Enables Non-Attachment**  
Wisdom underpins the Threefold Emptiness perspective, allowing practitioners to transcend attachment to the act of giving, its results, and the involved parties.  

**Merit Accumulation through Charity and Precepts**  
Both charitable acts and adherence to ethical precepts serve as foundational disciplines that accumulate merit and purify karma through virtuous conduct.  

**Realizing Ultimate Reality Beyond Concepts**  
Transcending attachment to phenomena and actions reveals ultimate reality, aligning with Dharma-Nature beyond all conceptual constructs.  

**Recipient and Donation Object Lack Inherent Existence**  
The recipient is understood as impermanent and without self-nature, while the donated item is devoid of intrinsic reality during charitable acts.
