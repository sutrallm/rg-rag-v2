**Title: The Four Dhyānas: Progression from Desire to Equanimity**

**Summary**
The text describes the Four Dhyānas as a sequential meditative practice, beginning with the First Dhyāna and culminating in the Fourth. This progression is defined by the gradual relinquishment of pleasurable sensations, such as joy and bliss, ultimately leading to a state of pure equanimity and mindfulness.

**The Sequential Nature of the Four Dhyānas**
The Four Dhyānas represent a structured path of meditative concentration. This practice is not a single state but a sequence of stages, each building upon the previous one. The First Dhyāna is explicitly identified as the foundational starting point of this entire progression.

**Progressive Relinquishment of Pleasurable Sensations**
The core process within the Four Dhyānas is the systematic abandonment of specific mental and sensory experiences. The practice involves moving through the stages by gradually letting go of feelings of joy and bliss. This shedding of positive sensations is central to advancing through each stage.

**The Culmination in Equanimity and Purity**
The ultimate objective of progressing through the Four Dhyānas is to achieve a state characterized by purity of both mindfulness and equanimity. The final stage, the Fourth Dhyāna, is marked by the complete transcendence of all prior sensations, resulting in a balanced and clear state of awareness.
