**Title: Celestial Regard for Spiritual Attainment**

**Summary:**
This report details the relationship between celestial beings (天众) and spiritual practitioners, specifically focusing on the conditions that elicit reverence from these beings. Celestial beings, acting as protectors of the Buddhist doctrine, hold profound respect for practitioners who embody specific virtues. The highest level of veneration is reserved for Saints (圣者), individuals who have achieved complete liberation and possess flawless virtue. A key distinction is made between merely possessing merit and the wisdom to remain unattached to it, with the latter being a critical component of the most esteemed spiritual achievements.

**The Basis for Celestial Veneration**
Celestial beings express respect and admiration for practitioners based on their spiritual development. They specifically honor those who have cultivated moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom. The primary objects of their reverence are the virtues, accomplishments, and merits attained through dedicated practice.

**The Supreme Regard for Liberated Saints**
Saints command the highest level of celestial respect. They are defined as enlightened beings who have completely eradicated all afflictions and perfectly embody every form of merit. Their flawless virtue and spiritual accomplishments, particularly their transcendence over all suffering and bondage, are what celestial beings hold in the highest esteem. In response to this profound respect, celestial beings offer their protection and support to these Saints.

**The Critical Role of Non-Attachment to Merit**
A practitioner's relationship to their own merit is a crucial factor. True spiritual accomplishment involves not being bound or limited by one's attainments. A practitioner with wisdom, even when possessing significant merit, does not cling to or dwell upon their spiritual states. This wisdom actively prevents the practitioner from becoming ensnared by their own功德 (merit/virtue), distinguishing a liberated state from mere possession of power.
