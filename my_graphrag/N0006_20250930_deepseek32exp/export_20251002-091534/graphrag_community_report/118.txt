**Report on the Bodhisattva Path and its Practice**

**Summary**

The Bodhisattva Path is a Mahayana Buddhist practice defined by the cultivation of wisdom and compassion. Its practitioners, having realized emptiness, voluntarily postpone their own nirvana to work for the welfare of all sentient beings. The path is fundamentally based on prajna wisdom and great compassion, and its practice is methodically carried out through the six paramitas, which include generosity, moral discipline, and patience.

**Key Characteristics of the Path**

**The Dual Foundation of Wisdom and Compassion**
The path is established on two core principles: prajna wisdom as its fundamental basis and great compassion as its motivating force. The realization of emptiness is the prerequisite for generating this great compassion.

**The Voluntary Postponement of Nirvana**
A defining action of a practitioner is the voluntary decision to delay their own entry into nirvana. This choice is made specifically to continue working for the benefit and welfare of all sentient beings.

**Systematic Practice Through the Six Paramitas**
The path is extensively practiced through a set of six perfections, or paramitas. These are the skillful means through which a practitioner cultivates the necessary qualities. The paramitas explicitly mentioned are generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom.

**The Goal of Achieving Unsurpassed Enlightenment**
The ultimate objective for a practitioner is the attainment of unsurpassed bodhi, or perfect enlightenment. This final achievement is described as the culmination of perfecting both merit and wisdom.
