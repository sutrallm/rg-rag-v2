**Title: Stages of Buddhist Enlightenment and Non-Attachment**

**Summary:**
This report details the progressive stages of spiritual attainment in Buddhist practice, known as the "fruits" or "stages of enlightenment." The path begins with the Stream-enterer, who severs three specific fetters, and progresses through subsequent stages where greed and hatred diminish, and sensual desires are overcome, culminating in complete enlightenment. A fundamental principle governing all stages is that an accomplished practitioner does not develop attachment or clinging to any spiritual achievement, including these stages themselves.

**The Four Progressive Stages of Spiritual Attainment**
The path to complete enlightenment consists of four distinct stages. The first stage is that of the Stream-enterer (Srotāpanna or 須陀洹), defined by the elimination of the first three fetters. The second stage is characterized by a significant diminishing of greed and hatred. In the third stage, the practitioner achieves freedom from sensual desires and attains a state of purity. The fourth and final stage represents the culmination of the path, involving the complete elimination of all mental defilements.

**The Defining Achievement of the Stream-enterer**
The initial stage of enlightenment, known as Stream-entry, is specifically marked by the severing of three foundational fetters (Sanyojana). This act of cutting off these three bonds is what defines a practitioner as a Stream-enterer and constitutes the entry point into the stages of spiritual attainment.

**The Principle of Non-Attachment to Spiritual Achievements**
A consistent and critical theme across all levels of spiritual progress is the absence of attachment. Practitioners who attain any stage, or "fruit," of enlightenment do not develop clinging, obsession, or dependency upon these achievements. Even after attaining liberation or a purified mind, the practitioner continues to cultivate their path without being bound or hindered by the states they have realized.
