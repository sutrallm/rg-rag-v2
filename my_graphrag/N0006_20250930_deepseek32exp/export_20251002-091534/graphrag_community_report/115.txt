**Buddhist Path to Liberation: Core Doctrines and Practices**

**Summary**

This report details the essential Buddhist doctrines and practices that constitute the path to liberation. The analysis reveals a systematic framework centered on the Four Foundations of Mindfulness (四念处) for purifying the mind, the principle of Dependent Origination (缘起法) for understanding reality, and the Middle Way (中道) as the guiding path. The progressive stages of spiritual attainment (果位), culminating in the state of an Arhat (阿罗汉), are achieved by eliminating erroneous views like Eternalism (常见) and Nihilism (断见), and by uprooting the fundamental delusion of self-view (我见). The ultimate goal is the direct realization of the True Nature of Phenomena (诸法实相) and the attainment of Nirvana.

**Core Doctrinal Framework**

**The Four Foundations of Mindfulness as a Systematic Practice**
The Four Foundations of Mindfulness constitute a systematic method for developing mindfulness, focused on contemplation of the body as impure, feelings as suffering, the mind as impermanent, and phenomena as non-self. This practice aims to purify the mind and body, eliminate deluded thoughts, and dismantle attachment to the self. It is practiced during all daily activities, enabling a practitioner to directly realize the Dharma-nature and understand the truth of the five aggregates being impermanent, suffering, and empty.

**Dependent Origination as the Causal Law of Existence**
Dependent Origination is the core teaching that all phenomena arise and cease due to causes and conditions, lacking an independent, permanent substance. It explains the causal chain of existence, from ignorance to old age and death, revealing the interdependent nature of all things. Direct comprehension of this principle naturally destroys all afflictions, transcends conceptual discrimination, and is the sole path leading to Nirvana. The specific content for observing Dependent Origination is the Twelve Links (十二因缘), and breaking any link in this chain can end the cycle of rebirth.

**The Middle Way as the Path Beyond Extremes**
The Middle Way is the ultimate practice path that transcends dualistic thinking. It avoids the extremes of indulgence in worldly pleasures and severe asceticism, as well as the metaphysical extremes of Eternalism and Nihilism. By following this balanced path, practitioners develop wisdom, attain eternal bliss, and penetrate the darkness of ignorance. A sage's speech and actions naturally accord with this principle, which is free from all false discriminations.

**Stages of Attainment and Key Realizations**

**Progressive Spiritual Attainment Through Four Stages**
Spiritual attainment is a progressive achievement, ranging from the initial stage of a Stream-enterer (須陀洹) to the final stage of complete awakening. A Stream-enterer severs the first three fetters. The path culminates in the fourth stage, that of an Arhat, who has permanently ended all defilements. Practitioners who attain these stages do not become attached to their achievements.

**The Arhat as the Fully Liberated State**
An Arhat is a sage who has completely eradicated greed, hatred, and delusion. Having reached the fourth and final stage of enlightenment, they have attained full liberation, their minds unobstructed and pure like space. They have realized the Patience with the Non-arising of Phenomena (无生法忍), and upon the death of the body, they undergo no further rebirth, abiding in the supreme peace of Nirvana.

**Eradication of Self-View as a Prerequisite for Liberation**
The view of a self is a fundamental affliction, an illusion of "I" and "mine" that obstructs liberation. To realize any spiritual attainment, this view must be completely severed. Its utter destruction is described as cutting down the root of birth and death like a reed.

**Essential Practices for Cultivating Wisdom**

**The Seven Factors of Enlightenment Leading to Nirvana**
The Seven Factors of Enlightenment are seven elements of practice that lead to awakening. Their full development results in the severance of all fetters and the realization of the tranquil Nirvana.

**Establishing the Middle Way View by Abandoning Extremes**
The correct Middle Way view is established by eliminating the two extreme views of Eternalism and Nihilism. This view arises from the practice of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness and from insight into the impermanent nature of the five aggregates. It naturally overcomes the two extremes.

**Realizing the True Nature of Phenomena and Equality**
The True Nature of Phenomena is the ultimate reality that transcends all dualistic concepts and is directly perceived by a mind in meditative concentration. It is the reality with which a fully awakened being is naturally in accord. The principle of the Equality of Dharma-nature states that all sentient beings possess the potential for awakening, which is obscured only by ignorance and deluded thoughts. One who thoroughly comprehends Dependent Origination understands this ultimate reality.

**The Ultimate Goal: Nirvana and Unshakable Liberation**

**Nirvana as the Final Goal of the Path**
Nirvana is the ultimate goal, the state of complete liberation achieved by an Arhat. It is the unshakable state of freedom where all afflictions are utterly destroyed and no further rebirth occurs.

**The Unattached State of Ultimate Attainment**
The state of ultimate attainment is one of complete accomplishment where the root of ignorance is destroyed, all craving is extinguished, and all afflictions are annihilated. The mind of one in this state is boundless like space, unattached to any phenomenal signs, yet naturally in accord with the True Nature of Phenomena. Such a being possesses great wisdom and power, is completely free in all situations, and teaches in accordance with the truth.
