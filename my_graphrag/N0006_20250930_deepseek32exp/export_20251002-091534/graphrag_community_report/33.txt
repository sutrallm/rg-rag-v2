**Title: The Path and Outcomes of Śamatha-Vipaśyanā Practice**

**Summary**
This report details the Buddhist practice of Śamatha-Vipaśyanā (Calm Abiding and Insight Meditation), a systematic method for achieving liberation. The practice integrates ethical discipline, concentration, and wisdom to stabilize the mind, subdue afflictions, and ultimately penetrate the true nature of reality. Through sustained practice, practitioners, including ordained monastics known as Bhikṣus, purify mental defilements, eradicate ignorance, and attain the ultimate goal of Nirvāṇa.

**The Dual Components of Śamatha-Vipaśyanā**
The practice is a unified method combining two elements: Śamatha (calm abiding or concentration) and Vipaśyanā (insight or wisdom). Śamatha involves focusing the mind to pacify distracting thoughts and achieve mental stability. Vipaśyanā employs this stabilized mind to gain penetrating insight into the true nature of phenomena.

**Systematic Progression from Stability to Insight**
The practice follows a sequential progression. It begins with the development of concentration through Śamatha, which quiets mental fluctuations and establishes a mind that is immovable. Once this foundation of calm is secured, the practitioner applies Vipaśyanā to examine and understand reality, moving from a superficial perception of phenomena to their ultimate essence.

**Integration with the Threefold Training**
This practice is a comprehensive method that incorporates the threefold training of ethical discipline (Śīla), concentration (Samādhi), and wisdom (Prajñā). It is through the systematic training in Śamatha-Vipaśyanā that the components of concentration and wisdom within this threefold path are specifically cultivated and developed.

**Purification of Mental Defilements and Ignorance**
A primary function of this practice is the purification of all mental contaminants. It subdues coarse afflictions, dismantles subtle attachments, and purifies habitual tendencies. Sustained practice leads to the complete eradication of beginningless ignorance, which is the fundamental cause of suffering.

**Penetration of Ultimate Reality**
The culmination of the practice is the direct realization of the fundamental characteristics of existence. With the power of insight developed through Vipaśyanā, the practitioner clearly sees the impermanent and conditioned nature of the five aggregates, understands that all phenomena are without a permanent self, and breaks through the darkness of ignorance to arrive at the ultimate truth.

**Attainment of Liberation and Nirvāṇa**
The ultimate objective is the attainment of complete liberation. By systematically practicing this method, practitioners sever all bonds of affliction, extinguish craving, and realize the supreme peace of Nirvāṇa. This state represents full awakening and final emancipation.

**The Role of the Bhikṣu**
A Bhikṣu is an ordained monastic who embodies this path. Such an individual possesses a sense of moral shame, maintains pure ethical conduct, understands the essence of the teachings, and diligently cultivates Śamatha-Vipaśyanā. Through the perfection of discipline and wisdom, a Bhikṣu progresses toward liberation, with their life naturally radiating peace and bliss.

**The Manifestation of Inherent Purity**
The practice enables the manifestation of the practitioner's inherently pure nature. As afflictions and habitual tendencies are purified through this systematic training, the mind's innate clarity and purity become apparent.
