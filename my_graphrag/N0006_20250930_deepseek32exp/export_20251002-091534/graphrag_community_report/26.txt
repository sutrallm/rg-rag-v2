**Title: The Role of Diligence in Spiritual Practice and Transcendence**

**Summary:**
The text establishes that unwavering diligence is the foundational attitude for spiritual progress. It is essential for mastering meditative concentration, overcoming negative states, and, most significantly, transcending the control of death. This principle, taught by the Buddha, is the root of liberation and is intrinsically linked to sustained effort.

**The Foundational Role of Diligence in Liberation**
Diligence is described as the fundamental root and foundation of liberation. It serves as the base of the path to liberation, functioning as a constant guard over the mind to prevent the intrusion of negative influences. This state of constant vigilance and sustained effort is a principle that must be consistently maintained by practitioners.

**Diligence as a Prerequisite for Mastering Meditative Concentration**
A direct and essential relationship exists between diligence and meditative concentration. True mastery of meditative methods and an understanding of its core essence and power can only be achieved through unwavering diligence. This alert and diligent state is necessary to maintain the practice, avoid distraction, and ultimately escape a state of negligence.

**Transcending the Control of Death Through Diligent Practice**
The consistent practice of diligence enables practitioners to avoid and ultimately transcend the domain and control of the ruler of death. By following the true Dharma with unremitting effort, practitioners can overcome the power of death. This transcendence is a direct outcome of the diligent and alert attitude taught by the Buddha.

**The Interdependence of Diligence and Sustained Effort**
Sustained effort finds its fundamental meaning and practical expression through the attitude of diligence. The two are mutually reinforcing methods in spiritual practice. The process of diligent practice requires a practitioner to continuously uphold this principle, as progress on the spiritual path is contingent upon its constant application.
