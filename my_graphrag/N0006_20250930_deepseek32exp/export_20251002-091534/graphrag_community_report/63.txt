**Report on the Nature of Reality and the Path to Liberation**

**Summary**

This report details the fundamental principles of reality, known as Dharmata, and the path to its realization. The core finding is that all phenomena are empty of independent existence, arising only through dependent origination. This emptiness is the true, equal nature of all things. All beings inherently possess the potential to realize this nature, but it is obscured by ignorance. Liberation is achieved through wisdom that understands these principles, leading to a state of non-constructed liberation and, for the Bodhisattva, the spontaneous arising of great compassion.

**The Ultimate Nature of Reality is Dharmata, Characterized by Emptiness and Equality**

Dharmata is the true nature of all phenomena, possessing an equal characteristic and being the essential reality beyond all dualistic oppositions. It is the true reality that is empty and equal. This nature is fundamentally described as the emptiness of dependently originated phenomena, meaning all things lack an independent, inherent existence and arise only through the combination of various conditional factors. This principle of dependent origination and emptiness is considered the core doctrine and the essential truth of all phenomena.

**All Sentient Beings Inherently Possess the Potential for Awakening**

The equal nature of Dharmata means that all sentient beings possess the Tathagata's wisdom, their inherent awakened nature. Every being has the potential to awaken to this本性 (original nature). However, this realization is obstructed by fundamental ignorance, which prevents beings from perceiving the truth of Dharmata's equality.

**Wisdom and Liberation Arise from Understanding Emptiness and Dependent Origination**

Wisdom is defined as the correct knowledge and vision that all phenomena are dependently originated and empty. Understanding dependent origination and emptiness breaks through fundamental ignorance. A practitioner who comprehends the Dharma can then attain the state of non-constructed liberation. In this state, their physical, verbal, and mental actions fully accord with emptiness, and the illusion of "I am the doer and experiencer" ceases. This realization also allows a practitioner to thoroughly see the empty nature of the five aggregates, which are essentially characterized by emptiness and lack inherent existence.

**The Bodhisattva Path is Founded on Emptiness and Great Compassion**

The practice of the Bodhisattva path is established on the foundation of realizing emptiness. Upon this realization, a practitioner spontaneously gives rise to great compassion. Therefore, emptiness is the basis for the cultivation of the Bodhisattva path.

**The Role of the Saint in Revealing Dharmata**

A saint is one who has thoroughly comprehended the Dharma and attained the state of non-constructed liberation. It is through the pure life of a saint that sentient beings are able to see the radiance of Dharmata.
