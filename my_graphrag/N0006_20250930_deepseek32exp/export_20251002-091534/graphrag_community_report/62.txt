**The Path and State of Liberation in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**
This report details the process and ultimate achievement of liberation as the final goal of spiritual practice. Liberation is a state of complete freedom from suffering, the cycle of birth and death, and all mental defilements. It is attained through a systematic path involving moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom, culminating in the direct realization of the nature of reality. A liberated being, or Saint, abides in a state of unshakable peace, free from attachment and karma.

**The Constituents of Liberation**
Liberation is the state of being utterly free from the bonds of mental afflictions and the cycle of rebirth. It is characterized by the complete destruction of greed, hatred, and delusion. In this state, no new karma is created, and the practitioner is no longer subject to the pull of future existences. The mind is unhindered and unobstructed, like space, abiding in great freedom within all phenomena.

**The Path of Practice**
A structured path of practice is essential for attaining liberation. The complete system of the Threefold Training—moral discipline, meditative concentration, and wisdom—forms the foundational path. Diligent effort is the fundamental root that enables practitioners to master this path, transcend worldly attachments, and sever the root of afflictions. The Noble Eightfold Path is the specific causal sequence that inevitably leads to this goal.

**The Role of Wisdom and Insight**
Wisdom is the crucial faculty that leads to liberation. It enables the practitioner to thoroughly understand the truths of impermanence, suffering, and non-self. This wisdom directly perceives the dependent origination of all phenomena, realizing that nothing exists independently. Through this insight, the practitioner abandons all clinging, cuts off the root of ignorance, and achieves mental freedom.

**The Object of Contemplation: The Five Aggregates**
Liberation is achieved by overcoming attachment to the five aggregates that constitute a being. These are form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness. The practitioner must observe their impermanent, unsatisfactory, and essenceless nature through sustained contemplation. The complete abandonment of clinging to these aggregates is a defining feature of liberation.

**The Supporting Practices**
Moral discipline provides the necessary foundation and protection for the journey. Meditative concentration leads to mental clarity and stability, allowing for the deep insight that results in freedom. Furthermore, the establishment of mindfulness is a direct path for realizing the true nature of reality.

**Characteristics of a Liberated Being**
A Saint who has attained liberation has eradicated all afflictions and dwells in the state of Nirvana, which is beyond suffering. Such a being is characterized by an unshakable, non-discriminating wisdom and maintains equanimity in all circumstances. They move through the world without being stained by it, unaffected by praise or blame, and are free from all dualistic concepts. Their past accumulated karma gradually dissolves, and they create no new karmic actions.

**Progressive Refinement on the Path**
Practitioners progress through stages. Beginners can subdue coarse afflictions, yet subtle attachments and latent tendencies may persist. These uneliminated tendencies can resurface and present obstacles. The state of disenchantment arises from the clear seeing of the aggregates' impermanence, which in turn leads toward the final goal.
