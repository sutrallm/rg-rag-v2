**Report on the Four Immeasurables and Equanimity in Buddhist Practice**

**Summary**
This report details the foundational relationship between the Four Immeasurables—loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity—and the state of equanimity in Buddhist practice. The cultivation of these qualities is essential for all practitioners, leading to unconditional compassion and the dissolution of self-other distinctions. Equanimity serves as the basis for the path to liberation, enabling individuals to remain undisturbed in all circumstances, akin to a lotus flower unsullied by the mud from which it grows.

**The Four Immeasurables as a Core Practice for Unconditional Compassion**
The Four Immeasurables are four vast, altruistic mental states to be cultivated by practitioners at all times: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity. Their practice is aimed at developing unconditional compassion. The cultivation of compassionate action is achieved through developing these four qualities.

**Equanimity as the Foundation for Liberation**
Equanimity is the foundation for the path to liberation. It is a mental state of treating all beings equally, without discrimination, similar to the earth supporting all things. It is characterized by a transcendent freedom beyond dualities, not rejecting suffering nor clinging to happiness.

**The Unshaken Mind of Liberated Beings**
For a saint who has ended all defilements and for a liberated practitioner, equanimity is the mental state maintained in all situations, favorable or adverse. Such individuals are unmoved by the "eight worldly winds" and remain unstained by worldly phenomena.

**The Symbolic Purity of Cultivated Equanimity**
A practitioner who maintains equanimity is compared to a lotus flower that grows from the mud yet remains unstained by it. This symbolizes the ability to remain pure and undisturbed amidst challenging circumstances.

**The Interdependence of Compassion and Equanimity**
The practice of compassion and loving-kindness requires the cultivation of equanimity towards all beings. This practice treats all beings equally and gradually dissolves the distinction between self and others. Compassionate action involves cultivating equanimity to achieve this equal treatment.
