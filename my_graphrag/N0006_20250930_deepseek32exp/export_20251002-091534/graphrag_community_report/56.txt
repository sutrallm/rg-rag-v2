**The Path to Nirvana: Practices, Obstacles, and Realization**

**Summary**
This report details the Buddhist path to Nirvana, the ultimate goal of spiritual practice representing complete liberation from suffering. The path is systematically achieved through the Threefold Training of precepts, concentration, and wisdom, primarily practiced via the dual method of Calm-Abiding and Insight. The fundamental obstacle is the Three Poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—which must be utterly extinguished. Practitioners, including monastics and enlightened beings, engage in methods like the Four Foundations of Mindfulness to purify the mind, realize the nature of reality, and ultimately attain Nirvana, a state of eternal peace beyond all conceptual duality and the cycle of birth and death.

**The Nature of Nirvana as Ultimate Liberation**
Nirvana is the final goal of Buddhist practice, representing the far shore of liberation and a state of complete release. It is characterized by the utter extinction of the fires of greed, hatred, and delusion, the permanent end of all afflictions, and the cessation of suffering, including birth, aging, and death. In this state, the mind achieves profound tranquility and coolness, transcending all conceptual discrimination, dualistic oppositions, and the cycle of rebirth. It is an ultimately secure state beyond time, space, and verbal description, which can only be known through personal realization. It is not annihilation nor a state of permanence, but an eternal, peaceful liberation that is the inherent, quiescent reality of all phenomena.

**Systematic Practice Through Calm-Abiding and Insight**
The path to Nirvana is systematically cultivated through the dual practice of Calm-Abiding and Insight. This practice begins with Calm-Abiding, which stabilizes the mind, pacifies distracting thoughts, and naturally subdues afflictions. Upon this foundation, Insight is applied to penetrate the superficial appearance of phenomena, contemplate the impermanence of the five aggregates, and realize the selfless nature of all things. This combined practice generates wisdom, severs fetters, purifies all defilements, dispels the darkness of ignorance, and ultimately leads to the direct realization of Nirvana. It is a core component of the Threefold Training and a central path to liberation.

**The Central Role of Extinguishing the Three Poisons**
The Three Poisons—greed, hatred, and delusion—are the fundamental afflictions that must be completely eradicated for Nirvana to be attained. They are the primary objects to be cut off and extinguished through practices such as the Four Foundations of Mindfulness. When these three fires are utterly and permanently quenched, the practitioner enters Nirvana. An enlightened being, having extinguished these poisons, possesses a mind that is pure, unstained, and no longer bound by karmic conditions or subject to future rebirth. The complete cessation of these three is therefore the defining mark and prerequisite for becoming a liberated saint.

**The Practitioner's Path and the Role of the Monastic**
The practitioner, or enlightened being, is one who, through spiritual practice, seeks to eradicate the Three Poisons and achieve ultimate liberation. A key representative of this path is the monastic, a renunciant who possesses a sense of moral shame, maintains pure ethical conduct, comprehends the essence of the teachings, and diligently practices Calm-Abiding and Insight. Through perfecting precepts and wisdom, such a practitioner's life naturally radiates bliss and light, culminating in the liberation of Nirvana.

**Essential Supporting Practices for Attainment**
Several specific practices are integral to the path. The Four Foundations of Mindfulness, involving sustained mindful observation of bodily, feeling, and mental processes, cuts off the flow of the Three Poisons, purifies consciousness, and leads to the far shore of Nirvana. The Noble Eightfold Path, when fully implemented, purifies mental impurities, exhausts suffering, and ultimately results in the serene liberation of Nirvana. Furthermore, the cultivation of non-outflow wisdom and the direct realization of dependent origination are the exclusive paths leading to this ultimate state.

**The State of the Liberated Being**
A saint is one who has realized the realm of Nirvana. Such a being has completely severed all afflictive bonds, permanently extinguished the Three Poisons, and cut the fetters of birth and death. Their mind, steadfast as Mount Sumeru, is no longer bound by karmic conditions and has transcended all dualistic concepts like good and evil. While having reached the far shore of Nirvana, the fully enlightened being does not cling to it, abiding in the Middle Way, free from all attachments and abiding in the wisdom of non-acquisition.
