**Report on the Path to Liberation and Key Spiritual Practices**

**Summary**

This report details the characteristics of a fully liberated being and the essential practices required to achieve that state. A liberated being is a sage who has transcended the cycle of birth and death, eradicated all afflictions, and attained a state of permanent peace and wisdom. The path to this liberation centers on guarding the six sense faculties, maintaining clear comprehension in all activities, and renouncing sensory desires. These practices enable a practitioner to perceive the empty and impermanent nature of all phenomena, cease the creation of new karma, and ultimately reach the shore beyond suffering.

**Characteristics of a Liberated Being**

A liberated being is a fully enlightened sage who has transcended the duality of good and evil, eradicated ignorance, and attained the light of wisdom. They have extinguished the fires of greed, hatred, and delusion, destroyed all craving, and no longer create new karmic actions, while their old karma gradually dissolves. Having transcended the cycle of birth and death, they have a complete insight into past lives, know that afflictions are forever exhausted, and have reached the other shore of eternal peace. They possess the three knowledges and six supernormal powers, clearly perceive the falseness of the five aggregates, and do not generate greed or attachment in any situation. Though they move in the world, they are undefiled by it, like a lotus flower emerging unstained from mud. Their minds are unbound, free from all clinging, and remain unmoved in both favorable and adverse circumstances. Their speech and actions naturally accord with the Middle Way. They relinquish attachment in all situations, neither indulging in sensual pleasures nor abiding in meditative absorptions, and they become a field of merit for the world.

**The Centrality of Guarding the Six Sense Faculties**

The six sense faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind—are the fundamental gateways through which beings perceive the world and where distractions and afflictions can invade. Practitioners must guard, restrain, and control these faculties with constant mindfulness and awareness, similar to guarding a city gate or taming a wild horse. This prevents the arising of greed and aversion and avoids the accumulation of new karma. When these faculties contact external objects, practitioners should maintain mindful observation, perceiving the process with equanimity and right knowledge without grasping onto pleasant or unpleasant sensations. By observing the functioning of these faculties with mindfulness, one can perceive their empty and impermanent nature, ultimately severing the stream of birth and death. A sage fully perceives their function but does not cling to the objects of sensation.

**The Role of Clear Comprehension in Spiritual Practice**

Clear comprehension is a core element of practice that must be maintained continuously alongside mindfulness. It is a state of clear understanding and correct awareness that stabilizes the mind in right understanding. In meditation, it is an important quality that, combined with concentration, helps practitioners escape suffering. In practical terms, it must be maintained in bodily actions, speech, and mental activity. It is used to observe the arising and passing away of physical and mental phenomena and is a method to cut off the causes for afflictions to invade, achieving complete protection of the mind. It must be maintained in all daily postures and activities, including walking, standing, sitting, lying down, eating, and dressing. When the sense faculties contact external objects, practitioners should maintain this state of awareness without generating greed. Maintaining it in all postures and activities prevents afflictions from arising.

**The Necessity of Renouncing Sensory Desires**

Renunciation is the practice of gradually abandoning greed for sensual pleasures to achieve a state of pure equanimity. It is a sequential practice from the first to the fourth meditative absorption, which includes withdrawing from worldly affairs, relinquishing attachment to the body, and eliminating self-grasping. It means giving up craving for sensual pleasures as thoroughly as escaping a poisonous snake. By destroying the root of afflictions, one achieves undefiled liberation. This practice requires maintaining an attitude of non-greed and non-attachment towards the objects of the six senses, training so that sensory desires gradually become faint, like water droplets on a lotus leaf. It is liberation from desires and sensory clinging, achieved by subduing the senses and preventing the accumulation of new karma, enabling a practitioner to be unstained like a lotus from the mud.

**The Nature of Sensory Objects and External Conditions**

The six sensory objects are forms, sounds, smells, tastes, tactile sensations, and mental objects. They are the external realms corresponding to the six sense faculties. Practitioners should not cling to these objects and must maintain an attitude of non-greed and non-attachment towards them to free themselves from the bondage of craving and aversion. Liberated beings remain undefiled and pure within these realms. Practitioners must thoroughly know their illusory nature and contemplate them as empty. External conditions are the objects contacted by the sense faculties. Through mindfulness training, practitioners learn not to be swayed by these external conditions. When facing them, practitioners do not grasp onto distinctions of good and bad. Through mindful observation of the contact process, one can recognize the impermanent nature of feelings. These conditions can also be objects that the mind may chase after; if indulged, this leads to the proliferation of desires.
