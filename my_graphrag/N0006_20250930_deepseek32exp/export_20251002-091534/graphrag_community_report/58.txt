**Title: The Path to Spiritual Attainment: Emptiness and the Four Stages**

**Summary**

This report details the progressive stages of spiritual attainment, known as 证果, culminating in the state of Arahant. The path is defined by the perfection of morality, concentration, and wisdom, and is fundamentally characterized by the state of 三轮体空—an emptiness of subject, object, and action. A key outcome of this attainment is the ability to remain unstained within the cycle of existence without developing attachment to the state of liberation itself.

**The Progressive Stages of Spiritual Accomplishment**

Spiritual attainment, or 证果, is a gradual process through four distinct stages: Stream-enterer, Once-returner, Non-returner, and Arahant. This progression represents a sequential liberation from afflictions, with Arahant being the final state of accomplishment.

**The Foundational Role of Morality, Concentration, and Wisdom**

The state of 证果 is achieved by perfecting three essential practices: 戒律 (morality), 禅定 (concentration), and 智慧 (wisdom). These are not isolated achievements but are integral and complementary components required for reaching enlightenment.

**The Centrality of Emptiness in Practice and Attainment**

The core characteristic of 证果 is the experience of 三轮体空, an emptiness concerning the three spheres of subject, object, and action. This state involves neither creating nor seeking, reflecting a profound understanding of non-attachment where the doer, the deed, and the recipient are all perceived as empty.

**Non-Attachment to Liberation**

A defining feature of those who attain 证果 is their relationship with 涅槃 (nirvana). They do not dwell in or become attached to nirvana even when attaining it. This allows them to remain unstained while continuing to exist within the cycle of existence, embodying liberation without fixation.

**The Application of Emptiness in Cultivation**

The principle of 三轮体空 is not only a characteristic of the final attainment but is also the method for practice. Cultivators are to use this empty contemplation to engage in and perfect all wholesome practices, thereby integrating the realization of emptiness directly into their spiritual development.
