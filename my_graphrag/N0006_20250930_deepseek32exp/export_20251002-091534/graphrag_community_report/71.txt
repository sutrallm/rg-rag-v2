**Report on Buddhist Meditation Practice and Its Effects**

**Summary**

This report details the core principles and outcomes of Buddhist meditation practice, known as 禅定. The practice is a central component of the threefold training of moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom. Its primary function is to overcome mental hindrances such as negligence, distraction, and afflictions, leading to states of inner peace, clarity, and liberation. The cultivation of mindfulness and sustained diligent effort are essential for mastering this practice and achieving its ultimate goal: the complete cessation of suffering through wisdom and the liberation of the mind.

**Core Components of the Threefold Training**

The spiritual path is structured around the threefold training of moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom. Moral discipline establishes the ethical foundation necessary for developing concentration. Concentration, achieved through meditation, serves as the intermediate stage that stabilizes the mind and prepares it for the emergence of wisdom. Wisdom is the final component that severs the roots of afflictions, leading to liberation. These three elements form an interdependent and sequential system for complete spiritual development.

**Mechanism of Mental Purification**

Meditation practice functions by focusing the mind on a single point while maintaining mindfulness and correct awareness. This process counteracts states of mental distraction, negligence, and sensory indulgence. A concentrated mind becomes stable, clear, and free from disturbance, akin to a calm body of water that reflects things accurately. This mental stability allows for the natural subsiding of afflictions and the subsequent manifestation of inherent wisdom, enabling clear insight into the true nature of phenomena.

**Overcoming Mental Hindrances**

The practice specifically targets and overcomes negative mental states that obstruct spiritual progress. These include afflictions such as greed, hatred, and delusion, as well as secondary states like negligence and distraction. Through consistent meditation, practitioners can eliminate these hindrances, achieve inner purity, and free the mind from their binding influence. The diligent guarding of the mind is crucial to prevent the intrusion of these disruptive states.

**Cultivation of Mindfulness and Diligence**

Sustained mindfulness and diligent effort are indispensable for success in meditation. Mindfulness protects the mind from afflictions and negligence, while diligence ensures continuous and vigilant practice. This combination allows practitioners to truly master the method of concentration, overcome habitual tendencies of carelessness, and maintain the state of collectedness necessary for deep insight and spiritual advancement.

**Achievement of Liberated States**

The ultimate outcomes of this practice are liberation and the attainment of Nirvana. Liberation is characterized by the complete cessation and extinction of all afflictions. A fully liberated being, a Saint, has eradicated every root of suffering and is freed from the cycle of birth and death. The mind of such a practitioner is liberated from attachments, afflictions, and defilements, achieving a state of ultimate peace, stability, and unshakable freedom.
