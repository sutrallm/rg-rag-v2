**Report on the Path to Mental Liberation through Contemplative Practice**

**Summary**

This report details the process and outcomes of achieving mental liberation (心解脱) through dedicated meditative practice. The core findings indicate that liberation is a state of mind attained by practitioners through meditation (禅修) and concentration (禅定), leading to profound wisdom (智慧). This wisdom enables the direct and thorough observation of the world's impermanent nature (世间无常) and the phenomena of arising and ceasing (世间生灭). A liberated mind is characterized by the complete absence of attachment (执著) to any state, achievement, or conditioned phenomenon (有為法), resulting in ultimate freedom and stability.

**The Role of Meditative Practice in Achieving Liberation**

Mental liberation is a state of spiritual freedom and fulfillment achieved by practitioners through meditative practice, specifically through the cultivation of concentration. This practice is the foundational method and pathway for attaining liberation. The key outcome of this practice is the liberation of the mind from attachments, afflictions, and defilements.

**Wisdom and Liberation as an Interdependent Process**

Wisdom and mental liberation are mutually reinforcing. Wisdom, developed through practice, enables the attainment of liberation by providing insight into the impermanent and non-self nature of all existence. Conversely, the state of liberation itself embodies and further cultivates this wisdom. This wisdom specifically involves understanding the impermanent characteristics of all conditioned phenomena and observing the constant arising and ceasing of mental and physical phenomena.

**Direct Observation of Impermanence and Arising-Ceasing**

A practitioner who has attained mental liberation gains the capacity to thoroughly observe and understand the fundamental truths of existence. They can clearly perceive the impermanent nature of the world and directly witness the phenomena of arising and ceasing within it. This observation is not intellectual but a direct, penetrating insight into reality.

**The Transcendence of Attachment and Conditioned Phenomena**

The defining characteristic of a liberated mind is the complete transcendence of attachment. This means the practitioner does not cling to any state, perception, spiritual fruit, or conditioned phenomenon. Even while diligently practicing or after achieving spiritual attainments, the liberated individual remains unstained and unbound by them. This state ultimately leads to nirvana, where afflictions are fully extinguished and the cycle of birth and death is transcended.
