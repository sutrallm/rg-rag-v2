### Title: The Role of Greed in Spiritual Practice and the Path to Liberation  

### Summary:  
Greed is depicted as a destructive force that pollutes the mind, binding individuals to the cycle of birth and death. It is likened to a poisonous snake or stagnant water, nurturing harmful attachments and obstructing spiritual progress. Practitioners must overcome greed through meditation, mindfulness, and wisdom to achieve clarity, peace, and liberation. The inevitability of death serves as a motivating reminder of life's impermanence, urging practitioners to eliminate greed and seek enlightenment.  

### Insights:  

**Greed as a Spiritual Obstacle**  
Greed is portrayed as a harmful obsession that consumes the mind and body, likened to a poisonous snake or stagnant water. It binds individuals to the cycle of birth and death, hindering spiritual progress. Practitioners must detach from greed to purify the mind and achieve liberation.  

**Meditation and Mindfulness as Tools for Overcoming Greed**  
Meditation and mindfulness are essential practices for eliminating greed and its attachments. By cultivating wisdom, practitioners can cut off the source of greed, achieving inner clarity and balance.  

**The Role of Death in Motivating Practice**  
The inevitability of death, symbolized by the concept of 死魔, serves as a reminder of life's impermanence. This awareness motivates practitioners to overcome greed and seek liberation from the cycle of birth and death.  

**Greed’s Binding Effect on Liberation**  
Attachment to desire, embodied by greed, prevents practitioners from achieving true liberation. Overcoming this attachment is a critical step on the path to enlightenment and the ultimate goal of 涅槃.  

**The Necessity of Eliminating Greed for Spiritual Growth**  
The eradication of greed is fundamental to spiritual practice. It is likened to cutting off stagnant water to remove insects, emphasizing the need to eliminate greed to achieve mental purity and progress toward enlightenment.
