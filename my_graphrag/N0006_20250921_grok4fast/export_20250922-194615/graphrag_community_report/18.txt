### Realization of No-Self and Liberation Through Contemplation

#### Executive Summary
The text outlines the profound realization of no-self as a core truth applying to all phenomena, dharmas, body, and mind, which lack permanent or inherent existence. This understanding, one of the four marks alongside impermanence, suffering, and emptiness, is achieved through practices such as meditation, wisdom cultivation, and contemplations on body and dharmas. These methods purify the mind, sever attachments, uproot afflictions like greed and hatred, eliminate suffering, and break the cycle of samsara, leading to nirvana and complete freedom for enlightened individuals and awakened beings.

#### No-Self Across All Phenomena
All conditioned things, dharmas, physical and mental elements, body, and the world lack a permanent, independent, or inherent self or ego, forming a fundamental characteristic observed through direct insight by wise and enlightened individuals. This realization arises from observing the arising and passing of body and mind phenomena, revealing the transient and empty nature of existence without any enduring core.

#### Attainment Through Mindful Practices
No-self is attained via mind liberation, meditation, non-negligence, sustained effort, and wisdom, particularly through contemplations on body and dharmas in awakened states. These practices involve reflective observation of impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and selflessness in body and mind, enabling penetration of these truths and culminating in purification of the heart.

#### Characteristics of Dharmas
Dharmas encompass all phenomena and principles in the world, inherently marked by impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and no-self, as discerned through wisdom. They are empty of inherent existence and characterized by transient events of arising and passing, a perception that awakened beings and saints hold as central to their understanding.

#### Role of Wisdom in Penetration
Wisdom discerns and fully penetrates the no-self nature in all dharmas and phenomena, recognizing their emptiness, impermanence, and absence of permanent ego. Cultivated through contemplations on suffering, emptiness, and no-self, it dispels ignorance, severs craving and attachments, uproots afflictions such as greed, hatred, and delusion, and purifies the mind for ultimate liberation.

#### Effects on Afflictions and Attachments
Realizing no-self severs greed, cuts off attachments to phenomena, eliminates clinging to aggregates and bases, and uproots the roots of all afflictions. This breaks delusions, detaches from temptations, and eradicates suffering, providing complete freedom from the cycle of rebirth, birth, aging, sickness, and death, beyond samsara.

#### Contemplation on Body and Mind
Body contemplation observes the no-self nature of the body, composed of four elements, to abandon attachments, while mind contemplation reflects on its impermanent, suffering, empty, and selfless characteristics to detach from clinging. These direct observations of arising and passing in body and mind purify the heart, gain a pure mind, and lead to insight that eliminates attachments.

#### Path to Nirvana for Awakened Beings
Awakened beings and enlightened individuals realize the no-self essence by directly seeing these truths in all dharmas, including worldly ones, through meditative insight. This realization grants eternal tranquility, transcends suffering, and achieves nirvana, with saints observing dharmas through wisdom as a core quality, resulting in heart liberation and freedom from all afflictions.

#### Breaking Attachments via the Four Marks
Sustained contemplation on the four marks—impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and no-self—breaks attachments, severs all afflictions, and discerns the selfless aspect of body and mind. Non-negligence in these practices enables perception of no-self as one of the three marks, while dharma contemplation leads to realizing no-self in all dharmas, fostering detachment and ultimate liberation.
