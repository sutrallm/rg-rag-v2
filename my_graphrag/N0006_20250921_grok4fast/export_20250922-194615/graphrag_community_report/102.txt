### Foundations of Mindfulness and the Three Trainings in Buddhist Practice

#### Executive Summary
The provided material outlines the three trainings of morality, concentration, and wisdom as essential for noble ones to eradicate afflictions like greed, hatred, and delusion, achieving ultimate liberation. Central to this is the practice of the four foundations of mindfulness—body, feelings, mind, and dharmas—through continuous internal and external observation of phenomena in various postures and states. These practices foster awareness of impermanence and impurity, enabling purification of the mind, distancing from attachments and worldly disturbances, and cultivating inner peace for mental liberation.

#### Three Trainings for Eradicating Afflictions
Morality, concentration, and wisdom form the core trainings fulfilled by noble ones to completely eliminate greed, hatred, delusion, and other afflictions, leading to ultimate liberation. These trainings collectively address the root causes of mental impurities, with wisdom providing clarity into the impure nature of all dharmas, ensuring comprehensive purification across emotional and cognitive states.

#### Structure of Mindfulness Foundations
The four foundations—body, feelings, mind, and dharmas—involve sustained observation of internal and external phenomena to maintain awareness and purify the mind from greed, hatred, and delusion. Dharmas contemplation specifically covers aggregates, hindrances, and senses, completing the framework and directly contributing to mind liberation through diligent practice.

#### Body Contemplation Practices
Continuous observation of the body in postures such as walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, along with internal and external aspects, reveals its impermanence and impurity. This practice eliminates attachments and delusions about the body, with practitioners maintaining mindfulness without clinging, thereby countering greed and fostering detachment.

#### Feelings Contemplation for Emotional Balance
Observing the arising and passing of painful, pleasant, and neutral sensations internally and externally prevents being swayed by feelings, distancing from attachment to pleasure and aversion to pain. By recognizing impermanence in sensations, this contemplation counters greed through non-attachment to pleasant states and hatred through avoidance of aversion, promoting mental purity.

#### Mind Contemplation and State Observation
Examining mental states—such as with or without greed, anger, distraction, or concentration—internally and externally uncovers their impermanent nature, preventing delusion by emotions. This practice directly addresses anger by observing states with and without it, understands greed's transience, and cultivates calm, enabling the practitioner to avoid being led by fleeting mind conditions.

#### Role of Afflictions in Purification Process
Greed, hatred, and delusion serve as primary targets for mindfulness practices, with observation of body, feelings, mind, and dharmas purifying the mind and distancing from these afflictions. The three trainings fully eradicate them, while specific contemplations like feelings address aversion (hatred) and attachment (greed), and mind contemplation overcomes emotional delusion, leading to complete mental freedom.

#### Benefits of Diligent Practice
Sustained engagement in mindfulness foundations grants inner peace, akin to steady progress in an unobstructed path, and enables staying away from worldly disturbances and laxity. Practitioners achieve mental purification and liberation by observing phenomena continuously, embodying the three trainings to eradicate afflictions and realize impermanence across all aspects of experience.
