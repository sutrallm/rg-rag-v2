### Guidelines for Refining Buddhist Scriptures and Related Doctrinal Writings

#### Executive Summary
The provided material outlines a structured approach to refining ancient or classical writings focused on Buddhist doctrines, emphasizing translation into plain, modern, simplified Chinese for everyday use, while systematically removing narrative elements like figures, locations, and poetic conclusions. Core doctrinal teachings are preserved through direct restatements, with figurative language and conversations transformed into straightforward explanations of fundamental principles. Formatting involves numbering sections for clarity, though the expected doctrinal material was not included in the submission.

#### Translation to Plain, Modern Chinese
All specified writings, including sutras and connected documents, must undergo conversion into an accessible, everyday form of Chinese to improve readability, serving as the primary style for the entire refinement process.

#### Removal of Figures and Persons
References to individuals, figures, or people appearing in sutras or similar documents are systematically eliminated to streamline the content and focus solely on doctrinal substance.

#### Elimination of Geographical References
Any mentions of locations or places within the material are deleted to maintain emphasis on universal principles rather than specific settings.

#### Omission of Concluding Poetic Verses
Poetic chants, stanzas, or verses at the end, known as gatha, are removed entirely from the final version of processed sutras or related writings to avoid extraneous elements.

#### Preservation of Doctrinal Nuances
Detailed aspects of religious or philosophical instructions, including specific teachings, are kept intact without alteration, ensuring the essence of Buddhist law or dharma remains fully represented.

#### Conversion of Figurative Language
Illustrative expressions or metaphors are reorganized, simplified, and rephrased into direct statements that elaborate core principles, transforming indirect imagery into clear expositions of doctrinal truths.

#### Restructuring of Conversational Parts
Spoken elements or dialogues are repurposed into straightforward doctrinal statements, rephrased as explanations of key ideas to directly convey philosophical meanings without narrative form.

#### Numbering and Clarity in Section Markers
Headings, section names, or markers are reformed into sequential numeric lists, refined for precision, and organized numerically to enhance overall structure and accessibility.

#### Focus on Core Principles
Metaphors and dialogues are directly transformed into elaborations of fundamental Buddhist truths, such as dharma, prioritizing reorganization that highlights their doctrinal significance over original phrasing.

#### Absence of Expected Doctrinal Material
The submission anticipated full scriptures or similar writings following a designated marker but provided none, leaving the refinement process applicable only to the described guidelines rather than actual content.

#### Uniform Application Across Writings
Rules for translation, removal, conversion, retention, and formatting apply consistently to both Buddhist sutras and any associated documents with doctrinal themes, ensuring standardized handling.
