### Zhi Hui's Integration with Buddhist Contemplative Truths for Liberation

#### Executive Summary
Zhi Hui emerges as a central wisdom cultivated via mindfulness and Zen Xiu, enabling discernment of Wu Chang across body, feelings, mind, and dharmas, alongside Ku, Kong, Wu Wo, and impurity. This discernment, grounded in Zen Ding, severs Zhi Zhuo to realms like Se Jie and Wu Se Jie, results such as Cheng Guo, and stages in Jing Jie, ultimately uprooting afflictions for Nirvana. Contemplations of aggregates, hindrances, and sense bases reveal arising, cessation, and conditioned impermanence, fostering Xin Jie Tuo among Sheng Zhe and Xing Xiu Zhe.

#### Wisdom Arising from Meditative Cultivation
Zhi Hui develops through Zen Xiu and mindfulness, allowing practitioners to perceive the arising and passing of phenomena without clinging. This process, supported by Zen Ding, illuminates truths like impermanence in the five aggregates, five hindrances, and six sense bases, as well as their internal and external conditions. Sheng Zhe embody this wisdom to dwell in Jing Qui Zhuang Tai, freely navigating Sheng Si De Tong Ku and worldly pains.

#### Discernment of Impermanence in Core Elements
Wu Chang manifests as the transient essence of body, feelings, mind, and dharmas, observed through their conditioned arising and cessation. Body contemplation highlights internal and external impermanence; feelings reveal it in painful, pleasant, and neutral sensations; mind uncovers it in states like greed, anger, distraction, and calm. Xin Jie Tuo further enables Xing Xiu Zhe to recognize this transience, breaking attachments and realizing truth.

#### Contemplation of Suffering and Its Eradication
Zhi Hui directly contemplates Ku, the suffering inherent in phenomena, to escape worldly bindings toward Nirvana. By addressing impurity in all dharmas, this wisdom severs the roots of afflictions, preventing rebirth and fundamental causes of pain. The combination uproots Zhi Zhuo, allowing liberated minds to pursue Cheng Guo without bondage.

#### Perception of Emptiness and No-Self
Kong and Wu Wo form essential truths discerned via Zhi Hui in meditation, revealing the empty and non-self nature of all dharmas. This perception eradicates attachments, achieving unconditioned freedom. Wise practitioners apply this to transcend clinging, viewing these as liberating elements that eliminate suffering's core.

#### Avoidance of Clinging to Meditative Realms and Stages
Jing Jie, Se Jie, and Wu Se Jie represent spiritual stages and realms pursued without attachment by those with Zhi Hui. Even in seeking enlightenment or liberation, Xing Xiu Zhe and Sheng Zhe avoid bondage to these, ensuring complete freedom. Zhi Hui facilitates non-clinging to results and states, transcending meditative attainments entirely.
