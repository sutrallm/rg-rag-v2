### Progression of Meditative Absorption: Chu Zen, Er Zen, San Zen, and Si Zen

### Executive Summary
The four levels of meditative absorption form a sequential path toward mind purification and deeper concentration. Chu Zen establishes initial focus through applied thoughts, rapture, and pleasure. Er Zen refines this by eliminating thoughts for internal clarity and intensified joy. San Zen advances to equanimity by detaching from rapture while preserving mindfulness and pleasure. Si Zen completes the progression with pure equanimity, free from both pleasure and pain, serving as the foundation for spiritual development. Each stage builds directly on the prior one, progressively shedding distractions to enhance stillness and awareness.

### Foundational Elements in Chu Zen
Chu Zen initiates meditative absorption with Xun Si, comprising initial and sustained thoughts that foster rapture and pleasure. These elements enable gradual purification by freeing the mind from distractions, establishing focus and joy as core components of early concentration.

### Refinement in Er Zen
Building on Chu Zen, Er Zen discards Xun Si to introduce Nei Jing, an internal clarity that deepens rapture and pleasure without reliance on applied thoughts. This shift enhances concentration by promoting a purer, thought-free state of joy, marking a progression toward greater mental stillness.

### Equanimity Achieved in San Zen
Advancing from Er Zen, San Zen incorporates Li Xi, a detachment from rapture, while maintaining mindfulness, awareness, and bodily pleasure. This allows the mind to abide in a stable, non-scattered condition, further purifying it by replacing rapture with balanced equanimity.

### Culmination in Si Zen
Si Zen represents the highest absorption, evolving from San Zen by eliminating both pleasure and pain to achieve pure equanimity and mindfulness. This final stage embodies complete purity and stability, providing the ultimate foundation for spiritual practice without the influences of sensory states.

### Sequential Abandonment of Mental Factors
The progression from Chu Zen through Si Zen involves systematically shedding elements like Xun Si in the second level and rapture in the third, leading to Nei Jing, Li Xi, and ultimately equanimous purity. This pattern underscores a deliberate refinement process that intensifies focus, reduces scattering, and elevates the mind to transcendent stillness.
