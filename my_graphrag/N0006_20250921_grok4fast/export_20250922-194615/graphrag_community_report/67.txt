# Principles of Mindfulness in Buddhist Contemplation

## Executive Summary
Mindfulness practice centers on the body as the primary focus, using breath and postures to cultivate awareness of impermanence, suffering, impurity, and non-self, leading to disenchantment and reduced attachment. Feelings are observed in their arising and ceasing to prevent clinging, while non-self contemplation highlights the absence of permanence across phenomena, promoting diligence and liberation. The foundations of mindfulness encompass systematic observation of the body, feelings, mind states like greed and hatred, and dharmas such as hindrances, culminating in a pure, concentrated mental state free from distraction.

## Contemplation of the Body for Disenchantment
The body serves as the foundational object in mindfulness, observed through breath's in-and-out movement, postures, and internal elements like muscles and bones. This practice reveals the body's impermanence, impurity, suffering, and non-self nature, generating disenchantment by underscoring its transient and unsatisfactory qualities. Repeated focus on these aspects reduces attachment and fosters a non-scattered concentration, enabling transcendence of worldly bindings through persistent awareness.

## Observation of Feelings to Break Attachment
Feelings manifest as pleasant, painful, or neutral sensations that arise and pass away, demanding direct, non-clinging awareness to disrupt their hold on the mind. By mindfully noting their impermanent flow without aversion or greed, practitioners prevent the binding influence of these states, promoting freedom from reactive patterns. This approach integrates with broader contemplation, enhancing overall diligence by highlighting the futility of grasping at ephemeral experiences.

## Understanding Non-Self for Liberation
Non-self denotes the lack of a permanent essence in the body and all phenomena, emerging from scrutiny of impermanence and suffering. Contemplated in conjunction with emptiness, it inspires unwavering practice by exposing the pointlessness of attachment, leading to dispassion and escape from samsaric cycles. This realization diminishes negligence, encourages non-attachment to sensory and mental objects, and directly supports liberation by dissolving the illusion of a fixed identity.

## Role of Breath and Postures in Maintaining Focus
Breath acts as the central anchor for mindfulness, with awareness of its rhythmic movement ensuring a steady, undistracted mind free from scattering or clinging. Postures and the body's internal composition complement this by providing tangible points for contemplation, starting with breath to build initial concentration and progressing to deeper insights into suffering and impurity. This methodical progression sustains pure mental states, underpinning the entire framework of body-centered practice.

## Examination of Mind States and Dharmas for Comprehensive Insight
The foundations extend to observing the mind's conditions, such as greed, hatred, and delusion, alongside dharmas including hindrances and fetters, to discern their transient impact. This culminates in a holistic awareness that parallels body and feelings contemplation, reinforcing non-self by revealing the mind's impermanent flux. Such observation dispels attachment across all domains, fostering diligence and a unified path toward unbinding from cyclic existence.
